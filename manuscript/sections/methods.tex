\section*{Methods}

\subsection*{Panel}

Predictions were run for 48 G-protein-coupled receptors --- 40 Class~A, 4
Class~B and 4 Class~F --- under two input conditions and on four co-folding
backbones, giving 380 receptor $\times$ backbone $\times$ arm cells and 9{,}490
scored predictions. Cells hold a median of 25 predictions (minimum 20; the
Boltz-2 cognate arm is short 10 rows, a documented gap).

Each receptor is paired with deposited active- and inactive-state reference
structures, and every receptor on the panel has both: the requirement for a
scorable state gap is what the panel has in common.
\textbf{[PI: was the panel every receptor meeting that requirement, or a subset
of them --- and if a subset, on what basis? The distinction decides whether this
is a census or a sample, and no selection rule is recorded in the delivered
data.]} The reference set comprises \textbf{[PI: 98 empirical / 89 as
previously stated]} unique Protein Data Bank entries across the panel --- 44 in
an active-role and 54 in an inactive-role assignment --- drawn from a total
reference collection of 167 entries. The three denominators are reconciled in
Table~S3. This denominator must be stated wherever the reference set is
enumerated, because the panel (48), the panel-PDB set (98) and the full
collection (167) are all defensible counts of different things.

\subsection*{Conformational predicates}

Two intrinsic geometric measurements are computed on each predicted structure
alone. Neither is a comparison to a reference.

\texttt{d\_npxxy\_oh} is the hydroxyl--hydroxyl distance between Tyr5.58 and
Tyr7.53. On activation Tyr7.53 of the NPxxY motif rotates into the receptor core
and packs against Tyr5.58: the ``downward movement of Y5.58 in TM5'' enables ``a
water-mediated hydrogen bond with Y7.53 of the NPxxY motif --- forming the
so-called `YY-lock'\,'' \citep[p.~3714]{georgiou2025heterogeneity}, described there
as the defining active-state polar lock. A prediction is called active on this
axis below 9.080~\AA.

\texttt{d\_gpcrdb\_tm6\_tilt\_246\_637\_ca} is a C$\alpha$--C$\alpha$ distance
capturing outward displacement of the cytoplasmic end of TM6, the canonical
activation signature \citep{hilger2020gcgr}. A prediction is called active above 14.932~\AA.

A prediction is called active only when both fire. The two axes are not
independent observations: in forming that lock Tyr7.53 ``adopts the unique
active state conformation \ldots{} strengthening the TM5-TM7 packing and
stabilizing the outward shift of TM6''
\citep[p.~3693]{georgiou2025heterogeneity}, so a structure that satisfies one
and not the other is the case a conjunction is meant to catch.

This conjunction is defined for class~A receptors only, and the two rules do
not travel together. Family~B activation ``involves structural rearrangements
that are similar to family A receptors, including the outward movements of TM5
and TM6,'' but the DRY/ERY, NPxxY and PIF motifs ``are absent in family B
receptors,'' which ``implies that the mechanisms of activation between family B
and family A receptors are substantially different from each other''
\citep[p.~6]{hilger2020gcgr}. Rule~(1) therefore carries over and rule~(2) has
no class~B analogue. For the 4 class~B receptors it is replaced by a TM6 kink
angle below
159.95$^\circ$; for the 4 class~F receptors, where neither substitution is
established, the tilt rule is used alone. Both are reported separately as
instrument scope and neither carries a headline claim.

Thresholds are carried as
row-level columns and were read from the data rather than reimplemented. Both
were derived from 80 tier-1 crystallographic reference rows by a midpoint
objective, and from no prediction. \textbf{Self-classification accuracy on that
set is a derivation-set figure, not a held-out one}, and is reported as such.

Both axes are individually familiar; requiring them jointly is not. Reported TM6
displacements in this literature are anchored at different residues and are
quoted as differences observed between two solved structures rather than as cuts
applicable to an unseen one: an outward swing of some 7--14~\AA{} at 6.26
\citep[p.~3714]{georgiou2025heterogeneity}; 18~\AA{} at 6.42 and 17.4~\AA{} at
6.36 in the same work \citep[pp.~3,~5]{hilger2020gcgr}; and around 14~\AA{} with
no residue named \citep[p.~13]{ye2026multistatebias}. On the NPxxY axis we are not aware of
a reported threshold on any atom pair, nor of a measured Tyr5.58--Tyr7.53
distance. What is new here is therefore not the choice of axes but the
requirement that both fire, at fixed thresholds, on a named atom pair, applied
identically to references and to predictions.

A learned alternative exists and should be named rather than left to a rebuttal.
\citet{paajanen2026activation} derive a single activation coordinate by
unsupervised PCA over aligned C$\alpha$ positions from more than a thousand
class~A structures, using ``only protein backbone coordinates from conserved
structural features of the receptor transmembrane domain'' (p.~3), and place a
Gaussian-mixture cut on it. The two instruments differ in kind rather than in
rigour. Theirs is one panel-wide coordinate whose activation meaning is assigned
after the decomposition, by observing which component separates the
ligand-annotated classes \citep[p.~10]{paajanen2026activation}; ours is two
named interatomic distances at fixed thresholds, reported per axis, which is
what lets a disagreement between the axes be read as a structural fact about one
prediction rather than as a position on a scalar. Neither is free of annotation
end to end --- our thresholds derive from 80 annotated reference rows --- so the
claim here is interpretability and per-axis attribution, not independence from
the labelling.

The thresholds are derived from geometry rather than fitted to a classifier, but
the 80 rows carry state annotations, and the provenance of those annotations
bounds how independent the instrument is: an instrument calibrated on a given
labelling cannot then adjudicate that labelling.

This is not a hypothetical concern. Curated activation-state labels are not
purely geometric --- they are assigned from ``GPCRdb annotations, structural
criteria, and ligand binding status'' \citep[p.~12]{khaleq2026hyaline} --- and
an unsupervised geometric index over 1{,}351 class~A structures recovers
entries ``whose state of activity was originally interpreted incorrectly''
\citep[p.~3]{paajanen2026activation}. The same work notes that many models
predate substantial active-state coverage, so ``it is also not certain that the
active states of the structures used in developing the models were correctly
identified'' (p.~3).

Our predicate follows the geometric pattern rather than the label-inheriting
one: it reads C$\alpha$ positions at GPCRdb generic positions
2$\times$46 and 6$\times$37 and hydroxyl positions at Ballesteros--Weinstein
5.58 and 7.53, using generic \emph{numbering} and not curated \emph{labels}. The residual exposure is the selection of the 80 tier-1
rows themselves. \textbf{[PI: were those rows selected by crystallographic tier,
with labels checked only afterwards, or selected by curated label?]} The first
leaves the instrument independent of the annotation; the second does not, and
would need saying. The same reference set's construct annotation contradicts the
RCSB record on roughly 40\% of evaluable entries, so its labelling is not
assumed reliable elsewhere in this work.
The models do not place water molecules; results are described as consistent
with the water-bridged arrangement, never as the bridge forming.

\subsection*{Prediction protocol}

Four co-folding models were used: Boltz-2, Chai-1, OpenFold3 and Protenix2. Each
received either the receptor sequence alone (apo arm) or the receptor sequence
together with its cognate G$\alpha$ subunit (cognate arm). No reference structure was supplied to any model at any point.
Templates were disabled on all backbones; this is established by launcher static
analysis, source-code defaults and a propagation test rather than by a per-row
record, as the run tables carry no template or MSA field \textbf{[PI: confirm
whether the post-hoc \texttt{runtime\_config} echo can be attached to these
rows]}. Multiple sequence alignments were generated by each backbone's default
pipeline; we do not claim a pinned alignment, as nothing in the released run
tables records the alignment configuration. Twenty-five seeds
were run per condition.

The apo arm is a computational reference condition, not a physical state --- no
ligand, no membrane, and no basal-activity equivalent. Real receptors show
constitutive activity that varies widely between receptors, and their allosteric
coupling differs correspondingly \citep{georgiou2025heterogeneity}; the apo arm
should not be equated with a physiological inactive state.

OpenFold3 seeds were generated as $5 \times 5$ outer/inner after a seeding fix,
so OF3 rows are less independent than the other backbones'. This qualifies any
cross-backbone comparison of correlation magnitudes. Scoring used a single
frozen scorer (version 0.1.0, commit \texttt{04243c45}) recorded per row.

\subsection*{Uncertainty}

The cluster bootstrap is authoritative: 1{,}000 resamples over paralog clusters,
seed 20260909. A receptor-level bootstrap is reported as secondary, in
supplementary tables only. The cluster interval is equal to or wider than the
receptor interval on every headline statistic, by a factor of up to 2.2.

\textbf{The resampling unit differs by campaign and is stated with every
interval.} The 48-receptor panel resolves into \textbf{29} paralog clusters, the
40-receptor panel into \textbf{26}, and the 36-receptor frame into \textbf{24};
the ligand-class campaign resamples \textbf{16}. The correction is weaker than
those counts suggest, because most clusters hold one receptor: \textbf{16 of the
29 are singletons (55\%)} and 14 of the 26 (54\%), so the paralogy correction
acts on 13 and 12 multi-member clusters respectively. All confidence intervals
quoted in the main text and in Tables~2--4 are cluster intervals.

\subsection*{Exclusions}

Five exclusion sets were pre-specified by cause and applied before results were
inspected. They are not a quality filter and are not applied as a union.

E1 (25 rows) removes one cell whose mean pLDDT falls below 50. E2 (4 rows)
removes model-side atom clashes with \texttt{d\_npxxy\_oh} below 2.4~\AA. These
two together remove 29 rows (0.31\%) and are applied everywhere.

E3 (4{,}890 rows) flags receptors whose reference fails its own predicate on a
given axis. \textbf{It is applied per axis} --- \texttt{excl\_E3\_npxxy}
(4{,}690 rows, 24 receptors) or \texttt{excl\_E3\_tilt} (2{,}000 rows, 10
receptors) --- and only where a reference value serves as a denominator or a
regression predictor. It merges two distinct causes with different
consequences, and we report them separately. On the NPxxY axis, 12 receptors
(2{,}295 rows) have no measurable value because the axis is undefined for them
--- EDNRB, for example, carries Leu at 7.53, so an NPxxY hydroxyl distance does
not exist --- while a further 12 receptors (2{,}395 rows) have a measurable
reference that fails the predicate. The first is missing data; the second is an
observation about reference quality. On the tilt axis all 10 flagged receptors
are of the second kind and none is unmeasurable. The E3 union is never applied, and E3 is irrelevant to raw
distributions, predicate firing rates and confidence correlations, which never
touch a reference separation.

E4 (1{,}495 rows) restricts to Class~A; Class~B and~F are reported separately as
instrument scope. E5 (500 rows) marks agonist-only actives and is shown as a
sensitivity contrast, never applied silently.

Every headline statistic was recomputed under every exclusion combination
(Table~5). No sign flip occurs anywhere and no statistic moves by more than
0.5\% from baseline.

\subsection*{Confidence aggregation}

Three aggregations of pLDDT were computed: the whole-complex mean, the mean over
the six state-defining anchor residues 3.50, 3.51, 5.58, 6.30, 6.34 and 7.53,
and the minimum over those anchors. Correlations are computed over approximately
1{,}600 rows and 32 receptors per backbone.

\textbf{The anchor-restricted mean was designated the primary aggregation post
hoc, after all three had been computed.} All three are reported in Table~4. The
disclosure, not the designation, is what the result rests on.

\subsection*{Training-set exposure}

No anti-memorization arm was run in this block, and the exposure is
substantial: of the 43 panel active-state references carrying a deposition date,
\textbf{35 (81\%) predate the Boltz-2 structural cutoff of 2023-06-01}, 19
(44\%) predate Protenix2's 2021-09-30, and 15 (35\%) predate Chai-1's
2021-01-12 \textbf{[PI: confirm the OpenFold3 cutoff]}. The reference active
states are themselves G protein complexes, so supplying a complete cognate
G$\alpha$ to a model trained on such complexes is closer to naming a deposited
structure than a held-out prediction would be.

We therefore make no claim that these predictions are prospective, and none
that they are free of memorization. The relevant evidence is indirect and lies
in the negatives: a model retrieving a remembered complex should reproduce that
complex's geometry, including how far its TM6 moves, yet receptor-to-receptor
amplitude is not reproduced on three of four backbones and only weakly on the
fourth. Retrieval predicts the opposite. This is an argument, not a control; a
date-stratified holdout is required to settle it and is reported separately.

The concern is not hypothetical for this class of model. Predicted conformational
outcomes have been shown to track the apo:holo ratio of available training
structures rather than the ligand supplied, with pLDDT unable to separate binders
from nonbinders \citep{yu2026domainmotion}, and memorization effects are
documented for structure prediction more broadly \citep{swapna2025memorization}.

\subsection*{Data-layer verification}

Both predicate axes were independently recomputed against non-scorer
implementations and agree bit-exactly (Pearson $r = 1.000000$). The predicate
booleans were recomputed from the raw axis values rather than trusted, and cell
counts were reconciled against the run manifest. Fold integrity was checked
independently: the TM6 helicity anchor holds on 96.3\% of rows.

\subsection*{Known data-layer issues}

Four rows carry sub-covalent hydroxyl--hydroxyl distances, which are model-side
clashes rather than measurements, and are excluded as E2. One 25-row cell passed
every assertion in the verification suite because the suite has no pLDDT floor,
and is excluded as E1. The NPxxY threshold was truncated to 9.08 from 9.082,
which changes the call for one borderline row.

\subsection*{Reference-set limitations}

The construct annotation carried with the reference set contradicts the RCSB
record on approximately 40\% of evaluable entries; the denominator for that rate
is \textbf{[PI: 162 empirical / 127 as previously stated]}. Several references
carry fusion partners or engineered residues inside the predicate windows. The
fusion partner is recorded per entry in Table~S1; the engineered-mutation count
is \emph{not} --- that column is empty on every row, and the four specific
mutations known to fall inside a predicate window are named in the block's own
caveat record rather than in the table. Nine references deviate from their expected
predicate call; five of the nine are unclassified as to cause, with the
remainder split between expected biology, curation error, a measurement
artifact, and one entry that could be either. Several entries annotated active
were solved with agonist bound but no transducer and do not form the NPxxY
network, which the predicate reports correctly.

That construct audit is a check on the \emph{receptor} chain, and no check
anywhere in this work reads the sequence of a partner chain. The consequence is
concrete. The active reference for rhodopsin, 4X1H, is annotated in the
reference set as carrying a native $\alpha$5-CT of the G$_t$ donor class; the
deposited peptide is eleven residues, differs from the bovine
G$\alpha_{\mathrm{t}}$1 C-terminus at four of those eleven positions, and is
described by the depositors as a derived peptide. The annotation is wrong, and
it is wrong in a way our procedure could not detect: the RCSB mutation record
for that entity is empty, because the peptide is deposited as its own molecule
rather than as a mutant of the subunit it derives from.

Twenty-five of the forty active references carry that same \emph{native}
annotation, so we compared each of their G$\alpha$ chains against its canonical
UniProt sequence directly
(\texttt{analysis/verify\_partner\_chains.py}). Eighteen could be compared
without alignment ambiguity, and \textbf{fifteen of the eighteen carry
engineered substitutions} --- eleven of them the canonical dominant-negative
signature S47N/G203A/E245A/A326S or a positional variant of it. The annotation
is therefore wrong far more often than it is right. Two further results decide
how much that matters. First, \textbf{none of those substitutions falls inside
the $\alpha$5 C-terminal 21 residues.} G$\alpha_{\mathrm{i}}$1 is 354 residues,
so the segment the title concerns is 334--354, and every substitution we found
lies outside it; the $\alpha$5-CT is identical to the canonical sequence in all
eighteen. 4X1H remains the only reference in the set whose $\alpha$5-CT itself
departs from the native sequence. Second, RCSB's own \texttt{pdbx\_mutation}
record is empty for ten of the fifteen, so a check built on that metadata rather
than on the deposited sequence would have passed two thirds of them --- which is
the same failure that hid 4X1H, and the reason the construct-contradiction rate
quoted above should be read as a floor rather than an estimate. The remaining
seven of the twenty-five are fusions or chimeras whose length differs from the
canonical sequence, and we report them as unchecked rather than assigning them a
verdict a length-mismatched comparison cannot support. No claim in this work
rests on the provenance of a reference's partner chain.

\subsection*{Partner-composition campaign}

A second campaign varies the partner rather than removing it. Its panel is
\emph{exactly} the 40 Class~A receptors of the first --- the two are nested, with
no receptor added and only the 4 Class~B and 4 Class~F receptors dropped --- and
it is scored by the same instrument, under four input arms on the same four
backbones: \textbf{apo}, no partner; \textbf{decoy}, the receptor's own cognate
G$\alpha$ subunit with the last 9--11 residues of the $\alpha$5 C-terminal tail
scrambled; \textbf{shuffled}, an intact G$\alpha$ from a family outside the
receptor's cognate and secondary coupling classes; and \textbf{cognate}, the
correct intact subunit. \textbf{Every partner arm supplies a complete
G$\alpha$ subunit.} The $\alpha$5 C-terminal 21-mer is a region we measure and
draw, not an input in either campaign.

Construct identity is verified by content rather than by generator version:
each decoy is length-matched to its parent and byte-identical over the first
339--349 residues, with the edit confined to the $\alpha$5-CT tail at a median
Hamming distance of 9.5, and each shuffled donor is checked against the
receptor's coupling record to lie strictly outside its cognate and secondary
classes. Both hold on 40 of 40 receptors. Only three distinct donor sequences
appear across the 40 shuffled arms.

Sampling is five dispatch seeds $\times$ ten samples per seed in every
receptor $\times$ arm $\times$ backbone cell, 32{,}000 predictions over 640
cells with no cell short and no seed collision. This is a declared
strengthening of the pre-registered five $\times$ five and was fixed before
dispatch, not after inspecting results.

The predicate is the one defined above, applied unchanged, with the NPxxY
threshold at the value the rows carry (9.08~\AA{}) and the tilt threshold at
14.932~\AA{}. Four receptors --- EDNRA, EDNRB, GRPR and HRH3 --- carry leucine
rather than tyrosine at 7.53, so the NPxxY axis is undefined for them by
biology rather than by data loss; every two-instrument number is computed on
the remaining 36 and states that frame, because the two denominators differ by
0.06--0.09 on conditional activation rates. Engagement is a separate,
non-predicate quantity: the distance from the $\alpha$5 tip to R3.50, thresholded
at 20~\AA{}. That cutoff is permissive --- roughly four times the cognate median
insertion depth of 12.19~\AA{} --- so engagement is reported alongside the
measured depth rather than as a bare Boolean.

Uncertainty is a cluster bootstrap over paralog clusters, 1{,}000 resamples,
resampling 26 clusters over the full 40 receptors and \textbf{24} on the
36-receptor frame, because excluding the four NPxxY-undefined receptors removes
the endothelin and bombesin clusters entirely;
clusters are resampled rather than receptors so that paralogues do not enter as
independent evidence. Receptor-level intervals are 0.90--1.26$\times$ the width
and change no claim's status; they are not reported. \textbf{The cluster map was
reconstructed during the audit from standard receptor-family taxonomy rather
than recovered from the run}, because no canonical map was found on disk.

Every row is scored against one pinned reference set
(SHA-256 \texttt{6ee2cad8}), carried in each row and recovered from the
scoring-time commit; the working copy at HEAD is a superset and was not used.
No reference structure is supplied to any prediction.

Templates were off on all four backbones. The evidence is launcher static
analysis and upstream defaults, with an explicit \texttt{--use-templates false}
on one backbone; a sweep of 20 job-status records, five per backbone, found no
\texttt{runtime\_config} block and no template-related key at any depth.
\textbf{A per-row runtime echo is not present on these outputs}, so this is
configuration evidence, not per-prediction confirmation --- the same evidence
class stated for the first campaign.

Where structures are shown, superposition is on TM1--TM5 and TM7 C$\alpha$ with
\textbf{TM6 excluded}, because including the helix whose displacement is the
subject partially cancels it; the scorer's own RMSD used a 7TM selection, and
the two differ deliberately.

\subsection*{A limitation of the curated reference set}

Within this Class~A reference set there is \textbf{no native heterotrimeric
G$_\mathrm{s}$ complex}. Every Class~A G$_\mathrm{s}$ active reference in it is
mini-G, chimera or nanobody-stabilised; 15 of 40 active references (37.5\%) are
non-native by this criterion. ADRB2's active reference here is 4LDE, and 3SN6
exists and was not selected.

This is a property of \emph{this curation}, not of the deposited record, and it
is stated as such. It bounds one claim in particular: any statement about
G$_\mathrm{s}$-anchored reference geometry on TM6 tilt is a statement about
engineered constructs. The reference-bias caveat does not, however, localise to
the cell that carries the identity-blindness result, which is 20 of 24
native-referenced and is reported both ways.

\subsection*{Ligand-class campaign}

A third campaign holds the transducer condition fixed and varies the ligand.
Forty Class~A receptors were dispatched; \textbf{36 landed} in the primary
corpus. The four losses are systematic rather than random and are named: two
receptors whose reference pair is non-human and failed a species-matching step,
and two whose inactive reference is stabilised by an allosteric modulator rather
than an orthosteric antagonist, which makes the ligand-class contrast undefined
for them.

Each prediction carries a ligand of declared role --- full agonist, neutral
antagonist, or a decoy drawn to match physicochemical properties without
pharmacological relationship --- under the same apo and cognate arms and the
same four backbones as above, against the same pinned reference set.

\textbf{The readout is continuous, and deliberately not the activation
predicate.} For each prediction we compute the C$\alpha$ RMSD of the
ligand-binding pocket against that receptor's active reference and against its
inactive reference, and take the difference. The 2$\times$2 contrast is then
ligand role crossed with reference role, evaluated within receptor and averaged
across the 23 receptors carrying all four cells. Uncertainty is the cluster
bootstrap used throughout this work, here over the \textbf{16} paralog clusters
spanning those 23 receptors, 5{,}000 resamples, seed 1234. Receptor-level
intervals are computed and reported as secondary.

\textbf{The activation predicate is not used as a Block~C readout and its
absence is not an oversight.} On this panel it is floor-pinned in the apo arm
and ceiling-pinned in the cognate arm, with roughly 65\% of receptor $\times$
backbone $\times$ ligand-class cells sitting on one boundary or the other. A
predicate built to separate conformational states cannot resolve a contrast
between two ligands that both leave the receptor in the same state.

The prospective test is a leave-one-receptor-out classifier over pocket and axis
features, trained to call ligand class from a single predicted structure, with
each receptor's own reference excluded from its training fold. It runs on the
15 receptors that survive that exclusion. Its threshold was pre-registered, as
was the ordinal recovery test reported alongside it.

Ligand placement was audited over all 40{,}000 predictions by the distance from
the ligand centroid to the pocket centroid, binned as in-pocket at or below
8~\AA, entrance-bound between 8 and 15~\AA, and off-site above 15~\AA.
\textbf{Entrance-bound is adjudicated a valid pose}, not a failure: a
substantial fraction of peptide-receptor complexes bind at the extracellular
vestibule by biology. Off-site rates are reported per arm and per ligand type,
never pooled across the two, because peptide ligands are off-site by this
definition on 66.5\% of rows against 1.52\% for small molecules in the same
cells.

\textbf{Reproducibility, stated accurately.} Inputs and analysis outputs are
pinned by SHA-256 and those hashes were checked. \textbf{There is no functional
rebuild-and-diff harness for this campaign}; we verified file integrity, not
that re-running the analysis reproduces the numbers. Two of the campaign's
source corpora were not delivered with its analysis bundle, so the two
surviving positive results could be checked for internal consistency across the
shipped summaries but could not be recomputed from the underlying rows.
