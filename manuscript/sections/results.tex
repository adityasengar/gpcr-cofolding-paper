\section*{Results}

\subsection*{The instrument, and its calibration}

Before applying them to predictions, both measurements were run on the deposited
reference structures, where the answer is already known
(Fig.~\ref{fig:f1}). Across 168 reference
rows --- 167 unique PDB entries, one of which is scored twice because it serves
two panel receptors --- the two-instrument predicate returned the expected call
on 159 and deviated on nine, five annotated active and four annotated inactive.
\textbf{This is a derivation-set figure, not a held-out one}: the thresholds
were fitted on 80 of these same reference rows, so 159/168 measures internal
consistency rather than generalisation.

The deviations are informative rather than a nuisance. Several entries annotated
active were solved with an agonist bound but no transducer, and these never form
the NPxxY network: the hydrogen-bond arrangement requires G$\alpha$ clamping, so
the predicate declines to call them active. That reading is supported
directly by experiment: the TM5/TM6 movement ``is observed only when both the
agonist and the G protein or mimetic Nb are present; no movement of TM5, TM6 is
observed when only the agonist is present'' \citep[p.~3711]{georgiou2025heterogeneity}.
Others reflect construct
engineering inside the measurement window rather than receptor conformation.
Five of the nine deviations are unclassified as to cause; the remainder split
between expected biology, curation error, a measurement artifact, and one entry
that could be either. We report the unclassified fraction rather than resolving
it by assertion.

The practical consequence is that ``active structure'' is not one thing. A panel
built on the assumption that every entry annotated active is geometrically
equivalent would inherit that heterogeneity silently (Fig.~1).

\subsection*{Partner-conditioned activation across four backbones}

Given the receptor sequence alone, predictions are predominantly inactive ---
the collapse onto a single basin, and its state, are established for this
receptor class \citep{heo2022multistate,chib2025gpcrstates}. Given
the receptor sequence together with its cognate G$\alpha$, they are
predominantly active. The direction is consistent on all four backbones across
the 40 Class~A receptors (Fig.~\ref{fig:ba2}, Table~2), though with a five-fold spread in
magnitude --- agreement in sign across four models is one bit of information, and
Chai-1's $+1.05$~\AA{} against Protenix2's $+5.31$~\AA{} should not be read as
four independent confirmations of a single effect size. It behaves as a switch rather
than a graded shift: 111 of 160 apo cells never fire the predicate on any of 25
seeds, while 108 of 159 cognate cells fire on all 25. The two denominators
differ by one because exclusions E1 and E2 remove every row of a single cognate
cell (ACM1 on Protenix2); no apo cell is emptied. The distributions behind
those calls, and the shift in magnitude rather than in call, are in
Fig.~S11.

The median outward TM6 tilt shift on adding the partner is
$+5.04$~\AA{} (Boltz-2, 95\% CI $[3.53, 5.55]$),
$+1.05$~\AA{} (Chai-1, $[0.36, 3.76]$),
$+4.81$~\AA{} (OpenFold3, $[3.95, 5.21]$) and
$+5.31$~\AA{} (Protenix2, $[4.63, 5.74]$).
Pooled row-level predicate-active rates move from 16.0\% to 83.4\% (Boltz-2),
32.7\% to 75.0\% (Chai-1), 24.2\% to 79.9\% (OpenFold3) and 14.5\% to 87.1\%
(Protenix2). All intervals are cluster bootstraps over 26 paralog clusters and
are the authoritative ones; the cluster interval is up to $2.2\times$ wider than
the receptor interval. Chai-1 is the soft predictor throughout --- a smaller
shift with a much wider interval --- which recurs across metrics.

\paragraph{Relation to prior work.}
That a co-folded G protein drives an active-state receptor is not new.
\citet{chiesa2025templatebias} showed on 63 post-cutoff Class~A receptor--G$_s$
pairs that ``the models explicitly modeling G-protein binding'' outperform
template-biased alternatives (p.~6302), and attribute the gain to co-folding
rather than to training-set composition (p.~6305);
\citet{ye2026multistatebias} report that ``large protein partners drive clear
conformational switching between states'' where small molecules do not (p.~2).
Our contribution is not the direction of the effect but the instrument, the
scale, and what the negatives show.

First, and most consequentially, **both prior studies score by RMSD to the
deposited active reference of the same complex.** That is not a stylistic
difference: their procedure cannot be run on a receptor whose active structure
has never been solved, and it makes ``active'' mean proximity to one deposited
answer. We apply an operationalised geometric predicate instead, computed on the
predicted structure alone, which is measurable wherever the sequence is.
Relatedly, 129 of the 145 references in \citet{chiesa2025templatebias} are
ligand-bound, so their target state is agonist-plus-G protein rather than an
intrinsic geometry.

Second, **the instrument is itself calibrated and its failures reported.** We
score the entire reference set with the same predicate used on predictions and
characterise every deviation by cause (Fig.~1b). We are not aware of a prior
study in this area that does so.

Third, **the effect is shown on four independently trained architectures.**
\citet{chiesa2025templatebias} test one model family, and must argue separately
that the advantage is not merely an active-state-biased training set --- the gain
``should not be attributed only to a more recent and active-state biased training
set, but rather to its ability in modeling allosteric effects during the
cofolding process'' (p.~6305). Architecture independence supplies that argument
directly rather than by inference. That study explicitly proposed its benchmark
as ``a baseline'' for newer models (p.~6299); this is that evaluation.

Fourth, **the evidence is distributional rather than a point estimate**: 9{,}490
predictions with cluster bootstraps over 26 paralog clusters, against pooled
means with standard deviations over 31 families.

The arm reported here supplies the complete cognate G$\alpha$ subunit. Whether a
reduced partner --- the $\alpha$5 C-terminus alone --- is sufficient, and whether
occupancy or sequence identity carries the effect, are separate questions
requiring separate arms; they are not addressed by the experiments below and are
reported elsewhere. What follows establishes the measurement, its calibration
against deposited structures, and the scale and limits of the whole-partner
effect.

A contrary result deserves naming. \citet{ku2026promise} find partner-induced
prediction failures substantially more common than ligand-induced ones, and
symmetric rather than one-directional. Three things separate that setting from
ours, and the first is decisive: \textbf{their benchmark excludes the motion we
measure, by construction, and they say so}. Their pair-extraction criterion
``preferentially captures large conformational changes'', with the consequence
that ``localized but biologically important motions, such as GPCR TM6
displacement or transporter pocket rearrangements, may be excluded''
\citep[p.~9]{ku2026promise}. Their partner is also any protein chain forming an
assembly rather than a cognate transducer, and their success criterion is
two-sided --- recovering every known state in a cluster --- which is a strictly
harder target than the directional question asked here.

The apo arm is a computational reference condition, not a physical state: no
ligand, no membrane, no basal-activity equivalent. It should not be read as a
physiological inactive receptor. In the cognate arm the G$\alpha$ is physically
docked rather than merely present: 98.2\% of samples meet the interface-contact
criterion, with a median of 66 receptor--G$\alpha$ contacts.

\subsection*{An orthogonal signature corroborates the direction}

The obvious objection is that two thresholds were chosen and the models happened
to cross them. Predictions were therefore additionally scored on the
P5.50--F6.44 PIF connector --- a different structural rearrangement, in a
different region, never used to set a threshold or to call anything (Fig.~S14).

The strong result is agreement at row level. Of predictions the predicate called
active, 204 of 256 (79.7\%) fall below the reference-inactive median on the
connector; of predictions it called inactive, 205 of 256 (80.1\%) fall above the
reference-active median. The direction matches the references on all four
backbones.

The magnitude is roughly one third of the reference scale (median prediction
delta $-0.56$~\AA{} against a reference delta of $-1.51$~\AA). The interval on
that delta, $[-1.20, +0.03]$, includes zero, as do all four per-backbone
intervals. We therefore report the connector as corroborating in direction and
in row-level agreement, and explicitly not as a signed magnitude effect.

\subsection*{Receptor-specific activation amplitude is not reproduced}

Real receptors do not all activate by the same amount; the allosteric coupling
between the orthosteric site and the cytoplasmic ends of TM5 and TM6 differs
markedly between receptors \citep[p.~3698]{georgiou2025heterogeneity}. If a model
had learned
activation rather than an active-looking shape, receptors whose reference
structures differ greatly should receive large predicted shifts and those whose
references differ little should receive small ones.

On the NPxxY axis --- the well-powered one, with a reference separation SD of
5.22~\AA{} --- the regression of predicted shift on reference separation gives
slopes of 0.04 (Boltz-2), 0.37 (Chai-1), 0.18 (OpenFold3) and 0.26 (Protenix2)
across 28 Class~A receptors (Fig.~3, Table~3). Three of four show no evidence of
amplitude reproduction. Protenix2's interval, $[0.07, 0.55]$, excludes zero: a
weak signed positive slope, roughly a quarter of the expected
receptor-to-receptor scaling, and far below unity. It excludes zero under all
three inclusion sets, so this is not an artefact of the restriction choice. No
backbone approaches a slope of one.

The tilt axis cannot answer the question at all. Its reference separation SD is
1.17~\AA{} against NPxxY's 5.22~\AA, and under Class-A restriction two of four
slopes are negative (Boltz-2 $-0.30$, Chai-1 $-0.66$). A negative slope is not
physically interpretable: it would mean a model moves receptors backwards in
proportion to how far they should move. Combined with the restricted range, this
is the signature of a predictor with essentially no dynamic range. We therefore
report no tilt amplitude slope in either direction and describe the axis as
uninformative for amplitude --- a property of the instrument, not a result about
the models.

This is the load-bearing negative result, and it must be read alongside the
fraction in Table~2, a median over 39 receptors. \textbf{The fraction is a mean;
the regression is a covariance.} A high mean shift with near-zero covariance
against reference separation is exactly what this corpus shows, and the two are
not in conflict.

\subsection*{Confidence is informative only at the state-defining residues}

pLDDT averaged over the whole complex is diluted by the G$\alpha$ chain, by loops
and by termini. Restricted to the six state-defining anchor residues (3.50,
3.51, 5.58, 6.30, 6.34, 7.53) it carries information about conformational
correctness --- on two of four backbones (Fig.~4, Table~4).

Whole-complex against anchor-restricted Pearson correlations are $-0.10$ (null)
against $-0.22$ (signed) for Boltz-2; $+0.07$ (null) against $-0.16$ (null) for
Chai-1; $-0.26$ (signed) against $-0.63$ (signed) for OpenFold3; and $+0.33$
(signed positive) against $+0.07$ (null) for Protenix2.

The Protenix2 row is the finding. Read over the whole complex its confidence
correlates positively with distance from the active state --- apparent
overconfidence --- and that reversal disappears entirely at anchor grain.
OpenFold3 moves the other way, strengthening from $-0.26$ to $-0.63$. Reporting
a single pooled pLDDT correlation would have produced two opposite and equally
misleading conclusions.

\textbf{Those correlations pool the apo and cognate arms while scoring every row
against the active reference, and that pooling --- not the backbones ---
produces the two-of-four split.} An independent re-verification raised this and
we recomputed it. The active reference is the appropriate target for a
cognate-arm row and the wrong target for an apo-arm row, and the two arms differ
in both variables at once: cognate rows carry higher anchor pLDDT and smaller
distances to the active reference. Conditioning on arm removes the split.
\emph{Within the cognate arm}, where the target is the right one, all four
backbones are negative and consistent --- $-0.30$, $-0.43$, $-0.42$ and $-0.16$
for Boltz-2, Chai-1, OpenFold3 and Protenix2 --- and OpenFold3 is no longer an
outlier. \emph{Within the apo arm}, three of four sit at or above zero
($+0.04$, $+0.01$, $+0.10$; OpenFold3 $-0.37$).

\textbf{The direction of the headline survives and its shape does not.}
Anchor-restricted confidence remains a weak negative predictor of distance from
the active state --- no backbone reaches $|r| = 0.45$ even where the reference is
correct --- so confidence still does not track state correctness. But the
per-backbone ranking above, and the claim that this holds on two backbones
rather than four, are artifacts of pooling two arms against one reference.
\textbf{[PI: the two-of-four framing and Table~4 should be restated on
arm-conditional correlations. The recomputation is in
\texttt{analysis/block\_a/DISCREPANCY\_REPORT.md} D-A-25; the choice of which
to report as the headline is not ours to make silently.]}

Confidence scores are not uninformative in general, and the distinction matters.
\citet{zhang2026generalization} report that interface predicted TM-score is ``a
useful decision metric'' for \emph{ligand pose} ($R^2 = 0.49$); their
pLDDT-against-receptor-C$\alpha$ result, $R^2 = 0.046$, is the same null we
report here. What a confidence score predicts well and what it predicts badly
are different questions, and pose accuracy is not conformational state. Others
find no usable relationship between pLDDT and conformation
\citep[p.~5]{bryant2024cfold}, and the same failure appears for peptide
placement \citep{junker2026peptidedesign} and for kinase state
\citep{sun2026kinconfbench}.

This is a finding about method rather than about these four models. On the
evidence here --- four backbones, one receptor class --- we would put it no
higher than a hypothesis worth testing elsewhere: that a confidence score
aggregated over a whole complex can invert relative to the same score read at
the residues that define the property in question. We are not aware of a prior
report of a confidence metric changing sign under re-aggregation.

\subsection*{Partner presence, partner sequence, and partner identity are separable}

The preceding results supply the whole cognate G$\alpha$ subunit or nothing. That
contrast cannot say \emph{what} about the partner the models are responding to.
A second campaign separates it into three steps, on 40 Class~A receptors $\times$
four backbones $\times$ four input arms $\times$ five dispatch seeds $\times$ ten
samples --- 32{,}000 predictions, every one of the 640 receptor $\times$ arm
$\times$ backbone cells delivering exactly 50 rows with five distinct seeds.

The arms are nested. \textbf{apo} supplies no partner. \textbf{decoy} supplies
the receptor's own cognate G$\alpha$ subunit with the last 9--11 residues of the
$\alpha$5 C-terminal tail scrambled --- length-matched and byte-identical to its
parent over the first 339--349 residues, verified by sequence hash on 40 of 40
receptors. \textbf{shuffled} supplies an intact G$\alpha$ from a family the
receptor does not couple to. \textbf{cognate} supplies the correct intact
subunit. Every arm that carries a partner carries a complete subunit; no arm
supplies an isolated peptide, and no arm carries an orthosteric ligand. What
varies across the four is the partner and nothing else.

Under the same two-instrument predicate, the active-call fraction rises
monotonically across those four arms: 0.158 [0.081, 0.256] apo, 0.558
[0.445, 0.664] decoy, 0.809 [0.747, 0.869] shuffled, 0.891 [0.838, 0.942]
cognate, on the 36 receptors whose NPxxY axis is defined, with cluster-bootstrap
intervals over 26 paralog clusters. The ordering holds on all four backbones
independently (Fig.~\ref{fig:bb1}). Both continuous axes move in the same
direction across the same four arms, NPxxY closing and TM6 tilt opening, though
the panel medians shipped with the campaign do not reproduce from the shipped
tables and are not quoted here (Discrepancy D-B-2).

The rise is a property of the models rather than of where we put the threshold.
If the predicate were merely reclassifying rows already sitting on its own
boundary, the partner arms would differ most in the band around the cut. They do
not: within $\pm$0.5~\AA{} of the tilt threshold the cognate arm carries
\emph{less} mass than the shuffled arm on three of four backbones (1.06\%
against 3.33\% on Boltz-2, 0.39\% against 1.89\% on OpenFold3, 0.00\% against
0.06\% on Protenix2). Chai-1 inverts, at 1.83\% against 0.56\%, and that
inversion is one receptor: 33 of OX2R's 50 Chai-1 cognate rows fall in the band,
and removing that receptor alone removes it. The difference between the arms
lives in the far-inactive tail instead --- 9.0\% of shuffled rows sit more than
2~\AA{} below the threshold against 4.1\% of cognate rows --- which is a shift
in the population, not a reclassification at the boundary.

\textbf{The ladder decomposes cleanly into two contrasts, and the shipped
three-term telescoping does not.} Supplying \emph{any} partner, even one whose
$\alpha$5 tail is scrambled, accounts for $+0.400$ [0.334, 0.464] of the total
rise --- 55\%, and this is the apo-to-decoy step. The remaining 45\%,
$+0.333$ [0.242, 0.432], is the decoy-to-cognate step, and \textbf{that step is
the only contrast in this campaign that isolates the $\alpha$5 C-terminus}: the
two arms carry the same subunit, of the same length, from the same G$\alpha$
family, differing only in the eleven C-terminal residues, and differing there by
a permutation that preserves composition. The two steps telescope exactly
($0.400 + 0.333 = 0.733$).

\textbf{We do not report the decoy-to-shuffled step as an $\alpha$5-CT
contribution, and the campaign's own decomposition does.} That step carries the
name \texttt{delta\_a5ct\_sequence\_decoy\_to\_shuffled} and a share of 34\%,
and it is not an $\alpha$5-CT contrast: the shuffled arm is a subunit from a
\emph{different} G$\alpha$ family, so the step changes the tail, the scaffold,
the length and the family at once. Its $+0.252$ [0.179, 0.320] is arithmetically
correct and interpretively confounded, and it is smaller than the clean contrast
it was standing in for.

Within the decoy-to-cognate step, getting the family \emph{right} --- the
shuffled-to-cognate contrast, which does hold the arm type fixed --- adds
$+0.082$ [0.047, 0.127]. On the probability scale that is 11\% of the total; on
the logit scale it is 17.4\%, and both are correct. The gap is not a discrepancy
but a compression: cognate rates sit at or above 0.98 in 24--34 of 40 cells per
backbone, and a probability-scale difference has little room left to move.
Wherever this term appears it is named with its scale
(Fig.~\ref{fig:bb2}).

The family term is also the least robust of the three, and we report it as such.
Its per-backbone logit shares span 14.2\%, 22.9\%, 23.6\% and 10.9\% for
Boltz-2, Chai-1, OpenFold3 and Protenix2 --- a factor of two, not a consensus
--- and only OpenFold3's interval excludes zero on its own
([$+0.46$, $+1.63$]); the other three span it. The panel term signs because the
four are pooled across 26 paralog clusters, not because each backbone signs it.
Getting the family right is the smallest of the three effects, and it is the one
a single backbone cannot see.

\textbf{The models respond to partner presence far more than to partner
identity.} Two thirds of the effect is already delivered by a subunit whose
recognition tail has been scrambled.

Whether the partner is \emph{engaged} is separable from whether the receptor is
\emph{active}, and the interesting cell is the one where they disagree. At a
20~\AA{} $\alpha$5-tip-to-R3.50 cutoff, the cognate arm engages on 0.998 of rows
and is active on 0.893 of those; shuffled engages on 0.967 and is active on
0.835; decoy engages on 0.704 and is active on 0.665. The engaged-but-inactive
decoy cell --- a partner seated in the cavity that has not opened it --- is
populated on every backbone, 1{,}699 rows pooled and 278--540 per backbone
(Fig.~\ref{sfig:engagement}). It is the only cell in the campaign that can support
a statement about chemistry rather than about occupancy.

An axis that never participates in the call corroborates the direction. On the
P5.50--I3.40--F6.44 C$\alpha$ connector, decoy cells that are engaged but not
active sit at 15.42~\AA{} (47 cells), against 15.35~\AA{} for apo and 16.00--16.04~\AA{}
for the active-engaged cognate, shuffled and decoy cells. Engaged-but-inactive
geometry is apo geometry on an axis the predicate never reads. Three of four
backbones sign cleanly; OpenFold3 signs in the same direction at smaller
magnitude (Fig.~\ref{sfig:bbconnector}).

What the models do \emph{not} do is read which family the partner belongs to on
the continuous axes beneath the predicate. On the largest and best-powered
mismatch --- a G$_\mathrm{s}$ donor placed on G$_\mathrm{i}$-coupled receptors ---
the tilt residual against each receptor's own active reference is $+0.024$~\AA{}
[$-0.29$, $+0.30$] across 24 receptors, and $-0.062$~\AA{} [$-0.41$, $+0.21$]
across the 20 whose active reference is a native complex. A pre-registered
alternative, that donors overshoot or undershoot the reference belonging to the
wrong family, is signed in \emph{zero} of nine qualifying stratum $\times$ axis
combinations at 95\% confidence (Fig.~\ref{sfig:residuals}).

Three limits bound all of this, and none is a detail.

\textbf{There is no bulk control.} No arm supplies a second chain of comparable
mass that is not a G$\alpha$. The occupancy term is the largest of the three and
the least controlled: nothing here distinguishes a G protein from a protein.

\textbf{The decoy arm is confounded.} Scrambling the $\alpha$5 tail removes the
recognition sequence and disrupts inter-chain MSA pairing at the same positions,
and on Chai-1 the edit does not reach the model as aligned MSA columns at all.
The fallback ladder that survives this --- apo, shuffled, cognate --- carries the
headline unchanged.

\textbf{One stratum carries the identity-blindness conclusion.} The
G$_\mathrm{s}\rightarrow$G$_\mathrm{i}$ cell is powered at 20 native-referenced
receptors. G$_\mathrm{s}\rightarrow$G$_\mathrm{q}$ collapses to 3 native
receptors with an interval of [$-2.79$, $+0.14$], and
G$_\mathrm{i}\rightarrow$G$_\mathrm{s}$ has no native-referenced receptor at all.
The result is a bulk-reading claim on one well-powered cell, not an equivalence
result, and the interval that supports it spans zero.

AA2AR is anomalous across several of these analyses. The leading explanation,
that its two references are too similar for the instrument to separate, does not
survive: its pocket-C$\alpha$ separation of 1.62~\AA{} ranks 34th of 40, in the
well-separated third of the panel. Pooled quantities are reported with and
without it.

\subsection*{Ligand class is written into pocket geometry, but only where the partner is absent}

The two campaigns above vary the transducer. A third varies the
\emph{ligand}, and asks a question the first two cannot: does a predicted
pocket occupied by an agonist differ in shape from the same pocket occupied
by an antagonist? Forty Class~A receptors were dispatched and 36 landed;
the four losses are systematic and named --- two receptors whose reference
pair is non-human, and two whose inactive reference carries an allosteric
rather than an orthosteric antagonist.

\textbf{The activation predicate used everywhere else in this work does not
function here, and we do not use it.} On this panel it is floor-pinned in the
apo arm and ceiling-pinned in the cognate arm, leaving roughly 65\% of
receptor $\times$ backbone $\times$ ligand-class cells on one saturation
boundary or the other. That is a property of applying a state predicate to a
question about ligand identity, not a failure of the predicate. Every result
in this section is therefore computed on continuous pocket-C$\alpha$ geometry,
and none of these numbers is comparable to the predicate rates reported above.

Measured as the difference between a prediction's pocket-C$\alpha$ RMSD to its
receptor's active reference and to its inactive reference, agonist-occupied
and antagonist-occupied pockets separate in the predicted direction on all four
backbones: $-0.306$~\AA{} on Boltz-2 [$-0.454$, $-0.164$], $-0.137$ on Chai-1
[$-0.216$, $-0.045$], $-0.252$ on OpenFold3 [$-0.405$, $-0.099$] and $-0.184$
on Protenix2 [$-0.277$, $-0.088$], over 23 receptors resampled as 16 paralog
clusters. Chai-1's interval reaches within 0.045~\AA{} of zero and is signed by
a narrow margin.

\textbf{This was intended to hold in the apo arm alone, and the shipped
artefact shows it does not.} The design reason for wanting an apo-only scope is
sound and we still hold it: with a cognate G$\alpha$ in the complex the contrast
cannot be attributed to the ligand, because the partner supplies both mass and a
strong conformational preference of its own. But the filter was not applied. The
2$\times$2 artefact records 2{,}800 rows in each agonist cell and 2{,}300 in
each antagonist cell, on all four backbones, while the campaign's design grid is
exactly 50 predictions per receptor $\times$ backbone $\times$ ligand role
$\times$ arm --- 800 cells at one size, with no exceptions across 40{,}000
scored rows. Apo alone would give $28 \times 50 = 1{,}400$ and
$23 \times 50 = 1{,}150$. Both arms give 2{,}800 and 2{,}300, which is what
shipped.

\textbf{So the interaction above is estimated with both arms pooled, and it is
therefore not yet a statement about the ligand in isolation.} We report it
because the estimate itself is sound --- properly bootstrapped over paralog
clusters, and signed on all four backbones --- and because the direction is the
one the pre-registered ordinal test recovers independently below on an apo-only
population. What we cannot yet say is how much of it survives when the
transducer arm is removed. \textbf{[PI: restating this on the apo arm needs
\texttt{rows.tier3.v2.csv}, which was not delivered; it is ask~1 of the Block~C
data requests and it also blocks the off-site gate. The estimate is not
withdrawn, its scope is.]}

A pre-registered ordinal test, locked before this campaign ran, recovers the
same ordering. Ranking ligand role against the same continuous axis gives a
positive and significant Kendall's $\tau$ on \textbf{65--87\% of receptors}
(Fig.~\ref{fig:bc2}) ---
that is a count of receptors, not a correlation --- and the median $\tau$ itself
is 0.26--0.39 across backbones, on the 23-receptor panel. On the 15-receptor
self-reference-excluded subset the receptor fractions are 67--87\% and the
median $\tau$ 0.20--0.45. Because its threshold was fixed in advance, this test
carries evidential weight the 2$\times$2 does not: it could have failed.

Whether that separation is usable prospectively is a narrower question, and the
answer is narrower still. A leave-one-receptor-out classifier trained to call
ligand class from a single predicted structure reaches an area under the ROC
curve of 0.852 on Boltz-2 and 0.825 on Protenix2, with cluster-bootstrap
intervals of [0.560, 0.974] and [0.528, 0.960] that exclude both chance and a
permutation null. On Chai-1 (0.706) and OpenFold3 (0.656) the same intervals
reach 0.351 and 0.382. \textbf{Those two backbones are inconclusive, not
negative} --- the test does not distinguish them from chance, which is a
different statement from showing they carry no signal. Boltz-2's bootstrap
median, 0.809, sits below its point estimate.

\subsection*{What this campaign set out to establish and did not}

A criterion for knowing in advance which receptors the method would work on was
pre-registered and \textbf{refuted}. The hypothesis was that receptors whose
active and inactive references are further apart would be easier to
discriminate, predicting a positive relationship between reference separation
and per-receptor classifier performance. The measured relationship ran
\emph{negative} on all four backbones, and excluding a single receptor
flattens it to an interval spanning zero. What remains is an empirical list of
receptors on which the method failed, compiled after the fact. \textbf{It
cannot be used to flag a failure in advance}, and we do not present it as
though it could.

Two receptors behave anomalously for unrelated reasons and are reported
separately rather than merged. AGTR1's active reference is stabilised by a
$\beta$-arrestin-biased agonist together with a biased-state-selective
nanobody, with no G$\alpha$ and no G$\beta\gamma$ --- the only such entry among
the 15, and a question about what that reference represents rather than about
the model. CXCR2's inversion appears on OpenFold3 and on no other backbone, and
a structure from that cell shows the ligand correctly seated in the pocket, so
it is a classifier-feature effect rather than a docking failure.

Pose accuracy, where it can be measured against a matched reference, is low:
6.93\% of predictions place the ligand within 3~\AA{} of its reference pose on
the strictly matched subset. Corpus-wide the per-receptor distribution is
bimodal --- median dock rates of 54--56\% on Boltz-2 against 0--18\% on Chai-1
and 0\% on the other two in the same cell --- and pooling across receptors, as
earlier drafts did, concealed that.

Two further points bound what may be concluded. Ligand placement was audited
across all 40{,}000 predictions: 15.1\% of apo-arm and 20.3\% of cognate-arm
ligands sit more than 15~\AA{} from the pocket centroid. Restricting to
small-molecule ligands in the apo arm brings that to 1.52\%, because peptide
agonists sit at a median of 17.5~\AA{} --- peptide receptors bind at the
extracellular vestibule, which is biology rather than a docking failure, and
counting it as error would report 66.5\% where the small-molecule figure is
1.52\%.

\textbf{That reassurance is computed on a wider population than the interaction
it reassures about, and on the interaction's own population the number is
larger.} The 2$\times$2 runs on 23 receptors, with no small-molecule
restriction. Across those 23, \textbf{18.92\%} of apo-arm agonist and antagonist
ligands are off-site, and the two classes are not off-site equally:
\textbf{35.85\% of agonist rows against 2.00\% of antagonist rows}. An asymmetry
in that direction is the exact shape an artifact would take, because a pocket
that never received its agonist is trivially different from one that received
its antagonist.

It is not, however, what produces the interaction. Ranking the 23 receptors by
their apo-arm agonist off-site fraction and correlating that against each
receptor's own median $\tau$ gives Spearman $\rho = -0.241$ ($p = 0.268$,
$n = 23$); splitting them at 50\% off-site separates no difference in $\tau$
either (Mann--Whitney $p = 0.436$, medians 0.282 against 0.386). The receptors
whose agonists are furthest from the pocket are not the receptors carrying the
signal. We report the asymmetry rather than the small-molecule figure, because
it is the one a reader would otherwise find themselves.

Finally, the possibility that the classifier learned reference identity rather
than ligand class is excluded \textbf{by construction rather than by audit}:
although routing labels differ across ligand roles, the reference PDB they
resolve to is identical for every receptor, so there is no reference-identity
information for a classifier to exploit. We did not search for leakage and fail
to find it; the design does not admit it.

\subsection*{Unsteered, the models are not bistable --- they are receptor- and backbone-specific}

Asked to fold a receptor with nothing supplied, and sampled 500 times per cell
rather than 25, the four backbones do not behave like a system with two
accessible basins. They behave like four different systems
(Fig.~\ref{sfig:apolandscape}). On $\beta_2$AR, Chai-1 calls 100\% of 500 apo
samples active and Boltz-2 calls 0\%; on LPAR$_1$ the arrangement inverts and
OpenFold-3 calls 91.8\% active while every other backbone is at or below 0.2\%.
Four cells clear a $+50$ percentage-point margin over the best other backbone on
the same receptor: Chai-1 on CNR2, rhodopsin and $\beta_2$AR, and OpenFold-3 on
LPAR$_1$.

\textbf{Those four cells reproduce on an independent corpus.} Scored on the
first campaign's apo rows at 25 samples per cell --- different predictions,
different run, same instrument --- all four still clear $+50$ points. This is
the strongest single result in the campaign, because it is the only one that
survives a change of corpus, and it says the pattern is a property of a
particular model on a particular receptor rather than of the panel it was
measured on.

\textbf{The claim this campaign set out to make was withdrawn, and the
withdrawal is the methodological point.} An earlier reading of the same tier
reported spontaneous apo bistability from backbone-averaged fractions at 100
samples per cell. Averaged over the four backbones, $\beta_2$AR sits near 50\%
active, which sounds like a receptor sampling two states and is instead two
backbones disagreeing completely. Raising the sample budget fivefold and
refusing to pool across backbones removed the finding. A pre-registered
secondary claim of two continuous basins was dropped on the same evidence: a
dip test fires on 2 of 112 cells, both rhodopsin $\times$ OpenFold-3, which is
a single-cell observation and not a panel-scale one. \textbf{No figure or
sentence in this work pools across backbones}, and this is why.

Three receptors --- CXCR4, GHSR and NPY$_1$R --- converge inactive on all four
backbones, never exceeding 6.6\% on any cell. One further receptor is worth
naming because it explains an artifact in the ligand-class campaign: on CNR2,
100\% of samples fall within 1~\AA{} of \emph{both} the active and the inactive
reference on all four backbones. The two deposited CNR2 structures are
pocket-C$\alpha$ close to each other, so that axis cannot discriminate on this
receptor at all, and the two-instrument predicate is the only instrument here
that can.

\subsection*{Steering works in one direction and not the other}

Supplying a partner that should drive the receptor active does exactly that
(Fig.~\ref{sfig:direction}). The cognate G$\alpha$ positive control
reaches at least 96\% predicate-active on \textbf{14 of 16} receptor $\times$
backbone cells; Boltz-2, Chai-1 and Protenix2 reach 100\% on all four of their
cells, and both misses are OpenFold-3 (58\% on M$_2$, 36\% on the
$\kappa$-opioid receptor). An active-directing nanobody reproduces that effect
on three backbones of four: on M$_2$, the cleanest per-receptor test because
AT$_1$R's apo state is already active-biased, adding the nanobody moves Boltz-2
from 42\% to 100\%, OpenFold-3 from 12\% to 82\% and Protenix2 from 0\% to
100\%. Chai-1 does not move at all.

\textbf{The inactive direction does not work, and the way it fails is
informative.} On the two receptors carrying an inactive-nanobody arm, no
backbone reliably steers inactive. Three of the four $\beta_2$AR cells do not
move; the fourth, Protenix2, inverts to 76\% active
(95\% CI $[61.8, 86.9]$) --- and the shipped structure shows why that is not a
scoring artifact: the inactive nanobody is correctly seated as a 125-residue
chain B, and the receptor underneath it has an NPxxY hydroxyl separation of
4.53~\AA{}, which we measured from the coordinates (Fig.~\ref{sfig:nbstructures}).
The model built an active receptor and docked an inactive-state binder onto it.
On the $\kappa$-opioid receptor all four backbones move \emph{toward} active
under a nanobody that locks the inactive state (Boltz-2 $+44$, Chai-1 $+8$,
OpenFold-3 $+10$, Protenix2 $+6$) --- a systematic prior that reads a nanobody
in chain B as an activating partner, not random noise. \textbf{The flagship cell
supports "approaches half" and not "a majority": Boltz-2 goes from 4\% to 48\%,
and at 50 samples that interval is $[33.7, 62.6]$.} And the whole of this
paragraph is bounded by a confound the campaign could not close:
\textbf{all four nanobody anchor structures were deposited between 2013 and 2020
and predate every training cutoff we could date.} Whether directed inactive
generation is unachievable, or merely unachievable for complexes these models
have already memorised, is not decided here. A search for a post-cutoff
inactive-nanobody complex was attempted and documented; it did not produce a
usable candidate, and it was closed rather than answered.

The instrument is not what failed. The two-instrument predicate was calibrated
on the nanobody anchor structures themselves and behaves correctly on all of
them: 4.21~\AA{} and passing on active M$_2$, 11.16~\AA{} and failing on
inactive $\beta_2$AR, 12.88~\AA{} and failing on the inactive $\kappa$-opioid
receptor.

\subsection*{Starving the MSA moves the predicate on all four backbones, for two different reasons}

Reducing MSA depth raises the predicate-active fraction on every backbone, and
the slopes are the largest effects in the campaign: $-1.68$
(Boltz-2, 95\% CI $[-2.69, -0.81]$), $-0.82$ (Chai-1, $[-2.38, +0.36]$),
$-2.73$ (OpenFold-3, $[-4.37, -1.15]$) and $-2.96$ (Protenix2,
$[-4.68, -1.58]$) percentage points per e-fold of depth. Read as a table of
slopes, the two strongest levers are OpenFold-3 and Protenix2.
\textbf{Read against a second axis, they are the two that are not levers at
all} (Fig.~\ref{sfig:depth}).

\textbf{Boltz-2 is a clean lever; Chai-1 is a muted one; OpenFold-3 leans to
degradation and Protenix2 is mixed and receptor-dependent.} Four independent
lines separate them. The predicate-active fraction rises on all four. The
fraction of samples within 1~\AA{} of the active reference rises 8.7 points on
Boltz-2 and \emph{falls} 20.9 points on OpenFold-3 and 8.7 on Protenix2.
Matched-seed backbone deviation between full depth and depth 8 stays at
1--4~\AA{} on Boltz-2 and reaches 10--14~\AA{} on OpenFold-3 and 16--20~\AA{} on
Protenix2. And median confidence tracks depth most strongly on exactly the two
backbones whose pockets move away from the active reference. Chai-1's slope,
finally, crosses zero under the cluster bootstrap: its point estimate is
negative and its interval is not, so it is reported as a point estimate and
nothing is built on it.

Two cells make the distinction concrete (Fig.~\ref{sfig:levervsdeg}). On
Boltz-2 with rhodopsin, dropping to depth 8 fires the predicate and moves the
pocket \emph{toward} the active reference, 1.25~\AA{} to 0.41~\AA{}. On
Protenix2 with AT$_1$R the predicate fires identically --- NPxxY from 10.93 to
3.40~\AA{}, tilt from 11.19 to 16.78~\AA{} --- while the pocket moves
\emph{away}, 0.76~\AA{} to 1.24~\AA{}, having started closer to active than it
ends. A binary predicate reports these two as the same result. They are not the
same result, and the earlier reading of this tier, which reported MSA depth as a
lever on all four backbones, was withdrawn on precisely this evidence.

\textbf{This bears on a published claim, and the relationship is not simple.}
\citet{xing2025purified} report that ``the successful sampling of alternative
states depends not on MSA depth but on sequence purity'' \citep[p.~3]{xing2025purified}.
On four AF3-lineage co-folding models, with a fixed two-state predicate and 26
receptors, we find depth \emph{does} move the state a model reports --- and that
on half the backbones tested the movement is degradation rather than steering.
Those two findings are compatible, and the second axis is what reconciles them:
if depth is not a mechanism for reaching a genuine alternative state, then a
depth effect on a coarse predicate is exactly what one should expect to see, and
only a pocket-level measurement separates the two. We read our result as a
refinement of theirs rather than a contradiction of it, and the one backbone
where depth survives that second axis --- Boltz-2 --- is the only place we would
defend depth as a lever.

We also do not claim the design as novel. MSA manipulation has been applied to
this architecture family \citep{kalakoti2026afsample3} and co-input partners have
been supplied to it \citep{chiesa2025templatebias,zhang2026generalization}, and
one recent method combines MSA clustering with co-folded binders directly
\citep{cheng2026af3cluster}. What this tier does not do is cross the two: depth
is varied in the apo arm only, so MSA depth and partner presence are never
varied together inside one model here either.

\subsection*{What the directed-generation campaign does not establish}

Four limits bound everything above. \textbf{The memorization confound is the
largest}: every nanobody anchor predates every datable training cutoff, so the
negative result in the inactive direction is not separable from an availability
effect, and OpenFold-3's cutoff we could not date at all. \textbf{One
cross-corpus disagreement is unresolved}: rhodopsin on Boltz-2 reads 38.8\% at
500 samples in the deep-apo tier and 10.0\% at 50 samples in the depth tier's
full-depth rung, a three-sigma gap between two cells that share a scorer, and we
flag it rather than retract either. \textbf{Chai-1's depth slope is unsigned},
as above. And \textbf{the directed tier rests on four receptors} --- four
paralog clusters, at which point a cluster bootstrap adds nothing to a receptor
bootstrap, and the panel is too small for the negative result to be more than
consistent with a general limitation.

To that we add a limit of our own. This campaign delivered its claim sheet, its
caveats, its withdrawal records and its audit reports, and \textbf{none of the
three row-level tables those documents name}. Its 42{,}180 predictions are not
in the bundle we received. Every panel composition, every cluster count, every
exact binomial interval quoted above, and every structural distance we report
was recomputed here; \textbf{no headline fraction and no slope interval could
be}. The pipeline's own audit of those slopes is unusually thorough --- it
refits them from scratch and finds they reproduce only under one predictor
convention --- but an audit that cannot be re-run is a report about a
verification rather than a verification, and the difference is recorded in the
figure captions rather than smoothed away.
