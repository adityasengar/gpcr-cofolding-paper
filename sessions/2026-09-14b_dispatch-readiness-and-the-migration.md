# 2026-09-14b — dispatch readiness, an eight-point audit, and the move off this laptop

## What happened

Aditya brought an external eight-point audit of the redo plan and asked whether it made
sense; then asked to finalise the campaign, decide where it lives relative to
`paper_af3`, and prepare the move to another machine. Nine subagents and a nine-agent
workflow verified the audit and designed the remaining pieces; the code was written
directly.

**The audit is a competent re-reading of `REDO_REFERENCE.md`, not an independent audit.**
Five of its eight points were already written in this repo, several verbatim with the
same proposed fixes. One point is genuinely new and serious (C7's chain-count confound).
Two are wrong in their characterisation (E8.3's "nominal label"; the 13× bracket). Two
understate the problem they name (the ligand MSA is 52 rows, not 22; Pillar 2 has three
unnumbered arms, not one). One is simply out of date — the pocket-RMSD columns it asks
for were added on 2026-09-12.

**The finding that outranks the entire audit came from inventorying their bundle:**
`partner_metrics.py:74`, `pick_ga_chain()` accepts a partner chain only if
`200 <= n <= 500`. Our ladder rungs are 11, 15, 21, 26, 36, ~368 and ~394. **Five of
seven return `None`**, so three partner columns land NaN — silently, on the 21-mer the
paper is named after. Not their bug; their code was written for a campaign where every
partner was a whole Gα. It becomes ours the moment we hand them an 11-mer.

**Then three code items took the campaign from un-dispatchable to three-items-away:**
the recording contract 49 → 79 columns, seeds pinned per receptor, and the partner
sequences shipped as bytes rather than hashes. Plus `run_receipt.py`'s first selftest
and a new layout check.

## What I got wrong and corrected

Named individually, because this is the section a future session needs.

1. **I told Aditya the bundle should be a separate generated `gpcr_redo_dispatch/`
   artefact — numbered folders, `BUNDLE.json`, `verify_bundle.py` — and then reversed
   it.** `redo/inputs/` already *is* the bundle: 67 files, every one hashed and
   generator-attributed. A second serialization of one truth is a second thing that can
   drift, and drift between two copies of one truth is the most common entry in this
   project's failure catalogue. What made `inputs/` feel un-shippable was three missing
   values, not its shape.

2. **I introduced a real bug in the recording contract and the selftest caught it the
   same hour.** I declared `partner_returned_sha256`; `run_receipt.py`'s R2 reads
   `partner_returned` and compares it to `partner_requested`, a construct id. The only
   available pair would have compared `"R3_ct21"` against a 64-hex digest — unequal on
   every row of every delivery, for a reason having nothing to do with the partner.
   Fixed by preferring the sequence-hash pair, which is also the stronger check.

3. **I shipped an rsync command that would have emptied 3,366 files into the root of the
   new clone.** `git status --ignored` reports directories with a trailing slash, and to
   rsync a trailing slash means "the contents of". Fixed with `-R` and stripped slashes,
   and *proved* against a throwaway destination rather than reasoned about.

4. **My first stranded-data audit missed `rows.tier3.v2.csv`** — 88 MB, gitignored, and
   the file `CLAUDE.md` calls the single highest-value ask in the project. Found only by
   sweeping `git status --ignored` exhaustively instead of listing the directories I
   remembered. That is why the move list is now generated by a script that refuses to
   report clean if it finds a path it cannot classify.

5. **One of my nine subagents wrote into `redo/protocol/`**, despite an explicit brief
   not to, and left two `__pycache__` directories from running their scripts in place.
   Confirmed ours by timestamp rather than assumed: bundle source dated 6 Sep, our
   interpreter `cpython-38`, artefacts dated 11/13/14 Sep with two matching the agent's
   file to the second. Removed, and L9 now checks it — the rule had been broken twice
   and a rule broken twice needs a check, not a sentence.

6. **A subagent's headline that I had to retract before reporting it.** It concluded
   from a real version delta (`propose.d9c646af.py` has 0 occurrences of
   `msa_a3m_path`, `source_bundle` has 33) that *"every Block A–D prediction fetched its
   MSA live."* The delta is real; the inference is not. `MSA_SUBSAMPLING.md:111`
   documents a different file, `build_tier_d3_manifest.py`, with its own
   `msa_a3m_path_for()`. Two files, both about a3m paths — the compare-like-with-like
   trap.

7. **A near-miss I did catch.** `REDO_REFERENCE.md:1267` says "47 columns" and I almost
   flagged it as the same staleness as the recording-spec drift. It is about
   `g2_systems.csv`, which genuinely has 47 columns. Different object, correct number.
   Checked before flagging.

8. **`REDO_REFERENCE.md` — my own document — carries both positions on the 47/49
   question.** It correctly refutes the stale claim in Part 12 (lines 3415, 5389) and
   then repeats it as fact at line 3935. It survived because
   `analysis/crosscheck_redo_reference.py`, which I said re-derives every load-bearing
   number, **does not cover the recording-spec column count**. It still reports 104/104.
   A checker's green is only as wide as its coverage, and I overstated that coverage.

9. **`HANDOVER.md` contradicted itself about chain A** and I found it by checking the
   data rather than the prose. Line 19 said decided at (b); line 148 said
   `PENDING:SEQ_RECEPTORS.md` on all 2,389 rows and that nothing dispatches without it.
   Zero rows are PENDING. Fixed. `GROUP1_SYSTEMS.md:103` still carries the stale claim.

10. **`redo/README.md` drifted on three counts** — 28 generators (42), 47 inputs (67 now,
    65 then), a catalogue of 33 experiments (45). Not yet fixed.
