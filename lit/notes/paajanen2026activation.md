The parameters used were a match score of 2, a mismatch score of −1, and an internal_gap_open_score of −10. All other options were left at the default value of −1." If the matched score was less than half the sequence length, the part was considered an addition to the sequence and was ignored." (p.9) This is neither subsampling nor state-filtering; do not collapse it into either.

## C. Conformational core

**Framing note, applies to this whole section.** This paper analyses **experimentally deposited structures with machine learning**; it predicts nothing. Fields in this section that presuppose a generative structure predictor are answered `NOT APPLICABLE` with one line of why, rather than being forced into a prediction reading. The fields that *do* transfer — `structural_priors_used`, `state_metric`, `oracle_leakage` (routes 2, 4, 6, 7), `anti_memorization_*`, `controls_run` — are answered in full, and they are where the paper's value to the corpus sits.

- **states_generated**: **NOT APPLICABLE — nothing is generated.** 1351 deposited structures are projected onto one learned coordinate (G_CA = PC1) and partitioned into two classes by a 1-D GMM (pp.10–11). The *analysed* state handling is **two-state**: "we used a one-dimensional GMM on the first principal component and decomposed the data into two classes." (p.10); "The analysis confirms that there are two distinct states for the inactive and active conformations." (p.7). Intermediates are acknowledged as existing in the annotation and then dropped: "Structures reported as 'intermediate' or 'other' have been ignored because their number is too low to be distinguished from the graph." (p.2, Fig. 1 caption). One continuum-like object is produced but only as a visualisation, not as structures: the Supplementary Movie interpolates along PC1 "from low (inactive) to high (active) values" (p.12) — interpolated Cα coordinates, explicitly a linear interpolation in the PCA subspace, not sampled conformations.

- **structural_priors_used**: **The entire input is deposited experimental structures carrying database state annotations. This is the design, not a defect.** Enumerated:
  1. **Database supplying the structures and the labels: GPCRdb.** "The structural data used in this work were collected from the curated GPCRdb database (Isberg et al., 2015). We collected all class A GPCRs from GPCRdb, assembling a total of 1010 PDB structures, filtering out only those that did not have a downloadable PDB file in the RCSB PDB." (p.8). Files themselves from the RCSB PDB (p.9). **Counts: 1010 for model building; 1006 train + 345 held-out = 1351 total in the final analysis (Supp. Tables 1–3, p.22).**
  2. **State annotations come from GPCRdb, not from the authors.** "Data identifiers are from GPCRdb, as listed for each structure … Annual deposits broken down by the activation state reported by GPCRdb, considering 'active' (in blue) and 'inactive' (orange) states." (p.2, Fig. 1 caption). The ligand-class labels (agonist / antagonist / inverse agonist / partial agonist / none) and the G-protein-bound flag that define every series in Fig. 2 are likewise GPCRdb annotations (p.4; Supp. Table 3, p.22).
  3. **Generic residue numbering from GPCRdb** is the featurisation backbone: "To featurize the data, we used the generic numbering of GPCR residues provided by GPCRdb." (p.9)
  4. **An active-state reference structure, 3P0G**, is used for rigid-body removal and as the visual "genuine active" comparator in Fig. 3: "using the structure 3P0G (Rasmussen et al., 2011) as the reference" (pp.9, 10); "TM6 and TM7 of the genuine active-state structure (3P0G) in red" (p.7, Fig. 3 caption).
  5. **The active/inactive anchor classes are defined by biochemistry known in advance**: G-protein + agonist bound = active; antagonist without G protein = inactive. "In this framework, structures bound to both G protein and agonist simultaneously correspond to the active state of the GPCR. Structures bound only to antagonist (without G protein) are predominantly in the inactive state." (p.4)
  6. **Prior activation indices are cited as the standard against which the new one is positioned** — the A100 index ("a linear combination of five inter-helix distances developed for class A GPCR proteins. It was trained on simulation data and validated on 268 experimental structures from the PDB", p.3) and the GPCRdb percentage-of-activation model ("structures are classified as fully inactive if a condition based on a certain distance between TM2 and TM6 is met, while fully active structures are G protein or arrestin-bound structures", p.3). Neither is used as a feature.
  7. **Simulation priors are explicitly excluded**, which is the paper's stated methodological selling point: "given that the model utilizes only experimental structural data, potential problems that might arise from including simulation data as part of the analysis are avoided." (p.11)

- **oracle_leakage**: **Not a prediction pipeline, so routes 1, 3 and 5 are answered on their own terms; the live routes are 2, 4, 6 and 7. Headline: the model is unsupervised in its *fitting* and label-driven in its *component selection, orientation and validation*, and the paper then declares a subset of those same labels wrong.** Route by route.

  **Route 1 — deposited structures used as input or template: PRESENT BY CONSTRUCTION, and it is the object of study, not a contaminant.** Every datum is a deposited structure; there is nothing to leak *into*, because nothing is predicted. Protocol on p.8 (Data collection and featurization) and p.10 (Structural index G_CA). Verbatim: "The machine learning (ML)-based model we developed utilizes only experimental structural data from the PDB, thus avoiding the sampling limitations of atomistic simulations." (p.3)

  **Route 2 — state annotations from a curated database (GPCRdb) driving templates or alignments: PRESENT, in a specific and limited way — GPCRdb annotations do not enter the PCA/GMM fit, but they select and orient the index and then validate it.**
  - The generic numbering (a GPCRdb product, not a state annotation) drives the alignment and the feature set: "To featurize the data, we used the generic numbering of GPCR residues provided by GPCRdb." (p.9)
  - The *state/ligand* annotations drive which principal component becomes the index: "The choice of the first component to calculate the index is based on our previous observation in Supplementary Fig. 4, which shows that the first component is able to distinguish active GPCR structures bound to G protein from inactive ones." (p.10)
  - And they supply the validation: "Figure 2 shows that the G_CA index distinguishes antagonist-bound structures from structures bound to both G protein and agonist … This confirms that the index serves as a reliable proxy of receptor activation." (p.4)
  - **The circularity to record**: the same annotation set that selected PC1 and certified it as "a reliable proxy of receptor activation" (p.4) is then declared wrong where it disagrees with the index: "All these structures are marked as active in the GPCRdb classification." (p.6) and "If any experimental structure was initially misinterpreted and labeled, the model can identify and correct it." (p.11). The paper offers independent structural evidence for those seven cases (Fig. 3, p.7), which is the right move — but no independent evidence anywhere for the agreement cases.

  **Route 3 — cluster labels derived from known states: NONE FOUND at fit time; PRESENT at interpretation time.** The GMM is fitted without labels — "Gaussian mixture models (GMM) were used to find explicit categories in the reduced dimensional space. Specifically, we used a one-dimensional GMM on the first principal component and decomposed the data into two classes." (p.10) — but *which* of the two clusters is "active" is assigned from the known ligand/G-protein annotations (p.4, quoted above). Choosing k = 2 also encodes the prior that there are exactly two functional states.

  **Route 4 — hyperparameters, sweep ranges, seeds or stopping criteria tuned against known states: PRESENT, twice, and once benignly.**
  - *Component selection against the evaluation labels* (the real instance): PC1 out of five candidate components is chosen because it separates the labelled classes — "only the first component distinguishes antagonist-bound vs. G protein and agonist-bound structures. This strongly suggests that only the first component indicates activation." (p.10) and again p.10 in the index definition. Per schema route 4 this is model selection made against the labels the model is later scored on, and it is leakage even though no per-structure value is tuned. Mitigating: the PCA itself is fitted with no labels, only one of five components is in play, and Supp. Fig. 3 shows the variance falls below 5% per component after the fifth (pp.9–10), so the choice is heavily constrained by the data alone.
  - *KDE bandwidth chosen for appearance*: "The distributions in Fig. 2 were calculated as kernel density estimates using the kdeplot function of the seaborn package, with the bw_adjust parameter set to 0.5 to achieve a higher resolution distribution." (p.10). The paper's central minority-peak claim ("the observed minority peak is significant", p.7) is read off exactly these smoothed curves. Underlying histograms are relegated to Supp. Fig. 6 (p.21). Not state-label tuning, but it is a display parameter tuned in the direction of the finding; recorded in `hides` on the Fig. 2 row.
  - *Threshold*: **not** hand-tuned — it falls out of the GMM plus a bootstrap, see `state_metric`.

  **Route 5 — success defined post hoc by RMSD to a structure they had: NONE FOUND as a success criterion.** No RMSD-to-reference score is used to judge anything. RMSD appears only as the objective of the rigid-body superposition: "This step minimizes the root mean square deviation (RMSD) between all structures and the reference." (p.10). Protocol is on pp.9–10. The one caveat worth stating is that the reference for that superposition is an active-state structure (3P0G), so the coordinate frame is anchored on one end of the activation axis.

  **Route 6 — best/worst labels assigned against a held reference: PRESENT for the anomaly analysis, and assigned by eye.** The seven "mislabelled" structures are adjudicated by visual comparison against 3P0G: "a closer look at the G protein-binding site shown in Fig. 3 reveals that these structures share key features of the inactive conformation: TM6 remains close to the center of the helical bundle of a GPCR, while TM7 is shifted outward—both being hallmarks of the inactive state." (p.6). No G_CA value, no distance, no angle is reported for any of the seven. → tag `visual-metric` applies to this sub-result specifically.

  **Route 7 — design-level oracle use (weaker than pipeline leakage; kept distinct): PRESENT and explicit.** The expected answer is declared before the index is read. The active and inactive poles are defined by prior biochemistry ("structures bound to both G protein and agonist simultaneously correspond to the active state", p.4); the interpretation of the PC1 motion is checked against the textbook expectation and reported as agreement ("The outward movement of TM6 is the most important indicator of GPCR activation … This feature is a common denominator in structural data and is also reflected in our index.", p.5); and the paper's conclusion about G-protein-bound structures is stated as an expectation in the same breath as the evidence ("These structural anomalies suggest that there is no reason to expect G protein binding to receptors in an inactive conformation.", p.6). → tags `design-level-oracle` and `oracle-leak` (the latter for the route-4 component selection only).

- **prospective**: **partial.** Prospective in one real sense and retrospective in the rest.
  - *Prospective element, genuine*: a frozen model was applied to structures that did not exist when it was built. "After training the model, the GPCRdb query was rerun, adding new structures published during the training phase to the analysis and updating some of the labels. The selection was kept static, and the model was not retrained with new data." (p.10) — 345 structures (Supp. Tables 1–3, p.22), analysed in Supp. Fig. 5 (p.20).
  - *Retrospective everywhere else*: the index is built on, selected against, and validated on the same annotated corpus, and every mechanistic conclusion is drawn from the distribution of already-deposited structures. There is no wet-lab measurement, no new structure, and no prediction tested against a later experiment.

- **coinput_composition**: **New in v3.1. NOT APPLICABLE** — nothing is generated and no input is supplied to any model. The analysed *structures* carry whatever their depositors crystallised, and the paper's three categories (apo, agonist-bound, G-protein-bound) are read off those depositions rather than constructed.

- **binding_order**: **New in v3.1. ADDRESSED DIRECTLY, and this is the corpus's clearest statement of the ligand-first route.** The paper argues a two-level mechanism, verbatim (p.3, near-identically p.8): *"class A GPCR activation is mainly regulated by conformational selection at the ligand level and complemented by the induced fit mechanism at the transducer level."* Sequence stated at p.3: *"The results confirm that ligand binding is the first event in a stimulated activation sequence."* And the prerequisite direction at p.6: receptor activation, *"particularly the outward movement of TM6—is a prerequisite for G protein binding"*, with apparent G-protein complexes in inactive conformations dismissed as *"experimental artifacts, construct design, or nonphysiological stabilization methods."* Tags `conformational-selection` `induced-fit`. **Note the tension with `georgiou2025heterogeneity`**, which documents pre-coupled receptor–G protein complexes forming before agonist; the two are reconcilable as different populations rather than a contradiction, but a manuscript sentence should not cite one as if the other did not exist.

- **state_metric**: **Dual — continuous coordinate + binary predicate. And the descriptors are LEARNED, not the classical TM6/DRY/NPxxY geometry.** This matters, because the paper is a candidate source for a state predicate.
  - **The continuous coordinate** is G_CA, the projection of a structure's aligned Cα coordinates on the first PCA component: "G_CA = w_PC1 · (x_target − x̄)", where "w_PC1 are the weights of the first component of the PCA model, x_target are the aligned coordinates of the target structure, and x̄ are the mean coordinates of all structures" (p.10). Higher = more active. **Units are NOT REPORTED** (the weight vector is a unit vector and the coordinates are in Å, so the index carries Å, but the paper never says so and never labels the axis with units — Fig. 2, p.4).
  - **The feature set is backbone Cα only, at GPCRdb generic-numbered positions present in every structure**: "it utilizes only protein backbone coordinates from conserved structural features of the receptor transmembrane domain, ensuring its general applicability" (p.3); "Once the structures were aligned, generic numbers that appeared in all structures were selected for further analysis" (p.9); "The analysis was done on the alpha carbon coordinates of those residues that are not missing in any structure." (p.9, Fig. 4 caption). Explicitly **no side chains**: "the model does not include structural details of proteins, such as side chain orientations. This makes it insensitive to the interpretation problems of noisy experimental structural data" (p.11). **Therefore: no DRY motif geometry, no NPxxY geometry, no ionic-lock distance, no TM3–TM6 or TM2–TM6 distance is measured anywhere in this paper.** The classical distance-based indices are described only as prior art to be improved on (A100, five inter-helix distances; GPCRdb TM2–TM6 condition — both p.3).
  - **The number of residues in the selected set is NOT REPORTED**, and neither are the generic numbers themselves. Fig. 4b (p.9) shows the selection graphically (green = present in all structures, orange = missing in at least one) but gives no count and no list.
  - **The binary predicate is a threshold on G_CA at −1.72, with a stated confidence interval**: "Structures bound to both G protein and agonist (Fig. 2b, blue line) are predominantly found with index values above –1.72, while G protein-free and antagonist-bound structures (Fig. 2a, orange) are mainly found below this threshold. Thus, index values above –1.72 define the active region, while values below this threshold define the inactive region." (p.4). Exact value with uncertainty: "**With a 95% confidence interval, this resulted in a threshold value of –1.72 ± 0.44, with the active region defined as index values greater than this threshold.**" (p.11)
  - **Threshold justification — REPORTED and reasonable.** It is not hand-picked: it is the decision boundary of a two-component 1-D GMM fitted on PC1, with the uncertainty from bootstrap resampling: "To estimate uncertainty of the threshold index, we performed bootstrapping analysis by generating resampled data sets from the original data and rebuilding the GMM. To obtain a robust estimate and minimize the statistical error, we used 10,000 resampled data sets." (pp.10–11). What is *not* justified is the choice of two components in the mixture (k = 2 is asserted, never selected by BIC/AIC or any model-selection criterion).
  - **Missing-residue handling** has a closed-form estimator (Supplementary Data, p.15): Ĝ_CA = G_N / Σ_{i∈N} w_i², i.e. the partial index divided by the retained weight mass. Claimed robust without numbers: "the error estimate depends on which residue is missing, but even if several residues are missing, the error remains relatively small." (p.10). No error magnitude is given for any number of missing residues.
  - **Reimplementability: NO, from this PDF.** The predicate is `G_CA > −1.72`, but G_CA needs the PC1 weight vector, the mean coordinate vector, the residue selection list and the 3P0G superposition — none of which are in the paper, and no repository URL is given despite three "openly available" claims (pp.1, 3, 8).
  - → tags `continuous-metric`, `binary-predicate`, and `visual-metric` for the Fig. 3 anomaly adjudication.

- **metric_saturation**: **No numeric saturation.** G_CA is an unbounded projection; it neither floors nor ceilings in any reported arm (observed range roughly −18 to +10 across Fig. 2a–b, p.4, and Supp. Fig. 5, p.20). The binary predicate is one-sided, so any structure far into the active region is indistinguishable from one just over −1.72, but that is a consequence of thresholding, not saturation of the underlying measure — and the continuous values are always shown alongside. The bounded quantity in the paper is the KDE density on the y-axis, which is a display normalisation, not a measurement. Figure-level display issues (categories omitted from Fig. 2, partial-year bar in Fig. 1, bandwidth choice) are recorded in `hides` on the relevant figure rows, per v3 rule 9.

- **directional_control**: **NOT APPLICABLE as a method property — there is nothing to instruct, because nothing is generated.** The paper's substantive result is nonetheless *about* which physical handle controls state occupancy, and that is the reusable content: the handles are (i) **ligand class**, which shifts the population but leaves it bimodal, and (ii) **G protein binding**, which is the only handle that collapses the population onto the active state. "Interestingly, structures bound to G protein but not to agonist, and structures bound to agonist but without G protein, are distributed over a broad front along the index, although both have a peak in the active region that represents the primary state." (pp.4–5); "when we studied the structures of G protein-bound receptors, we found that they almost unambiguously represent only the active state, suggesting that the function of G protein is to lock the receptor in the active state." (p.7). Antagonists and inverse agonists act as the opposite-direction handle: "antagonists attenuate the dynamics of their receptor along the activation coordinate, stabilizing the inactive state … inverse agonists act as stronger inactivators than antagonists" (p.5).

- **anti_memorization_design**: **YES — a temporally held-out set of 345 structures, defined by deposition/annotation after the model was frozen.** "After training the model, the GPCRdb query was rerun, adding new structures published during the training phase to the analysis and updating some of the labels. The selection was kept static, and the model was not retrained with new data. Eight of the new structures were ignored because they were missing a residue from the list." (p.10). n = 345 (Supp. Tables 1–3, p.22: 1351 total − 1006 train). **The cutoff is defined by "during the training phase", not by a date** — no calendar cutoff is stated anywhere, which makes the split unreproducible by anyone else. Composition of the held-out set (Supp. Table 1, p.22): aminergic 132, peptide 74, lipid 47, orphan 31, protein 19, alicarboxylic acid 19, nucleotide 8, sensory 7, steroid 6, other 2, melatonin 0; by species (Supp. Table 2): human 326, HCMV 8, bovine 3, mouse 3, *Hasarius adansoni* 3, HHV-8 2.
- **anti_memorization_control**: **RUN and analysed, not merely existing; adequately powered (n = 345), but reported with no number.** The frozen model was applied to the new structures and the result is a figure: "Supplementary Fig. 5 summarizes the analysis of the new structures." (p.10); Supp. Fig. 5 caption: "Results of the data not used for training." (p.20). Displayed series and n (read from the rendered panel, p.20): panel a (no G protein) agonist N = 45, antagonist N = 21, inverse agonist N = 13; panel b (with G protein) agonist N = 236, none N = 18 — 333 of the 345. The held-out distributions visibly reproduce the training-set pattern (agonist-without-G-protein peaked in the active region with an inactive shoulder; G-protein-bound almost entirely active). **What is missing is any quantity**: no agreement rate with GPCRdb labels, no classification accuracy, no distributional test between train and held-out, and no sentence in the main text stating what the held-out arm showed. The control was run; its result was never scored. NOT `UNPOWERED` (n = 345, no overlap with training by construction).

  **What is absent from the battery, and it is the obvious gap:** there is **no permutation or label-shuffle control** on the GMM/PCA separation, **no cross-validation or held-out refit** of the PCA itself, **no model-selection test for k = 2** in the mixture, **no comparison arm against the existing A100 or GPCRdb activation indices** on the same 1351 structures (both are described on p.3 and never computed), and **no resolution or construct-quality stratification** — despite the anomaly analysis concluding that the disagreements are caused by "experimental artifacts, construct design, or nonphysiological stabilization methods" (p.6), which is exactly the confound a resolution/construct arm would have addressed corpus-wide.

- **confidence_as_discriminator**: **NOT APPLICABLE — there is no predictor and therefore no pLDDT/pTM/ipTM.** The paper's only confidence-like quantities are statistical, not model-internal: the bootstrap 95% CI on the threshold (−1.72 ± 0.44, p.11) and the qualitative missing-residue error claim (p.10). Neither is used to judge conformational correctness of an individual structure. Experimental confidence measures (map resolution, B-factors, model-to-map fit) are **NOT REPORTED** and are not used to filter or weight any structure — a notable omission given the anomaly conclusion (p.6) rests on structure quality.

## D. Claims

- **central_conclusion**: Projecting 1351 deposited class A GPCR structures onto the first principal component of their aligned transmembrane Cα coordinates yields a single index (G_CA) whose two-component mixture separates active from inactive at −1.72 ± 0.44, and the distribution of structures along that index shows that both apo and agonist-bound receptors are bimodal — populating active *and* inactive conformations — while G-protein-bound structures are almost exclusively active. The authors read this as a hybrid mechanism: conformational selection at the ligand level, induced fit at the transducer level, with G protein, not agonist, being what locks the active state.

- **necessity_claims** — **verbatim, with pages. These are the load-bearing sentences for the G-protein-necessity contrast.**

  **On G protein being required to stabilise / lock the active state (the key question):**
  1. > "Agonist binding shifts this conformational ensemble towards the active state but does not fully stabilize it. Instead, a stable active state is only established upon G protein binding, which locks the receptor in its active conformation." — p.1 (abstract)
  2. > "Ligand binding stabilizes the active state, but G protein is required to lock the receptor into the active state conformation." — p.3 (Introduction)
  3. > "Agonist binding alone is sufficient to drive the receptor to the active state, but G protein is needed to stabilize this state" — p.5 (Results section heading)
  4. > "when we studied the structures of G protein-bound receptors, we found that they almost unambiguously represent only the active state, suggesting that the function of G protein is to lock the receptor in the active state." — p.7 (Discussion)
  5. > "On the other hand, the role of G proteins in locking the receptor into its active state conformation suggests that strategies to stabilize the active states bound to the transducer (e.g., using nanobodies, conformation-stabilizing antibodies, or bitopic/allosteric modulators) could improve the efficacy or lifetime of signaling." — p.8
  6. > "Experimental structure data hence reveals that class A GPCR activation is mainly regulated by conformational selection at the ligand level and complemented by the induced fit mechanism at the transducer level." — p.3 (also, near-identically, p.8: "In summary, the analysis of experimental protein structures performed in this work reveals that class A GPCR activation is mainly regulated by conformational selection at the ligand level, complemented by the induced fit mechanism at the transducer level.")
  7. > "These results support a hybrid activation mechanism in which ligand binding follows conformational selection, while transducer engagement is governed by induced fit." — p.1 (abstract)

  **On the converse — that agonist alone does NOT require, and does not achieve, a locked active state:**
  8. > "We find that receptors can populate both inactive and active-like conformations even in the absence of ligand or G protein, providing a structural basis for agonist-free basal activity." — p.1 (abstract)
  9. > "This leads to a key observation, the apo form of several class A GPCRs exists in both the inactive and active states, and the agonist-bound form also exists in both the inactive and active states. This indicates that the conformation of class A GPCRs spontaneously switches between the inactive and active states in both the absence and presence of ligand." — p.3
  10. > "Contrary to this expectation, however, we found that the apo form of several class A GPCRs exists in both the inactive and active states. Similarly, we found that the agonist-bound form also exists in both the inactive and active states. The distributions of the apo and agonist-bound forms are bimodal, with the dominant peaks in the inactive and active states, respectively. However, in both cases, the observed minority peak is significant, indicating that the conformation of class A GPCRs spontaneously switches between the inactive and active states in both the absence and presence of ligand." — p.7
  11. > "Yet the observation that apo structures are also found in the active region (Fig. 2a) suggests that class A GPCRs can spontaneously adopt active-like conformations, and they can switch between inactive and active-like conformations, even in the absence of ligand." — p.5
  12. > "First, on the one hand, a ligand-free protein can reach a conformation corresponding to the active state, and on the other hand, a ligand-bound protein can be in a conformation matching the inactive state." — p.7

  **On activation being a prerequisite for G protein binding (the reverse direction — TM6 must move out *before* G protein can bind):**
  13. > "These structural anomalies suggest that there is no reason to expect G protein binding to receptors in an inactive conformation. Such interactions, if observed, are likely to be due to experimental artifacts, construct design, or nonphysiological stabilization methods. This supports the view that receptor activation—and the associated structural rearrangement, particularly the outward movement of TM6—is a prerequisite for G protein binding." — p.6
  14. > "Since TM6 physically blocks the G protein-binding pocket in this conformation, these structures appear to be based on structural anomalies that still allow G protein binding." — p.6
  15. > "The results confirm that ligand binding is the first event in a stimulated activation sequence. Furthermore, the results reveal conformational changes that are common among these receptors—inward movement of TM7 and outward movement of TM6—that occur during activation and precede the binding of G protein to the receptor." — p.3

  **Methodological necessity claims:**
  16. > "However, this would not be possible without experimental data of receptor structures." — p.1
  17. > "However, progress in elucidating the activation mechanisms of GPCRs largely depends on the availability and quality of training data needed for AI analysis. This requires a large number of protein structures that represent different conformational states (inactive, intermediate, and active) and are sufficiently accurate, as well as reliable identification and annotation of these states." — p.3

- **stated_limits**:
  - **Methods are deliberately incomplete in this version**: "The methodology is described below in a way that highlights the main points. Certain details will be added once the article has been accepted for publication." (p.8)
  - **The distributions cannot discriminate binding order**: "While this behavior is consistent with the conformational selection mechanism, it may also be compatible with the induced fit model, as the structural distributions do not reveal whether the ligand initially binds to the inactive or active receptor conformation." (p.5)
  - **Some of the apparent helix motion is an artefact of the superposition**: "These smaller changes of TM1, TM2, and TM4 during larger movements of TM6 and TM7 may be due in part to the removal of rotation and translation required by the ML analysis. It is worth noting that this does not affect the calculated index values, but only the interpretation of the interpolated conformational changes (Supplementary Movie), as the additional rotation becomes a part of the model." (p.5)
  - **The extracellular half carries no shared signal**: "On the extracellular side, no significant differences in the index between the two states are observed, only a small shift in TM3, indicating that the detailed activation mechanisms of the ligand binding sites are not shared among the different receptors." (p.5)
  - **Each cryo structure is itself an average**: "In cryo-based microscopy experiments, the protein structure to be determined corresponds to an ensemble average of numerous structures." (p.7)
  - **Two-paradigm framing is acknowledged as a simplification**: "Although there is a risk of interpreting this complex process in an oversimplified manner, there are currently two main paradigms for GPCR activation." (p.2)
  - **Categories dropped from the analysis**: intermediate/other states — "Structures reported as 'intermediate' or 'other' have been ignored because their number is too low to be distinguished from the graph." (p.2); arrestins — "Arrestins have not been included in the analysis because there is not enough data to influence the graph." (p.2); electron crystallography — "There were not enough structures produced by electron crystallography to make their contribution concretely visible in the graph." (p.2); eight new structures — "Eight of the new structures were ignored because they were missing a residue from the list." (p.10)
  - **Model not updated with the new data**: "The selection was kept static, and the model was not retrained with new data." (p.10)
  - **Missing-residue error is structure-dependent**: "the error estimate depends on which residue is missing, but even if several residues are missing, the error remains relatively small." (p.10)
  - The authors also concede the literature currently leans the other way on their central mechanistic point: "Although there is more support for the induced fit mechanism (Asadollahi et al., 2025), both paradigms can lead to the same functional state." (p.3) — Asadollahi et al. 2025 is titled "The allosteric mechanism of G-protein-coupled receptors is induced fit, not conformational selection" (p.12).

- **stance**: **`precedent` (on findings) + `contrast` (on rigour and reproducibility). Provisional — the user's call.**
  - *Precedent*: this is an independent, experiment-only statement that agonist binding shifts the population but does not lock it, and that a stable active state is established only on G protein binding — asserted repeatedly and quotably (claims 1–7 above), on 1351 deposited structures. For any argument that a conformationally correct active-state model requires the transducer rather than the agonist, this is the structural-database precedent.
  - *Contrast*: the index is selected and validated against the same GPCRdb annotations it is then used to correct; there is no classification performance number anywhere (no accuracy, no AUC, no agreement rate — not even for the held-out arm that was run); the seven "mislabelled" structures are adjudicated by eye with no index values reported; the "openly available" tool has no URL; and the Methods are explicitly withheld pending acceptance. A corpus argument about operationalised state predicates can use this paper as an example of a predicate that is well-posed in principle (GMM + bootstrap CI) and unreproducible in practice.
