## C. Conformational core

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| `states_generated` | **ensemble + single-state.** Per complex, 25 independent predictions were generated for AF3/Protenix/Boltz-2/Chai-1 ("10 recycling steps, 200 diffusion steps, 5 seeds, 5 diffusion samples per seed", p34) and 20 trajectories for DynamicBind (p35) — an ensemble. That ensemble is then collapsed to one: "For each model, the best prediction per complex (lowest ligand pose RMSD) was selected from generated models." (p37). Every headline number is a mean of these single best-of-25 values. Note the object is a **ligand-pose ensemble per complex**, not a protein conformational-state ensemble; the paper never targets, names or scores a protein conformational state. |
| `structural_priors_used` | Heavy, and entirely legitimate as *design*-time knowledge — it is what the benchmark is made of. (1) Every complex is a deposited experimental structure, used as the scoring reference (p33–34). (2) The orthosteric/allosteric stratification is taken wholesale from curated structural resources: the MDT set (ref 39), the PLOC/PLA subsets (ref 86), and **KinCoRe**, "a geometry-driven classification system using DFG motif and αC-helix conformation" from which "we extracted 217 Type III/IV complexes" (p34). (3) Allosteric membership is asserted structurally: "All complexes were experimentally validated as authentic allosteric binders with sites spatially distinct from orthosteric pockets." (p34). (4) MDT selection itself uses a structural prior: "Structural challenge was enforced by requiring AlphaFold-predicted apo-pocket deviation from experimental holo-conformation >2.0 Å RMSD or pocket LDDT <0.80." (p33). (5) The frustration analysis is computed on experimental apo and holo structures (p36), not on model output. None of this is a defect; it is recorded here so that `oracle_leakage` records only what entered the *pipeline*. |
| `oracle_leakage` | **All seven routes worked below. Verdict: pipeline leakage on routes 5 and 6 (explicit and severe), partial on route 1; design-level oracle on route 7.** See the per-route table immediately following this section. |
| `prospective` | **no.** Fully retrospective. Every complex has its experimental reference in hand before prediction; success, pocket definition, model selection and stratum membership are all defined against that reference. The authors nowhere claim prospectivity for their own work; the one use of the word is about someone else's study ("In a prospective evaluation of 557 SARS-CoV-2 Mac1-ligand complexes Kim et al. …", p32). |
| `state_metric` | **RMSD-to-reference + binary predicate**, with a third, unacknowledged **visual** component. (i) Continuous: symmetry-corrected ligand pose RMSD via OpenStructure SCRMSDScorer, "which enumerates symmetry-equivalent atom assignments and returns the lowest RMSD" (p35); pocket backbone RMSD after TM-align superposition, pocket = "all protein residues with at least one atom within 8 Å of any reference ligand atom" (p35–36); QS-score = "the fraction of native ligand–protein contacts (4.5 Å cutoff) correctly recovered" (p36). (ii) Binary: success at **RMSD < 2.0 Å** (p9, Fig 2D caption p11). The 2.0 Å threshold is adopted from PoseBusters convention and is **never independently justified** in this paper. The 8 Å pocket radius, the 4.5 Å contact cutoff and the 4 Å binding-site radius used for frustration (p37) are likewise stated without justification. (iii) Visual: the paper's spatial frustration claims — connected 8–12 Å minimal-frustration "shells" around orthosteric pockets vs "isolated, spatially disconnected patches" at allosteric sites (p25, p28) — are read off surface renders (Figs 5, 6) with **no contiguity metric, no cluster-size statistic and no quantitative panel of any kind**. Frustration *classes* are Z-score thresholded (minimal Z > 0.78, highly Z < −1.0, neutral in between, p36–37), but their *spatial organisation*, which is the load-bearing explanatory claim, is called by eye. |
| `metric_saturation` | **Yes, on QS-score, at the ceiling.** QS-score is bounded at 1.0 and the orthosteric arm sits against that bound: "QS-scores exceeding 0.90 across all models" and "AF3 and Protenix approaching 0.95" (p9, p17); Fig 3K/3O show orthosteric QS swarms centred at ~0.95 with essentially no headroom. The paper reads the modest allosteric shift (0.70–0.85) as a *decoupling* result — "models frequently identify the correct regulatory region … but fail to resolve these interactions into a unique geometric arrangement" (p17) — but a compressed metric near ceiling cannot distinguish that reading from insensitivity of the metric. Protenix is the clean case: Fig 3M shows orthosteric ≈ 0.93 and allosteric ≈ 0.92, i.e. no separation at all on QS. Success rate at 2.0 Å is a floor/ceiling-prone binary predicate by construction but is not reported at either bound. **No axis-truncation issue is recorded here** — Fig 3K–O truncation and Fig 2 axis handling are figure defects and live in `hides` on rows `3K-O` and `2A-B`. |
| `directional_control` | **None. The method is not instructable, and this is not a method paper.** The only conditioning handle is the ligand itself (protein sequence + ligand SMILES, p34) — the models are run in their stock configuration and asked to place a ligand. Diversity within a complex comes only from stochastic sampling ("5 seeds, 5 diffusion samples per seed", p34); no seed, subsample depth, template, partner, nanobody or state-annotated input is used to steer toward a state. DynamicBind is the one architecture with an explicit conformational handle ("iteratively adjusts receptor structure to accommodate the ligand", p35) and it is not directed either. No arm anywhere in the paper attempts to *instruct* a model to find an allosteric site. |
| `anti_memorization_design` | **Partial, asymmetric, and stated only for the orthosteric side.** In the **Orthosteric Datasets** section: "The datasets were filtered to include only complexes deposited in the Protein Data Bank after 2020, ensuring they post-date model training sets. Complexes with >30% sequence identity to training data were excluded (verified via PDB70 and MMseqs2 clustering)." (p33). Three problems. (a) **Scope**: the sentence sits between the MDT description and the PLOC description; "The datasets" is plural but it is unclear whether the 1,863-structure PLOC subset is covered. n for the filtered set is therefore either 412 or 2,275 and the paper does not say. (b) **Cutoff definition**: "after 2020" is not post-cutoff for the models tested — AF3's training set runs to late 2021 and Protenix, Boltz-2 and Chai-1 are later still. A 2020 date filter does not establish held-out status for any of the five architectures, and no model-specific cutoff date is given for any of them. (c) **The allosteric side has none.** The **Allosteric Datasets** section (p34) states resolution (≤3.5 Å, ≤3.2 Å), molecular weight (≥250 Da), inhibitor type (Type III/IV geometry) and site distinctness — **no deposition-date filter and no sequence-identity filter at all**. The stratum that carries the entire headline claim is the one with no memorization control. |
| `anti_memorization_control` | **NONE RUN.** No arm compares pre-cutoff to post-cutoff complexes, on either stratum. No arm compares high- to low-sequence-identity-to-training complexes. The asymmetry in (c) above is never named, never tested, and never discussed. This is the single largest gap between what the paper claims — "This uniformity across diverse inductive biases argues against explanations rooted in training data or architectural idiosyncrasies." (p30) — and what it measured: **a training-data explanation is precisely what was not controlled for on the allosteric arm**, and the direction of the confound is unexamined. (An unfiltered allosteric set would if anything make the observed failure *more* striking, but only if training overlap were quantified, which it is not.) Not marked `UNPOWERED`: n is large (1,966 allosteric complexes); the defect is design, not power. |
| `controls_run` | See the `controls_run` table below. Short version: **the benchmark's only real control is the orthosteric stratum itself.** Four analyses are specified in Methods and never reported. |
| `confidence_as_discriminator` | **Declared, used for ranking, contradicted, and never validated or reported.** Methods specify ligand-centric confidence metrics — "L-pLDDT (mean pLDDT over ligand atoms) and L-PAE (average PAE involving ligand atoms) for AF3/Chai-1, and ipTM for Boltz-2. Confidence-Error Correlation was assessed by mapping confidence scores against actual RMSD." (p36) — and a further "Spearman's rank correlation was computed between per-residue frustration Z-scores and per-residue model confidence scores." (p37). **No confidence result, correlation coefficient, calibration plot or panel appears anywhere in the paper.** Worse, the two selection statements contradict: "The ligand-protein chain-pair iPTM score was used for ranking." (p34) vs "For each model, the best prediction per complex (lowest ligand pose RMSD) was selected" (p37). Since the reported numbers are best-by-RMSD, confidence was **not** in fact the discriminator for anything reported. The one confidence claim in the Results is qualitative and unbacked: "the spatial extent of these networks correlates with prediction confidence" (p26, repeated in the Fig 5 caption p27) — asserted with no correlation statistic and no panel. |

### `oracle_leakage` — all seven routes, worked separately

| # | Route | Verdict | Evidence (verbatim + page) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Structures used as input or template | **PARTIAL — YES for AF3 (templates on), unestablished for the other four** | Inputs are sequence-only: "All models received identical inputs: protein sequences from PDB structures, ligands in SMILES format." (p34) — no experimental coordinates are handed to any model as input. But template *retrieval* is on for AF3: "Predictions were generated with templates enabled (default) and without templates (AF3-NT)." (p34). With templates enabled against the PDB, the deposited holo structure of a target (deposited "after 2020", p33) is retrievable, so the target-state coordinates can enter the pipeline for AF3. The AF3-NT arm that would settle this is defined and **its results are never reported**. For Protenix, Boltz-2, Chai-1 and DynamicBind the template setting is not stated, so this route cannot be cleared for them either. |
| 2 | State annotations from a curated database (GPCRdb / KLIFS / KinCoRe) driving templates or alignments | **NONE FOUND as a pipeline driver** — the annotations drive *dataset construction only* | KinCoRe is used, and used structurally: "Kinase-specific allosteric complexes were derived from the Kinase Conformation Resource (KinCoRe) a geometry-driven classification system using DFG motif and αC-helix conformation. Type III inhibitors bind adjacent to the ATP site (distance >6.0 Å from hinge region, minimum three contacts with back-pocket residues). Type IV inhibitors bind >6.5 Å from both hinge region and conserved αC-helix Glu(+4) residue." (p34). But nothing in the model-execution protocol (p34–35) consumes those annotations: no state-annotated template is supplied, no state-filtered alignment is built. The DFG/αC labels select which complexes enter the allosteric stratum; they do not touch the prediction. That is route 7, recorded there. Protocol described p34–35. |
| 3 | Cluster labels derived from known states | **NONE FOUND** | Clustering appears once and is sequence-redundancy clustering for a training-overlap filter, not state labelling: "Complexes with >30% sequence identity to training data were excluded (verified via PDB70 and MMseqs2 clustering)." (p33). No conformational or state-based clustering is performed anywhere. Full protocol p33–37. |
| 4 | Hyperparameters, sweep ranges, seeds or stopping criteria tuned against known states | **NONE FOUND — but with one recorded near-miss** | Settings are fixed, uniform and adopted from an external benchmark, with no sweep of any kind: "Five state-of-the-art architectures were benchmarked following protocols adapted from PoseBusters. All models received identical inputs … For each complex, 25 independent predictions were generated (10 recycling steps, 200 diffusion steps, 5 seeds, 5 diffusion samples per seed)" (p34). No range is tuned on the evaluation set, so v3's "tuning a *range* is leakage" clause does not fire. **Near-miss to note:** the RMSD tool itself was changed after seeing the pipeline behave — "Several ligand RMSD tools such as LigRMSD, DockRMSD and spyRMSD which are commonly applied in docking evaluations were initially employed but large scale pipeline execution of diverse AI models require clean ligand definitions, consistent atom ordering, and stable residue naming conventions which appear to be highly heterogeneous and inconsistent across different AI models outputs. OpenStructure framework was adopted in the final protocol" (p35). The stated reason is technical (atom naming), not result-driven, and no before/after comparison of the two scorers is shown, so this is recorded as unaudited rather than as leakage. |
| 5 | Success defined post hoc by RMSD or TM to a structure they had | **YES — explicit, and the definition of every headline number** | Every reported quantity is referenced to the deposited structure the authors held. "All predicted protein structures were superimposed onto experimental references using TM-align for sequence-independent structural alignment." (p35). "The binding pocket was defined as all protein residues with at least one atom within 8 Å of any reference ligand atom." (p35–36) — i.e. the pocket the model is scored on is drawn around the *answer's* ligand. Success is a post hoc RMSD predicate: "At the stringent threshold of 2.0 Å for ligand pose RMSD, both AF3 and Protenix models exceeded 80% success rates" (p9); Fig 2D caption, "Fraction of predictions achieving ligand pose RMSD < 2.0 Å across models and datasets" (p11). This is normal for a docking benchmark, but it is route-5 leakage on the schema's terms and must be recorded as such. |
| 6 | Best/worst model labels assigned against a held reference | **YES — explicit, unhedged, and it sets the reported means** | "For each model, the best prediction per complex (lowest ligand pose RMSD) was selected from generated models. Average RMSD values represent the mean of best-run RMSDs across all systems in each dataset." (p37). The 25-member ensemble is ranked by distance to the answer and only the winner is reported. This directly contradicts the Methods statement that ranking was by confidence — "The ligand-protein chain-pair iPTM score was used for ranking." (p34) — and the paper never reconciles the two or reports a blind (iPTM-ranked, top-1) number. Consequence for the headline: the orthosteric–allosteric gap is a gap between two **oracle-selected best-of-25** values, not between two blind top-1 values, and the gap under blind selection is unknown and could be larger or smaller. |
| 7 | Design-level oracle — inputs or systems chosen because the expected answer is already known | **YES — design-level (weaker than pipeline leakage; label accordingly)** | The study is built on a stratification that presupposes its own outcome. Complexes enter the allosteric arm *because* they are already known to be allosteric: "All complexes were experimentally validated as authentic allosteric binders with sites spatially distinct from orthosteric pockets." (p34), selected by geometric criteria against the known site (p34, route 2 quotes). The expected result is declared before any prediction is read: "The central hypothesis put forward in the current study is that the emerging limitations in AI modeling of allosteric ligand binding may reflect not only algorithmic shortcomings of current AI co-folding architectures but rather represent a more fundamental biophysical mismatch between orthosteric and allosteric binding landscapes." (p5). And the MDT stratum is itself pre-selected for difficulty against the answer: "Structural challenge was enforced by requiring AlphaFold-predicted apo-pocket deviation from experimental holo-conformation >2.0 Å RMSD or pocket LDDT <0.80." (p33). **Nothing leaky is fed to the models here** — this is stratum construction, and it is the ordinary and defensible way to build a stratified benchmark. It is recorded as design-level so that the note does not conflate it with routes 5 and 6, which are pipeline. |

## D. Claims

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| `central_conclusion` | Five current co-folding models (AF3, Protenix, Boltz-2, Chai-1, DynamicBind) predict orthosteric ligand poses at near-experimental accuracy and all five degrade by roughly a factor of two on a matched stratum of allosteric complexes, in both pocket localisation and pose geometry, while retaining moderately high contact-topology scores. The authors attribute this architecture-independent gap not to training data or model design but to a biophysical asymmetry: orthosteric sites undergo ligand-induced frustration quenching that creates a steep energetic funnel, whereas allosteric sites remain neutrally frustrated in both apo and holo states and so present no directional signal for a pattern-recognition model to exploit. |
| `necessity_claims` | **Verbatim, with pages.** These are the impossibility/universality sentences the paper's argument is built on. <br>— "While all AI models excel in accurate modeling of orthosteric ligand binding, a universal and architecture-independent collapse emerges in prediction of allosteric complexes." (p2, Abstract) <br>— "These distinctions necessitate a shift to explainable biophysical AI frameworks grounded in the protein energy landscapes." (p5) <br>— "Our analysis confirms a significant and consistent performance gap identifying the allosteric blind spot as a universal property of current architectures." (p6) <br>— "This degradation is not model-specific identifying the allosteric blind spot as a universal phenomenon rooted in the nature of allosteric binding landscape rather than in algorithmic design." (p11) <br>— "AI models are often unable to accurately localize regulatory pockets, and even when pockets are approximately identified, AI predictions of allosteric ligand binding poses may deviate significantly from experimental solutions" (p12) <br>— "revealing the universal 'allosteric blind spot' across diverse AI architectures that may reflect fundamental differences in the biophysical organization of binding sites rather than model-specific limitations." (p13) <br>— "Across all panels, the persistent separation between orthosteric and allosteric distributions reveals an architecture-independent 'allosteric blind spot.'" (p16) <br>— "Allosteric binding prediction fails not because models cannot identify relevant interaction partners, but because they cannot resolve the energetic specificity required for geometric convergence." (p18) <br>— "Based on these findings, we argue that AI models can recover the vocabulary of allosteric interactions (which residues contact the ligand) but cannot determine the syntax (their precise spatial arrangement), because several syntactically distinct solutions are energetically comparable." (p18) <br>— "Despite fundamentally different inductive biases across A [sic] models, the architecture-independent nature of this collapse indicates that the allosteric blind spot is unlikely to be algorithmic in origin." (p20) <br>— "even when pockets are approximately localized, the absence of an extended energetic funnel prevents geometric convergence to a unique pose. The isolated minimal frustration patches may provide weak local constraints, anchoring specific interactions without specifying global orientation, but they are insufficient to resolve the full three-dimensional arrangement." (p28) <br>— "This uniformity across diverse inductive biases argues against explanations rooted in training data or architectural idiosyncrasies. It points instead to a common underlying constraint: the energetic landscapes of allosteric sites simply do not provide the signals current AI architectures are equipped to exploit." (p30) <br>— "However, AI models cannot resolve the syntax of allosteric binding (the precise three-dimensional arrangement) because the landscape itself offers no unique solution." (p31) |
| `novelty_claims` | **Near-absent, and that is worth recording.** The paper makes **no** "first", "novel to this work", "unprecedented" or "to our knowledge" claim about its own contribution anywhere in the 40 pages of body text (the words *novel* and *pioneered* occur only in descriptions of others' work, p4, p32, p33). The closest self-positioning statements, verbatim: <br>— "By linking prediction outcomes to frustration landscapes, this study recasts AI shortcomings in allosteric ligand binding as diagnostic indicators of fundamental biophysical constraints, establishing a physics-informed framework that turns the allosteric blind spot into mechanistic insight for next-generation landscape-aware predictive tools." (p2, Abstract) <br>— "we developed a dual explainable AI framework designed to distinguish between limitations imposed by training data and those imposed by the physical organization of the binding energy landscapes" (p6) <br>— "In conclusion, our study establishes a principled, biophysics-informed explainable AI framework that transforms the allosteric blind spot from limitation into insight, providing both a diagnostic tool for evaluating next-generation models and a concrete roadmap for developing energy-aware architectures capable of navigating nature's most sophisticated regulatory landscapes." (p33) <br>The paper also explicitly frames three concurrent studies as *converging with*, not preceded by, its own: "Three complementary studies released very recently, each addressing distinct aspects of co-folding performance, provide striking conceptual alignment with our findings and provides [sic] compelling external validation for the biophysical framework we have established." (p32; refs 87 Kim et al. on 557 SARS-CoV-2 Mac1 complexes, 88 PLINDER, 89 Zhang et al. on 218 covalent complexes). |
| `stated_limits` | **Thin — there is no Limitations section and no self-criticism of the benchmark design.** The only forward-looking limit stated: "The diversity of allosteric site architectures suggests that different allosteric mechanisms may present different prediction challenges; larger datasets with finer mechanistic annotation could enable stratification of allosteric sites by their frustration signatures and corresponding prediction outcomes." (p32). No ablation, no held-out arm and no training-overlap measurement backs that sentence. <br>3. **Scope of the post-2020 / 30 %-identity filter.** "The datasets were filtered…" (p33) appears in the Orthosteric section between the MDT and PLOC descriptions; whether it applies to MDT alone (n=412) or to MDT+PLOC (n=2,275) is unresolvable from the text. The allosteric section carries no such filter at all. <br>4. **Provenance of the apo ("ligand-free") structures used for frustration analysis.** Methods say indices "were computed for every residue pair in both apo and holo states" (p36) and Figure 4 legends read "ligand-free"/"ligand-bound", but the paper never says whether apo means a matched apo PDB entry, the holo structure with the ligand deleted, or an AlphaFold model (MDT's own selection criterion uses "AlphaFold-predicted apo-pocket", p33). The apo-to-holo frustration-quenching result — the paper's core mechanism — depends entirely on this. <br>5. **Why the Figure 2E–I histograms contain roughly a third of the stated complexes.** No exclusion criterion, failure rate or per-model attrition is reported. <br>6. **Whether the AF3-NT arm was run.** It is specified in Methods (p34) and appears nowhere else. If run and unreported, it is the missing template control (oracle route 1). <br>7. **What "n" is for every reported mean.** No panel or table in the paper states how many complexes contributed to any number. <br>8. **Tags I needed and could not use.** Three, all recorded rather than invented: (a) a tag for *declared-but-unreported analysis* — this paper specifies four analyses in Methods (AF3-NT, Confidence-Error Correlation, frustration-vs-confidence Spearman, LDDT-PLI) and reports none, plus a full Statistical Analysis section producing no p-value or effect size anywhere; that pattern has no vocabulary entry and `no-anti-memorization` only covers one instance of it. (b) A tag for *best-of-N-selected-against-reference* (oracle route 6) distinct from generic `oracle-leak` — route 6 is a specific, checkable and very common defect, and collapsing it into `oracle-leak` means a reverse lookup for it returns every paper that ever computed an RMSD. (c) A tag for *pocket-localisation* or *site-identification* as a task, distinct from pose accuracy — this paper's "compounded failure" claim is precisely that both fail, and the Site tags mark the site class, not the task. <br>9. **Figure 4's `n` slot could not be filled at all** — the histograms are frequency distributions over residues, and neither the number of residues nor the number of complexes behind any panel is stated. |

**Tag notes — why each was applied, and what was deliberately not applied.**

- `general-protein` + `kinase`: the corpus is genuinely general (76 + 83 Pfam families, >500 organisms, p33–34), and `general-protein` is the primary system tag. `kinase` is applied because 353 of the 1,966 allosteric complexes are **protein** kinases selected by DFG/αC geometry from KinCoRe (p34) — within the v3 scoping of `kinase` to protein kinases. **`gpcr` deliberately not applied**: GPCRs appear only as one of four families named inside the MDT set (p9), with no GPCR-specific dataset, stratum, number or figure anywhere. Tagging it would false-positive every GPCR-conformation query with a paper that reports nothing about GPCRs. `transporter`, `fold-switching`, `periplasmic-binding`, `atpase` all absent.
- `benchmark-only` + `cofolding`: both, and neither alone is right. `benchmark-only` is the method class — no predictor is built, nothing is fine-tuned. `cofolding` records that the objects benchmarked are co-folding models, so that a query for co-folding evidence finds this paper. The frustration-analysis half has no matching method tag in v3 (it is not `md`, not `enhanced-sampling`, not `af-cluster`); it is recorded in `method_class` as "other".
- `multi-backbone`: five backbones head to head (p6, p34) — comfortably over the two-model threshold. See `unresolved` item 2 for the caveat that four of the five are architectural relatives; the tag records the head-to-head comparison, not the independence claim.
- `templates-on`: AF3 was run with "templates enabled (default)" (p34). The tag is applied on that explicit statement. **`no-template-no-msa` deliberately not applied** — it is the opposite regime, and the one arm that would have established it (AF3-NT) is unreported. **`state-annotated-input` deliberately not applied**: KinCoRe's DFG/αC annotations select complexes into the dataset; they are never fed to a model (oracle route 2), so applying it would misfile this paper alongside genuine state-biasing methods.
- `ensemble` + `single-state`: the v3-sanctioned pair for a method that samples widely and collapses onto one. 25 predictions per complex (p34) reduced to the single lowest-RMSD member (p37). Note the ensemble is over ligand poses, not protein conformational states — flagged in `states_generated` so a state-sampling query is not misled.
- `binary-predicate` + `continuous-metric`: the 2.0 Å success threshold (p9, p11) and the continuous RMSD/QS-score axes (p35–36) are both used, throughout, as first-class metrics. **`rmsd-only` deliberately not applied** — QS-score is a genuine second axis and carries the paper's decoupling argument.
- `saturating-metric`: QS-score sits against its 1.0 ceiling on the orthosteric arm (>0.90, up to ≈0.95, p9/p17), and Protenix shows ortho ≈ allo ≈ 0.92–0.93 on Fig 3M — numeric saturation, not an axis artefact. The Fig 3K–O axis truncation is filed separately under `hides`, per v3 change 9.
- `visual-metric`: applied for the spatial half of the frustration argument only. Frustration *classes* are Z-thresholded and quantitative, but the claim that carries the explanation — connected 8–12 Å minimal-frustration shells at orthosteric sites vs "isolated, spatially disconnected patches" at allosteric sites (p25, p28) — is read off surface renders with no contiguity metric and no quantitative panel. So is "the spatial extent of these networks correlates with prediction confidence" (p26). That is calling a result by eye, which is what this tag exists for.
- `oracle-leak`: routes 5 and 6, both explicit and both load-bearing. Route 6 is the sharper one: "For each model, the best prediction per complex (lowest ligand pose RMSD) was selected from generated models." (p37) — every headline number is an oracle-selected best-of-25, and the blind top-1 gap is never reported. Route 1 is partially open for AF3 via enabled templates. Applied unhedged.
- `design-level-oracle`: route 7, kept distinct from `oracle-leak` as v3 requires. The strata are built from complexes already known to be allosteric (p34) and the expected outcome is declared as the study's hypothesis before any prediction is read (p5). This is stratum construction, not pipeline contamination, and is the weaker finding — but it is what makes "the models never found the allosteric site" a statement about a pre-labelled set rather than a discovery.
- `no-anti-memorization`: the decision was close and is recorded here. A date/identity filter **does** exist — "only complexes deposited in the Protein Data Bank after 2020 … Complexes with >30% sequence identity to training data were excluded" (p33) — but it is stated only in the **Orthosteric** Datasets section, "after 2020" does not post-date the training cutoff of any of the five models tested, and the **allosteric stratum, which carries the entire claim, has no date filter and no identity filter of any kind** (p34). No control arm of any sort is run on either side. Since the tag's purpose is to answer "does this paper's headline result have memorization controlled", the honest answer is no, and `anti-memorization` would be a false positive. The partial orthosteric-only filter is fully recorded in `anti_memorization_design` so the nuance is not lost.
- `ligand-driven` + `seed-only`: the only conditioning input is the ligand SMILES (p34) — `ligand-driven`; and the only source of within-complex diversity is stochastic sampling, "5 seeds, 5 diffusion samples per seed" (p34) — `seed-only`. Together they say the models were run stock and undirected, which is the correct reading of the benchmark. `directed-state`, `partner-driven`, `peptide-driven`, `g-protein-mimetic`, `nanobody`, `apo-sampling` all absent.
- `orthosteric` + `allosteric-site`: the two strata studied, 2,275 and 1,966 complexes. Both mark **what was studied**.
- `cryptic-pocket`: applied on two independent grounds, both real but neither quantified. (i) The allosteric panel is stated to contain them: "The representative panel includes buried allosteric pockets, surface-exposed allosteric grooves, and cryptic sites that form upon ligand binding." (p28); the framing sentence in the Introduction is about "allosteric binding sites and transient pockets that only form upon ligand binding" (p5). (ii) The orthosteric MDT stratum is itself selected for induced-fit character — "requiring AlphaFold-predicted apo-pocket deviation from experimental holo-conformation >2.0 Å RMSD or pocket LDDT <0.80" (p33) — i.e. 412 of the 2,275 orthosteric complexes are pockets that change substantially on binding. **Caveat carried deliberately:** cryptic sites are never counted, never separated into a stratum, and never reported on separately, so this paper answers "were cryptic pockets present" and not "how did models do on cryptic pockets". Applied so the paper is findable, with the caveat here so it is not over-read.
- `allosteric-failure`: applied, and this is the paper's **result**, distinct from the two site tags above which mark its subject. The v3 gloss is "no model or setting ever sampled the allosteric site"; this paper's finding is slightly weaker and should be read as such — the models often reach the right general region (allosteric QS 0.70–0.85, p17) but fail to localise the pocket (3.5–6.3 Å) or resolve the pose (5.2–6.8 Å, 25–35 % success at 2 Å), across all five architectures. It is a collapse, not a total absence of sampling. Applied because it is unambiguously the failure result the tag exists to surface, with the strength recorded here.
- `preprint`: what was actually read (p1, "not certified by peer review"). **`peer-reviewed` deliberately not applied**, even though a peer-reviewed version exists, because nothing in the peer-reviewed version was read and its numbers may differ. See `unresolved` item 1.
- `precedent` + `contrast` + `negative-result`: matching the dual `stance`. `negative-result` because the paper's entire contribution is a documented failure mode.
- `comparator-numbers`: 40-plus per-model, per-stratum values with pages in `metrics_reported`, directly tabulatable against any co-folding result. **`figure-exemplar` deliberately not applied** — the figures are the weakest part of this paper (see `hides` on rows 2C, 2D, 3A/C-E and 3K-O), and the tag is for figures worth emulating.
- **Considered and rejected: `confidence-as-discriminator`.** The methods do declare confidence use — L-pLDDT/L-PAE/ipTM and a "Confidence-Error Correlation … mapping confidence scores against actual RMSD" (p36), plus iPTM ranking (p34). But **no confidence result appears anywhere in the paper**, and the numbers that are reported come from best-by-RMSD selection (p37), not from confidence. Tagging it would return this paper to a query for papers that used confidence to judge conformational correctness, and the querier would find nothing. Recorded in `confidence_as_discriminator` and in `controls_run` instead.
- **Considered and rejected: `experimental-validation`, `experimental`, `prospective`, `unpowered`, `md`, `md-emulator`, `enhanced-sampling`, `latent-steering`, `msa-subsample`, `msa-state-filter`, `af-cluster`, `template-state-bias`, `two-state`, `continuum`, `threat`, `background`.** No wet-lab work is done (Materials Availability: "No materials were synthesized in this study.", p38); nothing is prospective; n is large so the study is not unpowered; no MSA, template, latent or sampling intervention of any kind is performed.
