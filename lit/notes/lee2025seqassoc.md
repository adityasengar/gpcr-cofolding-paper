### The method, stated precisely (the paper's contribution)

Recorded here because the schema has no dedicated field and this is what the note will be
queried for.

**How sequences are associated.** CF-random does *not* group or partition sequences at
all. It draws **uniformly random subsets** of the full ColabFold MSA at seven fixed, very
shallow depths (`--max-seq`:`--max-extra-seq` = 1:2, 2:4, 4:8, 8:16, 16:32, 32:64,
64:128), 5 AF2 models × 5 seeds at each depth, plus one deep pass at 512:5120 — 200
structures per target (p9). "Sequence association" is the *explanation* the authors give
for why this works, not a step in the algorithm: p4, "we hypothesized that AF2 may
generate alternative conformations of fold switchers through **sequence association:
relating features of homologous sequences to a learned structural landscape. This differs
from coevolutionary inference, which can be used to infer protein structure without prior
knowledge.**" The mechanism is that a *single* homologous sequence in a too-shallow
alignment can key AF2 into a fold it memorised in training. p6: "This approach is much
like homology modeling but more powerful because only a single 'template' sequence–rather
than an input structure–is needed to produce the alternative conformation."

**What governs the grouping.** Nothing groups the sequences. What governs the outcome is
(i) **sampling depth** — depth below the coevolutionary threshold is the operative
variable, p2: "Shallow sampling directs the AF2 network to predict structures from sparse
sequence information insufficient for robust coevolutionary inference"; and (ii) **which
homologs happen to survive the random draw** — which is why the Sa1 failure is a *sequence
availability* failure, p4: "although the initial iteration of sequence search for Sa1
yielded sequences unique to both folds, subsequent sequence-search iterations left its
final MSA without three-α-helix bundle sequences."

**How it differs from clustering by edit distance.** Clustering methods (AF-Cluster is the
named comparator) partition the MSA into groups of mutually similar sequences and predict
one structure per cluster, so the *composition* of each input alignment is determined by
sequence-space distance. CF-random imposes no distance structure whatsoever: subsets are
uniformly random and are far shallower than any clustering method's outputs — p2: "random
sequence sampling at such shallow depths has not been tested systematically; previous work
suggested a minimum sampling depth of 8:16", and "setting it apart from previously proposed
methods that used a minimum of 24 sequences." The paper's causal claim is that the
alternative conformation arises **not** from any coevolutionary signal a cluster would
concentrate, but from AF2's memory being triggered by an individual sequence: p4,
"Coevolutionary analysis of the MSAs that successfully produced each alternative
fold-switched conformation revealed few unique alternative evolutionary couplings,
indicating that predictions of alternative conformations of fold switchers are not
generally driven by coevolutionary inference." The *only* clustering in the pipeline is
**post hoc, over predicted structures** (Foldseek bitscore → PCA → HDBSCAN → K-medoids,
p10), i.e. a model-selection step, not an input-construction step.

## C. Conformational core

- **states_generated**: **ensemble + two-state.** A 200-member ensemble is produced per
  target (p9: "This leads to 200 predicted structures overall"), but the entire evaluation
  collapses it to exactly two named conformations. p9 Fold-switching predictions: "The
  total number of successes was the number of sequences from which both conformations
  could be generated; one conformation had a higher TM-score corresponding to Fold1 and
  the other to Fold2." Blind mode likewise returns "three representative structures" per
  HDBSCAN cluster (p10) and reports two per E. coli hit. No continuum or free-energy
  coordinate is produced anywhere.

- **structural_priors_used**: **Extensive, and mostly legitimate as design-time
  knowledge — recorded here rather than as leakage where it shaped target choice rather
  than the pipeline.**
  - **Both conformations of every benchmarked protein were already deposited and their PDB
    IDs held.** p9: "PDB IDs corresponding to both conformations of all proteins tested
    here (fold switchers and other conformational changes) are in Supplementary Tables
    1/2/3; regions of proteins that switch folds are also reported, if applicable."
  - **The fold-switching regions were pre-defined from the deposited structures**, and used
    to define the scoring region — p2: "Template modeling (TM)-scores of the fold-switching
    regions of each prediction are compared to their experimentally determined structures,
    since this has been shown to discriminate between fold switchers better than overall
    TM-score, though the overall score is considered also."
  - **Dominant vs alternative labels are themselves defined against prior structural
    knowledge**, p9: "the dominant conformation is typically defined as that which CF
    predicts most frequently from full MSA sampling, except in a few cases where
    memorization has been shown or is expected to overrule coevolutionary inference…. By
    exclusion, the alternative conformation is defined as that which AF2 rarely–if ever–
    samples from full MSAs and therefore requires an alternative sampling strategy."
  - **Sa1's three-helix-bundle homologs were found by BLASTing against the PDB**, p10:
    "Protein BLAST was used to search the sequence of Sa1 V90T (PDB ID: 8E6Y) against the
    PDB. Three hits corresponding to the 3-helix-bundle were identified with PDB IDs:
    2FS1, 2MH8, 1GJS."
  - **Foldseek searches against the PDB were used as a filter in the E. coli arm**, p7:
    "we performed Foldseek searches on all hits produced by blind mode and examined their
    oligomeric states. If the target had a close homolog (≥70% sequence identity) with an
    oligomeric state whose interchain contacts overlapped with contacts unique to the
    predicted alternative conformation, the prediction was discarded".
  - **Oligomer stoichiometry for the multimer arm was taken from the PDB**, p9: "The number
    of chains in predicted homo-oligomers matched those from the biological assemblies in
    the PDB."
  - **The 92-protein set was chosen partly *because* it is in AF2's training set** — see
    `oracle_leakage` route 7.

  mode prediction", "Fold-switching predictions", "Blind mode predictions", "E. coli

  - **Retrospective**: the entire fold-switcher benchmark (92), the SPEACH-AF 14, the OC23

  - **Sampling depth is the primary handle and it is not state-specific.** Depth controls
    *how much* coevolutionary information is removed, not *which* state emerges. p2:
    "Shallow sampling directs the AF2 network to predict structures from sparse sequence
    information insufficient for robust coevolutionary inference." Depth-to-state is
    reported as an empirical observation after the fact (p2: 72% of successes at 4:8 or
    below), never as a lever set in advance.
  - **Random seed and model index** are swept (5 models × 5 seeds per depth, p9); these are
    diversity handles, not direction handles.
  - **MSA composition IS a genuine state handle, demonstrated once, and it is oracle-
    dependent.** Adding sequences of homologs known to adopt the target fold aims the
    prediction at that fold: p6, "When the MSA was rebalanced with 3-α-helix… and
    α/β-plait, CF-random successfully predicts both Sa1 structures. It predicted the
    α/β-plait from the full MSA and the 3-α-helix bundle with --max-seq = 2,
    --max-extra-seq = 4." And p6: "only a single 'template' sequence–rather than an input
    structure–is needed to produce the alternative conformation." **This is a real
    directional handle but it is only usable when you already know which fold you want and
    can find a homolog that adopts it** — i.e. it converts the method into sequence-level
    homology modelling.
  - **Oligomeric context (AF2-multimer weights with PDB-derived chain counts) shifts which
    conformation is produced** in 6 cases: p3, "Of the 32 successful predictions of fold
    switchers, six were improved from additional molecular context supplied by the
    AF2-multimer model." This is context, not a state instruction; and it yielded only one
    genuinely new alternative conformation across all 92 (p3: "only one additional
    alternative conformation was found: the dimeric form of… DAN").
  - No ligand, partner-protein, nanobody, peptide, state-annotated template or
    state-filtered MSA handle is offered in the shipped tool.

- **anti_memorization_design**: **NONE. Not merely absent — inverted.** The benchmark was
  deliberately built from proteins expected to be *in* AF2's training set. p2: "We tested
  CF-random on 92 fold-switching proteins likely in AF2's training set". p4: "AF2 has
  memorized some alternative conformations of fold switchers, we hypothesized that AF2 may
  generate alternative conformations of fold switchers through sequence association". p9:
  "Much like homology modeling, sequence association requires prior learning of protein
  structure." **No training-set cutoff date is defined anywhere in the paper. No held-out
  set, no post-cutoff set, no temporal split, no n for such a set.** The E. coli blind
  screen is a *novelty* screen (no deposited alternative conformation exists for the 52
  hits), not a temporal holdout — the target proteins themselves are in RefSeq and their
  dominant folds are largely known, and the paper never claims otherwise.

- **anti_memorization_control**: **NONE RUN.** There is no arm, in any form, that separates
  memorised from non-memorised targets, no post-cutoff arm, and no analysis of prediction
  success versus deposition date or training-set membership. The closest thing in the paper
  is a **negative control on the rival mechanism, not on memorisation**: the coevolutionary
  analysis (p4, Fig 5a) shows couplings unique to the alternative conformation are *weaker*
  than those unique to the dominant, which the authors use to argue *for* memorisation-
  based sequence association rather than against it. The paper treats memorisation as the
  explanation, not as the confound to be excluded — p9: "To our knowledge, this is the
  first study showing that AF2 sometimes works by sequence association."

## D. Claims

- **central_conclusion**: Randomly subsampling a full MSA at depths far below the
  coevolutionary-inference threshold (as few as 1–2 sequences) lets ColabFold/AF2 produce
  alternative conformations of fold-switching proteins more often and with ~6× less
  sampling than MSA-masking or MSA-clustering methods; the mechanism is argued to be
  "sequence association" — AF2 keying an individual homologous sequence to a fold it
  learned in training — rather than coevolutionary inference from the alignment. A
  reference-free "blind mode" that clusters the predicted ensemble by Foldseek structural
  similarity was run on 2126 E. coli proteins and returned 52 putative fold switchers,
  from which the authors extrapolate that up to 5% of E. coli proteins switch folds.

- **stance**: **`precedent` + `contrast`. PROVISIONAL — the user's call, not settled here.**
  - **Precedent on findings and on method framing.** It is a peer-reviewed, proteome-scale
    demonstration that ultra-shallow *random* MSA subsampling beats both MSA-masking
    (SPEACH-AF, AFSample2) and MSA-clustering approaches, with head-to-head numbers on
    three benchmark sets and a clean statement of the mechanism ("sequence association")
    that is a memorisation account rather than a coevolution account. It also supplies a
    reference-free selection procedure (Foldseek → PCA → HDBSCAN → K-medoids) for picking
    conformations without knowing the answer, which is the hard part of any prospective
    pipeline.

    targets chosen because they are in AF2's training set, with a threshold that was
    lowered for at least one target without stating the new value, with sampling extended
    until the known answer appeared in 6/7 cases, with accuracy reported as best-of-200
    against the reference, and with no held-out or post-cutoff arm of any kind. The
    proteome-scale claim (5%) rests on 52 predictions with **zero** new experimental
    confirmation and no false-positive control on non-switching proteins.
