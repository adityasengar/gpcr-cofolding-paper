## B. Scope

- **system**: GPCR. Class A and class B. No other protein family appears.
- **n_targets**: three distinct scopes, which must not be collapsed.
  - **Design campaigns: 3 receptors** — Apelin Receptor (APJR, class A), Glucagon-Like Peptide-1 Receptor (GLP-1R, class B), Growth Hormone Secretagogue Receptor (GHSR, class A) — p2, pp7–10.
  - **Structure-prediction benchmark: 45 GPCR–peptide complexes** — "Data from GPCRdb released after June 30, 2021, filtered for 45 high-quality complexes (30 from class A and 15 from class B), was used to benchmark GPCR−peptide structure prediction across various folding models" (p11).
  - **Confidence-screening evaluation: 1 receptor** — CXCR4, from the PPI-Affinity data set (pp4–5, p11). Denominator implied at 56 peptides by the random-predictor hit rate 17/56 (p7); never stated directly.
- **method_class**: co-folding + template-biasing + **peptide sequence design (inverse folding)** + experimental validation. Four components in one pipeline: (i) backbone seed retrieval by FoldSeek from the PDB; (ii) sequence generation by an inverse folding model / ProteinMPNN; (iii) co-folding of each candidate against a **state-specified** receptor by HelixFold-Multistate, with state supplied through state-matched templates; (iv) cell-based functional assay. Not MSA-subsampling and not MSA-state-filtering — neither appears anywhere in the paper.
- **backbones**: AlphaFold-lineage only. HelixFold-Multimer (a stated reproduction of AlphaFold-Multimer, p2), fine-tuned to give **HelixFold-Multistate (HF-Multistate)**, the paper's model. Benchmarked head to head against **four folding-model configurations**: AF-Multimer, AF-Multistate, HF-Multimer, HF-Multistate (p4). Note this is 4 *configurations* across **2 codebases** (AlphaFold and HelixFold), all AF2-derived — no AF3, Boltz, Chai, OF3 or Protenix is run. Judgement recorded under `unresolved`: not tagged `multi-backbone` on the ground that only one backbone family is represented.
  - Sampling protocol differs between the compared models and is disclosed: "AF-Multimer and AF-Multistate utilize an ensemble of five models, each executed five times with different random seeds for each prediction. In contrast, HF-Multimer and HF-Multistate achieve competitive results using a single model executed five times with varying seeds." (p4)
- **templates**: **on, and state-annotated — this is the state handle.** "for both general protein−peptide and GPCR−peptide complexes, we employed the corresponding template processing pipelines from AlphaFold-Multimer and AlphaFold-Multistate. Specifically, for GPCR templates, we selected those that matched both the same class and activation state as the target receptor. This approach ensures that the templates provide a biologically relevant and consistent structural basis for accurate predictions and evaluations." (p11)
- **msa_handling**: **NOT REPORTED.** No MSA depth, subsampling, clustering, pinning or state filtering is described anywhere. The only statement bearing on it is "Our data processing methodology aligns with the workflow used in AlphaFold-Multimer" (p11), which implies default full MSAs but never says so. Protocol section is §4.1, p11.

## C. Conformational core

- **states_generated**: **two + single-state.** Two, in that every candidate is folded under both an active-conditioned and an inactive-conditioned receptor and the two are compared — Filter 2 is defined as "pAE_inactive < pAE_active or pLDDT_inactive > pLDDT_active" (p6). Single-state, in that exactly one operator-declared state is retained per design and everything off it is discarded: "Peptides whose predicted structures do not match the target state are systematically filtered out, ensuring that only those with the highest functional relevance are retained for further analysis." (p12). No ensemble, no continuum, no sampling of the transition. Five seeds per prediction is the only within-condition sampling stated (p4), and it is stated only for the benchmark, not for the design campaigns.

- **structural_priors_used**: extensive, and this is the substrate of the whole method rather than a defect.
  1. **Deposited GPCR–peptide co-crystal structures supply the peptide backbones.** "we use the FoldSeek tool with default settings to search the Protein Data Bank for crystallized structures similar to the target structure. The search results are then parsed to identify hits that exhibit high contact density in the desired interface area" (p11); "we apply this model to design a diverse set of candidate peptide sequences derived from cocrystal structures of GPCR targets and peptide backbones" (p11).
  2. **Deposited state-annotated GPCR structures supply the templates**, matched on class *and* activation state (p11, quoted above).
  3. **Known endogenous ligands used as seeds with their binding modes fixed.** "we employed Ghrelin and LEAP2 as the seed sequences. Given their established binding modes, we constrained key hotspot residues and utilized ProteinMPNN to generate a focused library of 200 peptide candidates." (p10)
  4. **A published receptor activation mechanism used as a filter.** "We utilize the GHSR state-transition mechanism as an additional filtering method. The distance (dWF) of the WFF cluster indicates peptide activity. A shift toward the active state suggests an agonist, while a shift toward the inactive state implies antagonistic activity." (p10)
  5. **Deposited reference complexes for interaction analysis**: Apelin-13–APJR (PDB 8XZG, p6), GLP-1–GLP-1R (PDB 6X18, p8), SSR2 active 7XAU / inactive 7XNA and CML1 active 7YKD and CALCR active 8F0K as benchmark illustrations (p3).
  6. **>7000 GPCR–peptide and protein–peptide complexes** from Propedia v1.0 and GPCRdb used to fine-tune the model (p11).

- **oracle_leakage**: **PRESENT.** Routes 1, 2, 4, 5, 6 and 7 all fire; route 3 is absent. The important discrimination for our purposes is *where* the leak sits: routes 1 and 2 leak in **training, in conditioning and in benchmark evaluation** simultaneously, and the paper is open about all three.

  **Route 1 — deposited structures used as input or template. FIRES, at conditioning time.**
  "Specifically, for GPCR templates, we selected those that matched both the same class and activation state as the target receptor." (p11)
  "All candidate peptides, generated by the peptide design module, are paired with state-specific GPCR targets and undergo structural prediction by using this model." (p12)
  Peptide backbones are likewise lifted from deposited complexes: "the highest-ranking hit is selected to extract the backbone of the interface segment, which serves as the seed for protein sequence design" (p11).

  **Route 2 — state annotations from a curated database driving templates or alignments. FIRES, and in three distinct places.**
  - *Training*: "We utilized data from the Propedia v1.0 database and GPCRdb (data released before June 30, 2021), comprising over 7000 entries, to fine-tune HelixFold-Multimer to enhance its capabilities in predicting GPCR−peptide complex structures" (p11). The fine-tuning corpus is GPCRdb's state-annotated GPCR–peptide complexes; the model is trained on exactly the annotation that later becomes its conditioning signal.
  - *Conditioning*: templates matched on annotated activation state (p11, above); "we compiled the data list and utilized the same multistate data pipeline as mentioned in AlphaFold-Multistate to process state-specific input features" (p11).
  - *Evaluation*: **the state label for each benchmark target is taken from the deposited annotation of the very structure being predicted.** "The model was conditioned on the specified receptor state, which was derived from the annotations in the experimental data." (p4) This is the single most revealing sentence in the paper for our purposes.

  **Route 3 — cluster labels derived from known states. NONE FOUND.** No clustering step exists anywhere in the pipeline; the protocol is described in §4.1–§4.3, pp11–12, and contains no clustering, and no cluster labels appear in Results.

  **Route 4 — hyperparameters, thresholds or sweep ranges tuned against the evaluation set. FIRES, twice.**
  - The Filter-1 confidence threshold is anchored to a peptide whose measured affinity was already known, and the hit definition uses the same known affinities: "The reference peptide is from the CXCR4-peptide affinity data set with a reported IC50 of 966 nM and was selected as a reference because its affinity is close to the 1 μM threshold used to define hits." (p6) and "Here, a hit is defined as a peptide with an affinity below 1 μM." (p7). Filter-1's discriminating power is then reported *on the same CXCR4 set* from which the reference and the hit threshold were drawn.
  - Per-target confidence cutoffs vary between campaigns with no stated justification: "70 candidates with pLDDT > 60 and pAE < 10 are selected" for APJR (p7); "After applying essential filtering criteria (pLDDT > 85 and pAE < 6.5), around 1400 peptides remained" for GLP-1R (p9); "candidates with a PLDDT value of >70 were selected" for GHSR (p10). Three different thresholds on three targets, never justified.
  - Note in the paper's favour: no per-target tuning against a *held structure* occurs in the design campaigns, because no structure of the designed complex existed.

  **Route 5 — success defined post hoc by RMSD/TM to a structure they had. FIRES for the benchmark only.**
  "All evaluation metrics were computed with respect to the experimentally resolved GPCR−peptide complex structures." (p4). Metrics are DockQ, iRMS, % correct (DockQ > 0.8), % correct (DockQ > 0.49), % correct (iRMS < 2.0), % correct (TM RMSD < 2.0) (p4). This is standard and unavoidable for a benchmark, but it is route 5.
  **Absent for the design campaigns**, where success is EC50/IC50 in a cell assay and no structure of the designed complex exists (pp7–10, §4.4 p12).

  **Route 6 — best/worst case labels assigned against a held reference. FIRES.**
  "To demonstrate HF-Multistate's improved modeling of functionally relevant GPCR regions over both AF-Multimer, we selected cases based on TM6 RMSD (Figure 2i)." (p4) The three showcase renders (CML1, CALCR, SSR2) in Figure 2i–k are chosen by their agreement with the reference.

  **Route 7 — design-level oracle use (pipeline-clean, expected answer declared first). FIRES, and it is the defining feature of the method.** Label as design-level, not pipeline leakage.
  - The operator declares the target state before any design happens, and the desired modality determines which state is declared: "This framework enables the tailored design of agonists or antagonists based on the active or inactive conformational states of GPCR structures." (p1); "A state-specified folding model (Figure 1b) is then employed to define the active or inactive states of GPCR targets" (p2).
  - The receptors were chosen because both states and peptide co-complexes are deposited and the endogenous peptides are known: APJR with Apelin-13 (p7), GLP-1R with GLP-1/Avexitide (p7), GHSR with Ghrelin and LEAP2 (p10).
  - The "correct" state in the screening evaluation is asserted a priori rather than measured: "The CXCR4−peptide affinity data set used in this study focuses on inhibitory peptides, where the inactive receptor state represents the correct functional conformation." (p5)

- **prospective**: **partial, and the two halves point opposite ways.**
  - **Prospective** in the three design campaigns: 2400 (APJR), ~5000 (GLP-1R) and 200 (GHSR) sequences were generated, filtered, synthesized and assayed, and no measurement of them existed beforehand (pp7–10). Peptide-I3 and Peptide-I4 are asserted as genuinely new: "Although the searched peptide backbones (marked with an asterisk) do not include known antagonists, our approach successfully generated novel antagonist peptides Peptide-I3 and Peptide-I4." (p9)
  - **Retrospective** in the structure-prediction benchmark that justifies the model: the target state is read off the deposited annotation of the structure being predicted (p4, route 2 above), templates are state-matched (p11), and showcase cases are picked by RMSD to the reference (p4).
  - Confounded by non-reporting: "In certain instances, we identified peptides with picomolar affinities, although these findings have been omitted here due to commercial conflicts with our partners." (p11) — an undisclosed number of campaigns are withheld, so the reported success rate is not a complete prospective record.

- **state_metric**: **binary predicate + RMSD-to-reference + visual only.** All three are in use and none subsumes the others.
  - *Binary predicate*, thresholds stated: % correct (DockQ > 0.8), % correct (DockQ > 0.49), % correct (iRMS < 2.0 Å), % correct (TM RMSD < 2.0 Å) — p4, Fig 2a–e. No justification is given for any of the four cut points.
  - *Continuous / RMSD-to-reference*: TM3, TM6, TM7 RMSD in Å (Fig 2d–h), iRMS in Å (Fig 2j), TM6-RMSD per complex (Fig 2i) — p4.
  - **Binary predicate with the threshold never stated** — the state filter that actually runs inside the design pipeline: "Complexes that do not align with the specified GPCR state, such as those with the binding pocket located within the membrane or those exhibiting a significant RMSD in the state-specific TM domain compared to the expected state, are filtered out." (p2). "Significant RMSD" is never operationalised. Same for the GHSR dWF filter: "A shift toward the active state suggests an agonist, while a shift toward the inactive state implies antagonistic activity" (p10) — no dWF cut point, no dWF values, no dWF figure.
  - *Visual only*: the three showcase state calls are made by eye from renders — "AF-Multimer (Left) fails to distinguish between the active and inactive complex states, whereas HF-Multistate (Right) successfully differentiates the two states" (p4, Figure 2k), with no number attached.
  - *Functional (wet-lab) state readout*: agonist/antagonist modality by calcium mobilization, EC50/IC50 (§4.4.2, p12). This is a functional not a conformational metric.

- **metric_saturation**: **YES, admitted by the authors.** The binary % correct metric ceases to discriminate for class B and they abandon it: "For class B GPCRs, the comparison of % Correct (TM RMSD < 2.0) is not very significant. Therefore, we included the distribution of all data points for clarity." (p4) — hence Fig 2f–h are box plots of raw RMSD rather than % correct bars. Also near-floor behaviour in Fig 2d (class A, TM3 RMSD < 2.0): AF-Multimer and HF-Multimer sit at roughly 10% while the two multistate models sit at roughly 40–50%, so the non-state-conditioned models are close to the floor of the metric. Exact bar values are never printed. Axis breaks and truncations are recorded in the figure table `hides` column, not here (Fig 2I-J scatters, Fig 2F-H y-axis).

- **directional_control**: **YES — the method is instructable, and the handle is the operator's state label, not the peptide.** This is the crux and it needs to be stated exactly.
  - **The handle is a state-annotated template set selected by an operator-supplied state label**, routed through the AlphaFold-Multistate state-specific input-feature pipeline: "for GPCR templates, we selected those that matched both the same class and activation state as the target receptor" (p11); "we compiled the data list and utilized the same multistate data pipeline as mentioned in AlphaFold-Multistate to process state-specific input features" (p11).
  - **The state is chosen before the peptide is scored, and it is chosen by the human.** "All candidate peptides, generated by the peptide design module, are paired with state-specific GPCR targets and undergo structural prediction by using this model. The resulting GPCR−peptide complex structures are rigorously assessed to ensure they align with the specified GPCR activation state." (p12). The authors' own phrasing makes the dependency explicit: "suggesting that HF-Multistate, when specifying the GPCR state, can partially capture functional shifts in peptide design" (p10).
  - **The peptide is not the state handle.** The peptide is the object being designed and scored. Where the peptide does enter a state-related decision it is only as follows: each candidate is folded twice, once under each operator-imposed state, and Filter 2 asks which imposed state the model is *more confident* about — "candidates must show stronger model confidence when conditioned on the correct receptor state (inactive), i.e., pAE_inactive < pAE_active or pLDDT_inactive > pLDDT_active" (p6). The receptor conformation in each run is fixed by the conditioning, not moved by the peptide; what the peptide changes is which of two pre-declared conformations scores better.
  - Secondary, weaker handle: the GHSR dWF/WFF-cluster structural readout used as an extra filter (p10) is measured *on the predicted complex*, so it is nearer to a peptide-sensitive conformational readout — but no dWF numbers, distributions or figure are reported anywhere, so it cannot be assessed.

- **anti_memorization_design**: **YES, and it is a good one — double cutoff, on date and on receptor identity.**
  - Cutoff definition: training data are "Propedia v1.0 database and GPCRdb (data released before June 30, 2021)" (p11); evaluation is "Data from GPCRdb released after June 30, 2021 … filtered for 45 high-quality complexes (30 from class A and 15 from class B)" (p11).
  - Receptor-identity holdout on top of the date: "We curated a recent data set of agonist and antagonist peptide−GPCR structures from GPCRdb, applying cutoffs for both release date and GPCR type. This process guarantees no overlap in GPCR types between the evaluation and training sets." (p4)
  - n = 45 (30 class A + 15 class B).
  - **No anti-memorization design at all on the design side**: the three design targets have deposited co-complexes, and their peptide backbones and hotspot residues are taken from those structures (pp10–11).

- **anti_memorization_control**: **PARTIAL — the held-out set is the benchmark, but no matched control arm isolates memorization.**
  - The 45-complex post-cutoff set was actually run and analysed (Fig 2, p3), so it is not a held-out set left unused. n = 45, so not `UNPOWERED`.
  - **But there is no pre-cutoff / seen-data arm to compare against**, so the size of any memorization effect is never measured, only avoided for HF-Multistate.
  - **The baselines are uncontrolled for this.** AF-Multimer and AF-Multistate carry their own, different and unstated training cutoffs. The paper's June 2021 boundary protects HF-Multistate's training set but says nothing about whether the AlphaFold baselines saw the 45 post-2021 structures. Baseline training cutoffs are `NOT REPORTED`. This makes the head-to-head comparison in Fig 2 asymmetric in an unexamined direction.

  | AF-Multistate arm (state conditioning, **no** GPCR–peptide fine-tuning) | that the gain is all state conditioning and the fine-tuning is redundant | p4, Fig 2a–h (p3) |

  **Controls NOT RUN, each of which bears directly on the state claim:**
  - **No state-swapped ablation.** The pipeline is never run with the *wrong* state declared to show that the designs then fail. This is the single control that would test whether the state conditioning is causal for the observed agonist/antagonist split.
  - **No scrambled-sequence or shuffled-peptide arm** at any point, computational or experimental.
  - **No no-peptide / vehicle-only arm** reported in the figures (buffer baselines are implicit in "max−baseline" but not shown as an arm).
  - **No off-target receptor selectivity arm** — no designed peptide is tested against a GPCR other than its target.
  - **No unconditioned (state-agnostic) design arm** — the pipeline is never run without the state filter to show the filter changes the outcome.

- **confidence_as_discriminator**: **YES, centrally, and validated only weakly — the authors say so themselves.**
  - Used for ranking: "we incorporate pAE interaction and peptide pLDDT metrics, derived from the folding model, to further refine the selection. A smaller pAE interaction value and a larger peptide pLDDT indicate a better peptide" (p12).
  - **Used to call the conformational state**, which is the aggressive use: Filter 2 decides a peptide's modality by comparing confidence under the two imposed states (p6, quoted under `directional_control`).
  - Validation attempted, and it is thin: correlation of confidence with measured CXCR4 affinity gives Pearson Rp = −0.23 (HF-Multistate pAE), −0.10 (AF-Multistate pAE), 0.20 (HF-Multistate pLDDT), 0.07 (AF-Multistate pLDDT) — Figure 3a,b, p5. Authors' own verdict: "our analysis reveals a weak correlation between these metrics and GPCR-−peptide binding affinity" (p5).
  - The state-discrimination use is validated only on a set where the correct state was **asserted, not measured** — "the inactive receptor state represents the correct functional conformation" (p5) — so the validation of confidence-as-state-discriminator is circular with route 7.
  - Downstream filter performance is reported: after Filter 1, HF-Multistate 11/26 (42%) vs AF-Multistate 16/48 (33%); after Filter 1 + Filter 2, 11/22 (50%) vs 7/20 (35%), against a random baseline of 17/56 (30%) (p7).

## D. Claims

- **central_conclusion**: A GPCR–peptide-specialised co-folding model (HelixFold-Multistate, HelixFold-Multimer fine-tuned on GPCRdb + Propedia and given AlphaFold-Multistate's state-specific input features) predicts GPCR–peptide complexes in a *specified* activation state more accurately than AF-Multimer, HF-Multimer and AF-Multistate on a 45-complex post-cutoff benchmark; and its confidence scores, evaluated under the operator-declared state, can be used as a filter to select designed peptides that turn out to be agonists or antagonists as intended. Three prospective campaigns (APJR, GLP-1R, GHSR) yielded assayed peptides down to EC50 4.2 nM (APJR agonist) and IC50 874 nM (GLP-1R antagonist, better than Avexitide at 1913 nM).

- **necessity_claims** (verbatim + page):
  - "Effective GPCR-targeting peptides must not only achieve high binding affinity but also selectively stabilize specific receptor conformations, thereby modulating the energy landscape to favor the desired functional outcomes." (spans p1–p2)
  - "Therefore, integrating GPCR conformational states and state-specific interactions into peptide design strategies is critical for achieving precise modulation of the receptor activity." (p2)
  - "However, these methods do not allow for precise control over the GPCR's functional state (e.g., active or inactive). This limitation hinders the ability to design peptide agonists and antagonists that accurately correspond to specific receptor states." (p2, on HelixDiff and similar)
  - "Traditional peptide design methods often overlook the multiple conformational states of GPCRs, which can significantly impact ligand binding and receptor activation." (p10)
  - "Our state-specific peptide design approach has achieved nontrivial success, although enhancing its performance to identify peptides with subnanomolar binding affinities remains a formidable challenge. Transforming agonist peptides to antagonist peptides proves to be even more demanding." (p11)
  - "Designing antagonistic peptides for GPCRs is likely more challenging than agonists as most existing peptide therapeutics for GPCRs are agonists." (p2)

- **novelty_claims** (verbatim + page):
  - "To address this, we developed an efficient, state-specific peptide design pipeline targeting GPCRs. This framework enables the tailored design of agonists or antagonists based on the active or inactive conformational states of GPCR structures." (p1, abstract)
  - "In this study, we present a computational method for the state-specific peptide design targeting GPCRs (Figure 1)." (p2)
  - "Notably, this peptide−GPCR complex structure prediction and state-specific filtering strategy, enabled by the fine-tuned HelixFold-Multistate model, represent the core contribution of this work." (p12)
  - "Our computational pipeline addresses this critical gap by explicitly incorporating the GPCR activation state into the peptide design process." (p10)
  - "This is a significant advancement over recent rational design methods like EvoBind, EvoPlay, or PepMLM, which do not consider GPCR state transitions." (p10)
  - "This integrated approach allows us to design unconstrained, state-specific functional peptides, addressing a gap left by methods like HelixDiff, which focuses solely on structure-stable helix peptides." (p10)
  - "Although the searched peptide backbones (marked with an asterisk) do not include known antagonists, our approach successfully generated novel antagonist peptides Peptide-I3 and Peptide-I4." (p9)
  - "The development of state-specific peptides targeting G protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs) represents a significant advancement in the field of drug discovery and peptide therapeutics." (p10)

- **stated_limits**:
  - Agonist/antagonist asymmetry: "we acknowledge that the performance difference between agonist and antagonist design highlights an area for future refinement, particularly in the design of peptides that must prevent receptor activation." (p10)
  - Sub-nanomolar affinity out of reach: "enhancing its performance to identify peptides with subnanomolar binding affinities remains a formidable challenge" (p11).
  - The GHSR campaign used a different, weaker protocol: "unlike the APJR and GLP-1R experiments, we only utilized canonical peptide seeds Ghrelin and LEAP2 to design peptides for GHSR without the FoldSeek-based diverse seeds discovery process, and the designed sequences are not as diverse as previous two experiments" (p10); "This target was investigated earlier than APJP and GLP-1P, so we did not use FoldSeek for automated backbone retrieval" (p10).
  - Inverse folding mode collapse: "This result (Table 3) highlights the importance of diverse peptide seed backbones because current inverse folding based models may converge to several sequences given limited backbones." (p10)
  - Training data gap for antagonists: "our discriminator model, HelixFold-Multistate, lacking training on ample antagonist peptide structural data" (p11); "while the GPCRdb database contains few antagonist peptides, most GPCR-targeting antagonists are small molecules" (p2).
  - Confidence metrics are weak affinity predictors: "our analysis reveals a weak correlation between these metrics and GPCR-−peptide binding affinity" (p5).
  - The binary state metric fails for class B: "For class B GPCRs, the comparison of % Correct (TM RMSD < 2.0) is not very significant." (p4)
  - Selective reporting, disclosed: "In certain instances, we identified peptides with picomolar affinities, although these findings have been omitted here due to commercial conflicts with our partners." (p11)
  - FoldSeek explicitly de-emphasised as non-essential: "It should be noted that FoldSeek is not central to our method, and alternative approaches or manual searches for peptide backbones can be equally applied." (p7)
  - MMGBSA de-emphasised: "The MMGBSA step served as an auxiliary filter and was not central to the overall selection criteria." (p7)

- **stance**: `precedent on findings + contrast on mechanism and rigour` — **provisional, the user's call.**
  - *Precedent*: this is the nearest published work to peptide-and-state co-modelling in a structure-prediction model, with experimental confirmation. It establishes that a state-conditioned co-folding model's confidence, evaluated under a declared state, correlates with designed-peptide modality well enough to be a useful filter, and it does so with wet-lab EC50/IC50 in hand.
  - *Contrast*: the state is an **operator-supplied conditioning input routed through state-matched templates** (p11), not a property emerging from the peptide co-input. No experiment varies a peptide with the state input held fixed and reports the resulting conformation. No state-swap ablation is run. The state filter's own threshold ("significant RMSD in the state-specific TM domain", p2) is never operationalised, and the GHSR dWF filter is used without a single reported number.

## The three decisive questions, answered explicitly

**Q1. Is the receptor's conformational STATE a conditioning input, and how is it supplied?**

**YES — the state is a conditioning input, supplied as an operator-chosen state *label* that selects state-matched *templates*, not as a full 3D structure of the specific target complex and not inferred from anything.** The state is chosen by the operator before the peptide is ever scored.

- "The model was conditioned on the specified receptor state, which was derived from the annotations in the experimental data." (p4)
- "Specifically, for GPCR templates, we selected those that matched both the same class and activation state as the target receptor." (p11)
- "we compiled the data list and utilized the same multistate data pipeline as mentioned in AlphaFold-Multistate to process state-specific input features" (p11)
- "A state-specified folding model (Figure 1b) is then employed to define the active or inactive states of GPCR targets, predict the structures of the GPCR−peptide complexes, and assign a confidence score to each complex." (p2)
- "All candidate peptides, generated by the peptide design module, are paired with state-specific GPCR targets and undergo structural prediction by using this model." (p12)

**Stated plainly, as instructed: the state was chosen by the operator beforehand, and the peptide is not doing the selecting.** The receptor conformation the model produces is fixed by the template/label conditioning; the peptide's only role in any state-related decision is to make one of two *already-declared* states score better than the other (Filter 2, p6). The authors themselves make the dependency explicit: "HF-Multistate, **when specifying the GPCR state**, can partially capture functional shifts in peptide design" (p10, emphasis added).

Nuance worth keeping straight so we are fair to them: it is *not* the case that they hand the model a solved structure of the exact target complex in the target state. What is supplied is a class-and-state-matched **template set** drawn from other GPCRs, in the AlphaFold-Multistate manner. That is weaker than handing over the answer structure, and stronger than a bare label. It is still an operator-chosen state.

**Q2. Is the peptide an OUTPUT being designed, or an INPUT driving the receptor into a state?**

**OUTPUT. Unambiguously.** The pipeline direction is: seed backbone from the PDB → inverse-folding sequence generation → co-fold each candidate against a *pre-state-specified* receptor → filter and rank by confidence → synthesize and assay.

- "The workflow begins with the generation of a diverse pool of candidate peptides using a peptide design module (Figure 1a). A state-specified folding model (Figure 1b) is then employed to define the active or inactive states of GPCR targets, predict the structures of the GPCR−peptide complexes, and assign a confidence score to each complex." (p2)
- "we apply this model to design a diverse set of candidate peptide sequences derived from cocrystal structures of GPCR targets and peptide backbones. These peptide candidates are then evaluated using HelixFold-Multistate to identify the most promising sequences for further investigation." (p11)
- "The fine-tuned HelixFold-Multistate model is employed to identify promising peptide candidates by accurately predicting their binding conformations to GPCR targets." (pp11–12)

The peptide is of course also a *sequence input to the folding model* at the scoring step — every co-folding run takes receptor + peptide. But it is an input to a **scorer**, downstream of a state that has already been fixed, and the thing being optimised over is the peptide. The state is upstream and independent. There is no arm in which a peptide is supplied in order to move a receptor into a state.

**Q3. Is there any experiment in which a peptide sequence is varied and the resulting receptor conformation changes as a consequence?**

**NO — not computationally.** No experiment in this paper varies a peptide sequence with the receptor state input **held fixed** and reports a resulting change in predicted receptor conformation. The protocol that would have to contain such an experiment is §4.3 "State-Specific Folding Model to Select Promising Peptides" (**p12**), together with §4.1 (**p11**) and the Figure 2 benchmark description (**p4**); none of them describes one. The absence is checkable at those pages.

The three nearest approaches, and why each falls short:

1. **Figure 2k, SSR2 (p3–4).** Two peptides differing by two residues (agonist FCFWKTCT vs antagonist FCYWKTCY) yield distinct active and inactive complexes under HF-Multistate but not under AF-Multimer: "the agonist peptide FCFWKTCT and the antagonist peptide FCYWKTCY are challenging to differentiate due to their subtle sequence differences. AF-Multimer (Left) fails to distinguish between the active and inactive complex states, whereas HF-Multistate (Right) successfully differentiates the two states." (p4) **This is not peptide-driven state change**, because the whole of Figure 2 was run under the rule "The model was conditioned on the specified receptor state, which was derived from the annotations in the experimental data" (p4) — the state label co-varied with the peptide, supplied from the deposited annotation. What the panel shows is that a state-conditioned model *follows* the state label while an unconditioned one does not. (Residual ambiguity noted in `unresolved` item 4: the Fig 2k caption does not restate the conditioning.)

2. **Filter 2 (pp6–7).** Each peptide is folded twice, under both imposed states, and the confidence scores compared: "candidates must show stronger model confidence when conditioned on the correct receptor state (inactive), i.e., pAE_inactive < pAE_active or pLDDT_inactive > pLDDT_active" (p6). The receptor conformation is imposed in each run; **what varies with the peptide is the score, not the conformation.**

3. **The GHSR dWF filter (p10)** is the only place a receptor-side structural coordinate is read *off the prediction* and used to classify a peptide: "We utilize the GHSR state-transition mechanism as an additional filtering method. The distance (dWF) of the WFF cluster indicates peptide activity. A shift toward the active state suggests an agonist, while a shift toward the inactive state implies antagonistic activity." (p10) This is the closest thing in the paper to a peptide-sensitive conformational readout — but **no dWF value, distribution, threshold or figure is reported anywhere**, it is applied to one target (GHSR, the weakest campaign), and the paper does not say whether the state conditioning was held fixed while it was measured. It cannot be assessed as evidence.

**Separately, and it must not be confused with the above: YES experimentally.** Varying the peptide sequence does change the receptor's *functional* state in the wet lab — that is what the agonist/antagonist assays measure, and some peptides built on an agonist scaffold came out as antagonists: "some peptides derived from the Ghrelin scaffold displayed antagonistic activity" (p10); Peptide-I3 and Peptide-I4, which resemble the agonist GLP-1, act as antagonists (p9). But these are **calcium-mobilization functional readouts** (§4.4.2, p12), not structural measurements. **No structure of any designed complex was ever determined.** Predicted conformation and confirmed function are entirely separate in this paper and must not be merged.

**What was experimentally validated, and what was not.** Confirmed: EC50/IC50 and agonist-vs-antagonist modality for 23 designed peptides across 3 receptors, in cell-based calcium assays with native-ligand and drug comparators (Tables 1–3, pp7, 9, 10). Predicted only: every structure in the paper's design half — all GPCR–peptide complexes for the designs (Figs 4e, 5d–i) are HF-Multistate outputs, never solved. The benchmark half (Fig 2) compares against real deposited structures, but those are other people's structures, not validations of these designs.

## A note on v3, as requested

- **`states_generated` dual-value guidance still under-determines the "two conditioned runs" case.** This paper produces two structures per candidate, but they are two *separately conditioned* runs, not two states sampled from one process. "two" reads as a capability claim it has not earned. The schema would be sharper with a way to mark whether the multiple states come from sampling or from repeated conditioning — the difference is exactly what separates a state-sampling method from a state-obeying one.
- **`directional_control` has no slot for *who holds the handle*.** The field asks "can the method be instructed which state to produce" and "name the handle". Both this paper and a hypothetical peptide-driven method answer "yes", and both would list a molecular-sounding handle. The distinction that actually matters — operator-declared label vs. emergent from a co-input — has to be smuggled into prose. Suggest the field take a second value: `handle_holder: operator | co-input | sampler`.
- **Figure panel splitting worked cleanly** with the "split on mark or measure, not facet" rule; Fig 2a–e (five different predicates, one bar mark) collapsing to one row and Fig 4e splitting into a RENDER row and a MATRIX row were both unambiguous under it. The MATRIX form was needed and used twice (Fig 4e bottom, Fig 5i); those interaction-fingerprint strips would have been mangled by PLOT.
- **`metric_saturation` numeric-only is now clean**, but note that this paper's saturation was *diagnosed by the authors and worked around mid-figure* (bars for class A, boxes for class B, p4). A field that only asks "does it saturate" loses that the authors noticed. Recorded in prose.
- **`si_in_scope` earned its place here**: the 45-complex identities, every seed sequence, and all per-complex benchmark values are in an SI the corpus does not hold, while the main text prints almost no benchmark numbers at all. Without this field the note would have looked like a paper that reported nothing quantitative about its own benchmark.
