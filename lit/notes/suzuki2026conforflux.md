## B. Scope

- **system**: general protein + transporter, with membrane transporters carrying the paper's two headline results. Four ConforMix categories: domain motion (n=38), membrane transporter cycling (n=35 after augmentation), cryptic pocket formation (n=33), fold switching (n=15) — p4–p5, p19. Plus a separate 12-pair post-cutoff transporter arm (p5–p6, p20) and a single-target human dopamine transporter case study (p7, p23). No GPCRs, no protein kinases.
- **n_targets**: **121 (target, category) evaluation rows in the main benchmark; 131 unique proteins across everything (arithmetic, never printed).**
  - ConforMix: "The four sets total 101 (target, category) pairs over 98 unique proteins, with three proteins appearing in both domain motion and cryptic pockets and evaluated under both protocols" (p19).
  - Transporter category augmented with "20 within-cutoff pairs from Wu and Feng [2025] (after excluding 5 that overlap with the ConforMix set), bringing it to 35 targets" (p5). 38+35+33+15 = 121 rows in Table 1; Table 2's difficulty bins (49+35+37) corroborate 121.
  - Post-cutoff arm: 12 EGF transporter pairs (p5, p20). DAT case study: 1 target, "DAT is not in the post-cutoff transporter set" (p7).
  - Hyperparameter sweep set: 10 targets (p20).
  - 98 + 20 + 12 + 1 = 131 unique proteins is my arithmetic, not the paper's.
- **method_class**: **other** — inference-time latent-space intervention (trunk-level particle guidance: a pairwise Cα-RMSD repulsion gradient back-propagated onto Boltz-2's trunk single and pair embeddings). "ConforFlux is an inference-time procedure that leaves Boltz-2's weights and inputs unchanged." (p3). Nine baselines *actually run* span MSA-subsampling (CF-random), MSA column masking (AFsample3), MSA clustering (AF-cluster), embedding scaling (Boltz-sample), coordinate guidance (ConforMix-RMSD on Boltz-1 and Boltz-2), and a trained ensemble generator (BioEmu) — p5, p16–p17.
- **backbones**: **Boltz-2 v2.2.1** is the only model modified (p16). Boltz-1 v0.3.0 appears only as the substrate of the ConforMix-RMSD-Boltz-1 baseline, which the authors also port to Boltz-2 precisely so the comparison is backbone-matched (p16, p20). BioEmu is run as a separately trained ensemble generator on the same MSA (p17). AF2-backbone EGF numbers are **cited, not run**: "the original AF2-backbone Entropy Guided Fold (EGF) reported from their Table 3" (p5). `multi-backbone` NOT tagged — see `unresolved` item 3 for the judgement call.
- **templates**: **NOT REPORTED.** The word "template" never appears in the paper outside a reference title (grep over the full text). Table 5 "Base model inference parameters" (p16) lists diffusion steps, recycling, step scale, σ range, σ_data, ρ, γ, noise scale — no template row. The only related statement is "leaves Boltz-2's weights and inputs unchanged" (p3).
- **msa_handling**: **full, fixed, and shared across every arm.** "All methods generate 500 samples per target on the same MSA, computed with the ColabFold MMseqs2 server [Mirdita et al., 2022]." (p16). Subsampling / masking / clustering occur **only inside the MSA baselines** (CF-random depths {2,4,8,16,32,64,128}; AFsample3 mask rate 0.40; AF-cluster DBSCAN with ε chosen to maximise cluster count — Table 7, p17). No state-filtering anywhere: no MSA is ever substituted for a state-specific alignment. ConforFlux itself is explicitly *downstream* of the MSA — "The trunk output lies downstream of the input MSA and upstream of the diffusion module, so the intervention retains the full MSA-derived signal" (p8).
- **experimental-method conditioning**: Boltz-2 carries X-ray / MD conditioning at inference; the two default baselines bracket both, and "All other Boltz-2-based methods, including ConforFlux, use X-ray conditioning." (p5).

## C. Conformational core

- **states_generated**: **ensemble + two-state.** The method produces a 500-sample ensemble per target (M=5 particles × 5 σ values × 20 seeds — Table 6, p16), and every headline metric collapses that ensemble onto exactly **two** deposited reference states per target ("Each (target, reference) pair contributes a binary success indicator", p18; "Each target has two experimentally resolved reference conformations", p19). The DAT case study extends to **three** states (outward / occluded / inward), but only under a bandwidth set widened beyond the shipped configuration (p7). The dual form is required because the single-state half is real and load-bearing: under the production σ set the DAT ensemble collapses — "Re-running ConforFlux on DAT with the default σ ∈ {0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5} Å configuration (20 seeds, 5 particles, 500 samples) yields zero confidently-inward predictions" (p25). Note the collapse-onto-one-basin behaviour is also what the *default* Boltz-2 baseline does and what the paper exists to fix: "their default inference samples concentrate around a single dominant conformation" (p1).

- **structural_priors_used**: **Extensive, entirely at design and scoring time; none enters the model's input.**
  1. **Every benchmark target exists only because two deposited states exist.** "Each target has two experimentally resolved reference conformations corresponding to distinct functional states." (p19). Sources: BioEmu test split + OC23 (domain motion), IOMemP/TP16 (transporter), BioEmu cryptic-pocket set, Porter & Looger (fold switching) — p19; EGF transporter set of Wu & Feng — p20.
  2. **DAT was chosen because the full cycle had just been solved.** "Li et al. [2024] solved five cryo-EM structures of DAT (PDB 8Y2C, 8Y2D, 8Y2E, 8Y2F, 8Y2G) that together capture the outward-facing, occluded, and inward-facing states of the alternating-access cycle, all post-cutoff." (p7).
  3. **Curated residue annotations define the scored region for cryptic pockets.** "For cryptic pocket, RMSD is measured on the BioEmu pocket residues after superposition on the BioEmu alignment residues" (p18); Q16539 dropped "because the BioEmu assets carry no pocket annotation for it" (p18).
  4. **The DAT mechanism metrics and the superposition scaffold are lifted from the experimental paper.** Four Cα indicators of Li et al. 2024 (TM1a tilt, R85–D476, F76–G425, F76 displacement) — p23–p24; "Each prediction is superimposed onto 8Y2G on the scaffold Cα atoms (TM3, TM4, TM8, TM9; 93 residues from the 8Y2G secondary-structure annotation)" (p23).
  5. **Scoring thresholds are calibrated on deposited-pair geometry** (τ, FR eligibility, DAT margin) — see route 4 below, where the same fact is also a leakage route because the calibration set *is* the evaluation set.

- **oracle_leakage**: **PRESENT. Routes 4, 5, 6 and 7 all fire; routes 1, 2 and 3 are genuinely absent.** Protocol pages: p3–p4 (method), p5 (benchmark protocol), p16–p17 (hyperparameters and baselines), p18–p19 (evaluation), p19–p20 (datasets), p20–p22 (ablations), p23–p25 (DAT).

  **R1 — deposited structures used as input or template: NONE FOUND.** No structure is supplied to the model at any point. "ConforFlux is an inference-time procedure that leaves Boltz-2's weights and inputs unchanged." (p3); "parallel trajectories initialized from the same trunk outputs" with the trunk fed only sequence and MSA (p2, p3). Templates are never mentioned anywhere in the paper (see `templates`), so the route is absent by silence rather than by an explicit templates-off statement. Protocol page for checking: p3 (Section 3.1) and p16 (Table 5).

  **R2 — state annotations from a curated database (GPCRdb, KLIFS, Kincore) driving templates or alignments: NONE FOUND.** No such database appears. The only curated annotation used anywhere is the BioEmu cryptic-pocket residue list, and it drives the **scoring region only**, not the alignment or any input: "For cryptic pocket, RMSD is measured on the BioEmu pocket residues after superposition on the BioEmu alignment residues [Lewis et al., 2025]." (p18). Protocol pages: p18 (evaluation details), p19–p20 (datasets).

  **R3 — cluster labels derived from known states: NONE FOUND.** ConforFlux uses no clustering. The AF-cluster baseline clusters the MSA on a structure-free criterion: "DBSCAN clusters the MSA following the original implementation [Wayment-Steele et al., 2024]; the ε that maximizes the cluster count is selected" (Table 7, p17). The DAT PCA is fitted on prediction and reference vectors jointly (p24) but is used for visualisation, with classification done by nearest-reference RMSD (also R6, below).

  **R4 — hyperparameters, sweep ranges, seeds or stopping criteria tuned against known states: PRESENT, and this is the sharpest finding in the note. There is no held-out or tuning split anywhere in the paper** (grep for "held-out", "validation set", "tuning set", "development set" returns nothing but the DAT boundary-class usage on p24).

  *(a) The hyperparameter sweep set is drawn from the evaluation benchmark and, worse, is selected by the evaluation outcome itself.* Verbatim, p20: **"We sweep four hyperparameters around the defaults: step size η_s = η_z = 0.02, kernel bandwidth σ = 2.0, particle count M = 5, and update interval k = 3. The sweep uses ten domain-motion and transporter targets under 500 residues, drawn one per decile of the per-target default-vs-ConforFlux improvement."** The stratification variable — per-target default-vs-ConforFlux improvement — is computed from worst-case Cα RMSD to the held reference structures on the benchmark the paper then reports. So the tuning subset is not merely *inside* the evaluation set, it is *chosen using the evaluation result*.

  *(b) The values reported in that sweep are reference-RMSD values.* Table 10 (p22) scores every condition by "Worst-case alternate-reference Cα Kabsch RMSD" against the deposited alternate state, over the same 10 targets.

  *(c) The production σ range was fixed by that sweep.* Verbatim, p4: "The bandwidth σ is held constant per particle batch but swept across a small fixed set ({0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5} Å) so that the resulting samples span a range of repulsion radii rather than concentrating at a single σ." Justified at p20: "Performance is flat across σ ∈ [1, 2] and degrades sharply for σ ≥ 4 (kernel saturation)." This is the **schema's explicit route-4 case: a *range* tuned on the evaluation set, with no per-target value chosen.**

  *(d) The σ range was then widened for one target after the shipped range failed to reach a state the authors knew was there.* Verbatim, p7: **"For this case study we widen the repulsion bandwidth set to σ ∈ [0, 5] Å (Appendix L). The inward basin is reached only at the upper end of this set, beyond the benchmark σ values used in Tables 1 and 2."** Confirmed at p25: "Re-running ConforFlux on DAT with the default σ ∈ {0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5} Å configuration ... yields zero confidently-inward predictions and 90 boundary Inward/Occluded predictions." The DAT inward result — the paper's mechanistic centrepiece — exists only under a per-target hyperparameter change made with the target state in hand. Disclosed cleanly, but it is leakage.

  *(e) M=5 chosen on a compute/reference-RMSD trade-off, against a sweep that says M=7 is better.* Verbatim, p20–p21: "Success improves monotonically with particle count across M ∈ [3, 7], with diminishing returns; M =5 saves ∼30% compute relative to M =7 at a cost of 0.3 Å alt-RMSD." Table 10 (p22): M=7 gives 2.81 Å worst alt-RMSD vs M=5's 3.11 Å.

  *(f) The embedding target is chosen against the same 10-target sweep, and the chosen setting is worse than both alternatives on both metrics.* Verbatim, p21: "updating only the pair representation (z̄) achieves the lowest mean worst-case alt-RMSD (2.17 Å, best on 6 of 10 targets) at clashscore 11.3, below the combined update's 12.4. Updating only the single representation (s̄) yields the lowest mean clashscore (9.9) at slightly higher RMSD (2.74 Å). **We use the combined s̄ + z̄ update.**" Table 10 (p22) gives the combined update 3.11 Å and clashscore 12.4 — worse than z-only on RMSD and worse than s-only on clashscore. No justification for the choice is given.

  *(g) Stopping criterion: **HOW IT WAS CHOSEN IS NOT REPORTED.*** Table 6 (p16) lists "Stop fraction 0.8 — Active for the first 80% of denoising steps". It is not in the sensitivity sweep of Appendix I (which covers only η, σ, M, k — p20), is never justified in text, and no alternative value is reported anywhere.

  *(h) Metric thresholds calibrated on deposited-pair geometry from the evaluation set.* Verbatim, p18: "Domain motion uses τ = 2.0 Å on the full structure, **tightened from BioEmu's 3 Å for parity with the transporter scale (median inter-reference Cα RMSD 5.20 Å for domain motion vs 4.92 Å for transporter)**." The tightening rationale is a statistic of the held reference pairs. Fill-ratio eligibility likewise: "We therefore restrict FR to targets with T_ref < 0.95 across all categories" (p19), T_ref being the inter-reference TM-score. And the DAT classifier margin: "The margin is necessary because the RMSD between 8Y2D and the nearest inward reference is only 1.15 Å, comparable to Boltz-2's own prediction spread" (p24).

  *Seeds are clean:* "Seeds 20 — Random seeds (42–61)" (Table 6, p16), fixed and shared with the default baseline's 42–51 (Table 7, p17).

  **R5 — success defined post hoc by RMSD/TM to a structure they had: PRESENT, and it is every headline number.** Verbatim, p18: "Each (target, reference) pair contributes a binary success indicator 1{min_i RMSD(s_i, r) ≤ τ} over the 500 samples." Verbatim, p19: "RMSD_worst({s_i}) = max( min_i RMSD(s_i, r_1), min_i RMSD(s_i, r_2) ), which indicates whether the sampling distribution reaches both experimentally resolved states or only the dominant one." Fill ratio is TM-score to the two references (p19). The DAT inward count is nearest-reference classification (p24). Every reported success is a best-of-500 max taken against a deposited structure the authors held. The authors state the consequence themselves — see `stated_limits`, p8.

  **R6 — best/worst model labels assigned against a held reference: PRESENT, and it drives every structure render.** Verbatim, Fig. 1 caption p3: "ConforFlux reaches both references, with **the closest sample to each shown**, mutually aligned and colored by per-residue Cα RMSD." Verbatim, Fig. 2 caption p7: "middle **the best ConforFlux prediction** overlaid on each reference". Verbatim, Fig. 3 caption p8: "bottom **the best ConforFlux prediction** overlaid on each experimental reference." Verbatim, Fig. 4 caption p9: "(B) For each major state, **the best ConforFlux prediction** overlaid on the experimental reference." Also the DAT state labels: "Each prediction is assigned to a state by nearest reference in global Cα RMSD" (p24). The authors flag the limit of this labelling: "The state classifier reflects relative reference proximity, not basin occupancy." (p24).

  **R7 — design-level oracle use (input conditions or systems chosen because the expected answer is known): PRESENT. Weaker than the pipeline leakage above; label it as design-level.** Every target enters the study only because both states are deposited (see `structural_priors_used` 1). DAT was selected because its full cycle had just been solved (p7). The framing is explicit at p1: "Even when a target's alternate state is well-characterized in the literature, sampling at default settings rarely reaches it." Nothing leaky is fed to the model — the expected state is simply known before the result is read.

- **prospective**: **no.** No arm is prospective. Every target is chosen because both states are deposited (R7); every headline metric is a best-of-500 against those deposited structures (R5); the hyperparameter set was fixed against reference-RMSD on ten targets drawn from the evaluation benchmark by outcome decile (R4a); the σ range was widened for DAT after the shipped range failed to reach the known inward state (R4d). The **post-cutoff arm (12 pairs) is the closest thing to a prospective test and it is genuinely blind at the entry level**, but the authors themselves qualify it: "Homologous transporters in other conformational states may appear in pre-cutoff training data, so this evaluation tests generalization to unseen sequences and entries." (p6). Even there, success is scored against the post-cutoff structures the authors hold, so it is a memorization control rather than a prospective prediction. No structure is predicted before its alternate state was known.

- **state_metric**: **binary predicate + continuous coordinate, both anchored on distance to a deposited reference.**
  - *Binary predicate — per-state success rate*, at **category-specific thresholds** (p18): domain motion τ = 2.0 Å full structure (tightened from BioEmu's 3 Å, justified by median inter-reference Cα RMSD 5.20 vs 4.92 Å); membrane transporter τ = 2.0 Å full structure (the EGF benchmark threshold of Wu & Feng); cryptic pocket τ = 1.0 Å **on the BioEmu pocket region** (tightened from BioEmu's 1.5 Å "since the metric evaluates only the curated 10–30 pocket residues"); fold switching τ = 3.0 Å full structure (following Wayment-Steele et al.). Post-cutoff transporter arm uses τ = 2.0 Å throughout: "The 2.0 Å success threshold of the original benchmark is applied throughout." (p6). **All four thresholds are justified — an improvement on the usual practice** — but three of the four justifications are inherited from source benchmarks and the fourth is calibrated on the evaluation set's own reference geometry (R4h).
  - *Continuous — worst-case minimum Cα Kabsch RMSD* over 500 samples (Eq. 3, p19).
  - *Continuous — fill ratio* (Kalakoti & Wallner), TM-score based, 100 bins along the inter-reference segment, restricted to T_ref < 0.95 (p19).
  - *Continuous — DAT mechanism metrics*: R85–D476 Cα distance, F76–G425 Cα distance, F76 Cα displacement, TM1a tilt (p23–p24). These are the one place the state is called by something other than reference distance, and they are the reason `rmsd-only` is **not** tagged.
  - *Classification* — DAT nearest-reference assignment at a 0.3 Å margin, with a boundary class held out (p24).
  - Statistical treatment: "Succ. is mean ± std over 1000 state-level bootstrap trials" (Table 1 caption, p5); "We resample (target, state) pairs with replacement to obtain 1000 bootstrap replicates" (p19). Post-cutoff arm uses a one-sided Wilcoxon with Bonferroni correction (p6).

- **metric_saturation**: **Yes — three numeric saturations, two of them stated by the authors.** (Axis truncations are recorded in `hides` on the Fig. 5 and Fig. 7 rows, not here.)
  1. **The per-state success indicator is explicitly saturated at 500 samples.** Verbatim, p18: "The within-state Monte Carlo variance is essentially saturated at this sample count, so the only meaningful uncertainty is across (target, state) pairs, motivating this deterministic formulation rather than success@N."
  2. **Fill ratio degenerates as T_ref → 1, and targets are excluded to avoid it.** Verbatim, p19: "The metric collapses when the inter-reference TM-score T_ref approaches one, because the inter-reference quadrant shrinks to a narrow diagonal band into which almost any plausible fold projects. We therefore restrict FR to targets with T_ref < 0.95 across all categories." 5 of 33 cryptic-pocket targets and 2 of 15 fold-switching targets are dropped on this rule (p19), so the FR column of Table 1 is computed on n = 38 / 35 / 28 / 13 while the Succ. column is on 38 / 35 / 33 / 15.
  3. **The easy difficulty stratum is at ceiling.** Table 2 (p6): default (X-ray) 86.7, default (MD) 86.7, CF-random 90.8, ConforFlux 90.8 — the top two methods are tied at the same value and the whole column spans 75.5–90.8. The paper's own summary concedes it: "ConforFlux exceeds default by 4.1 pp on easy targets" versus +13.5 pp on hard (p5).
  4. **Granularity, not saturation, but it matters for reading the deltas:** fold switching is n=15 targets → 30 (target, state) pairs → one pair moves the rate 3.3 pp, and the paper's own fold-switching gap is exactly 3.3 pp ("ranks second on fold switching by 3.3 pp", p5), i.e. one (target, state) pair. On the 12-pair post-cutoff arm one target is 8.3 pp.
  - Separately, the *guidance kernel* saturates at large σ ("degrades sharply for σ ≥ 4 (kernel saturation)", p20), which is a method property, not a metric one.

- **directional_control**: **NONE. The method offers diversity only — it cannot be told which state to produce. The authors say so outright.**
  **Verbatim, p8:** *"ConforFlux therefore does not prescribe a direction of conformational change. Its repulsion only drives particles apart, and the alternative states come from the trunk itself, leaving the exploration to the model."*
  **Verbatim, p9 (Conclusion):** *"Because the trunk has been reported to encode more than one conformation, the repulsion only has to drive particles apart, and the model supplies the states themselves."*
  Handles that exist: kernel bandwidth σ (a repulsion **radius**, not a direction), particle count M, update interval k, step size η, random seed (Table 6, p16). No partner, no ligand, no nanobody, no peptide, no state-annotated template, no state-filtered MSA, no subsample depth. The DAT result makes the distinction concrete: widening σ from {0.5–2.5} to [0, 5] Å was what let samples reach the inward basin (p7, p25) — i.e. the only lever is *how far apart* to push, not *where to go*, and finding the right distance for a target required already knowing the answer.

- **anti_memorization_design**: **PRESENT — 12 post-cutoff transporter pairs, plus a post-cutoff single-target case study.**
  - *Cutoff definition*: Boltz-2 training cutoff **2023-06-01**, defined as initial PDB release date. Verbatim, p20: "Of the 32 retained, 20 have both references released before the Boltz-2 training cutoff (2023-06-01) [Passaro et al., 2025] and 12 have at least one post-cutoff reference (Table 9)." Restated at p25: "the Boltz-2 cutoff (initial PDB release before 2023-06-01, Passaro et al., 2025)"; the Boltz-1 cutoff (2021-09-30) is given too.
  - *n and construction*: the EGF set of Wu & Feng has 37 pairs; "We use 32 of the original 37 pairs, excluding slc39, mdfA, AAC3, PF0708, and murJ for overlap with ConforMix transporters." (p20). 20 within-cutoff → folded into the Table 1 Transporter category; **12 post-cutoff → the held-out arm**, listed per target with PDB IDs, lengths and T_ref in Table 9 (p20).
  - *Definition scope, stated honestly*: "Here 'post-cutoff' refers to the release date of the specific deposited entries. Homologous transporters in other conformational states may appear in pre-cutoff training data, so this evaluation tests generalization to unseen sequences and entries." (p6).
  - *DAT*: "All five entries were deposited 2024-01-25 and released 2024-08-14, postdating the Boltz-2 training cutoff (2023-06-01)" (p23).
  - *Within-/post-cutoff split is clean and audited*: "7PQG/7VAG, with both references released in May 2022, is one of the 20 within-cutoff pairs." (p20).

- **anti_memorization_control**: **RUN — a real arm, analysed, with statistics. This is the strongest rigour feature of the paper.** Not `NONE RUN`.
  - Section 4.2 (p5–p6) runs four configurations on the same 12 post-cutoff pairs. "The three Boltz-2 columns share backbone, MSA, and sample count, so the only varying factor among them is the guidance method." (p6). Aggregate in Table 4 (p7), per-target in Table 9 (p20), per-target scatters in Fig. 14 (p29).
  - Significance tested: "one-sided Wilcoxon p = 0.0017 vs default, p = 0.0007 vs EGF on Boltz-2, both Bonferroni-significant" (p6).
  - A **second, homolog-level** memorization control is run on DAT and reported as a *negative* for the authors' own claim (Appendix L.6, p25): "Inward conformations of SLC6 transporters predate both Boltz training cutoffs. The human serotonin transporter inward conformation (PDB 6DZZ, released 2019-04-24) and the bacterial LeuT inward-open conformation (PDB 3TT3, released 2012-01-04) are both before the Boltz-1 cutoff ... and the Boltz-2 cutoff ... The DAT-specific cryo-EM entries 8Y2C/E/F are post-cutoff at the entry level, but the inward conformation type itself is not novel to either model."
  - **NOT marked `UNPOWERED`** by the schema's rule: n = 12 ≥ ~10, and the set does not overlap training at the entry level. But it is marginal — one target moves the success rate 8.3 pp, so 4/12 → 9/12 is five targets, and the authors' own homolog caveat (p6, p25) means the set is post-cutoff at the *entry* level only, not at the *conformation-type* level.

- **confidence_as_discriminator**: **No — and they tested it and report the negative.** Confidence is never used to select or rank conformations; ConforFlux's 500 samples are reported unfiltered, and ConforMix's confidence filter is explicitly removed for parity (p17, p21). Appendix C (p17) runs the comparison as a quality check: "Per-category medians differ by at most 0.01 and upper 90th-percentiles by at most 0.005. The diversified samples thus remain among the conformations Boltz-2 scores as confidently as its default outputs. At the same time, confidence barely registers the conformational variation ConforFlux introduces". Stated as a limit at p8: "Predictor confidence does not discriminate correct alternative conformations from incorrect ones [Chakravarty et al., 2024], and Boltz-2 confidence barely registers the variation ConforFlux introduces (Appendix C)." Not tagged `confidence-as-discriminator` — see `unresolved` item 4.

## D. Claims

- **central_conclusion**: Applying a pairwise Cα-RMSD repulsion gradient to Boltz-2's trunk single and pair embeddings, coupling M=5 parallel diffusion trajectories, broadens conformational coverage at inference time with no trained or per-protein parameters and without touching the diffusion module — improving per-state success over default Boltz-2 by 3.3–17.1 pp across four conformational-change categories, raising 2 Å success on 12 post-cutoff transporter pairs from 4/12 to 9/12, and reaching all three states of the DAT alternating-access cycle (under a widened bandwidth set) while keeping clashscore inside the range spanned by the two default baselines. The method supplies only separation; the states themselves come from what the trunk already encodes, so it cannot be pointed at a chosen state, and fold switching plateaus near 60% across every inference-time method tested.

- **necessity_claims** (verbatim + page):
  - p8: "Without such references, identifying which generated conformations are genuine has no reliable systematic method, and ConforFlux is no exception [Cui et al., 2025]."
  - p8: "Predictor confidence does not discriminate correct alternative conformations from incorrect ones [Chakravarty et al., 2024], and Boltz-2 confidence barely registers the variation ConforFlux introduces (Appendix C)."
  - p8: "ConforFlux therefore does not prescribe a direction of conformational change."
  - p8: "ConforFlux ensures only that local physical and geometric quality is not sacrificed relative to default sampling, so a geometrically clean sample can still be a hallucinated state rather than a real one."
  - p9: "Closing this gap likely requires changes beyond retraining on conformational splits [Bryant and Noé, 2024]."
  - p9: "The principal limitation is fold switching, where success plateaus across every inference-time method we tested, a ceiling that tracks the trained predictor rather than the guidance procedure (Section 5)."
  - p19: "The metric collapses when the inter-reference TM-score T_ref approaches one, because the inter-reference quadrant shrinks to a narrow diagonal band into which almost any plausible fold projects."
  - p21: "Both factors are needed under this sweep (Table 10)."
  - p21: "With n=10 targets and across-target standard error ∼0.6 Å, we cannot resolve non-additivity between the two factors."
  - p24: "The margin is necessary because the RMSD between 8Y2D and the nearest inward reference is only 1.15 Å, comparable to Boltz-2's own prediction spread, so a fraction of predictions genuinely sit near the occluded–inward boundary."
  - p24: "The state classifier reflects relative reference proximity, not basin occupancy."

- **novelty_claims** (verbatim + page): **The paper makes no priority claim.** A grep of the full text for "first", "novel", "unprecedented", "to our knowledge" returns only "the first 80% of denoising steps" (Table 6, p16) and "the inward conformation type itself is not novel to either model" (p25) — neither is a novelty claim. What it claims instead is *distinctness*, always relative and always cited:
  - p1: "Unlike prior methods that act on the trunk representation, this coupling uses no trained or per-protein parameters."
  - p2: "The procedure operates on the trunk's conditioning representation, complementary to prior particle-guidance work on the diffusion variable, and introduces no trained parameters."
  - p2: "ConforFlux back-propagates a related pairwise repulsion one level upstream, to the trunk's conditioning representation, and decodes through the unaltered diffusion module (Section 5)."
  - p2: "ConforFlux uses no trained or per-protein-optimized parameters, operates post-Pairformer, and couples multiple particles through a shared pairwise-repulsion gradient."
  - p3: "This makes the trunk a viable site for inference-time guidance, distinct from the diffusion-variable-level interventions of prior particle-guidance work [Corso et al., 2024, Jing et al., 2022]."
  - p6 (a result, not a method claim): "ConforFlux newly succeeds on five targets (VMAT2, OCT1, NaS1, ABCG25, CHT1) and is the only method to succeed on ABCG25."
  - Concurrency is flagged in the bibliography rather than argued away: ConforNets is annotated "Concurrent work; posted 2026-04-20" (p11).

- **stated_limits**:
  1. Fold switching plateau: "Fold switching saturates near 60% across our inference-time baselines on the Boltz-2 backbone. MSA, embedding, coordinate, and trained-ensemble interventions converge to the same ceiling, which localises the limit to the trained predictor." (p8–p9); "Our methods primarily surface conformations already encoded in the predictor's representations" (p9).
  2. No direction of conformational change can be prescribed (p8) — see `directional_control`.
  3. No way to tell a real state from a hallucinated one without references; confidence does not help; geometric cleanliness is not evidence of reality (p8).
  4. Cost: "The procedure also increases memory use and runtime over default sampling (Appendix H)." (p9); 2.2× diffusion time, 5.6× peak memory, two targets >800 residues OOM without gradient checkpointing (p20).
  5. Post-cutoff generalisation is at the entry level only: "Homologous transporters in other conformational states may appear in pre-cutoff training data" (p6); "the inward conformation type itself is not novel to either model" (p25).
  6. The DAT inward result depends on a widened σ range not used in the benchmark (p7, p25).
  7. TM1a tilt disagrees with experiment (38 ± 10° vs ≈25°), attributed to first-principal-axis amplification of helix bend (p8, p25).
  8. Ablation set is small: "With n=10 targets and across-target standard error ∼0.6 Å, we cannot resolve non-additivity between the two factors." (p21).
  9. Fill ratio collapses at high T_ref, forcing target exclusions (p19).
  10. State classification is relative, not basin occupancy; 122 boundary samples are partial transitions excluded from the headline 35 (p24).
  11. ConforMix baselines are run without their post-hoc pLDDT-and-clash filter, which the authors disclose inflates those baselines' clashscore (p17, p21).

- **stance**: **`precedent` on findings + `contrast` on rigour. PROVISIONAL — the user's call.**
  - *precedent*: the closest published instance of an inference-time intervention on an internal Boltz-2 tensor with no trained parameters; supplies a nine-baseline matched comparison, a genuine post-cutoff control arm with statistics, and per-target numbers (Table 9) our results can sit beside.
  - *contrast*: the hyperparameter set is fixed against reference-RMSD on ten targets drawn from the evaluation benchmark and stratified by the evaluation outcome itself, with no held-out split anywhere (R4a); the σ range is widened per-target for the case study after the shipped range fails to reach a known state (R4d); every headline number is best-of-500 against a held reference (R5); the method has no directional handle at all (p8).
  - See `unresolved` item 5: `threat` is arguable given how directly this occupies the adjacent ground, and is deliberately not asserted here.
