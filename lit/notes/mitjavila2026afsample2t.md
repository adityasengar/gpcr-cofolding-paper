## B. Scope

- **system**: **GPCR**, restricted to **class A** GPCRs. "We focused on class A GPCRs, which share a conserved tertiary structure composed of seven transmembrane (TM) helices connected by extracellular and intracellular loops." (p.2)
- **n_targets**: **10 class A GPCRs.** Named in Table 1 (p.6): 5-HT1A, A2A, D1, D2, H1, M1, M4, MT1, μ-opioid, TAAR1. Supporting structural material: 119 experimentally determined crystal/cryo-EM structures of these 10 receptors, "the number of structures for each protein ranged from 4 to 35 (Table S2)" (p.3); after filtering for resolution, duplicate ligand−receptor complexes and binding-site mutations, "61 structures were retained for benchmarking of binding-site accuracy" (p.3). All 119 were used for docking (p.5). **Generality is claimed well beyond the 10**: "the AFsample2T approach can readily be applied to other protein classes" (p.1); "AFsample2T could readily be applied to other drug target classes, such as kinases" (p.7). No non-GPCR target is run.
- **method_class**: **other — targeted MSA column masking** (a spatially localised variant of MSA masking), combined with a **retrospective virtual-screening benchmark**. It is *not* MSA depth subsampling and *not* state-filtering: masking hides individual MSA *columns* (residue positions) within a defined sequence window while retaining full alignment depth. "Column masking selectively hides positions in the MSA during model inference, thereby reducing coevolutionary signals at those positions and allowing AF2 to explore alternative backbone and side-chain arrangements." (p.2). Inference-stage **dropout** is enabled alongside masking (p.2). Built on AFsample2 (ref 33, Kalakoti & Wallner) — the contribution is the *targeting*, not the masking operation itself.
- **backbones**: **AF2 only.** "Protein structure prediction from sequence was performed using AF2 version 2.3.1" (p.8), with **AF2-Multimer** used for the active-state (receptor + Gα/Gβ/Gγ) predictions (p.8). No AF3, Boltz, Chai, Protenix or OF3 arm is run — cofolding methods are discussed only as future work ("evaluation of cofolding methods that directly model receptor−ligand complexes", p.8). **Not** `multi-backbone`.
- **templates**: **off.** "All AF2-based models were generated using the no-template option." (p.8). Templates are named only as a future direction: "Further development of this GPCR modeling workflow will be focused on the use of templates in AF2 to improve the description of the different receptor states" (p.8).
- **msa_handling**: **Full-depth MSA, column-masked in a targeted window.** "MSAs were generated using the default AF2 protocol and were modified only if column masking was applied using AFsample2." (p.8). Masking probabilities 0, 10, 20, 30% (and 50% in the sweep) applied **only within the defined binding-site window**; the comparison arm AFsample2 applies 15% masking to the **full** MSA. Depth is never reduced; sequences are never filtered by state. **Do not record this as `subsampled` or as `state-filtered` — it is neither.**

## C. Conformational core

- **states_generated**: **two + ensemble.** Two dimensions, produced by two different mechanisms:
  - The two *global* GPCR states are produced explicitly and separately, by a partner toggle: "The inactive receptor state was predicted using only the receptor sequence. The active state was modeled by using the sequences of the receptor and heterotrimeric G protein, which binds to an intracellular pocket and leads to large conformational changes in TM6 of the receptor." (p.2). "For each masking probability, 250 structures were generated, with equal numbers of active and inactive receptors." (p.8)
  - Within each state, an *ensemble* of binding-site conformations is produced by masking: 1,000 AFsample2T models per receptor (250 each at 0/10/20/30%) plus 1,000 default-AF2 models, "resulting in a set of 2,000 models for each receptor" (p.8).
  The masking itself generates **local side-chain and backbone heterogeneity in the pocket**, not alternative global states: "As our focus was to increase sampling in the orthosteric binding site rather than exploring large conformational changes in the receptor, we used a targeted sampling approach." (p.2)

- **structural_priors_used**: **Extensive, class-level and target-level, at design time.** This is a docking paper built on the deposited GPCR structural record; none of the following is by itself a pipeline leak, and it belongs here rather than in `oracle_leakage`:
  1. **The masking region was chosen from the deposited GPCR structural record.** "Based on determined GPCR structures, the MSA masking region was defined as the extracellular TM region and the part of extracellular loop 2 (EL2) that interacts with orthosteric ligands in many receptors (Figure 1B)." (p.2)
  2. **GPCRdb generic residue numbering and conserved class-A microswitch landmarks** are used to place the window transferably: the PIF motif (P5x50, I3x40, F6x44) and the conserved EL2 cysteine (p.8, quoted in full under `oracle_leakage` route 1).
  3. **The knowledge that class A GPCRs share a conserved fold and a conserved orthosteric-pocket location** is the stated reason the method works here: "Our approach is particularly well suited to GPCRs due to the conserved fold and binding-site location." (p.7)
  4. **The activation mechanism** (G protein binding → TM6 outward swing) is taken from the structural literature and used to construct the active-state input (p.2, ref 35).
  5. **119 deposited structures of the 10 targets** define both the accuracy reference and the docking comparison set (pp.3, 5).
  6. **Apo docking sites are defined by transferring ligand coordinates from a deposited holo structure of the same receptor** (p.8; see route 1).

- **oracle_leakage**: **PRESENT, by routes 1 (evaluation/docking-site definition), 4 (operating point and docking grids tuned on the evaluation set), 5, and 7.** The *masking window itself* is the interesting case and is **cleaner than it first looks** — it is defined by sequence-transferable landmarks, not by a deposited structure of the specific target. Each route separately:

  **Route 1 — deposited structures used as input or template.**
  *Into AF2: NONE FOUND.* Templates are off and no coordinates are supplied to the predictor: "All AF2-based models were generated using the no-template option." (p.8); the only inputs are the receptor sequence, and for active models the Gα/Gβ/Gγ sequences (p.8).
  *Into the masking-window selection: NOT target-specific.* The window is defined by conserved sequence/fold landmarks, addressable through GPCRdb generic numbering, so it transfers to a receptor whose structure is unknown. **Verbatim selection procedure, p.8:**
  > "To enhance structural diversity within a GPCR binding site, we used AFsample2T, which introduces targeted MSA column masking specifically applied to residues in the extracellular TM region. We selected residues in the TM region extending up to one helical turn below the PIF motif, a conserved microswitch involved in receptor activation35 (P5x50, I3x40 and F6x44; superscripts indicate GPCRdb numbering69), as well as residues in the EL2 between the conserved cysteine and TM5.33 The specific residues defining these ranges are listed in Table S9 for each of the 10 GPCRs. Within these defined ranges, columns in the MSA were randomly selected for masking. Masking probabilities of 0, 10, 20, and 30% were applied to promote the generation of more structurally diverse binding-site models." (p.8)

  and the design-level statement, p.2:
  > "Based on determined GPCR structures, the MSA masking region was defined as the extracellular TM region and the part of extracellular loop 2 (EL2) that interacts with orthosteric ligands in many receptors (Figure 1B)." (p.2)

  **Reading:** "Based on determined GPCR structures" refers to the *class* record, not to a deposited structure of each target. The residue ranges are anchored on P5x50 / I3x40 / F6x44 and the conserved EL2 cysteine — all identifiable from sequence via the GPCRdb alignment — so **a known structure of the specific target is NOT required to place the mask.** The per-target residue lists are in Table S9 (SI, not held), so this could not be verified against the actual lists.
  *Into the docking/evaluation pipeline: PRESENT and target-specific.* The binding site used for RMSD scoring, for pocket-volume calculation and for docking-grid placement is read off a deposited structure of the same receptor:
  > "For each receptor, the binding site was defined as residues within 5 Å of the ligands bound in the determined structures." (p.8)
  > "the symmetry-aware side-chain RMSD values were computed for residues within 5 Å of the ligands" (p.3)
  > "The apo structures used were: 5HT1A (PDB accession code: 7E2X), H1 (PDB accession code: 8X5X), and M4 (PDB accession code: 6KP6). In these cases, the binding site was defined by aligning each apo structure to the highest-resolution holo structure of the receptor, followed by transferring the ligand coordinates and removing ligand atoms within 2.5 Å of the receptor. **For AF2-based models, the same alignment and transfer of ligand atoms procedure was applied.**" (p.8)
  The last sentence is the sharpest instance: **every AF2 model is docked into a site whose coordinates were transferred from a deposited holo structure of that same receptor.** The authors state this limitation themselves (see route 7 / `stated_limits`).
  > "Binding-site volumes were calculated with Schrödinger's SiteMap tool using a reference residue in the orthosteric pocket to define the binding region (Table S3)." (p.8)

  **Route 2 — state annotations from a curated database driving templates or alignments.**
  **NONE FOUND as state annotation.** GPCRdb is used, but only for its **generic residue-numbering scheme** (ref 69, "Generic GPCR Residue Numbers"), to place the mask window — not for active/inactive state labels, and not to select or filter sequences. "(P5x50, I3x40 and F6x44; superscripts indicate GPCRdb numbering69)" (p.8). No KLIFS, Kincore, or state-annotated sequence set appears anywhere. Protocol described pp.2 and 8.

  **Route 3 — cluster labels derived from known states.**
  **NONE FOUND.** No clustering of any kind is performed on the MSA or on the models; masking columns are chosen at random within the window ("columns in the MSA were randomly selected for masking", p.8). Model grouping is by masking level and by G-protein presence only (p.8). Protocol described p.8.

  **Route 4 — hyperparameters, sweep ranges, seeds or stopping criteria tuned against known states.** **PRESENT, and it is the load-bearing leak.** Two distinct instances:

  *(4a) The masking-percentage sweep and the chosen operating point were both evaluated on, and selected from, the same 10-target evaluation set, scored against those targets' deposited structures.*
  > "we generated AF2 models without masking (the default) and with targeted masking probabilities of 10%, 20%, 30%, or 50% applied to residues forming the orthosteric binding site (Figure 1B). For comparison, we also generated models using the standard AFsample2 technique, which applies column masking (15%) to the full MSA. ... **For each masking level, we evaluated the structural accuracy of models for a benchmarking set of 10 class A GPCRs.**" (p.2)
  > "**To further optimize prediction accuracy, we combined sets of models generated at different masking levels, which we refer to as the AFsample2T ensemble. A combination of 250 models each from masking levels of 0%, 10%, 20%, and 30% resulted in the highest structural accuracy of the evaluated ensembles (AUC = 0.63, Figure 2A) and maintained high model confidence (median pLDDT = 84.4).**" (p.4)
  > "These results indicated that moderate MSA masking (10−30%) enhances binding-site structural accuracy without substantially compromising model confidence, whereas extensive (50%) or global masking (AFsample2) leads to large reductions of model accuracy (Figure 2A-C)." (p.4)

  The operating point was therefore chosen by **binding-site RMSD accuracy against the deposited structures of the evaluation targets**, with median pLDDT as a secondary guardrail — **not** by screening enrichment, and **not** on a separate development set. Under v3 this is leakage even though the *range* 0–30% rather than a single per-target value is what was fixed. There is no held-out set on which 0–30% was confirmed.

  *(4b) Docking grids and partial charges were optimised per receptor against that receptor's known ligands and known binding modes.*
  > "For the AF2-derived models, grid parameters and sphere sets were optimized using a subset of 100 models per receptor, and the resulting parameters were then applied to all AF2-based structures. The same docking parameters were optimized individually for all the experimentally determined structures. The receptor grids were optimized through a grid search in which the radii of the thin spheres were systematically varied, starting from 0.1 Å and then from 0.25 Å to 2.0 Å in 0.25 Å increments." (p.8)
  > "**The thin spheres were optimized to maximize both ligand enrichment and accurate binding modes of known ligands.**" (pp.8–9)
  > "Partial charge distributions were adjusted to increase the polarity of specific residues, thereby enhancing electrostatic interactions with ligands. Modified partial charges were applied to two receptors: N6x55 in the A2A adenosine receptor, and S5x43 and N6x55 in the D1 dopamine receptor." (p.9)
  This tunes the *scoring* apparatus, per target, on the same actives/decoys used to report enrichment. It is standard DOCK3.8 practice and is applied even-handedly to models and to experimental structures, but it is tuning on the evaluation set.

  **Route 5 — success defined post hoc by RMSD/TM to a structure they already held.** **PRESENT, and it is the definition of the primary structural metric.**
  > "A set of 1,000 models per receptor was generated and accuracy was assessed by calculating the fraction of experimental binding-site structures reproduced within root-mean-square deviation (RMSD) thresholds ranging from 1 to 2 Å (RMSD ≤ threshold). For each masking level, the 1,000 models were aligned to the corresponding experimental structures, and the symmetry-aware side-chain RMSD values were computed for residues within 5 Å of the ligands. The accuracy of the different masking levels was then analyzed over a range of different RMSD thresholds and quantified by calculating the area under the curve (AUC) (Figure 2A)." (p.3)
  The AUC that selects the operating point (route 4a) *is* this post-hoc RMSD score. RMSF agreement with the same experimental set is scored the same way (p.4).

  **Route 6 — best/worst model labels assigned against a held structural reference.** **NONE FOUND for a structural reference; a ligand-oracle equivalent is present and is explicit.** The models reported as the headline result are selected by **ligand enrichment**, not by RMSD to a reference:
  > "In the third step, all binding-site models are ranked by ligand enrichment and the top-performing 1% are selected for further analysis." (p.8)
  > "The maximum ligand enrichment reflects performance when known ligands can guide structure selection through enrichment calculations. The median enrichment reflects the expected performance if an AF2-based or experimental structure is selected at random, a scenario relevant when there are no available ligands." (p.5)
  The authors report both arms and are explicit that the selected arm requires known ligands (see `stated_limits`). So: no structural-reference selection, but the top-1% number is a **best-of-1,000 selected on the same actives/decoys used to report it**, with no ligand-side hold-out.

  **Route 7 — design-level oracle use (input conditions or systems chosen because the answer is known).** **PRESENT; weaker than the pipeline leakage above and labelled as design-level.**
  - Targets were chosen for structure and ligand availability: "A total of 119 experimentally determined crystal or cryo-electron microscopy (cryo-EM) structures of these receptors were available, and the number of structures for each protein ranged from 4 to 35 (Table S2)." (p.3)
  - The active/inactive label is assigned to the *input condition* a priori, not read off the result: "The inactive receptor state was predicted using only the receptor sequence. The active state was modeled by using the sequences of the receptor and heterotrimeric G protein" (p.2); "If the experimental structure represented an active state, the G protein was also included in the models." (p.8) — i.e. the state of the model is chosen to match the state of the reference before comparison.
  - Training overlap is acknowledged, not controlled: "it should also be noted that structures of 4 out of the 10 GPCRs in our benchmarking set were used in the training of AF2, and our approach may therefore not perform equally well for receptors of unknown structure." (p.8)

- **prospective**: **no.** Entirely retrospective. Every enrichment number is computed against ChEMBL actives and property-matched decoys for receptors whose structures and ligands were already in hand, and the operating point was fixed on the same 10 targets. The authors say so: "Prospective studies will be required to further evaluate AFsample2T models, and we suggest the following protocol for virtual screening applications." (p.8). Figure 6 (p.7) is a *proposed* prospective workflow, not one that was executed here.

- **state_metric**: **Dual, and neither half is a conformational-state call.** Recorded honestly rather than forced into a state reading:
  - **RMSD-to-reference, thresholded** — symmetry-aware side-chain (and backbone) RMSD of binding-site residues (those within 5 Å of the bound ligand) to the corresponding deposited structure, swept over **thresholds 1.0–2.0 Å** and integrated to an AUC (p.3). Thresholds are *not* justified against any structural or functional criterion; the range is simply asserted ("RMSD thresholds ranging from 1 to 2 Å", p.3) and 1.5 Å is used as the headline reporting point (p.4) with **no stated justification**. Companion continuous measure: per-residue RMSF, compared to the experimental spread (p.4), and binding-site volume from SiteMap (p.8).
  - **Continuous ligand-ranking coordinate — this is the primary evaluation.** aLogAUC and EF1% from docking ROC curves. "Ligand enrichment over decoys was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves. For each receptor structure, the logarithm of the area under the curve (LogAUC) and the ROC-based enrichment factor at 1% (EF1%) were calculated. ... The adjusted LogAUC (aLogAUC) is obtained by subtracting the area corresponding to random enrichment, and positive values indicate better-than-random discrimination between ligands and decoys." (p.5)
  **No binary active/inactive predicate is ever evaluated in the main text.** The only conformational-state check is a TM6 comparison for four receptors, reported entirely in SI: "comparison of the AF2 models with experimentally determined structures of active and inactive states of four receptors confirmed that this approach reliably captures the characteristic conformations of TM6 (Table S1)." (p.2) — **Table S1 is not held.**

- **metric_saturation**: **Yes, numerically, in Figure 2A (p.3).** The fraction-of-captured-binding-sites curve is bounded on [0, 1] and, over the swept threshold range, **ceilings at 1.00 at the loose end**: the 0%, 10%, 20% and AFsample2T (0−30%) curves all reach ~1.0 at the 2.0 Å threshold and are mutually indistinguishable there, while at the tight end (1.0 Å) every arm including the default falls to ~0.0. The AUC that selects the operating point therefore integrates a curve that is saturated at both ends of the swept window, compressing the discriminating range to roughly 1.2–1.8 Å. The AUC values themselves (0.38–0.63) do not saturate. The pocket-volume and RMSF measures do not saturate. aLogAUC has a hard floor at 0 for random discrimination (p.5), and the experimental arm in Table 1 goes **negative** for H1 (−0.6), M4 (−2.5) and MT1 (−1.0) (p.6) — i.e. worse than random, so no floor is hit in practice. *(Fig 2A's reversed and truncated x-axis is a figure defect and is recorded in `hides` on the Fig 2A row, not here.)*

- **directional_control**: **Split — the method can be pointed at a REGION but not at a STATE.**
  - *Site directionality: yes, and it is the paper's contribution.* The mask window is a user-chosen sequence range, and the authors state it is relocatable: "The AFsample2T strategy is flexible, and masking can also be directed toward other regions, such as allosteric sites of GPCRs." (p.7). Handle: **the residue range supplied to the column masker** (p.8).
  - *State directionality: NOT from the masking.* Column masking only broadens local sampling; it carries no state instruction. Global state is set by a completely separate handle — **presence or absence of the heterotrimeric G protein sequences in the AF2-Multimer input**: "In our approach, the two major conformations of GPCRs are instead modeled explicitly by including the G protein to predict the active state, which has been demonstrated to be an accurate and reliable strategy." (p.7); "Active models were generated by including the G protein α, β, and γ subunit sequences alongside the GPCR sequence in the AF2-Multimer input. Inactive models were generated using only the receptor sequence, resulting in structures more closely representing this state." (p.8). The authors position their method as *complementary* to state-directing methods, not as one: "Our approach complements strategies based on either column masking applied to the full MSA or subsampling of the MSA, both of which are more suitable for capturing large conformational differences, e.g., the active and inactive receptor states." (p.7)
  - *Selection directionality:* which model is used downstream is decided by **ligand enrichment**, i.e. a ligand oracle, not by a state instruction (p.8).
  - Secondary handles: masking probability (0/10/20/30/50%) tunes the *degree* of local variability — "The masking probability level controls the degree of binding-site variability. A higher masking level introduces more extensive sampling by releasing evolutionary restraints." (p.2) — and inference-stage dropout (p.2). Seeds are not discussed.
  **Bottom line: the method improves binding-site sampling and screening enrichment; it cannot be instructed to produce a named conformational state.**

- **anti_memorization_design**: **NONE.** No held-out set, no post-cutoff set, no train/test split, no cutoff date defined anywhere. The opposite is stated: "it should also be noted that structures of 4 out of the 10 GPCRs in our benchmarking set were used in the training of AF2, and our approach may therefore not perform equally well for receptors of unknown structure." (p.8) — 4 of 10 targets are known to be inside AF2's training data, and which four is not stated in the main text.

- **anti_memorization_control**: **NONE RUN, and `UNPOWERED` even if it had been.** The 4-in-training / 6-not split is acknowledged (p.8) but never analysed: no arm splits AUC, RMSF, volume or enrichment by training membership, and no per-target training-status column appears in Table 1 (p.6) or Table 2 (p.7). n = 10 targets total (4 known-contaminated), well below the ~10 clean-target threshold. Ref 60 in the reference list is Lazou et al., "AlphaFold2 May Remember Too Much" (p.11), cited only in passing (p.7) and not acted on.

  **Not run:** no scrambled-sequence or decoy-region masking arm (masking a matched-size window elsewhere in the receptor), which would be the direct control for "is it the *location* of the window or merely its size?"; no seed-only arm at equal sample count; no training-membership split; no non-GPCR target despite the generality claim.

- **confidence_as_discriminator**: **Used as a guardrail on the method's own hyperparameter, never as a discriminator of conformational or docking correctness, and never validated.** pLDDT and PAE distributions are reported per masking level (Fig 2B-C, p.3) with reference lines at pLDDT 80 and PAE 5, and confidence is one of the two criteria that fixed the 0–30% operating point: "moderate MSA masking (10−30%) enhances binding-site structural accuracy without substantially compromising model confidence" (p.4); "our AFsample2T approach generates structural ensembles of high quality by balancing conformational diversity with model confidence (Table 2)" (p.7). But the models actually carried forward are ranked by **ligand enrichment**, not by pLDDT (p.8), and **no analysis anywhere correlates pLDDT or PAE with binding-site RMSD or with enrichment.** The reference values 80 and 5 are asserted, not derived: "with a value of 5 used as a reference for acceptable accuracy" / "a value of 80 used as a reference for acceptable confidence" (Fig 2 caption, p.3). Note the direction of the trade: AFsample2T is *more* accurate and *less* confident than AF2 (pLDDT 84.4 vs 91.1; PAE 3.3 vs 2.4, Table 2 p.7) — i.e. within this paper confidence is **anti-correlated** with binding-site accuracy, which the authors observe but do not turn into a finding.

## D. Claims

- **central_conclusion**: Masking MSA columns only within a sequence window covering the orthosteric pocket and part of EL2 — rather than across the whole alignment — lets AF2 generate binding-site ensembles whose backbone and side-chain plasticity approaches that seen across deposited structures, while leaving the rest of the fold constrained. Pooling models from 0/10/20/30% masking captures 73.8% of experimental binding sites at 1.5 Å versus 60.7% for default AF2, and the top-1% ligand-enriching members of that ensemble dock about as well as the median (not the best) experimental structure. The gain is realised only when known ligands are available to select the model; with random model selection AFsample2T is no better than AF2 and both are far worse than experimental structures.

- **necessity_claims** (verbatim + page):
  > "**A limitation of our workflow is that model selection relies on prior knowledge of the binding site location and access to ligands. Virtual screening for receptors with no known ligands remains very challenging, and our results show that model performance will be substantially worse if ligand-guided selection is not possible.**" (p.8)
  > "This explains the poor performance of AF2 compared to experimental structures in virtual screening benchmarks, indicating a need for further model refinement to enable drug discovery applications." (p.1)
  > "Moreover, AF2 models performed substantially worse than experimental structures in retrospective benchmarking studies on diverse drug targets, suggesting that binding-site refinement may be required." (p.6)
  > "Targeted column masking enables efficient conformational sampling in the binding site region, while maintaining the evolutionary constraints needed to maintain the overall structure of the receptor." (p.7)
  > "Docking to sets of AF2-based models has already been applied in prospective virtual screens against TAAR1, but the general application of this approach may require accounting for receptor conformational energies that are challenging to estimate." (p.7)
  > "If several experimental structures of the receptor in complex with ligands are available, one of these is generally preferable for virtual screening applications." (pp.7–8)
  > "Compared to AF2-based modeling, MD simulations will be orders of magnitude more computationally demanding and remain technically challenging to perform for membrane proteins and predicted protein structures." (p.7)
  > "Prospective studies will be required to further evaluate AFsample2T models" (p.8)
  > "as AF2 was trained to generate a single structural model, this method does not capture the conformational flexibility of proteins, limiting its utility for structure-based drug design" (p.1, Abstract)

- **novelty_claims** (verbatim + page): **No explicit first/unprecedented claim about this work is made anywhere.** The only "unprecedented" in the paper describes deep learning in general, not the authors' method: "Recently developed deep-learning methods are able to predict the three-dimensional structures of proteins with unprecedented accuracy" (p.6). The self-directed claims are contribution claims, not priority claims:
  > "We present the AFsample2T approach, which achieves increased binding-site plasticity by performing targeted conformational sampling using MSA column masking." (p.2)
  > "AFsample2T overcomes a major limitation of the default AF2 method by enhancing the conformational heterogeneity of the generated models in a specific region of the protein." (p.7)
  > "In addition, AFsample2T yielded larger binding-site volumes for the majority of evaluated GPCRs, highlighting an advance over AF2 that often predicts collapsed binding-site models." (p.7)
  > "Notably, receptor models generated using the standard AFsample2 approach, which applies column masking to the full MSA, yielded the lowest binding-site accuracy (AUC = 0.38), highlighting the effectiveness of targeted sampling." (pp.3–4)
  Novelty-by-contrast with prior work (the closest thing to a priority claim):
  > "A notable limitation in several previous virtual screening studies is that the performance of a single model and experimental structure of each protein was evaluated." (p.6)
  > "In this work, we evaluated ensembles of both predicted and experimental structures, demonstrating that virtual screening performance varies greatly depending on the selected binding-site structure." (p.6)
  Prior art is credited explicitly rather than displaced — the masking operation is AFsample2's (ref 33), the ±G-protein state trick is Chiesa et al.'s (ref 17): "In agreement with the work of Chiesa et al., comparison of the AF2 models with experimentally determined structures of active and inactive states of four receptors confirmed that this approach reliably captures the characteristic conformations of TM6" (p.2); "Similar approaches have previously been applied to identify homology models of GPCRs suitable for virtual screening, and the gain in ligand enrichment is comparable to that obtained by combining AF2 models with induced-fit docking." (p.6)

- **stated_limits**:
  - **Model selection needs a known site and known ligands** — "A limitation of our workflow is that model selection relies on prior knowledge of the binding site location and access to ligands. Virtual screening for receptors with no known ligands remains very challenging, and our results show that model performance will be substantially worse if ligand-guided selection is not possible." (p.8)
  - **4 of 10 benchmark receptors are in AF2's training data** — "it should also be noted that structures of 4 out of the 10 GPCRs in our benchmarking set were used in the training of AF2, and our approach may therefore not perform equally well for receptors of unknown structure." (p.8)
  - **Conformational selection, not induced fit** — "A limitation of our protocol is that AF2-based models may not capture relevant receptor conformations if ligand binding leads to substantial induced-fit effects. On one hand, binding site refinement in the presence of a ligand may better capture induced fit than our approach, which is based on conformational selection." (pp.7–8)
  - **Experimental structures still preferred when available** — "If several experimental structures of the receptor in complex with ligands are available, one of these is generally preferable for virtual screening applications. This is based on the observation that at least one of the experimental GPCR structures typically achieves virtual screening performance comparable to, or better than, all the generated AFsample2T models." (pp.7–8)
  - **Confidence is degraded by the method** — "As column masking reduces the amount of sequence information used in the structure prediction, the model confidence can be expected to decrease." (p.4)
  - **RMSF agreement is weak for most receptors, blamed on structure scarcity** — "The per-residue binding-site RMSF values from the AFsample2T models and experimental data exhibited a weak correlation (r = 0.20−0.40) for the 10 GPCRs (Figure S4). This result may be due to the fact that a small number of structures are available in each case, which will not accurately reflect the conformational heterogeneity of the receptors." (p.4)
  - **Suitability is GPCR-specific** — "Our approach is particularly well suited to GPCRs due to the conserved fold and binding-site location." (p.7)
  - **Rigid-receptor docking caveat, applied symmetrically** — "As molecular docking calculations are generally carried out with the protein held rigid, the virtual screening performance is sensitive to small differences in binding-site conformations. The rigid-receptor-approximation affects the performance of both AF2 models and experimental protein structures. However, because widely used benchmarking sets (e.g., DUD-E) have selected experimental structures displaying high ligand enrichment, comparisons to randomly selected AF2 models may be misleading." (p.6)
  - **Not prospective** — "Prospective studies will be required to further evaluate AFsample2T models" (p.8)

- **stance**: **`precedent` + `contrast`** — provisional, for the user to confirm.
  - **precedent (on findings and on method design)**: the closest published precedent for *spatially targeted* MSA perturbation as opposed to global depth reduction or global masking, with a clean internal specificity control (global AFsample2 is the worst arm, AUC 0.38 vs targeted 0.63, pp.3–4). It also supplies exactly the numbers a docking-enrichment comparison needs (Tables 1 and 2, pp.6–7) and an explicit, reusable four-step operating protocol (Fig 6, p.7).
  - **contrast (on rigour)**: the masking level and ensemble composition are fixed by maximising accuracy against the deposited structures of the same 10 evaluation targets (p.4); docking grids and charges are tuned per receptor on the same actives (pp.8–9); 4/10 targets are inside AF2's training set with no control arm; there is no conformational-state predicate at all; and the headline "comparable to experimental" claim rests on a top-1% selection made with known ligands, whose median counterpart (AFsample2T 4.2 vs experimental 11.2 aLogAUC, p.6) is relegated to SI figures the corpus does not hold.

**Tag notes (not tags):**

- **`msa-subsample` is applied under protest.** The method is MSA *column* masking at full depth, which the v3 vocabulary cannot express (see `unresolved` 6). `msa-state-filter` would be flatly wrong. Read this tag on this paper as "MSA manipulated at inference", not as "depth reduced".
- **No Protocol tag is applied at all.** Templates are explicitly off ("the no-template option", p.8), so `templates-on` is wrong; a full-depth MSA is used, so `no-template-no-msa` is wrong; there is no state-annotated input, so `state-annotated-input` is wrong. The v3 Protocol group has no value for "templates off + full MSA", and none was invented (see `unresolved` 6). This paper is therefore invisible to any Protocol-tag query.
- **`two-state` + `ensemble`** are both applied: the two global GPCR states are produced explicitly by the ±G-protein toggle (p.2, p.8), and within each state a 1,000-model ensemble of binding-site conformations is produced by masking. Neither alone is honest.
- **`rmsd-only` + `continuous-metric`**: RMSD-to-reference is the structural metric, but the paper's primary endpoint is a continuous docking-enrichment coordinate (aLogAUC/EF1%) that no Metric tag covers.
- **`saturating-metric`** is applied for the Fig 2A fraction-captured curve, which ceilings at 1.0 for four arms at the loose end of the swept threshold range (p.3).
- **`oracle-leak`** is applied for routes 4 and 5: the masking level and the 0–30% ensemble composition were fixed by maximising accuracy against the deposited structures of the same 10 evaluation targets (p.4), the docking grids and partial charges were tuned per receptor on those receptors' known ligands (pp.8–9), and every AF2 model is docked into a site whose coordinates were transferred from a deposited holo structure of that receptor (p.8).
- **`design-level-oracle`** is applied separately for route 7 (targets chosen for structure and ligand availability; the active/inactive input condition chosen to match the reference structure's state before comparison) and is **weaker** than the route-4/5 leakage above. Both are kept distinct.
- **`directed-state` + `partner-driven`**: the method *can* be pointed at a state, but not by the masking — only by including or omitting the Gα/Gβ/Gγ sequences (p.2, p.7, p.8). The masking is directable in *site*, not in *state*.
- **`ligand-driven` is deliberately NOT applied.** No ligand is ever supplied to AF2 — all models are generated apo. Ligands drive *model selection* (p.8), not model generation, and tagging this `ligand-driven` would false-positive every query for ligand-conditioned prediction.
- **`apo-sampling`** is applied: every AF2-based model is generated without ligand and without template, and three apo experimental structures are included in the comparison set (p.8).
- **`allosteric-site` is NOT applied** — allosteric masking is named only as a future direction (p.7), never run. `orthosteric` is the only site actually studied.
- **`confidence-as-discriminator` is deliberately NOT applied.** pLDDT/PAE are reported and used as a guardrail when choosing the masking level (p.4), but never to judge conformational correctness and never validated against accuracy; models are selected by ligand enrichment (p.8).
- **`multi-backbone` is NOT applied** — AF2 v2.3.1 only (p.8).
- **`prospective` is NOT applied** — no prospective screen was run; Fig 6 is a proposal (p.7, p.8).
- **`experimental-validation` is NOT applied** — no wet-lab work in this paper.
- **`benchmark-only` is NOT applied** — a method (AFsample2T) is proposed, even though a large retrospective benchmark is the vehicle.
- **`figure-exemplar` is deliberately NOT applied.** Fig 2A (fraction-captured-vs-threshold with AUC in the legend) is a reusable design, but the tag carries a "must be excluded from gap analysis" instruction and this paper is squarely inside our field and is being kept for its content.
