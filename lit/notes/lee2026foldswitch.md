| Protein language models (ESM-2, ESM-3) | Trained "for broad tasks, such as recognizing evolutionary information and inferring function" | "ESM-2 disables dropout … during inference, yielding more homogeneous structure predictions. | Diffusion generative models (EigenFold; AF3's diffusion head) | Expected "to produce more structural diversity by denoising inputs in different ways" | "EigenFold performs well on local conformational rearrangements but poorly on fold switchers (27)"; "this potential may be limited by their upstream transformer models" | pp. 6, 13 |
| SimpleFold (Apple), MSA-free generative | "a generative AI model for predicting protein structures and alternative conformations with a simpler architecture than AlphaFold that does not use MSAs" | An **evaluation-design** criticism: "SimpleFold benchmarks focused on the overall protein structure only. ## D. Claims

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| `central_conclusion` | Fold-switching proteins are the stress test that exposes conformational-ensemble prediction: deep-learning models predict alternative conformations by association with structures seen during training rather than by coevolutionary inference or learned folding physics, so they succeed on conformational changes that preserve the dominant contact map or that have a training-set homolog and fail otherwise; MD-based approaches are physically grounded but have only ever mapped landscapes between two *already known* folds. The proposed measure of real progress is predicting new fold switchers from genomic sequence. |

**What evidence would be required to demonstrate genuine multi-state prediction — VERBATIM, the two falsifiable tests the review proposes:**

34. p. 7 — "First, if different coevolutionary signals are necessary for predicting different protein structures, an appreciable difference would be observed between the sequence embeddings used to generate different conformations of the same protein. Instead, the CFold study found that diverse conformations resulted from very similar embeddings (47)."
35. pp. 7–8 — "Second, if AF2 has learned how to infer coevolution in general–rather than specific ECs or conservation patterns learned implicitly from structures during training–we would expect the AF2 architecture to infer any coevolutionary pattern effectively regardless of what's in its training set and leverage it to predict the appropriate corresponding structure. ### `unresolved`

1. **No `review` tag exists in the v3 vocabulary, and I could not honestly substitute `experimental`.** A sibling extractor reportedly applied `experimental` to a review on its literal definition ("marks a paper with no structure prediction in it at all"). That definition is **false for this document**: it contains 1,200 original CFold structure predictions (p. 8, Fig. 3). Applying `experimental` here would make the tag mean two incompatible things and would false-positive any reverse lookup for wet-lab-only papers. I have therefore **not** applied it, and instead tagged `benchmark-only`, which describes the original analysis accurately. **v3 needs a `review` tag.** Recording rather than inventing, per instruction.
2. **12 vs 13 clusters.** The body text says "Wayment-Steele et al.'s 12 sequence clusters" and "1200 structures sampled (100/cluster)" (p. 8), and earlier "12/345 (4%) of their clusters" (p. 7); the Figure 3 caption says "these 13 clusters" (p. 10) and the figure plots 10 RfaH + 3 Mad2 = 13 boxes × 100 = 1,300 structures (p. 9). Either the total is 1,300 or one cluster is excluded from the count. Unresolvable from the document.
3. **Figure 4 cross-references are mislabelled throughout p. 12.** The body text cites "Figure 4B" for Sa1 V90T, "Figure 4C" for interleukin-6, "Figure 4D" for pro-interleukin-18 and "Figure 4E" for MP20, but the figure itself is lettered A (sub-cases 1–3) and B (sub-cases 1–2), so the intended targets are A2, A3, B1 and B2. The figure table above uses the figure's own lettering.
4. **"human interleukin 16" vs "interleukin-6".** Body text p. 12 says "Recently solved structures of human interleukin 16 showed two distinct domain-swapped forms"; Figure 4's caption and label say "Human interleukin 6". Almost certainly a typo for IL-6, but I have not resolved which.
5. **Section numbering error.** Section 2.4 is printed as "4.4. Mechanistic reasons for predictive limitations of DL models" (p. 12), between 2.3 and 3.
6. **No Methods section and no supplementary material.** The Figure 3 protocol is a caption plus one body sentence. CFold version/weights, recycle count, seeds, MSA depth per cluster, TM-score implementation and alignment mode, and the selection rule for the Figure 3B representative structures are all unstated.
