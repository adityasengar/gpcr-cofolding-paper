## B. Scope

- **system**: general protein + transporter. Three datasets: OC23 (open/closed proteins), TP16 (membrane transporters, inward/outward-facing), MS15 (curated panel spanning "six functional categories, including a kinase, GTPases, periplasmic binding proteins, fold-switching proteins, membrane transporters, and an intramembrane protease", p9). Transporters are the plurality: TP16 entirely, plus 6 of 15 MS15 targets (Table 2, p10). No GPCRs.
- **n_targets**: 58 total across three datasets — OC23 = 23 proteins (p9); TP16 = 20 sequences after excluding SPF1, evaluated at construct level (p3, p9); MS15 = 15 targets (p4, Table 2 p10). Combined n = 58 is corroborated by p5 ("18 targets (31% of the combined dataset)"; 18/0.31 ≈ 58) but the number 58 is never printed.
- **method_class**: other — inference-time latent-space intervention (uniform scalar rescaling of the Pairformer pair representation), not an input perturbation. Baseline arms actually run include MSA-subsampling, MSA random masking, and MSA clustering (AF-Cluster-inspired), plus a "Laplacian" spatially-structured scaling ablation (p9–p11).
- **backbones**: Boltz-2 version 2.1.1 (p12) is the only model modified. AlphaFold 3 Server is run as an external reference at default settings, 5 models per target (p3, p10, Table 3 p11). Two backbones, so `multi-backbone` NOT tagged (schema requires more than two head to head).
- **templates**: off. "All Boltz-2 based methods were run without input templates to strictly evaluate the sampling capabilities." (p10). Template usage inside the AF3 Server arm is NOT REPORTED.
- **msa_handling**: full (fixed, shared across methods) for the primary method; subsampled / masked / clustered only in the baseline arms; sequence-only in an ablation. "Input MSAs were collected from the AlphaFold3 server [16] (Fig. 7). To strictly isolate the contribution of sampling strategies, we utilized these fixed MSAs across all benchmarked methods" (p9). No state-filtering anywhere — the MSA is never substituted for a state-specific alignment. Subsampling is p ∈ {0.05, 0.10, …, 0.50} (Table 3, p11).

## C. Conformational core

- **states_generated**: ensemble (500 models per target for Boltz-sample), evaluated as two states. Both discrete and continuous responses are reported: "For adenylate kinase (P69441), the ensemble shifts smoothly with β, tracing a continuum between the two conformations. In contrast, the ADP/ATP translocase (G2QNH0) and EF-Tu (P0CE48) exhibit a more discrete, mode-switching response" (p5).

- **structural_priors_used**: **New in v3. Two kinds, and the paper's central claim is about the second.**
  1. **Design-time, in the benchmark.** OC23 and TP16 are inherited wholesale from the AFsample2 benchmark, *"datasets from the AFsample2 benchmark [10] that provide paired reference structures for distinct conformational states such as open/closed or outward/inward-facing forms."* (p3); TP16 is re-cut at construct level to 20 sequences after excluding the very long SPF1 (p3). MS15 is curated by the authors against a pairwise TM-score ≤ 0.85 criterion. Every target therefore has both states deposited before a single prediction is made, and the worked example is scored against PDB **3TEE** and **3VJP** (p2). No deposited structure enters the pipeline.
  2. **Inside the model, and this is the argument.** The paper's causal story is that β-scaling exposes structural knowledge Boltz-2 already holds, rather than amplifying coevolutionary signal. Verbatim, p1: *"our analysis of sequence-only inference reveals that the method does not rely solely on coevolutionary signals but actively unlocks the structural priors internalized by the model to recover alternative states even without MSA information."* Restated at p5: *"internal scaling does not merely randomize the output, but effectively leverages the model's internalized structural priors to recover alternative conformations even without explicit coevolutionary signals."*

  **Why the two interact badly.** The mechanism the authors propose is memorised structural priors, and the benchmark contains no memorisation control (see `anti_memorization_control`). The paper's explanation and its largest methodological gap are therefore the same fact seen from two sides, which is worth saying plainly whenever it is cited.

- **oracle_leakage**: **PRESENT — six routes, none of which is "best β picked per target by agreement with a known structure".** Enumerated separately below. Protocol pages: p9 (β sweep and datasets), p10 (baselines, MSA source), p11–p12 (metrics and selection).

  **R1 — β sweep range chosen empirically on the same benchmarks used for evaluation (the field flagged in the task).**
  Verbatim, p9: "We swept β from −0.75 to 0.75 in fixed increments. This range was empirically determined to maximize the exploration of conformational diversity while preventing projection collapse or severe steric clashes associated with extreme interaction strengths."
  Verbatim, p3: "In practice, we swept β over a moderate range that preserves stable inference while measurably shifting the generated ensemble."
  **How the scalar was selected, precisely**: (i) the *range* [−0.75, +0.75] is a global hyperparameter stated to be "empirically determined", with no held-out or tuning split described anywhere in Methods; the stated selection criteria are "conformational diversity" and avoidance of "projection collapse or severe steric clashes". In this paper conformational diversity is only ever operationalised as ΔTM range and fill-ratio, both of which are computed **against the two deposited reference structures** (p11–p12). So the range was tuned against reference-dependent quantities measured on the evaluation sets themselves. (ii) A *single best β* is never chosen. All 10 β values (β ∈ {±0.15, …, ±0.75}, β = 0 excluded as it duplicates vanilla; Table 3, p11) are pooled into the 500-model ensemble, and the paper explicitly disclaims any β→state mapping: "Accordingly, we do not observe a consistent mapping between the sign of β and a particular conformational state across datasets." (p6). (iii) Prospective per-target *model* selection is by mean pLDDT, not by reference agreement (p12). Net: **leakage at the level of the global hyperparameter range and of the tuning-set/evaluation-set identity, not at the level of a per-target oracle β.**

  **R2 — success is defined post hoc as best-of-N agreement with the held reference structures.**
  Verbatim, p11: "we identified the best-scoring model for each reference structure independently. Given a target with two experimentally determined states (denoted as reference 1 and reference 2), we computed the maximum TM-score achieved within the ensemble for each state, TMmax_ref1 and TMmax_ref2. We report the Best minimum TM-score as min(TMmax_ref1, TMmax_ref2)".
  Verbatim, p11: "Dual-state coverage is defined as the percentage of targets for which both TMmax_ref1 and TMmax_ref2 exceed a threshold of 0.8."
  Every headline number (dual-state coverage, best-min TM, fill-ratio) is a max over 500 samples taken against a structure the authors held.

  **R3 — an explicit oracle selector is run as an arm (disclosed as an upper bound, not hidden).**
  Verbatim, p12: "These strategies were benchmarked against an oracle selector that identifies the sampled model maximizing the minimum TM-score to both reference states."
  Verbatim, p7: "sign-based selection achieved an AUC of 0.58 on MS15, closely tracking the oracle upper bound of 0.60".

  **R4 — dataset construction requires both deposited states; MS15 targets were curated on a structure-similarity criterion.**
  Verbatim, p4: "we assembled the MS15 dataset, a curated panel of 15 proteins characterized by distinct conformational states (pairwise TM-score ≤ 0.85)."
  Verbatim, p9: "Sequences were obtained from UniProt [31], and experimental structures for the alternative states were taken from the RCSB Protein Data Bank (PDB) [32]."
  Verbatim, p9: "OC23 includes 23 proteins with open and closed states satisfying a pairwise TM-score ≤ 0.85."
  No state-annotated database (GPCRdb / KLIFS / Kincore) is used, and none of these structures enters the pipeline as input or template.

  **R5 — post hoc labelling of β direction and of "representative" models against the known references.**
  Verbatim, p6: "we compute a per-β bias score, b(β) = TMref1(β) − TMref2(β), and visualize b(β) as heatmaps (Fig. 6A)."
  Verbatim, p5: "For EF-Tu, negative β favors the 1OB2-like state in Fig. 4."
  Verbatim, p5: "for the transcription factor RfaH (P0AFW0), default inference is concentrated near the β-barrel state (PDB 6C6S), and positive scaling (β > 0) shifts sampling toward the alternative α-helical hairpin state (PDB 2OUG)."
  Verbatim, Fig. 4 caption p6: "For each target, representative models from distinct conformational regimes are superimposed."

  **R6 — model-level memorization of the target states, asserted by the authors as the mechanism, with no cutoff control.**
  Verbatim, p8: "our findings suggest that Boltz-2 has internalized a rich set of structural priors from the PDB training distribution. In this context, β-scaling acts not merely as a noise generator but as a probe that unlocks these latent priors, allowing the model to access alternative states that are suppressed under standard inference conditions."
  Verbatim, p5: "This systematic skew toward higher accuracy indicates that internal scaling does not merely randomize the output, but effectively leverages the model's internalized structural priors to recover alternative conformations even without explicit coevolutionary signals."
  The reference structures (Table 2, p10: 4AKE/1AKE, 2OUG/6C6S, 2QKE/5JYT, 1OMP/1ANF, …) are deposited PDB entries; the Boltz-2 training cutoff is never stated and no overlap analysis is performed.

  **Routes explicitly ABSENT (checkable negatives):** no deposited structure is supplied as input or template — "All Boltz-2 based methods were run without input templates to strictly evaluate the sampling capabilities." (p10); no state-filtered or state-annotated MSA — the same fixed AF3-server MSA is used for every method (p9); the clustering baseline's own hyperparameter is chosen on a structure-free criterion — "We varied eps from 0.05 to 0.50 and selected the value that maximized the number of clusters." (p11).

- **prospective**: **partial.** Prospective in the sampling stage and in one selection arm — β is applied blind to all targets, no reference structure enters inference, and the confidence-based selectors use mean pLDDT only ("To assess practical utility without reliance on reference structures, we evaluated heuristics based on model confidence", p12). Retrospective everywhere else: the β range is tuned on the evaluation benchmarks (R1), all reported metrics are best-of-500 against held references (R2), the targets exist in the benchmark only because both states are deposited (R4), and the authors attribute the mechanism to memorized PDB priors (R6). No target is modelled before its alternative state was known.

- **state_metric**: continuous coordinate + binary predicate, both anchored on RMSD-free structural similarity to deposited references.
  - TM-score to each of two reference structures, computed with US-align (p11).
  - Best minimum TM-score = min(TMmax_ref1, TMmax_ref2) — "quantifies the prediction quality for the harder-to-sample conformation" (p11).
  - Dual-state coverage = binary predicate at **TM-score ≥ 0.8** for both states (p3, p11). **Threshold justification: NOT REPORTED** — 0.8 is asserted without argument. The related dataset-construction threshold (states must have pairwise TM ≤ 0.85, p4/p9) is also unjustified, and note the two thresholds are only 0.05 apart.
  - Fill-ratio: models projected onto the diagonal segment between the two references in (TMref1, TMref2) space, N = 100 bins, symmetric parabolic weighting favouring the end bins; "the weighted fraction of bins that contained at least one model" (p12). Adapted from AFsample2.
  - ΔTM range (ensemble spread) and per-β bias b(β) = TMref1(β) − TMref2(β) (p6).
  - Success curves: fraction of targets with min(TMref1, TMref2) ≥ t, swept over t (p6).

- **metric_saturation**: **Yes, ceiling on OC23, plus pervasive axis truncation.**
  - Verbatim ceiling, p3: "On OC23, all methods achieved high best-minimum TM-scores with medians near 0.9. Boltz-sample slightly improved accuracy relative to vanilla and performed comparably to MSA clustering." A best-of-500 max statistic against a reference is structurally ceiling-prone, and on OC23 it has already saturated for every arm, which is why the OC23 accuracy comparison is uninformative and the paper's argument shifts to spread/fill-ratio.
  - Dual-state coverage is a hard-thresholded binary predicate (TM ≥ 0.8) over 15–23 targets, so it moves in steps of 4.3–6.7 percentage points and cannot resolve small differences.
  - **Axis truncations** (read from rendered pages): Fig. 2 and Fig. 3 best-min TM-score box panels do not start at 0 (OC23 panel runs ~0.7–1.0); Fig. 2/3 TM-score scatter panels are truncated to ~0.4–1.0 on both axes; Fig. 4 per-target scatters are truncated to narrow target-specific windows (e.g. 0.66–0.98 for P69441, 0.53–0.91 for P0AFW0); Fig. 5 best-min TM-score axis starts at 0.4; Fig. 6B x-axis starts at 0.5. Computation-time panels use a log axis (Fig. 2, Fig. 3), which visually compresses a difference the text states as "less than 10⁻² s per prediction" versus clustering (p3).

- **directional_control**: **Partial and, by the authors' own statement, not state-addressable.** The handle is the sign and magnitude of the scalar β applied to the pair representation — not a partner, ligand, nanobody, peptide, template, or state-filtered MSA. Two directions exist and are useful: "the sign of β effectively defines distinct search directions in the latent space" (p7). But which physical state each direction reaches is target-dependent and not predictable: "Many targets show systematic changes in bias as β varies, but the direction of the shift depends on the target: increasing β can favor either reference state depending on the system. Accordingly, we do not observe a consistent mapping between the sign of β and a particular conformational state across datasets." (p6). So the method can be instructed *to explore in two directions*, but cannot be instructed *to produce a named state*. Secondary handles held fixed: 5 seeds × 10 diffusion samples (Table 3, p11).

- **anti_memorization_design**: **NONE.** No held-out set, no post-cutoff set, no training-cutoff date for Boltz-2 stated anywhere, no PDB-release-date filter on the benchmark targets. MS15 was curated from the PDB with no recency criterion — the stated selection rule is only "distinct conformational states (pairwise TM-score ≤ 0.85)" (p4) and "experimental structures for the alternative states were taken from the RCSB Protein Data Bank (PDB)" (p9). OC23/TP16 are inherited wholesale from AFsample2 (p9). Some MS15 references are recent depositions (8Y5Y/8Y5Z, 8J7X/8J7W, 7AAR, 6N3I; Table 2 p10) but the paper never identifies them as such or uses them as a recency stratum.

- **anti_memorization_control**: **NONE RUN.** The one ablation that touches memorization is the sequence-only (no-MSA) arm, and it is run in the *opposite* direction — as evidence that memorized priors are doing useful work, not as a control against them: "our findings suggest that Boltz-2 has internalized a rich set of structural priors from the PDB training distribution" (p8); "18 targets (31% of the combined dataset) improved by at least 0.05, whereas only a single target (1.7%) worsened by the same margin" (p5). No arm stratifies results by deposition date, training-set membership, or sequence novelty. The gap is load-bearing here because the paper's own causal story (R6) is memorization.

- **controls_run**: **New in v3. A well-populated baseline grid, plus one ablation that is doing double duty.**

| control | what it rules out | page |
|---|---|---|
| **Vanilla (standard) Boltz-2 inference** | that the gain is not real; it is the reference for every reported number. Dual-state coverage 43% vanilla → 78% with β-scaling | pp2–3 |
| **MSA subsampling** | that depth reduction already achieves it | pp3, 10 |
| **MSA random masking** | that column masking already achieves it | pp3, 10 |
| **MSA clustering** | that AF-Cluster-style clustering already achieves it — the closest baseline, reaching 70% against 78% | pp3, 10 |
| **Sequence-only (no-MSA) arm** | that the effect is coevolutionary rather than internal. 18 of 58 targets (31%) improved by ≥ 0.05, one (1.7%) worsened | pp5, 8 |
| **Selection-strategy comparison against an oracle upper bound and a random baseline** | that the confidence-based selector is doing nothing. p8: *"Strategies include Oracle as the theoretical upper bound, Random as the baseline, Per-β selection which chooses one model per step, and Sign-based selection which selects only the top model per sign direction."* Sign-based reaches AUC 0.58 against an oracle 0.60 and random 0.47 | pp6–8 |
| **A reported failure case** | over-claiming. p5: for MAD2 (Q13257) *"the method did not recover the alternative state, indicating that internal scaling has limitations in retrieving alternative conformations for certain targets where the dominant state is strongly favored"* | p5 |
| **ABSENT — any memorization control** | the paper's own proposed mechanism. No temporal split, no training-set stratification, no sequence-novelty stratum, despite several MS15 references being recent depositions | pp9–10 |
| **ABSENT — a held-out split for the β sweep** | that β's range was not tuned on the same benchmarks that report the result | p9 |
| **ABSENT — a semantic direction for β** | that the sign means anything transferable. The authors say so, p6: *"we do not observe a consistent mapping between the sign of β and a particular conformational state across datasets."* So the handle steers breadth and separates directions, but it cannot be told *which* state to produce | p6 |

  **The last row is the one that matters to us.** This is the corpus's cleanest architecture-internal steering result, and its own authors record that the control it provides is undirected: β separates the sampling space into two regimes without either regime corresponding to a nameable conformational state. Any comparison we draw against it should be on that axis, not on coverage numbers.

- **confidence_as_discriminator**: **Yes, and partially validated — but validated for ranking, not for judging conformational correctness.** "Given the monomeric nature of the targets, we used the mean pLDDT score as the ranking metric." (p12); ipTM/pTM are not used (monomers). Validation is via success curves against an oracle upper bound and a random baseline: "despite being restricted to just two candidates, sign-based selection achieved an AUC of 0.58 on MS15, closely tracking the oracle upper bound of 0.60 and significantly outperforming the random baseline of 0.47 (Fig. 6B)" (p7). What is NOT validated: that pLDDT distinguishes state 1 from state 2, or that a high-pLDDT model is in the correct basin — pLDDT is only used to pick the single best model *within* each β-sign pool, after the sign has already defined the split. The "significantly" at p7 is not backed by a named test or p-value for the AUC comparison.

## D. Claims

- **central_conclusion**: Uniformly multiplying Boltz-2's latent pair representation by a single scalar (1 + β) at each Pairformer recycle, with all inputs and weights untouched, broadens the predicted conformational ensemble enough to raise dual-state recovery substantially over vanilla inference (43→78% on OC23, 25→70% on TP16, 33→80% on MS15) at essentially zero extra compute, approaching but not matching MSA clustering. The gain survives removal of the MSA, which the authors read as evidence that the alternative states are stored in the model's learned priors rather than only in coevolutionary signal, and the sign of β defines two search directions from which a plain pLDDT pick recovers near-oracle dual-state models.

- **necessity_claims** (verbatim + page):
  - p2: "However, existing approaches act primarily on the input alignment or rely on generic stochasticity during inference and do not offer an explicit, interpretable parameter for modulating how internal representations are used to explore a multi-state ensemble."
  - p2: "Consequently, it remains unclear how existing interventions modulate the internal representations during inference, and whether they provide a predictable, interpretable handle for shifting the relative weights of alternative conformations."
  - p1: "Understanding how deep learning models represent this conformational diversity, and how alternative states can be accessed in a steerable way, remains an open problem in structural biology and biophysics [4, 5]."
  - p1: "Traditional approaches rely on molecular dynamics or enhanced sampling to generate ensembles, which can be computationally demanding and require system-specific expertise to parameterize."
  - p1: "In practice, however, such MSA-based procedures require ad hoc choices of alignment depth, subsampling strategy, and number of trials, and it is often unclear how much sampling is needed to encounter a desired state in prospective modeling."
  - p7: "Consequently, selecting the most confident representative from each direction is sufficient to recover high-quality dual-state models, eliminating the need for ad hoc post-processing steps such as PCA or clustering to identify distinct states."
  - p9: "Practically, Boltz-sample eliminates the need for expensive stochastic trials or ad hoc clustering by defining distinct search directions in the latent space."
  - p8 (impossibility, self-directed): "Consequently, steering is effective only when the alternative state corresponds to a latent direction accessible via scalar rescaling. In cases where the minority signal is effectively averaged out by the dominant basin, global modulation cannot retrieve it."
  - p10: "While the original approach relies on exhaustive clustering to map the full evolutionary landscape [24], such a strategy is computationally prohibitive for routine inference tasks."
  - p7 (sufficiency, the mirror claim): "Our results demonstrate that the latent pair representation in Boltz-2 [17, 18] contains sufficient information to recover alternative conformational states without retraining or altering input alignments."

- **novelty_claims** (verbatim + page):
  - p1 (abstract): "We introduce Boltz-sample as a systematic steering strategy for Boltz-2 that modulates conformational sampling by uniformly rescaling the latent pair representation."
  - p1 (abstract): "In contrast to heuristic input perturbations such as MSA subsampling that rely on trial-and-error to induce variability, this operation systematically modulates the effective strength of pairwise couplings while preserving the input sequence and alignment."
  - p1 (abstract): "Crucially, our analysis of sequence-only inference reveals that the method does not rely solely on coevolutionary signals but actively unlocks the structural priors internalized by the model to recover alternative states even without MSA information."
  - p1 (abstract): "Boltz-sample thus offers a transparent framework to navigate the latent conformational landscape and shifts the paradigm from unpredictable input variation to systematic latent space steering."
  - p2: "Here, we introduce Boltz-sample, a strategy for systematic latent space steering in Boltz-2 that modulates conformational sampling by rescaling the latent pair representation with a single scalar β. This operation provides a transparent mechanism to steer the diffusion process by adjusting the global signal-to-noise ratio of pairwise couplings, avoiding the computational redundancy of heuristic input perturbations."
  - p3: "Crucially, this intervention is mechanistically distinct from classifier-free guidance because it requires neither an auxiliary classifier nor an unconditional branch, thus avoiding the computational cost of a second forward pass."
  - p5: "This observation demonstrates that the potential for both topological states is encoded within the model's latent representations, and that β acts as a control handle to amplify the signals required to manifest the alternative state."
  - p7: "This establishes a direct link between the magnitude of internal feature representations and the resulting structural diversity [21]."
  - p8: "The ability of Boltz-sample to improve prediction quality even in sequence-only settings challenges the view that alternative states are solely encoded in coevolutionary signals [23]."
  - p8: "The observation that spatially structured Laplacian scaling [27, 28] offers no performance advantage over uniform scaling is particularly revealing. It implies that the global magnitude of the interaction signal, rather than specific local contact patterns, serves as the primary order parameter governing the transition between conformational states in the model."
  - p9: "This framework thus enables the efficient and transparent exploration of protein dynamics, shifting the focus from random input perturbation to direct navigation of the deep learning latent landscape."
  - No explicit "first" / "unprecedented" / "for the first time" claim appears anywhere in the paper.

- **stated_limits**:
  - Outright failure case, p5: "However, for MAD2 (Q13257), the method did not recover the alternative state, indicating that internal scaling has limitations in retrieving alternative conformations for certain targets where the dominant state is strongly favored or the latent signal is insufficient."
  - Mechanistic boundary, p8: "Across benchmarks, the failure to recover alternative states for targets like MAD2 delineates a critical boundary. Unlike clustering approaches that explicitly disentangle multimodal coevolutionary signals, Boltz-sample modulates the aggregate representation globally."
  - No state addressability, p6: "we do not observe a consistent mapping between the sign of β and a particular conformational state across datasets."
  - Beaten by clustering on the headline metric, p3: "Dual-state coverage rose from 25% for vanilla inference to 70% with Boltz-sample, while clustering reached 85%." And p4: "While MSA clustering reached a slightly higher coverage (93%), Boltz-sample approaches this performance ceiling without the associated computational cost."
  - Ablation scoped narrowly, p6: "This comparison suggests that, for the Laplacian design considered here, the observed diversity is largely explained by the overall strength of the pair representation rather than by detailed spatial structure in the scaling weights."
  - Target excluded for cost, p9: "We excluded SPF1 because its substantially longer sequence would dominate computational cost under a fixed sampling protocol, and we report results on the remaining 20 targets."
  - MSA still dominant, p8: "While MSAs provide the primary driver for diversity, our findings suggest that Boltz-2 has internalized a rich set of structural priors from the PDB training distribution."
  - NOT stated as a limit anywhere: absence of any anti-memorization control; the empirical, benchmark-tuned choice of the β range; the absence of a threshold justification for TM ≥ 0.8; the OC23 ceiling.

- **stance**: **`precedent` + `contrast`** — PROVISIONAL, user's call.
  - *precedent*: it is a direct, close precedent for architecture-internal steering of an AF3-class co-folding model — a single scalar on the pair latent, applied at inference with no retraining, no template, and no MSA substitution, with per-arm compute cost and a confidence-only selection protocol that is genuinely usable without reference structures. Its dual-state coverage and fill-ratio numbers are ready-made comparators.
  - *contrast*: rigour. Every headline number is best-of-500 against a structure the authors held (R2); the β range is "empirically determined" on the same benchmarks it is then evaluated on with no tuning split (R1); there is no anti-memorization design or control at all, while the paper's own mechanistic explanation is that Boltz-2 memorized the states from the PDB training distribution (R6); the 0.8 dual-state threshold is unjustified and the OC23 accuracy metric is saturated; and the method cannot be told which state to produce (p6), which is the property a steering claim would need.
