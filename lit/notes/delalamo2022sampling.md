## B. Scope

- **system**: membrane proteins — **five transporters + three GPCRs**, eight targets total.
- **n_targets**: **8.** GPCRs, verbatim: *"We also included three GPCRs, which were distributed
  across classes A (CGRPR, …), B1 (PTH1R, …), and F (FZD7, …; to serve as points of comparison,
  we used the active conformation of FZD7 and the inactive conformation of the nearly identical
  FZD4)."* **None is in our panel**, and the class-A assignment of CGRPR is the paper's own.
- **method_class**: MSA subsampling at inference + reduced recycling; a released pipeline
  (`github.com/delalamo/af2_conformations`).
- **backbones**: **AF2** only.
- **templates**: **ON, and this matters** — *"the parameter 'subsample_templates' was set to
  True and the template similarity cutoff was reduced from 10% to 1%."* **So this is not an
  MSA-only manipulation.**
- **msa_handling**: **subsampled, with depth as the swept parameter.** *"To diversify the models
  generated by AF2, we reduced the number of recycles to one and restricted the depth of the
  randomly subsampled MSAs to contain as few as 16 sequences."* Implementation: *"depth values
  were controlled by modifying 'max_msa_clusters' and 'max_extra_msa' parameters prior to
  execution."*

## C. Conformational core

- **states_generated**: **two-state per target**, sampled as an ensemble across MSA depths;
  for GPCRs the two states are **inactive and active** — *"Comparison of AF2 models with
  inactive/active or IF/OF experimental structures as a function of multiple sequence alignment
  (MSA) depth for GPCRs (top) and transporters (bottom)"*, with *"Teal: similarity to IF
  (transporters) or inactive (GPCRs) conformation. Orange: similarity to OF (transporters) or
  active (GPCRs) conformation."*
- **state_metric**: **TM score to each deposited reference**, plus PC1 of the model ensemble.
  **No operationalised activation predicate** — no TM6 distance, no NPxxY rule, no threshold on
  a geometric microswitch. Same limitation as most of this literature.
- **directional_control**: **MSA depth is the handle**, and the paper is explicit that there is
  no universal setting: *"they also argue against an optimal one-size-fits-all approach for
  sampling the conformational space of every protein with high accuracy."*
- **input_factor_design**: **v3.2.** **MSA**: varied (depth, down to 16 sequences).
  **templates**: varied/on (subsample_templates=True, cutoff 1%). **ligand**: absent.
  **partner**: absent. `crossings:` **MSA × templates approached but not cleanly crossed** —
  a template arm is run (*"we modeled PTH1R using either inactive or active templates"*) and
  the paper's finding is that *"the information content provided by the templates diminishes as
  the depth of the MSA increases."* **MSA × partner and MSA × ligand: NOT APPLICABLE — neither
  is ever supplied.**
- **anti_memorization_design**: **present and stated as the paper's own framing** — targets
  chosen so at least one conformation was absent from the AF2 training set: *"AF2 can be
  manipulated to accurately model alternative conformations of transporters and GPCRs whose
  structures were not available in the training set."*
- **confidence_as_discriminator**: pLDDT reported per residue; *"pLDDT refers to each residue's
  predicted accuracy, with a value of 100 indicating maximum confidence."* `UNRESOLVED` whether
  it is validated as a state selector.

## D. Claims

- **central_conclusion**: Reducing MSA depth (to as few as 16 sequences) and recycling in AF2
  yields ensembles containing both experimentally known conformations of five transporters and
  three GPCRs, including targets whose alternative state was absent from the training set — but
  the optimal depth is target-specific.

- **comparable_to_ours**: **Directly, and it must be met rather than omitted. Four caveats
  bound it, and all four are real:**
  1. **Templates are ON** (`subsample_templates=True`, cutoff cut to 1%) and the paper shows
     templates carry state information at shallow depth. **So it is not an MSA-only result**,
     and it is not a clean test of "shallow MSA alone".
  2. **Three GPCRs, none in our panel**, spanning classes A/B1/F — one per class, so there is no
     within-class replication.
  3. **No operationalised state predicate** — TM score to a deposited reference, which is the
     instrument our own paper criticises.
  4. **Quote 6 cuts the other way and is the most useful sentence for us**: the authors
     attribute performance differences to training-set composition — *"many active GPCRs"* were
     in it. **If active is the memorised default for GPCRs, then reaching active by subsampling
     is not evidence of steering; reaching inactive would be.** That is the question to put to
     this paper, and it is the same argument `yu2026domainmotion` makes at scale.

     **Sharpened 2026-09-14 into a design constraint (recorded as `DECISIONS.md` F-33).** The
     point is not only that active may be the memorised default — it is that **any method
     selected for reaching active has been selected for agreeing with the prior.** Reaching
     active is what the null does. **So this paper does not just need rebutting; it fixes which
     direction our own MSA arm must be scored on: the informative comparison is INACTIVE, and an
     MSA control that reports only active-state recovery cannot distinguish steering from
     memorisation.** The corresponding rule: **choose a method or parameter on a property of the
     INPUT — alignment depth, sequence diversity, cluster count — never on a property of the
     output**, and where performance must decide, decide it on a held-out system set outside the
     reported panel and say so.
