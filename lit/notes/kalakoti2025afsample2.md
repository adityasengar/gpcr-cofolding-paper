## B. Scope

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| `system` | **General protein + transporter + fold-switching.** Three benchmark sets and one case study. (i) **OC23** — 23 proteins "with distinct open and closed states" (p11), identified in the figures only by UniProt accession (A2RJ53, O76728, P31133, P00558, P40131, Q7DAU8, P21589, A0QTT2, Q5F9M1, Q9X6R4, Q18A65, Q9ERE7, P62495, A0A075Q0W3, P71447, P33284, Q9Z4N6, A6UVT1, B7IE18, B3EYN2, Q53W80, Q9SS90, Q9X9P9 — Fig 2b p4, Fig 4c p7); family composition is in Table S1, which is not in the PDF. (ii) **TP16** — 16 membrane protein transporters with inward-/outward-facing states (p9, p11). (iii) **OC>85** — 22 proteins with TM ≥ 0.85 between states, used as a negative-diversity control (p11). (iv) One engineered/**fold-switching** S6 ribosomal protein that switches between an α/β-plait (FS1, 8e6y_A) and a 3α-helical bundle (FS2, 2f1s_A) with temperature (p9, Fig 9 p11). **No GPCR and no protein kinase appears anywhere in the paper.** |
| `n_targets` | **62 proteins across four sets** (23 OC23 + 22 OC>85 + 16 TP16 + 1 fold switcher), assuming no overlap between sets — overlap is never stated. The headline claims rest on OC23 (n=23) and TP16 (n=16); the statistics section confirms "The sample sizes were 23 for OC23 and 16 for TP16." (p12). Generality claim is made on that basis ("a wide range of proteins", p3). |
| `method_class` | **Other — random MSA *column* masking, applied at AF2 inference time** (schema list has no matching class; nearest listed is *enhanced sampling*). "AFsample2, which employs MSA column masking by randomly changing columns to 'X' to diminish the constraints exerted by co-evolutionary signals in MSA" (p2). Dropout is enabled simultaneously (p3). A **second, separable contribution** is a reference-free *state-selection* layer — confidence screening plus extremity selection from the highest-confidence model (p7) — plus a new diversity metric, the fill ratio (p6, Fig 10 p12). MSA-subsampling and MSA-clustering appear only as **comparator arms** (MSAsubsample, AFcluster), not as the method. |
| `backbones` | **AF2 only.** "The entire prediction pipeline is based on AlphaFold v2.3.1" (p11), using all ten monomer weight sets (5 original + 5 pTM) (p11). No AF3, Boltz, Chai, OF3 or Protenix is run. Not `multi-backbone`. Portability is asserted, not tested: "The idea is not limited to the AF2 inference system but could be readily applied to other MSA-based prediction systems, such as RosettaFold, OpenFold, or AF3." (p11) |
| `templates` | **off.** "Templates were not used in any of the runs." (p11). The AFsample comparator likewise: "increased recycles, and templates were not used." (p12) |
| `msa_handling` | **full depth, column-masked in place.** This is *not* subsampling and *not* state filtering. Depth is never reduced and no sequence is ever removed: "the user-defined parameter –msa_rand_fraction determines what fraction of MSA columns that should be randomly replaced with 'X', ignoring the first row, which is the target sequence." (p11). MSAs built with the stock AF2 DataPipeline (HHblits + jackhmmer over UniRef90, BFD, Uniclust30, MGnify, default parameters) (p11). Two comparator arms *do* alter depth/composition: **MSAsubsample** ("max_extra_msa was used to randomly set the depth to any of these values: [16, 32, 64, 128, 256, 512, 1024, 5120] and max_msa_clusters was set to half the depth or maximum 512", pp11–12) and **AFcluster** (default `ClusterMSA.py`, p11). The paper's own Fig 1a states the distinction explicitly: "(top) MSA subsampling act on the rows of the MSAs, (bottom) column masking, break co-evolving residues" (p2). |

## C. Conformational core

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| `states_generated` | **ensemble + two-state.** The generator produces a 1,000-model ensemble per target that populates the open↔closed axis including points between the end states; the analysis then extracts two end-state representatives. Ensemble evidence: "AFsample2 also improves the diversity by generating 70% more conformations between the end states when compared to the AFvanilla" (p3); "AFsample2 is able to generate both states and models between the states for a majority (15/23) of the targets in OC23" (p6). Two-state evidence: the whole reporting frame is "best open" / "best closed", and the selection layer is explicitly two-state — "This strategy works best for two-state systems but could potentially be extended to more states by iterating" (p7). Not a `continuum`: the fill-ratio metric bins the state1→state2 diagonal into 100 bins and AFsample2's median occupancy is well under half (Fig 4b–c p7), so coverage is patchy, not continuous. |
| `structural_priors_used` | **Substantial, at design and analysis time — and none of it enters the generator.** (a) **Target selection requires two deposited states.** "A set of 23 proteins with distinct open and closed states was selected as defined by a TM-score difference between the open and closed state <0.85 (Table S1)." (p11); TP16 "was manually curated in a recent study to benchmark protein structure prediction methods for membrane proteins that show substantial conformational changes" (p11, ref 28). (b) **The evaluation coordinate system is built from the deposited pair** — both axes of every diversity plot are TM-score to reference open and reference closed (p6, Figs 4a, 5, 7h, 9a-b), and the fill-ratio diagonal is anchored at TMref, "the TM-score between experimental states" (p12). (c) **Per-residue ΔCα between the deposited open and closed structures** is the reference signal the ensemble RMSF is validated against (p6, Fig 6). (d) **PDB mining for intermediates**: "searching in the PDB for intermediate structures with similar sequences (sequence similarity > 90%) to the target sequences" (p6). (e) The fold-switch case study's two reference folds are deposited structures 8e6y_A and 2f1s_A (Fig 9 p11). **The generator itself receives only the UniProt sequence and its MSA**, with templates off (p11) — that separation is the honest part of this paper and should not be collapsed into `oracle_leakage`. |
| `oracle_leakage` | **PRESENT — routes 4, 5, 6 and 7. Routes 1, 2, 3 are clean.** Enumerated separately below. |
| — route 1: structures as input or template | **NONE FOUND.** Protocol described on p11 (Methods, "MSA generation and randomization" and "OC23"): "Templates were not used in any of the runs." (p11) and "In summary, all predictions were performed on the full protein sequence from UniProt for each protein." (p11). The deposited structures are deliberately *not* used even as the source of the input sequence: "Protein sequences extracted from the experimental open and closed structures could have been directly used to run AFsample2 predictions. However, due to irregularities such as (i) Unequal chain length, (ii) missing residues, and (iii) incomplete information, we chose to retrieve the full protein sequence from Uniprot." (p11) |
| — route 2: state annotations from a curated database | **NONE FOUND.** No GPCRdb, KLIFS or Kincore appears in the paper. MSAs are stock database searches with no state annotation: "The MSAs were produced using the DataPipline implemented in AF2 with default parameters using HHblits and Jackhammer searches on Uniref90, BFD, Uniclust30, and MGnify sequence databases." (p11) |
| — route 3: cluster labels derived from known states | **NONE FOUND for AFsample2's own pipeline.** Masking positions are uniform-random over columns and carry no state information: "the column-based masking is completely unbiased and provides a general solution without the need for any additional information." (p3). The state-selection layer is explicitly reference-free and label-free — it keys off the model's own confidence ranking, not off known states: "the method relies on the tendency of the AF2 inference system to favor a particular state by default." (p7). The AFcluster comparator clusters MSAs, but unsupervised and at default settings (p11). |
| — route 4: hyperparameters / sweep ranges / stopping criteria tuned against known states | **PRESENT — this is the paper's principal leak, and it is exactly the v3 "range tuned on the evaluation set" case.** The masking fraction was chosen by scoring the sweep against the deposited open/closed structures **of the OC23 evaluation targets themselves**: "The aggregate TM-score for the best alternate (open) conformation increases from 0.80 for no masking to 0.88 with 15% masking while showing a marginal improvement from 0.89 to 0.90 for the closed conformation. Beyond 30% masking, performance drops first for the open conformations and subsequently for the closed conformations." (p3) → "Overall, 15% randomization seems to perform marginally better than other settings." (p3) → "For comparative analysis and simplicity, AFsample2 using 15% masking was used for the downstream analysis." (p3). The **sampling budget** is fixed the same way, off curves scored against the same references: "considering the trade-off between speed and performance, 1000 samples at 15% masking is a reasonable default." (p3). The **confidence-screening threshold** of the selection layer is likewise tuned against a reference-defined optimum: "Around 90% and 85% of the highest confidence seems to be the optimal threshold for AFcluster and AFsample2, respectively." (p7), where optimality is MAE against "The best possible selections… the two models from the generated ensemble that are most similar to the two reference states." (p7). The authors are candid that a *single* value is not transferable — "we do stress that for real applications, the amount of masking should be sampled for optimal performance." (p3) — but under v3 that does not clear the leak: the **range and its optimum were established on the evaluation set**. Two passages go further and pick per-target values post hoc against the references: "it can be concluded that these cases would also have been improved with AFsample2 if a larger fraction of MSA masking had been used, see Fig. 2b. For both cases, 50% MSA masking, instead of the 15% used, would have yielded similar or better results compared to MSAsubsample." (p6) and, for the one preferred-state loss, "In this case, a lower fraction of MSA masking (0% or 5%) would have been ideal." (p6). |
| — route 5: success defined post hoc by RMSD/TM to a structure they had | **PRESENT, and it is the definition of every headline number.** "TM-score calculated using TM-align was used as the evaluation metric for comparing similarity to reference states" (p12); success is a threshold on that: "Success: TM-score > threshold (for both states)." (Fig 3c caption, p5; Fig 8c caption, p10). Improvement is a delta on the same quantity: "models generated by AFsample2 demonstrate higher (ΔTM > 0.05) or comparable quality compared to all other methods" (p6). |
| — route 6: best/worst model labels assigned against a held reference | **PRESENT, and it is the paper's default reporting mode.** Every "best open"/"best closed" model in Figs 2a, 2b, 2c, 3a, 3b, 3d, 3e, 8a, 8b, 8d is the member of a 1,000-model ensemble with the highest TM to the corresponding deposited structure — a best-of-1000 selected by the oracle. The paper says so, and names it an upper bound: "The best possible selections are the two models from the generated ensemble that are most similar to the two reference states. This is an upper bound on the quality of the selected models." (p7). **Mitigating**: §"Selecting protein states" (pp6–7, Fig 7) exists precisely to run the reference-free alternative and measure the gap to that oracle (MAE ≈ 0.06 TM units, p7). That arm is the honest one; it is a subsection, not the headline. |
| — route 7: design-level oracle (systems chosen because the answer is known) | **PRESENT, and label it design-level, not pipeline-level.** All 62 targets were admitted only because both of their states are already deposited — OC23 by construction ("selected as defined by a TM-score difference between the open and closed state <0.85", p11), OC>85 by the mirror-image criterion ("A set of 22 proteins with smaller differences between open and closed states, TM ≥ 0.85", p11), TP16 by inheritance from a curated two-state benchmark (p11), and the fold switcher because both folds are solved (Fig 9 p11). The expected answer is therefore declared before any result is read. This is weaker than the route-4/5/6 pipeline leaks above and is the ordinary condition of a retrospective two-state benchmark. |
| `prospective` | **no — retrospective throughout, with one partially prospective sub-result.** Targets are chosen for having deposited answers (route 7), the masking fraction and sampling budget are tuned on the evaluation set (route 4), and success is TM to the held references (routes 5–6). The one arm that is procedurally prospective is the **reference-free state selection** (p7, Fig 7), which consumes no reference at run time — but it is *evaluated* against the references and its own threshold was tuned against them, so it is prospective in mechanism and retrospective in validation. The PDB-intermediate mapping (p6, Fig 5) is likewise a post hoc confirmation, not a prediction registered in advance. |
| `state_metric` | **Triple: RMSD-to-reference (TM-score form) + binary predicate + continuous coordinate.** (i) *TM-to-reference*: "TM-score calculated using TM-align was used as the evaluation metric… TM-score was calculated using a fixed d0 = 3.5Å to avoid the problem of long proteins achieving artificially high TM-score" (p12) — the fixed d0 is stated and justified. (ii) *Binary predicate*: "Success: TM-score > threshold (for both states)" swept over thresholds for the AUC curves, with the quoted headline read at **TM > 0.8** ("AFsample2 generates models of both states with TM-score > 0.8 for 78.3% of the targets", p5); improvement/deterioration is thresholded at **ΔTM > 0.05** ("|Δ|TM < 0.05 in gray, green AFsample2 improved, red AFsample2 worsen", Fig 3d caption p5). Neither 0.8 nor 0.05 is justified anywhere in the paper. (iii) *Continuous coordinate*: the **fill ratio**, a weighted occupancy statistic over 100 bins along the state1→state2 diagonal of the diversity plot, with a parabolic weight w(k) = 1 + 16(k/(bins−1) − 0.5)² that up-weights the two ends, "To ensure that generating alternate conformation remains the highest priority" (p12); models outside quadrant Q1 are discarded (Fig 10 caption, p12). No `visual only` call: even the fold-switch case study is read off TM-score cutoffs (p10). |
| `metric_saturation` | **Yes — the preferred (closed / FS1) state ceilings, and the fill ratio floors.** *Ceiling*: "nearly all methods successfully predict the closed state, with all but three targets achieving TM-scores > 0.8 across all methods" (p4), and the aggregate best-closed TM moves only 0.89 → 0.90 across the entire 0–15% masking sweep (p3). With the preferred state pinned near the top of a bounded [0,1] metric, the closed-state arm has almost no headroom in which either an improvement or a degradation could register — which is precisely the arm the no-degradation claim is made on. The paper acknowledges the mirror problem on TP16, where the bias is absent: "This makes it difficult to achieve significance for the generation of inward- and outward-facing states since the improvement is shared between both states." (p9). *Floor*: fill ratio is exactly 0.00 for many target/method cells (AFvanilla 5 zeros, AFcluster 12 zeros of 23, Fig 4c p7), so the metric bottoms out and cannot rank the failures against each other. **Axis truncation is a figure defect and is recorded in `hides` on rows Fig 2A-left, 2A-right, 2C, 3A, 3D, 7B-E, 8A, 8D — not here.** |
| `directional_control` | **NO. The method samples; it cannot be pointed at a state.** There is no partner, ligand, nanobody, peptide, state-annotated template or state-filtered MSA anywhere in the paper. The only handles are (a) the masking fraction `--msa_rand_fraction`, (b) the number of samples, and (c) the choice among 10 AF2 weight sets and the run seed — **all three are stochastic-intensity knobs, none of them names a state.** The absence of direction is stated by the authors as the *selling point* against SPEACH_AF: "Thus, SPEACH_AF relies on having either experimental structures or that models can be predicted for one of the states. AFsample2 does not have this limitation since the column-based masking is completely unbiased and provides a general solution without the need for any additional information." (p3). Direction, such as it is, enters **only after generation**, as a *selection* rule that identifies "the alternative state" as a high-confidence model far in TM from the top-ranked one (p7) — and even that names the state only as "not the default one", never as *open* or *active*. Note the contrast with the follow-up AFsample2T (`mitjavila2026afsample2t`), which adds a spatial handle by restricting the mask to a residue window; **no such targeting exists here — masking is uniform-random over the whole alignment.** |
| `anti_memorization_design` | **NONE as a set.** No held-out split, no post-cutoff set, and **no AF2 training cutoff date is stated anywhere in the paper.** The closest thing is a single-target case study whose target is asserted to be outside AF2's training data: the engineered temperature-dependent S6 fold switcher (refs 23–24, p9), for which the paper claims "AFsample2 shows that it is possible to utilize the AF2 inference to make predictions for proteins that have not been part of its training set and that AF2 can generalize to new unseen proteins." (p10). **n = 1, and the cutoff is never defined** — the claim of non-membership is asserted, not demonstrated. The three benchmark sets are built from long-deposited PDB entries with no recency filter of any kind. |
| `anti_memorization_control` | **UNPOWERED (n = 1).** A comparison arm *was* actually run on the one putatively unseen target — 1,000 AFsample2 models and 1,000 AFcluster models on the S6 switcher, with both folds scored and the alternate fold recovered at TM > 0.8 but at lower confidence (0.75 vs 0.88) (pp9–10, Fig 9 p11). So this is not `NONE RUN`. But n = 1, no cutoff was defined, no vanilla-AF2 or MSAsubsample arm is shown for this target, and the ~62 benchmark proteins are never re-examined for training-set membership. The generalization sentence on p10 rests entirely on this single case. |

| `confidence_as_discriminator` | **Yes — used, partly validated, and explicitly criticised by the authors themselves.** *Used*: confidence is step (ii) of the reference-free selection ("Confidence screening—for filtering models below a certain threshold", p7), with the alternate state defined as "a high-confidence model that is structurally distinct from the best model" (p7). *Validated*: the threshold is swept and the resulting MAE against the oracle-best selection is plotted (Fig 7b,e p9); for AFsample2 the screen turns out to be nearly unnecessary — "For AFsample2 there is actually almost no need for a threshold, as the selection is equally good even without any threshold (0% of highest)" (p7) — whereas AFcluster needs it. *Criticised*: "as AF2 was trained to estimate high-quality models for a single conformation, the confidence metric might not be a reliable indicator while looking for alternative states as the AF2 inference system is bound to rank these structures lower. This was also observed in the OC23 set, where the open conformation was consistently less favorable in terms of model confidence" (p10); the alternate fold in the case study scores 0.75 against 0.88 for the preferred fold (p10). Their conclusion is to demote confidence from *discriminator* to *filter*: "above a minimum confidence score, the similarity to the highest confidence state would serve as a better indicator of model diversity." (p10). Separately, confidence is shown to degrade monotonically with masking — "a 2% drop in confidence for every 5 percentage points of masking" (p3) — and the authors warn it is not comparable across masking levels: "model confidences from different MSA masking levels are not directly comparable." (p6) |

## D. Claims

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| `central_conclusion` | Replacing a random 15% of MSA *columns* with "X" — full alignment depth retained, dropout on, templates off, 1,000 models per target, inside AlphaFold2 v2.3.1 — improves the alternate-state model over vanilla AF2 on 9/23 open-closed proteins and 11/16 transporters (ΔTM > 0.05), lifting the aggregate best-alternate TM from 0.80 to 0.88 while leaving the preferred state essentially untouched (0.89 → 0.90 aggregate; a single target loses > 0.05). It also fills the space between the end states, generating ~70% more distinct intermediate conformations than vanilla AF2, four targets' worth of which match deposited intermediates. Separately, a reference-free selection rule (confidence screen + maximal TM-distance from the top-ranked model) recovers both states to within ~0.06 TM units of the oracle-best pick, on AFsample2 and AFcluster ensembles alike. The method carries **no directional handle**: it broadens sampling, it cannot be aimed. |

| `stance` | **`precedent` on findings + `contrast` on rigour. Provisional — the user's call.** *Precedent*: this is the origin paper for MSA column masking as an integrated AF2 intervention and the direct ancestor of both `kalakoti2026afsample3` (same masking idea moved to AF3) and `mitjavila2026afsample2t` (same masking idea restricted to a binding-site window). It supplies clean, budget-matched head-to-head numbers against five alternative methods on two benchmarks and a genuinely negative result for informed masking (SPEACH_AF) — the most reusable comparator table in this part of the corpus. It is also honest where it matters most: it runs the reference-free selection arm and measures the gap to the oracle instead of hiding it. *Contrast*: the masking fraction, the sampling budget and the selection threshold are all tuned on the evaluation set against deposited references (route 4); every headline number is a best-of-1000 chosen by TM to a held reference (routes 5–6); the target sets exist because both answers are deposited (route 7); there is no held-out or post-cutoff set and no stated AF2 cutoff; and the method offers no way to ask for a *named* state — only for more diversity. |

### Tag notes

- **`msa-subsample` is applied under protest and must not be read as the method class.** AFsample2 masks a random fraction of MSA **columns** with "X"; the alignment keeps every sequence and its full depth — "the user-defined parameter –msa_rand_fraction determines what fraction of MSA columns that should be randomly replaced with 'X'" (p11). Subsampling reduces *depth* and the paper implements it separately, by setting `max_extra_msa` and `max_msa_clusters` (pp11–12); state filtering substitutes a state-specific alignment and nothing here does that. The paper's own Fig 1a (p2) exists to draw exactly this row-vs-column distinction, and the two interventions behave differently in its results (fill ratio: AFsample2 vs MSAsubsample is *not* significant, p < 0.51; alternate-state TM: AFsample2 beats MSAsubsample at p < 0.01, pp4, 6). The tag is nonetheless applied for a second, independent reason: **MSAsubsample is implemented and benchmarked here as a full comparator arm at a matched 1,000-model budget**, so a reverse lookup for MSA-subsampling comparator numbers should legitimately return this paper. Read the tag as "MSA manipulated at inference / contains MSA-subsampling results", never as "this method reduces MSA depth". The missing `msa-column-mask` tag is logged under `unresolved` item 1 and was **not invented**.
- **`enhanced-sampling`** is the least-wrong available tag for what the method actually is: an inference-time noise injection that broadens the sampled ensemble at a fixed architecture. It is also the family the paper places itself in — the antecedent AFsample raised sampling and enabled dropout (p1), and AFsample2 adds MSA noise on top of dropout (p3).
- **`af-cluster`** is applied for the comparator-arm reason only: AFcluster is run at default settings on every benchmark (p11) and its ensembles are the second test bed for the reference-free selection layer (pp7–8, Fig 7 p9). AFsample2 does not cluster MSAs. See `unresolved` item 12 on whether comparator arms should earn method tags at all.
- **`ensemble` + `two-state`** together, per the v3 rule that state tags may be combined. The generator emits a 1,000-model ensemble that populates the space between the end states (fill ratio, 15/23 targets with both states plus intermediates, p6); the reporting and the selection layer are two-state. Not `continuum` — median diagonal occupancy is well below half (Fig 4c p7). Not `single-state` — the paper's whole point is that vanilla AF2 collapses and AFsample2 does not.
- **`continuous-metric` + `binary-predicate` + `saturating-metric`.** Continuous: TM-score to each reference, and the fill ratio ∈ [0,1] (p12). Binary: "Success: TM-score > threshold (for both states)" with the headline read at TM > 0.8 (p5), and ΔTM > 0.05 for improvement (Fig 3d p5) — neither threshold is justified. Saturating: the preferred state sits at TM ≈ 0.89–0.90 across the whole sweep with essentially no headroom, and fill ratio floors at exactly 0.00 for many target/method cells. **`rmsd-only` deliberately not applied** — see `unresolved` item 11; the metric is TM not RMSD, and a genuine binary predicate is also used.
- **`oracle-leak` + `design-level-oracle` are both applied and are distinct here.** `oracle-leak` covers routes 4, 5 and 6: the masking fraction, the 1,000-sample budget and the confidence threshold were all fixed by scoring against the deposited structures of the evaluation targets (p3, p7), success is TM to those structures, and every headline "best" model is an oracle-selected best-of-1000. `design-level-oracle` covers route 7 separately: all 62 targets were admitted *because* both states are deposited (p11). Keep them apart — the second is the ordinary condition of a retrospective benchmark, the first is a tuning dependency that will not transfer.
- **No `prospective` tag.** The reference-free selection arm (p7) is the only procedurally prospective component and its own threshold was tuned against the references, so it does not qualify the paper.
- **`no-anti-memorization` + `unpowered`.** There is no held-out set, no post-cutoff set and no stated AF2 cutoff. The single fold-switch case study (p9–10, Fig 9 p11) is the only generalization evidence and it is n = 1 with the training-set exclusion asserted rather than shown, hence `unpowered` alongside rather than `anti-memorization`.
- **`seed-only` stands for "stochastic-only, no directional handle".** The available knobs — masking fraction, sample count, and the choice among 10 weight sets — set the *intensity* of the noise, never its direction. There is no partner, ligand, nanobody, peptide, state-annotated template or state-filtered MSA anywhere in this paper, so no `directed-state`, `partner-driven`, `ligand-driven`, `peptide-driven`, `g-protein-mimetic`, `nanobody` or `apo-sampling` tag applies. This is the sharpest single contrast with the corpus's state-directing methods, and with this paper's own descendant `mitjavila2026afsample2t`, which adds a *spatial* handle (a residue window) though still not a state handle.
- **No Site tag.** No pocket, ligand or allosteric-site analysis is performed anywhere in the paper; the unit of analysis is always the global fold. Not `allosteric-failure` either — no allosteric site was ever targeted, so nothing failed.
- **No Protocol tag** — templates off plus a full-depth MSA has no value in the v3 Protocol group (`unresolved` item 2).
