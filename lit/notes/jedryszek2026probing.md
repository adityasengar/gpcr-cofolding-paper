## B. Scope

- **system**: **General protein.** No protein family is targeted. The corpora are generic: 84,074 unlabeled proteins for SAE training (21.9M residues) and a fixed 486-protein evaluation set drawn from SwissProt / AlphaFold DB (p.2). One named individual protein appears in the whole paper — held-out **A6NI15**, used as the single steering illustration (Fig. 2, p.4). No GPCR, kinase, transporter or fold-switching system is studied.
- **n_targets**:
  - SAE training: **84,074 proteins / 21.9M residues** (p.2).
  - Evaluation set: **486 proteins**, with three overlapping label sources — sparse SwissProt (99 proteins, 44,968 residues), dense DSSP from AlphaFold DB structures (393 proteins, 154,299 residues), dense DSSP from Boltz-1's own predictions (486 proteins, 196,136 residues) (p.2; Table S3, p.17).
  - Steering: **389 training / 97 held-out** proteins (p.2, p.9). The pLDDT-stratified analysis splits the 97 into low-pLDDT n=51 and high-pLDDT n=46 (Fig. S15, p.16). The ablation arm runs on **n=24** only (Fig. S13, p.15) — an order of magnitude smaller than the additive arm and not flagged as such in the main text.
  - Sequence-identity leakage control: 485 evaluation proteins, ~117,000 train–test pairs (p.9).
- **method_class**: **other — mechanistic interpretability (linear probes + sparse autoencoders) plus inference-time causal steering of an internal representation.** No structure-prediction method is proposed, no benchmark of predictors is run. Verbatim framing, p.1 abstract: "We analyze per-residue activations from the Pairformer trunk and diffusion module of Boltz-1 using linear probes, sparse autoencoders (SAEs), and causal interventions."

Whether Boltz-1 ran with templates on or off cannot be determined from the PDF. Because the paper's object is the activations rather than accuracy, the omission is not called out by the authors.
- **msa_handling**: **full, precomputed, third-party.** "We run Boltz-1 with precomputed MSAs from the OpenFold set [2]" (p.2). Never subsampled, never clustered, never state-filtered; MSA depth is not a variable anywhere in the study.

## C. Conformational core

**Framing note for the whole section.** This is an interpretability study of a predictor's internal representations, not a conformational-state method. The manipulated and measured quantity is **secondary-structure content (DSSP helix / strand / coil fraction) of a single predicted structure**, not a conformational state of a target. Fields that presuppose a two-state or ensemble reading are answered on their own terms and marked NOT APPLICABLE with a reason rather than forced.

- **states_generated**: **NOT APPLICABLE — no conformational states are generated or sought.** What is produced is one Boltz-1 structure per (protein × intervention condition × dose): baseline, concept-add, matched-norm random-add, and ablation, at k ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8, 16}, all denoised "from identical resampled noise" (p.9). There is no attempt to produce two states of one protein, no ensemble, no continuum. The nearest thing to a two-state result is a single held-out protein driven both ways along one axis (A6NI15: baseline helix 0.528 / coil 0.472 → helix-steered 0.705 / 0.295 → coil-steered 0.264 / 0.736; Fig. 2 right, p.4) — that is one protein's secondary-structure composition moved, not two conformational basins.

- **structural_priors_used**: **Deposited and predicted structures are the label source, and this is a labelling choice, not a defect.**
  - **Dense DSSP labels computed from AlphaFold DB structures** (393 proteins) are the primary secondary-structure ground truth (p.2; Table S3, p.17).
  - **Dense DSSP labels computed from Boltz-1's own predicted structures** (486 proteins) are the self-consistency label source (p.2, p.8).
  - **SwissProt curated annotations** (99 proteins) supply secondary structure, signal peptide, disulfide bond, glycosylation, compositional bias, binding site and modified-residue labels (p.2; Table S3, p.17).
  - The steering directions themselves are fit against DSSP labels on 389 proteins (p.9), i.e. the intervention vector is derived from structural knowledge by construction — that is the method, and the paper is explicit about it.
  - The steering readout is DSSP recomputed on the newly generated structure, so no deposited reference of a target state is needed to score it.

- **oracle_leakage**: **No pipeline leakage of the classic kind — there is no target state to leak, and the readout is self-referential (DSSP on the generated structure). The real exposures are selection-against-the-evaluation-set at two points (route 4). Enumerated route by route.**

  **Route 1 — deposited structures used as input or template: NONE FOUND (as leakage).** Structures enter only as *labels*, never as model input. Protocol, p.2: "We run Boltz-1 with precomputed MSAs from the OpenFold set [2]. From the Pairformer trunk (48 layers, recycle 1), we extract per-residue activations at every second layer (layers 0–47)." The steering intervention is on activations, not inputs: "we steer a unit direction u ... into the final trunk single representation, s, which conditions the diffusion module; the pair representation, z, is not modified" (p.2). Whether Boltz-1 itself received templates is NOT REPORTED (see `templates`), which leaves this route not fully checkable from the PDF.

  **Route 2 — state annotations from a curated database (GPCRdb / KLIFS / Kincore) driving templates or alignments: NONE FOUND.** No conformational-state database is used anywhere. The only curated database is SwissProt, and it supplies *evaluation labels*, not inputs: "Evaluation uses a fixed 486-protein set with three overlapping label sources: sparse SwissProt annotations (99 proteins) ... dense DSSP labels ... and dense DSSP labels computed from Boltz-1's own predicted structures" (p.2).

  **Route 3 — cluster labels derived from known states: NONE FOUND.** No clustering of any kind drives an input or a split. Splits are by protein, and stratified by helix content, not by any state label: "The train/held-out split is stratified by helix content and seeded, and is fixed before any steering" (p.9).

  **Route 4 — hyperparameters, sweep ranges, seeds or stopping criteria tuned against known labels: PRESENT at two points, both disclosed by the authors.**
  1. *Concept selection filtered by evaluation-set score.* Verbatim, p.8: "Main single-feature comparisons are restricted to informative concepts whose held-out probe-raw F1 clears the corresponding null by more than 0.05 in at least one stack." The set of concepts compared is therefore chosen using scores computed on the same evaluation set the comparison is reported on. This is a range/threshold set against the evaluation data even though no per-concept value is tuned.
  2. *Best-of-many SAE latent selection.* The SAE steering direction is "the decoder column of the single highest-F1 latent (helix latent F1 0.72, coil 0.71, strand 0.47)" (p.9), and single-feature readouts are explicitly in-sample: "Because SAE-1feat and neuron-1feat are in-sample best-of-many statistics, they can exceed held-out probes on rare concepts through winner's-curse effects. We therefore use them only as feature-alignment measures." (p.8). The authors diagnose this rather than hide it (Fig. S8, p.13) and downgrade the affected numbers accordingly.
  3. *Mitigating counter-evidence.* Probe hyperparameters are fixed, not searched: "Logistic regression uses fixed inverse regularization C = 1.0, the lbfgs solver, a maximum of 2000 iterations, and balanced class weights. We do not perform hyperparameter search." (p.8). The steering dose grid k ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8, 16} is fixed and reported in full (p.9). One earlier run on a subset is disclosed rather than dropped: "an earlier 40-protein run used an ID-ordered, pre-steering subset of the same 97 and gave the same qualitative pattern with smaller magnitudes, driven by fewer low-pLDDT proteins" (p.9) — a prior look at a subset of the same evaluation proteins, reported transparently, with no parameter carried forward.

  **Route 5 — success defined post hoc by RMSD or TM to a structure they had: NONE FOUND, structurally impossible here.** Steering success is measured as the change in DSSP fraction of the *newly generated* structure relative to a matched-norm random direction on the same protein and the same noise: "Effects are reported paired per protein as (concept-add − random-add) on the concept's own DSSP fraction, with 95% paired-protein bootstrap CIs (B=10,000)" (p.9). No reference structure of a target state is compared against, and no RMSD or TM-score appears in the paper.

  **Route 6 — best/worst model labels assigned against a held reference: NONE FOUND.** No per-protein model selection occurs. Every held-out protein contributes one paired difference; nothing is picked as best. Protocol, p.9: "Steering directions are fit only on the 389-protein training split and applied to all 97 held-out proteins over the full dense-DSSP helix gradient (range 0.06–0.95)." The one displayed protein (A6NI15, Fig. 2, p.4) is an illustration on top of a reported population effect, not a selected result standing in for one.

  **Route 7 — design-level oracle (systems or conditions chosen because the expected answer is already known): NONE FOUND, and the paper's headline result is the reason.** The three steering concepts were chosen for being decodable, but the decisive case is the one where the expected answer was *not* declared in advance and came back negative: a strand probe at F1 0.82 with precision ≥ 0.93 produced a null steering effect (−0.001 [−0.003, +0.001], z = +0.3; Table S2, p.17). The mechanistic account of that null is explicitly labelled as a hypothesis formed afterwards: "We hypothesize that β-strand pairing is a property of residue pairs" (p.3) and "A possible architectural explanation is ... Direct interventions on the pair representation are needed to test this hypothesis." (p.5). The held-out split was fixed before steering (p.9). Recorded as design-level oracle **absent**, and the concept-selection filter is recorded under route 4 where it belongs.

- **prospective**: **partial.** Prospective within the steering experiment — the 97-protein split is fixed before any intervention and is outcome-independent ("The train/held-out split is stratified by helix content and seeded, and is fixed before any steering, so the held-out evaluation set is outcome-independent", p.9), directions are fit only on the 389 training proteins, and the strand null was not the anticipated outcome. Retrospective in the decoding half, where the concept set compared is filtered by scores on the same evaluation set (p.8, route 4 above), and in the SAE-latent selection, which is in-sample best-of-many (p.8).

- **state_metric**: **DUAL — continuous coordinate + binary predicate.**
  - *Continuous:* the DSSP fraction of the steered state, "the share of residues DSSP assigns to it in the new structure" (p.3), reported as a paired per-protein difference against a matched-norm random direction with 95% bootstrap CIs (p.9). Decodability is likewise continuous, F1 on a 0–1 scale, "where 1.00 is perfect, and permutation nulls sit near zero" (p.3).
  - *Binary:* Table S2 (p.17) collapses each concept to a **Verdict** column, "steers" or "null", adjudicated by whether the paired CI excludes zero and the z-score against a 19-direction matched-norm random null clears the null. The z thresholds are never stated numerically; the verdicts run +11.5, +6.2, +5.0 → "steers" and +0.3, +1.0, −0.1 → "null", so the implicit cut sits somewhere in 1.0 < z < 5.0 and is **NOT REPORTED**.
  - F1 itself is a thresholded metric with an unusual definition worth carrying: "Precision is computed per residue. Recall is computed per domain: a positive domain is counted as recalled if at least one positive prediction overlaps it." (p.8), and probe F1 is "the best F1 over a fixed set of percentile thresholds applied to the pooled out-of-fold scores. Thus, the probe metric should be interpreted as a held-out ranking/decodability score rather than as the F1 of a calibrated classifier at threshold 0.5." (p.8).

- **metric_saturation**: **Yes — the positive control ceilings, by design, and one reported number is a hard 1.00.** Amino-acid identity, the calibration control, reaches "F1 ≈ 1.0 across all trunk depths (0.996 at output)" (Fig. S2 caption, p.10), and Table S1 (p.17) prints it as **1.00** at trunk L47. That is the top of the F1 scale, and it is intended: "Amino-acid identity, our positive control, reaches 0.996 at the trunk output, confirming that labels and activations are aligned" (p.3). No conformational metric saturates, because none is used. The one *floor* is the null: permutation nulls "sit near zero" (p.3), and the strand steering effect sits on it. Note separately that the same F1 numbers are *depressed* rather than saturated when scored against sparse labels (helix 0.85 → 0.42, strand 0.83 → 0.33; Fig. S1, p.10), which the authors argue is a labelling artefact and which makes all SwissProt-scored F1 in this paper a lower bound, stated verbatim in the abstract (p.1): "such scores are therefore lower bounds."

- **directional_control**: **PARTIAL, and the partition is the paper's result. Directed control along one axis (helix ↔ coil); no control at all over strand, where the same protocol yields an off-target perturbation instead.**
  - *The handle:* a unit vector added to the final Pairformer trunk single representation s at layer 47, either a supervised logistic-probe direction or an SAE decoder column, at additive dose x′ = x + k·mean|(x − µ)·u|·u, k ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8, 16} (p.9). Not MSA, not template, not seed, not partner.
  - *Directed, for helix and coil:* the effect is signed, dose-dependent, above a matched-norm random null, and bidirectional on the same protein. "Both the helix and the coil directions raise their own state, dose-dependently and against a null of 19 matched-norm random directions, while mean pLDDT stays stable at ∼79" (p.3), and "Driving one held-out protein each way swaps its helix and coil content" (p.3). Fig. S14 (p.16) gives the specificity matrix: helix (probe) → Δhelix +0.033 / Δstrand −0.000 / Δcoil −0.033; coil (probe) → Δhelix −0.065 / Δstrand −0.000 / Δcoil +0.066. Caption verbatim, p.16: "Helix and coil occupy the anti-diagonal—steering one raises it and lowers the other—while the strand column is ≈ 0 throughout (strand is neither induced nor a byproduct)."
  - *Perturbation only, for strand:* "adding a highly predictive strand probe direction (F1 0.82, precision ≥ 0.93) to the single representation produces no detectable change in strand content; instead, it converts helix into coil." (p.3). Fig. S14 (p.16) quantifies the off-target conversion: strand (probe) → Δhelix −0.022 / Δstrand +0.001 / Δcoil +0.021. The intervention does something to the structure; what it does is not what it was aimed at.
  - *Sufficient but not necessary, even where it works:* "removing the direction from the trunk, from the diffusion module, or from both fails to reduce the concept's content (Appendix Fig. S13), so the trunk signal is sufficient for decodability but not necessary for generation" (p.3); Fig. S13 (p.15) numbers: trunk-only +0.0031, diffusion-only −0.0007, both +0.0010, all n=24.
  - *Scope caveat the authors impose on themselves*, p.5: "causal conclusions should be restricted to the intervention site and intervention family that were tested."
  - *Magnitudes are small in absolute terms:* the largest population effect is a **+0.061 DSSP coil fraction** (Table S2, p.17), i.e. roughly six percentage points of residues, at the top dose.

- **anti_memorization_design**: **NONE, with respect to the model under study.** There is no post-cutoff set, no training-date cutoff, and no statement anywhere about whether the 486 evaluation proteins are in Boltz-1's training data. The word "cutoff" does not appear. What does exist is a **probe-level** held-out design, which is a different thing: grouped five-fold cross-validation with proteins as groups, "so residues from the same protein never appear in both train and test folds" (p.8), and a fixed 389/97 protein split for steering (p.9). Those guard against probe overfitting, not against the predictor having memorised the proteins.
- **anti_memorization_control**: **NONE RUN against Boltz-1's training set.** No arm compares proteins Boltz-1 could have seen against proteins it could not. The one leakage control that *was* run is again probe-level, and it is well done and worth recording: all-vs-all BLOSUM62 global alignment between training-fold and held-out proteins, reported verbatim, p.9: "Across the 485 evaluation proteins, only 3.3% of held-out test proteins had any training-fold neighbor above this threshold, and only 9 of approximately 117,000 train–test protein pairs exceeded it. Thus, train–test sequence-identity leakage across folds is negligible and cannot plausibly explain the observed probe-raw performance." The authors also argue the module comparison is immune by symmetry: "Because both modules are evaluated on the same proteins with the same fold assignments, any remaining inflation would affect the Pairformer and diffusion readouts equally and therefore cannot explain the module-specific attenuation of sequence-chemical information." (p.9). They still list the residual as a limitation (p.6): "Probe folds are grouped by protein but not clustered by sequence identity, though we find train–test sequence-identity leakage negligible (Appendix A)."

- **confidence_as_discriminator**: **No — pLDDT is used as a sanity check and as a stratifier, never as a discriminator of correctness, and its use is not validated.** As a distribution check: "mean pLDDT is monitored as a check that the representations stay within the model's learned distribution of protein representations (stable ∼79 at k=16)" (p.9). As a stratifier, and this is a real finding rather than a control: "Effects are several-fold larger in low-confidence structures, suggesting that the conditioning matters most where the fold is not yet settled" (p.3), quantified as coil +0.094 (low pLDDT, n=51) vs +0.025 (high, n=46) and helix +0.062 vs +0.013, with per-protein effect correlating with pLDDT at r ≈ −0.57 for coil (Fig. S15, p.16). No arm asks whether pLDDT predicts whether a steered structure is *right*; there is no correctness reference to ask it against.

## D. Claims

- **central_conclusion**: Across Boltz-1's trunk–diffusion boundary, geometric information (secondary structure, disorder) survives essentially unchanged into the diffusion module while sequence-chemical information (amino-acid identity, signal peptides, disulfide bonds) is strongly attenuated. Steering the final trunk single representation along supervised probe directions moves helix and coil content dose-dependently against matched-norm random controls, but an equally decodable strand direction moves strand content not at all — so linear decodability and causal controllability are distinct properties of the same representation. Two secondary conclusions: F1 scored against sparse SwissProt annotations is a lower bound, because unlabelled residues the model gets right count as false positives; and supervised probes beat single SAE latents both for decoding and as steering directions wherever a label already exists.

- **necessity_claims** (verbatim, with page):
  - "linear decodability does not imply causal influence at the site we tested" (p.1, abstract).
  - "Linear decodability therefore does not imply causal use at this site." (p.3).
  - "We hypothesize that β-strand pairing is a property of residue pairs, which are carried in z, but in Boltz-1 information flows z → s but not the reverse, so steering s cannot reach it." (p.3) — the impossibility claim the strand null rests on, and stated as a hypothesis, not a demonstration.
  - "so the trunk signal is sufficient for decodability but not necessary for generation" (p.3).
  - "The geometry is redundantly reconstructed: for helix and coil the trunk single-representation direction is sufficient to shift the prediction but not necessary for it." (Fig. S13 caption, p.15).
  - "Direct interventions on the pair representation are needed to test this hypothesis." (p.5).
  - "Causal interventions and activation patching are required to test which representations are actively used by the model." (p.8).
  - "The readouts measure decodability and feature alignment, not causal use: a concept can be linearly decodable without being necessary for prediction, and a high-scoring SAE latent need not be a faithful causal steering direction." (p.8).
  - "claims about represented information should specify the readout class and avoid treating linear decodability as direct evidence of causal use. Second, causal conclusions should be restricted to the intervention site and intervention family that were tested. Third, evaluation against sparse biological annotations should include controls for missing labels whenever possible." (p.5).
  - "such claims require independent validation because best-of-many feature selection can inflate scores for rare concepts." (p.5).
  - "Thus, train–test sequence-identity leakage across folds is negligible and cannot plausibly explain the observed probe-raw performance." (p.9).

- **novelty_claims** (verbatim, with page):
  - "No prior work has systematically contrasted the trunk/diffusion boundary itself, nor combined decodability in structure prediction models with causal steering to test whether decodable signal is actually used." (p.2) — the paper's sole first-ness claim, and it is a conjunction claim, not a claim to have invented either half.
  - "We make four contributions." (p.2), enumerated on the same page: the trunk/diffusion representational split; the decodability-vs-steerability dissociation; the sparse-annotation false-positive inflation; and probes outperforming single SAE features.
  - "Zarzecki et al. [9] steer a generative structure model with SAE features, whereas our results suggest supervised probes are more reliable steering directions." (p.2) — a priority-adjacent contrast with the nearest prior steering work.
  - Concurrency is conceded rather than contested, p.2: "Concurrently with our work, Kim et al. [5] scale SAEs to the diffusion modules of Boltz-2 and AlphaFold3 and likewise find a geometric rather than biochemical focus, consistent with what we report here for Boltz-1's diffusion module".
  - Generalisation is asserted without evidence, p.2: "We expect these cautions to generalize beyond this study."

- **stated_limits** (authors' own, p.6 §4.1 unless noted):
  - "This study analyzes one AF3-class model, one main SAE recipe, and a limited set of residue-level labels."
  - "Steering is applied only to the secondary-structure axis, so causal use of the sequence-chemistry concepts the trunk uniquely retains remains to be tested."
  - "Probe folds are grouped by protein but not clustered by sequence identity, though we find train–test sequence-identity leakage negligible (Appendix A)."
  - "There is also scope to explore the pair representation z, especially in the context of follow-up experiments on steering of strand secondary structure and other features such as disulfide bridges."
  - p.8 (A.4): "Our train/test folds are grouped by protein but are not clustered by sequence identity. Near-identical homologs could therefore appear in different folds. This may inflate absolute probe F1 values".
  - p.8 (A.4): "The readouts measure decodability and feature alignment, not causal use".
  - p.8 (A.1): "Concepts with fewer than five positive residues are skipped. ... Rare labels are otherwise handled only through balanced class weights; we do not upsample positives or use stratified protein clustering."
  - p.5: SAE value is downgraded to hypothesis generation — "SAEs are most useful for generating hypotheses about unlabeled features, but such claims require independent validation".
  - Not listed by the authors, recorded here: **no anti-memorization control against Boltz-1's own training data**, and **the ablation arm runs on n=24 while the additive arm runs on n=97** (Fig. S13, p.15) without the discrepancy being discussed.

- **stance**: **`precedent` on method + `contrast` on control — provisional, the user's call.**
  - *Precedent:* this is a worked, heavily controlled instance of `latent-steering` on an AF3-class predictor — intervention site named (trunk single representation s at layer 47), dose grid, matched-norm and 19-direction random nulls, paired bootstrap CIs, sufficiency and necessity arms, and a released artefact set.
