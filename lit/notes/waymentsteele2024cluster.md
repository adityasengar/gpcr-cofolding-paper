## B. Scope

- **system**: general protein. Specifically metamorphic / fold-switching soluble proteins, plus one screen over small single-domain families. No membrane proteins, GPCRs, kinases or transporters are studied (transporters appear only as prior work, ref. 14, p1).
- **n_targets**: heterogeneous; count depends on the arm.
  - Deep worked example: **KaiB** — KaiB<sub>TE</sub> (T. elongatus), KaiB<sub>TV-4</sub> (T. elongatus vestitus), KaiB<sub>RS</sub> (R. sphaeroides) + KaiB<sub>RS</sub>-3m mutant (pp3–4).
  - KaiB family survey: **487 variants** in a curated phylogenetic tree, one prediction each (p3).
  - Additional fold-switchers tested: **5** — RfaH, MAD2, selecase, lymphotactin, CLIC1 (p5). Two succeeded (RfaH, MAD2), three failed (p5).
  - Engineered point-mutation benchmark: **12 sets** of G<sub>A</sub>/G<sub>B</sub> point mutants (p7, p11, p23).
  - Novel-state screen: **628 protein families** (52–150 residues, >1,000 sequences in MSA) from ref. 30 (p5, p10); one candidate followed up (Mpt53), plus **10** Mpt53 DALI structure homologues (p6) and **1** negative control (ubiquitin, PDB 1UBQ, p6).
  - Flag: general-methodology claims ("many more uncharacterized functional states", "1–8 million fold-switching proteins", p7) rest on 2/5 successful additional fold-switchers plus 1 unvalidated screen hit.
- **method_class**: MSA-clustering (the paper's own framing is MSA-subsampling): "We demonstrate that a simple MSA subsampling method—clustering sequences by sequence similarity—enables AF2 to predict both states of the metamorphic proteins KaiB, RfaH and MAD2." (p3). Pipeline definition: "Hereafter, we refer to this entire pipeline as AF-Cluster—generating a MSA with ColabFold, clustering MSA sequences with DBSCAN and running AF2 predictions for each cluster." (p3)
- **backbones**: **AF2 only**, run through ColabFold (p3, p9). MSA Transformer (ref. 36) is used as a second, unsupervised model but only for contact-map analysis, not structure prediction: "MSA transformer is an unsupervised learning method, which signifies that its contact predictions purely reflect evolutionary couplings learned in sequences, rather than being supervised on structure as is the case AF2." (p9). Not a multi-backbone structure comparison — do **not** tag `multi-backbone`.
- **templates**: **NOT REPORTED.** Templates are never mentioned in the Methods (pp9–11) or anywhere else; the word "template" appears only in reference titles and in a molecular-biology sense ("using WT KaiBRS as a template", p9). ColabFold default (templates off) is neither stated nor confirmed.
- **msa_handling**: **clustered**, with several comparison arms. Clustering is by edit distance with DBSCAN on a ColabFold/MMseqs2 MSA: "We therefore clustered the MSA by edit distance using DBSCAN35, and ran AF2 predictions using these clusters as the input" (p3); pre-filter "Before clustering, we removed sequences from the MSA containing more than 25% gaps." (p9). Arms compared: full/complete MSA (p3, p5); **uniform subsampling** at |MSA| = 10 and 100, 500 samples each (p3, p9) — note this is depth reduction and is reported as a *distinct and weaker* arm, not the method; closest-*N*-by-edit-distance MSAs (50, 100 sequences, p3; closest 10 for the phylogenetic tree, p3); single-sequence / no-MSA mode (p4, p6, p7); and wild-type G<sub>A</sub>/G<sub>B</sub> MSAs as a hand-supplied alternative (p11, p23). No state-filtered alignment is built — nothing substitutes a state-specific MSA, so `msa-state-filter` does **not** apply.

## C. Conformational core

- **states_generated**: **ensemble, interpreted as two.** Per target the method emits one model per MSA cluster, i.e. a distribution: "we found that the AF2 predictions from our MSA clusters comprised a distribution of structures, with the highest-scored regions of the distribution corresponding to the ground and FS state" (p3). What is reported and claimed, however, is two discrete states per target (Figs 1e, 4b,d, 5c–e). The distribution is explicitly **not** a Boltzmann ensemble — see `stated_limits`. For the three oligomeric fold-switchers only one state was produced: "AF-Cluster was unable to predict the oligomeric state for selecase, lymphotactin and CLIC1." (p5)

- **oracle_leakage**: **Routes enumerated separately below. Two are PRESENT and load-bearing (scoring/selection); the input-side routes are NONE FOUND.**

  **Route 1 — cluster labels derived from known states: NONE FOUND.**
  Clustering uses only sequence edit distance; no structural or state information enters it. "We therefore clustered the MSA by edit distance using DBSCAN35, and ran AF2 predictions using these clusters as the input (Fig. 1d)." (p3). Methods, "Clustering": "In brief, DBSCAN35 clusters datapoints by identifying core density regions in which at least k points fall within distance epsilon from one another. Points farther than epsilon from points in core density regions are excluded as noise." (p9). Cluster *state* labels ("FS-state-predicting clusters" / "ground-state-predicting clusters", Fig. 3a, p5) are assigned **after** AF2 prediction, from the predicted models, not from deposited structures — but the model→state assignment itself is by RMSD to the deposited structures (Route 3).
  Protocol pages for checkability: p3 (main text), p9 (Methods, "MSA generation" and "Clustering").

  **Route 2 — structures or state annotations used as input, templates, or to build the alignment: NONE FOUND.**
  MSAs come from an unmodified ColabFold/MMseqs2 UniRef search (p9); no template input, no curated state database (no GPCRdb/KLIFS analogue), no ablation of known-state contacts, no supplementation with known alternative-state sequences. The paper positions itself explicitly against those approaches: "Methods proposed to deconvolve sets of states when previous knowledge about one or more states is known include ablating residues corresponding to contacts of a known dominant state18 and supplementing the original MSA with proteins that are known to occupy a rarer state19." (p1), and states its own aim as "if we could deconvolve sets of evolutionary couplings without adding previous knowledge and input these sets separately into AF2" (p3).
  Protocol pages: p9 ("MSA generation", "Clustering"). Caveat: the screening set is drawn from "a database of MSAs associated with crystal structures30" (p3, p10) — the association is to *one* reference structure per family, not to state annotations.

  **Route 3 — success defined post hoc by RMSD/TM-score to a structure they already had: PRESENT, and this is the primary success criterion throughout.**
  Most revealing quote: *"Figure 1e shows the top five models within 3 Å of crystal structures for each state, ranked by plDDT."* (p3)
  Same route, other instances:
  - Fig. 1f caption: "The r.m.s.d. of AF2 structure predictions for all clusters relative to the ground and FS state. The highest-confidence regions of the AF-Cluster distribution for KaiBTE are within 3 Å r.m.s.d. of crystal structures of both the ground and FS state." (p2). The two references are PDB 2QKE (ground) and 5JYT (FS) (p3).
  - Comparator arms scored the same way: "We found that, for uniformly subsampled MSAs of size 10, 1 out of 500 samples was within 3 Å of the ground state" and "Uniformly subsampled MSAs of size 100 did not sample the ground state at all." (p3)
  - Extended Data Fig. 2e caption: "The plDDT values of models within 3 Å RMSD of the ground and FS state from the clustered sampling method are statistically significantly higher than the rest of the models." (p14)
  - The novel-state screen selects candidates by RMSD to the deposited reference: "and compared the plDDT to the r.m.s.d. from the reference structure" (p6); Fig. 5b caption: "Candidates for further sampling were selected by looking for outlier predictions with a high r.m.s.d. to the reference structure and high plDDT." (p7)
  - Mpt53 state assignment: "The crystal structure of the reduced state of M. tuberculosis Mpt53 (PDB: 1LU4), which corresponds to state 1 in the sampled landscape" (p7); Extended Data Fig. 7a plots "plDDT vs. RMSD for AF-Cluster sampling on oxidoreductase Mpt53" (p20).
  - G<sub>A</sub>/G<sub>B</sub> benchmark uses TM-score to the two known folds: "The TM-scores of resulting models and their pLDDTs are plotted in Extended Data Fig. 10c." (p11); scoring is "Predictions for which the highest plDDT is incorrect are marked with an X" (p23), i.e. correctness is defined against the known most-stable fold.
  - Mpt53 homologue follow-up: "We used AF-Cluster to test ten proteins with the lowest alignment-weighted r.m.s.d. from DALI to the original state" (p6) — the ten follow-up targets were chosen by structural distance to a deposited structure.
  No justification is given anywhere for the 3 Å threshold.

  **Route 4 — best/worst model labels assigned against a held reference: PRESENT.**
  Most revealing quote: *"Figure 1e shows the top five models within 3 Å of crystal structures for each state, ranked by plDDT."* (p3) — the displayed "top five" per state are the subset already filtered to be near the reference, then ranked by plDDT. Same construction in Fig. 5d: "The top five models by plDDT for the known state (top) and the putative alternative state (bottom)" (p7), where "known state" membership is RMSD-to-1LU4 defined (p20, Extended Data Fig. 7a,b).

  **Route 5 — hyperparameters (epsilon, min cluster size, cluster count) tuned against known states: CONTESTED-BY-CONSTRUCTION; record both halves.**
  The *selection rule itself* is structure-free — epsilon is chosen at the peak of the cluster-count curve: "Clustering the KaiB MSA with varying epsilon values resulted in a peak in the number of clusters returned (Extended Data Fig. 2a). We termed the epsilon corresponding to this peak epsmax." (p9), and the authors claim it is a priori: "We selected DBSCAN to perform clustering because we found that it offered an automated route to optimizing clustering a priori (Methods and Extended Data Fig. 2)." (p3).
  But the sentence that *introduces* that rule states the criterion by which it was judged, and it is KaiB's two known states:
  *"We found that our method for parameter selection in DBSCAN35 empirically optimized predicting KaiB's two states from its MSA with no prior information about the KaiB landscape in the following way."* (p9)
  And the epsilon sweep was evaluated by plotting the resulting landscapes in RMSD-to-both-known-states coordinates: "We investigated the effect of varying epsilon on resulting AF2 predictions for the protein KaiB. Extended Data Fig. 2c depicts clusters in sequence space ... and Extended Data Fig. 2d depicts the structure landscape of these clusters. Epsilon was varied between 3 and 20 with step size 0.5." (p9); Extended Data Fig. 2d caption: "Corresponding KaiB landscape of predictions for these epsilon values." (p14) — axes are "RMSD (Å), Ground state" and "RMSD (Å), FS state" (p14). The rationale for the balance point is likewise argued from the known KaiB outcome: "if clusters are too large, they may dilute the signal from some states, an extreme case of this is exemplified in how KaiB predicted using its entire MSA resulted in only the FS state." (p9).
  Minimum cluster size / *k*: **NOT REPORTED** — the DBSCAN *k* parameter is described (p9) but no value is given anywhere. Number of clusters is an output, not a set parameter. For the 628-family screen the sweep was subsampled for speed only: "For the preliminary scan of 628 protein families, this sweep on epsilon was performed on a randomly selected 25% of the MSA to accelerate computation." (p9)

  **Route 6 — seeds / stopping criteria tuned against known states: NONE FOUND, but run settings are largely unreported.**
  The only stated AF2 settings are for two side experiments: G<sub>A</sub>/G<sub>B</sub> — "MSAs were used as an input to AF2 runs in all 5 models with 0 recycles and 8 random seeds." (p11); the KaiB<sub>RS</sub> mutation scan — "plDDT from AF2 (no MSA, 12 recycles, model 1) for all combinations of 8 possible point mutations most enriched from FS state analysis" (p17). Seeds/recycles/models for the main KaiB, RfaH, MAD2 and screen runs are **NOT REPORTED**.

  **Route 7 (reverse direction, recorded because it bears on independence of validation) — the prediction was used as input to the experimental structure determination.**
  *"For the AUDANA automation steps, our predicted model of KaiBTV-4 was used as a structural starting point (Fig. 2d)."* (p10). The reported agreement between the NMR structure and the AF-Cluster model (1.89 ± 0.13 Å, p4) is therefore not fully independent of the model. The NOE-based evidence for the FS β-sheet topology *is* model-independent: "several unambiguous nuclear overhauser effects (NOEs) were assigned manually including those that already defined the β-strand topology unique to the FS state" (p10), and Extended Data Fig. 4b,c report the specific inter-strand NOEs "used in confirming KaiBTV-4 is in the fold-switched state" (p16).

  **Route 8 — training-set overlap of the reference structures: NOT REPORTED / NOT DISCUSSED for the validation targets.** Whether 2QKE, 5JYT, 5OND, 2LCL, 1S2H, 1DUJ or 1LU4 were in AF2's training set is never addressed. The only training-set question the paper asks is for the *novel* Mpt53 state (see `anti_memorization_control`).

- **prospective**: **partial — prospective in two experimental arms, retrospective in every scoring and tuning decision.**
  - Prospective: KaiB<sub>TV-4</sub> was predicted first and its structure solved afterwards by the authors (NMR structure deposited as PDB 8UBH, p11) — "We experimentally tested the AF-Cluster predictions on a KaiB variant in Thermosynechococcus elongatus vestitus that was predicted to favour the fold-switched (FS) state. Using nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy, we could indeed verify our AF-Cluster prediction." (p3). Likewise the KaiB<sub>RS</sub>-3m triple mutant was designed from predictions and then characterised by NMR (p4). The Mpt53 alternative state is prospective but **unvalidated** — "putative" throughout (p7).
  - Retrospective: state assignment, the 3 Å criterion, the "top five models" selection, candidate selection in the screen, the choice of 10 Mpt53 homologues, and the epsilon-sweep evaluation all reference structures already in hand (Routes 3–5). KaiB<sub>TE</sub>, RfaH, MAD2 and G<sub>A</sub>/G<sub>B</sub> are entirely retrospective benchmarks.
  Follows from `oracle_leakage`, not from the authors' word: the paper does not use the word "prospective" anywhere.

- **state_metric**: **RMSD-to-reference with a binary 3 Å predicate**, plus TM-score in one arm and visual/PCA assignment elsewhere.
  - Primary: r.m.s.d. of predicted model to each deposited crystal structure, thresholded at **3 Å** to call a state — "the top five models within 3 Å of crystal structures for each state" (p3); "within 3 Å r.m.s.d. of crystal structures of both the ground and FS state" (Fig. 1f caption, p2); "<3 Å RMSD, FS state" / "<3 Å RMSD, Ground state" as the categorical axis of Extended Data Fig. 2e (p14). **No justification for 3 Å is given.** RMSD computed in MDTraj (p11).
  - G<sub>A</sub>/G<sub>B</sub>: **TM-score** to the two folds, no threshold stated; correctness scored as "the highest-pLDDT model from AF-Cluster correctly predicted the most stable folds for 10 out of 12" (p7), with incorrect calls marked by an X on the panel (p23).
  - Mpt53 and RfaH/MAD2: state identity assigned **visually / by PCA on contacts**, not by a stated threshold — "Figure 5c shows all of the AF-Cluster models for Mpt53, visualized by principal component analysis (PCA) on the set of closest heavy-atom contact distances. Two prominent states are observed" (p6); Fig. 4b,d axes are "PC1 (on contacts)" / "PC2 (on contacts)" with no RMSD axis (p6).
  - Experimental side: secondary structure propensity from backbone chemical shifts (TALOS-N) as the state read-out, a qualitative agreement call — "The secondary structure propensity determined by NMR backbone chemical shifts, calculated using TALOS-N57 for KaiBTV-4, fully agrees with the FS state predicted by AF-Cluster." (p4); populations from NMR peak-intensity ratios over **n = 5 residues** (p4, p10).
  - DALI comparisons use a length-normalised RMSD defined on p11: "Weighted r.m.s.d. = r.m.s.d. / fraction aligned".

- **metric_saturation**: **Yes — a saturating binary predicate, plus systematic colour-scale truncation across nearly every figure.**
  - The 3 Å criterion is a hard predicate: models are in or out, so degrees of nearness within 3 Å and gradations of failure beyond it are unresolvable. It floors the comparator arms outright — |MSA| = 10 gives 1/500 and |MSA| = 100 gives 0/500 (p3), i.e. the uniform-subsampling arm is at the floor and cannot be ranked further.
  - **plDDT colour scale is truncated to 50–90 in essentially every figure that uses it**: Fig. 1d,f (p2), Fig. 2a (p4), Fig. 3b (p5), Fig. 4b,d (p6), Fig. 5c,d (p7), Extended Data Figs 5, 7, 8 (pp17, 19, 21). Values below 50 and above 90 are rendered identically, so confidence saturation at both ends is invisible.
  - **Axis truncations**: Fig. 1f axes stop at 15 Å in both RMSD dimensions (p2); Extended Data Fig. 8 panels stop at 15 Å (p21); Fig. 5b plDDT axis runs 30–90 and Δr.m.s.d. 0–20 Å (p7); Extended Data Fig. 2e plDDT axis runs 30–100 (p14); Extended Data Fig. 7a plDDT axis 30–100 and RMSD axis 0.0–1.5 (p19). No axis **breaks** were observed in any panel.
  - The one metric the authors themselves flag as non-monotonic with what it is used for is plDDT vs stability — see `stated_limits`.

- **directional_control**: **No instructable handle — the method samples, it cannot be told which state to produce.** There is no partner, ligand, nanobody, peptide, state-annotated template, or state-filtered MSA in the pipeline. The available knobs are:
  - **Cluster identity** — but clusters are unlabelled a priori; which cluster gives which state is only known after running AF2 and scoring against the references (Routes 3–4). Post hoc, cluster membership does correlate with state (Fig. 3a, p5).
  - **MSA depth / composition** as a coarse, non-directional knob: closest 50 sequences → ground state, closest 100 → FS state, complete MSA → FS state (p3); "predicting this variant in single-sequence mode in AF2 incorrectly predicts the ground state" (p6).
  - **Epsilon**, which changes the granularity of clustering but is set by a structure-free rule (p9).
  - **Point mutations to the protein itself** — the only demonstrated way to *direct* the outcome, and it directs the molecule, not the sampler: "three mutations—I68R, V83D and N84R—were sufficient to switch a prediction of KaiBRS from the ground state to a prediction of the FS state" (p4).
  - Seeds, dropout and model index are explicitly reported as **not** working as handles: "We tested other methods for introducing noise in AF2 using KaiBRS with no MSA as a test—sampling across the five models, incorporating dropout and using random seeds—and found that none of these cause AF2 to predict any models of the FS state" (p6).

- **anti_memorization_design**: **NONE as conventionally defined — no held-out set, no post-cutoff set, no cutoff date stated anywhere.** AF2's training cutoff is never mentioned; none of the reference structures (2QKE, 5JYT, 5OND, 2LCL, 1S2H, 1DUJ, 1UBQ, 1LU4) is checked against it. Two design elements are adjacent to anti-memorization and are recorded here:
  1. **One prospectively solved structure**, n = 1: the KaiB<sub>TV-4</sub> NMR structure (PDB 8UBH), determined by the authors after the prediction and deposited with this paper (p4, p11). No AF2 model could have memorised it.
  2. **A PDB-similarity search for the novel state**, framed explicitly as a training-set question: "Workflow for using DALI91 to screen for structure homologues to both Mpt53's original state and predicted alternate state to search for any similar structures in the PDB that might have been in AF2's training set." (p20, Extended Data Fig. 7d caption). Also an intent-level control on the KaiB side: "we wanted to rule out the possibility that non-KaiB proteins with similar folds to the FS state were contributing to the prediction" (p3), addressed by the 487-variant phylogenetic analysis rather than by a training-set check.
  3. A **negative control target** (not an anti-memorization control): "As a control, AF-Cluster models of ubiquitin, a protein that is well characterized to have no alternative states, returns only models with high confidence and low r.m.s.d. to the crystal structure PDB 1UBQ." (p6), n = 1.

- **anti_memorization_control**: **NONE RUN as a prediction-accuracy control arm. UNPOWERED.** No arm compares performance on pre- vs post-cutoff structures; there is no cutoff and no such split. The DALI search in item 2 above *was* actually run and analysed — 1,822 hits for the known state and 1,245 for the alternative state, 1,055 after CD-HIT, of which "A total of 479 of these were hits for both the known and alternative state, with 368 exclusively for the known and 208 exclusively for the alternative state" (p11), with the conclusion "7 of 9 DALI hits with lower alternate state RMSD contained an alpha-helix positioned in similar same way as in the Mpt53 alternate state" and "One structure (3EMX) also contained an N-terminus beta-strand positioned similarly to the alternate state" (p20) — but this is a *structural-novelty* check on one predicted state for **one target (n = 1)**, not a control on prediction accuracy, and it is not run for KaiB, RfaH, MAD2 or the G<sub>A</sub>/G<sub>B</sub> set. Mark **UNPOWERED**.

- **confidence_as_discriminator**: **Yes, centrally — and validated only in the weak sense of "near-native models score higher than background", while explicitly disclaimed in the sense that matters.**
  - Used as the discriminator: "Importantly, we show that, using our method, AF-Cluster, both states are sampled and scored with high confidence by AF2's learned predicted local distance difference test (plDDT) measure." (p3). plDDT ranks the displayed models (Fig. 1e, p2; Fig. 5d, p7), selects screen candidates jointly with RMSD (Fig. 5b, p7), and defines correctness in the G<sub>A</sub>/G<sub>B</sub> benchmark ("the highest-pLDDT model from AF-Cluster correctly predicted the most stable folds for 10 out of 12", p7).
  - Validation attempted: near-reference models score significantly above background, with p-values — Extended Data Fig. 2e, "P-values for sample comparisons with p < 0.05 indicated, calculated via a two-sided test ... n = 500 models for the two Uniform sampling methods, n = 230 for AF-Cluster sampling" (p14), panel p-values p = 3e-102, p = 1e-62, p = 5e-34 (p14); Extended Data Fig. 7b for Mpt53, "n = 1642 models total", p = 1e-235 and p = 1e-94 (pp19–20).
  - Disclaimed where it counts: "First, the pLDDT metric itself cannot be used as a measure of free energy. This was immediately evident in our investigation of KaiB, for which, in our models generated using AF-Cluster, the thermodynamically disfavoured FS state still had a higher pLDDT than the ground state (Extended Data Fig. 2e)." (p6). So plDDT discriminates *foldedness/consistency*, not *which state is populated* — the paper says so itself and still uses highest-plDDT as the correctness rule in the G<sub>A</sub>/G<sub>B</sub> arm.

## D. Claims

- **central_conclusion**: Clustering a ColabFold MSA by sequence edit distance with DBSCAN and running AF2 on each cluster (AF-Cluster) makes AF2 sample both states of the metamorphic proteins KaiB, RfaH and MAD2 with high plDDT, where the full MSA and uniform subsampling do not; applied to KaiB it correctly predicted, and NMR then confirmed, that the KaiB<sub>TV-4</sub> variant is stabilised in the fold-switched state and that three designed point mutations flip KaiB<sub>RS</sub> to the FS state. Screening 628 families without known fold switching returned a putative — computationally unvalidated — alternative fold for M. tuberculosis Mpt53.

- **stated_limits** (the authors' own, all p6–p7 unless noted):
  - plDDT is not a free energy, and ranks the thermodynamically disfavoured KaiB state above the favoured one: "the thermodynamically disfavoured FS state still had a higher pLDDT than the ground state" (p6).
  - Model counts per state are not Boltzmann weights; they reflect database composition (p6).
  - Failure on monomer↔oligomer switches: selecase, lymphotactin, CLIC1 (p5); "All of these proteins pose starting points for future improvements to AF-Cluster." (p5)
  - Novel folds may be practically unvalidatable if the switching stimulus is unknown (p7).
  - "conformational substates not present in the evolutionary signal may require alternative methods" (p7).
  - The 628-family screen is explicitly preliminary: "a handful of proteins in this preliminary screen" (p6); "for this preliminary work, we selected only proteins with a sequence length of between 52 and 150 residues" (p10).
  - The Mpt53 result is labelled "putative" throughout (pp1, 3, 7).
  - Remaining G<sub>A</sub>/G<sub>B</sub> failures are attributed to retrieval/clustering rather than AF2: "suggesting that the limitation is in either the sequence retrieval or clustering stages, rather than the structure module of AF2" (p11).
  - RfaH CTD placement is undefined: "Note that the CTD orientation is not defined due to the flexible linker between the two domains." (p6)

- **stance**: **precedent + contrast.**
  - *precedent* — this is the origin paper for MSA-clustering as a multi-state sampler, it supplies directly citable comparator numbers (uniform subsampling 1/500 and 0/500 vs clustered; 10/12 vs 8/12 on G<sub>A</sub>/G<sub>B</sub>; plDDT deltas for RfaH), and it is one of the few papers in the corpus with genuine wet-lab prospective validation of a predicted state.
  - *contrast* — on rigour: state identity is defined by an unjustified 3 Å RMSD predicate against structures already in hand, the displayed "top five" models are pre-filtered by that reference, there is no held-out or post-cutoff arm and no training-set discussion for any validation target, plDDT is used as the correctness rule in one arm while being disclaimed as a state discriminator in the Discussion, and the epsilon rule is introduced as having been "empirically optimized" to predict KaiB's two known states.
  - Provisional — the user's call.

- **comparable_to_ours**: **NONE directly** — this paper reports no per-state success rate over a target set, no TM-score/lDDT-to-reference distribution over a benchmark, and no per-system table; its comparators are one-off counts on individual proteins. Hedged candidates: (i) the uniform-subsampling arm (1/500 and 0/500 at 3 Å) is the closest thing to a baseline sampling-efficiency number, but it is a single target and a binary threshold, not an accuracy distribution; (ii) the G<sub>A</sub>/G<sub>B</sub> 10/12 vs 8/12 is a genuine head-to-head fraction but n = 12 on an engineered 56-residue system; (iii) the RfaH plDDT deltas (84.2 / 73.9 vs 68.6) are usable if and only if the comparison is explicitly framed as confidence, not accuracy — the paper itself disclaims plDDT as a state/stability discriminator (p6).
