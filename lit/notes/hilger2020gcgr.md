## B. Scope

- **system**: **GPCR.** A deliberate head-to-head of one family B (class B1) receptor against one family A receptor, extended to a nine-receptor kinetic panel. → tag `gpcr`.
- **n_targets**: **Three different numbers, and they must not be collapsed.**
  1. **Structures determined: 1** — the ZP3780-bound GCGR–G<sub>s</sub>–Nb35 complex (p.2, p.3).
  2. **Receptors compared biophysically head-to-head: 2** — GCGR (family B) and β<sub>2</sub>AR (family A). Every DEER, bimane/NBD fluorescence, FRET, GTP-turnover, [³H]-GDP-release and Bodipy-GTPγS experiment is this pair (pp. 6–9).
  3. **Receptors in the NanoBiT G<sub>s</sub>-dissociation panel: 9** — family A (5): β<sub>2</sub>AR, 5-HT<sub>4</sub>, DP (prostaglandin D2 receptor), H<sub>2</sub>R, V<sub>2</sub>R; family B (4): GCGR, GLP-1, PAC1, PTH1 (p.9, Fig. 7A p.10). Verbatim: "In total, we tested three family B GPCRs, in addition to GCGR—GLP1, PAC1, and PTH—and four family A GPCRs, other than β<sub>2</sub>AR, that couple primarily to G<sub>s</sub>" (p.9).
  - **Generality claim on a small n, flagged per schema.** The family-A-vs-family-B mechanistic conclusion rests on n = 1 receptor per family for the conformational (DEER/fluorescence) evidence and n = 5 vs 4 for the kinetic evidence, with a single pooled two-tailed *t* test (P = 0.019). The authors state the generalisation as a suggestion, not a demonstration: "the requirement of G<sub>s</sub> binding to initiate structural changes in TM6 **might be** a general mechanism in family B GPCRs" (p.9).
- **method_class**: **other — experimental structural biology and biophysics**, plus a small MD arm. Concretely: single-particle cryo-EM (3.1 Å); Q-band DEER spectroscopy; bimane and NBD site-directed fluorescence spectroscopy; ensemble Cy3B/Sulfo-Cy5 FRET; GTPase-Glo GTP-turnover assay; [³H]-GDP single-turnover release assay; Bodipy-FL-GTPγS binding; NanoBiT split-luciferase G-protein-dissociation assay in HEK293A cells; HTRF cAMP accumulation assay with alanine-scanning mutants; radioligand saturation and competition binding; ELISA and flow-cytometry surface-expression controls; **all-atom MD** (5 replicas × 300 ns per system, NAMD 2.12 / CHARMM36m, pp. 15–16). **No structure prediction of any kind.** → tags `experimental`, `md`.

## C. Conformational core

**Framing note, applies to this whole section.** This is a **wet-lab structural-biology and biophysics paper with no structure prediction in it**. The fields in section C that presuppose a generative model — `states_generated` (in the generative sense), `oracle_leakage` routes 1–6, `prospective` as a pipeline property, `anti_memorization_design`, `anti_memorization_control`, `confidence_as_discriminator` — are answered `NOT APPLICABLE` with one line of why, per the parent instruction, rather than being forced into a prediction reading. The fields that **do** transfer, and where this paper's value to the corpus sits, are `structural_priors_used`, `state_metric`, `directional_control` and `controls_run`; those are answered in full.

- **states_generated**: **NOT APPLICABLE — equilibrium displaced, nothing generated.** (The schema's own idiom for a wet-lab paper.) What the paper *resolves* experimentally, for the record:
  - **One atomic structure**: the nucleotide-free, agonist-bound, G<sub>s</sub>-coupled, Nb35-stabilised active state at 3.1 Å (p.2, p.3; PDB 6WPW).
  - **Two ensemble states probed by DEER**: apo and agonist-bound, which turn out to be **indistinguishable** — a single conformation each, both matching the inactive state. "In the apo (unliganded) state, the distance distributions between TM4-TM6 and TM4-TM5 show **single conformations** in detergent (Fig. 6, A and B, gray shading) as well as HDL particles (fig. S7C, dark gray shading), which are in good agreement with the inactive state of the receptor." (p.8)
  - **Four spectroscopic conditions per receptor** in the fluorescence arm: apo, +agonist, +agonist +G<sub>s</sub>, +agonist +G<sub>s</sub> +nucleotide (Fig. 6C–F, p.8).
  - **A three-state model** proposed but not measured as structures: inactive / intermediate / fully active (Fig. 8, p.11).
  - **A conformational ensemble from MD**: 5 replicas × 300 ns for each of ZP3780- and NNC1702-bound GCGR (pp. 15–16), reported only as fig. S8E, which is **not in this PDF**.

- **structural_priors_used**: **Extensive, disclosed, and entirely legitimate — this is design-time structural knowledge in a wet-lab paper, not a methodological sin.** Enumerated with pages:
  1. **PDB 5YQZ** — the partial-agonist NNC1702-bound GCGR crystal structure (17). Used three ways: as the **initial model for cryo-EM model building** ("The initial template of GCGR was derived from the crystal structure of GCGR (PDB 5YQZ)", p.12); as the **inactive/partial-agonist comparator** in Figs. 1B–D, 2B–C, 3A–B (pp. 3–5); and as the **starting point for the partial-agonist MD system** ("The NNC1702-bound structure was obtained by deleting H1 and mutating D9 to E", p.15).
  2. **PDB 5VAI** — active GLP-1R–G<sub>s</sub> complex (4). "The structural coordinates of PDB 5VAI (4) were used as initial models for the G<sub>s</sub> and Nb35." (p.12) **Note what this means: the G protein and nanobody of a *family B active-state complex* were the starting coordinates for the G protein and nanobody of this one.**
  3. **PDB 5XEZ** — the inactive, peptide-ligand-free (Fab-bound) full-length GCGR (19), used as the apo/inactive comparator in Fig. 1B–D (p.3 caption).
  4. **PDB 3SN6** — the β<sub>2</sub>AR–G<sub>s</sub> crystal structure (50), the family A reference against which every family-A-vs-B structural statement is made (Fig. 4C p.6, Fig. 5A p.7).
  5. **PDB 6EG8** — GDP-bound Gα<sub>s</sub>, the nucleotide-bound comparator for the G-protein conformational change (Fig. 4A, p.6).
  6. **Structure-guided design of the DEER spin-label sites** — the single most consequential prior here, because the negative result depends on having probed the right pair: "**Guided by the GCGR-G<sub>s</sub> complex structure and the available inactive structure of GCGR**, we engineered cysteine residues, pairwise, at the cytoplasmic ends of TM4 and TM5 (265C to 335C) and TM4 and TM6 (265C to 345C) to probe distance changes upon agonist binding (fig. S7B)." (p.8) The pair positions were chosen so that the *known* active-inactive displacement would be visible; that no change appeared is therefore informative rather than a sampling failure.
  7. **Structure-guided choice of the bimane/NBD reporter site**, by analogy to the established β<sub>2</sub>AR reporter: mC-GCGR labelled "at position 349C in TM6 with bimane and compared it with β<sub>2</sub>AR modified at the endogenous cysteine residue 265 in TM6" (p.8), using a fluorophore "previously utilized as a conformational reporter of TM6 activation of β<sub>2</sub>AR and other GPCRs (43–45)" (p.8).
  8. **Structure-guided alanine scanning.** The mutants (Q232<sup>3.37</sup>A, D370<sup>ECL3</sup>A, R378<sup>7.35</sup>A, D385<sup>7.42</sup>A, L242<sup>3.47</sup>A, F322<sup>5.54</sup>A, L395<sup>7.52</sup>A) were picked off the new structure's contacts and then tested functionally (Figs. 2D, 3C; pp. 4, 5).
  9. **Peptide design from prior structure-activity work**: ZP3780 was built by iterating the C terminus of glucagon while retaining the N-terminal residues known to carry efficacy — "Building on our previous efforts in designing glucagon analogs (15), we retained the native sequence in the N terminus of the peptide, which is important for ligand efficacy (16)" (p.2).
  10. **Nb35**, the G-protein-stabilising nanobody from the β<sub>2</sub>AR–G<sub>s</sub> work (50), reused here (pp. 12–13).
  11. **The published family B active-state complexes (GLP-1, CTR, CGRP, PTH)** as the comparison set for the kinked TM6: "As reported for the previously determined active structures of other family B GPCRs [glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1), calcitonin receptor (CTR), calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP), and parathyroid hormone receptor (PTH)], the kink pivots the intracellular half of TM6 to move outward by ~18 Å, while the α-helical structure of the extracellular half partially unwinds (1–5)." (p.3)

- **oracle_leakage**: **NOT APPLICABLE as a pipeline property — there is no prediction pipeline to leak into.** Every atomic coordinate in this paper is measured, not predicted. The routes are nonetheless answered separately, because two of them have honest wet-lab analogues that a reader should be able to check.
  - **Route 1 — deposited structures used as input or template: PRESENT, disclosed, and not a defect.** PDB 5YQZ and 5VAI were the starting models for real-space refinement against the experimental map (p.12); 5XEZ, 3SN6 and 6EG8 are comparators only. The map, not the starting model, is the evidence: "The cryo-EM map shows well-defined density that allowed unambiguous modeling of the secondary structure and side chain orientations of all components of the GCGR-G<sub>s</sub> complex, including the full agonist peptide ZP3780 (figs. S2F and S3A)." (p.2). Cross-validation against a withheld half-map is reported: "FSC curves were calculated between the resulting model and the half map used for refinement as well as between the resulting model and the other half map for cross-validation." (p.12) That is the correct control for starting-model bias, and it was run.
  - **Route 2 — state annotations from a curated database (GPCRdb, KLIFS, Kincore) driving templates or alignments: NONE FOUND.** No structural database is queried; the only database-derived convention is the family B/Wootten generic residue numbering used for superscripts (p.3). Protocol: pp. 11–16 (Materials and methods).
  - **Route 3 — cluster labels derived from known states: NONE FOUND.** The cryo-EM 2D/3D classification is unsupervised particle sorting with no state labelling: "A subset of 296,516 particles were selected after one round of two-dimensional (2D) and 3D classification." (p.12) The DEER distance distributions are fitted model-free, not to labelled state templates: "Background correction and **model-free fitting analysis** of dipolar evolution functions were performed with the program LongDistances933" (p.15).

  - **Route 5 — success defined post hoc by RMSD or TM to a structure they had: NONE FOUND.** No RMSD-to-reference is used to score anything.   - **Route 7 — design-level oracle use (weaker than pipeline leakage; kept distinct): PRESENT IN FORM, AND THE RESULT CONTRADICTED IT — which is the opposite of the defect this route exists to catch.** The expected answer *was* declared in advance: the spin-label pairs were placed using both the active and the inactive GCGR structures precisely because family A precedent said agonist should move TM6 outward (p.8, quoted at `structural_priors_used` item 6). The measurement then refused: "**Surprisingly**, addition of the full agonist ZP3780 at saturating concentration (5x molar excess, 0.5 mM) did not change the distance distribution" (p.8). A design-level oracle that predicts X and returns not-X is evidence, not contamination. → tag `design-level-oracle` is **NOT** applied; see the Tags section for why.
  - **The one genuine caveat, recorded here because it belongs to nobody else's field**: the DEER negative result is a **null**, and a null is only as strong as the probe's sensitivity. The paper's own defence is the internal positive control (the same DEER pair *does* resolve distinct distributions; the β<sub>2</sub>AR comparison by the same technique in ref. 38 *does* show the agonist-induced population) and the fluorescence arm, where the same TM6 reporter *does* respond when G<sub>s</sub> is added (Fig. 6C, p.8). Both are recorded in `controls_run`.

- **prospective**: **NOT APPLICABLE as a pipeline property; the underlying finding is genuinely prospective in the sense that matters to us.** Nothing was predicted and later checked. But the central conformational result was **not** the expected one, was obtained by direct measurement, and reversed the family A prior: the paper set out expecting agonist-induced TM6 movement and reports its absence with the word "Surprisingly" (p.8). No aspect of the DEER, fluorescence or kinetic outcome could have been read off a deposited structure in advance, because no deposited structure reports an *agonist-only* GCGR conformational state.

  - **Threshold / predicate status: there is no operationalised binary activation predicate in this paper.** The kink angle (105.5°, V368–G359–K344) and the TM6 displacement (18 Å at T351<sup>6.42</sup> Cα, 17.4 Å at F345<sup>6.36</sup>) are the two quantities stated precisely enough to be reimplemented as a geometric measure on a coordinate file. No cut-off separating "active" from "inactive" is proposed for either. Everything else is a difference spectrum or a rate.
  - **The visual half.** All structural state calls — "partial unwinding of helix 6 above the PxxG motif" (p.5), "P356<sup>6.47</sup> rotates away by approximately 90°" (p.5), the ICL2 comparison (p.6) — are made by superposition and inspection in PyMOL/ChimeraX (p.16), with no per-residue quantitative panel. → the `visual-metric` tag applies to the structural interpretation layer specifically, not to the DEER/fluorescence/kinetic layer, which is fully quantitative.

- **metric_saturation**: **Yes, in one place, and it is a genuine numeric ceiling on the load-bearing measurement.** DEER distance determination has a hard upper limit set by the dipolar evolution time, and the paper computes and draws it: "The upper limit of reliable distance (r) and width (σ) determination are shown as gray and black bars, respectively" (Fig. 6A–B caption, p.8), with the closed forms r<sub>max</sub> ≈ 5·(t<sub>max</sub>/2)^(1/3) and σ<sub>max</sub> ≈ 4·(t<sub>max</sub>/2)^(1/3) (p.15; **the exponents are the one place where the OCR is unreliable and the 200-dpi render of p.15 was not taken — see `unresolved` item 6, and do not quote these formulae from this note**). On the rendered Fig. 6A–B the reliable-r bar sits at ≈ 48 Å and the reliable-σ bar at ≈ 46 Å, against an x-axis running to 65 Å. **Consequence, stated plainly because it bears on how strongly the null can be read**: an agonist-induced population at a TM4–TM6 distance beyond ~48 Å would be at or past the reliability ceiling. The apo peak is at ~36 Å and the expected active-state shift in β<sub>2</sub>AR is of order +5–10 Å, so the relevant window is well inside the limit — but the ceiling exists, is drawn, and is not discussed in the text. No other measurement in the paper floors or ceilings numerically; the GTP-turnover, GDP-release and GTPγS curves all reach plateaux that are fitted endpoints, not instrument limits. Figure-level display problems (the dual-axis inset in Fig. 5B, the per-panel wavelength windows in Fig. 6) are recorded in `hides` on the relevant figure rows, per v3 rule 9.

- **directional_control**: **NOT APPLICABLE as a method property — nothing is generated, so nothing can be instructed.** But the paper is *about* which physical handle controls the state, and that is the reusable content. The handles tested, and the verdict on each:
  - **Full agonist alone (ZP3780, 5× molar excess, 0.5 mM saturating)** — **does NOT produce the active TM6 conformation.** DEER: no change in either TM4–TM6 or TM4–TM5 (p.8). Bimane: only "a small but statistically significant" λ<sub>max</sub> shift, attributed to local environment rather than TM6 displacement (pp. 8–9).
  - **Partial agonist (NNC1702)** — produces extracellular rearrangements only; the crystal structure 5YQZ it yields is inactive on the intracellular side (pp. 3–5).
  - **Heterotrimeric G<sub>s</sub> (GDP-bound, then nucleotide-free)** — **the handle that works.** "The outward movement of TM6 of GCGR is only observed upon interaction with G<sub>s</sub>" (p.1). The active-state coordinates only exist as an agonist + G<sub>s</sub> + Nb35 + apyrase complex.
  - **Nb35 nanobody** — a G-protein-stabilising nanobody used to hold the complex, not a receptor-directed handle (pp. 12–13).
  - **Apyrase (nucleotide depletion)** — "To stabilize the nucleotide-free complex, Apyrase (1 unit, NEB) was added and incubated overnight at 4°C." (p.13). Nucleotide removal is the handle that locks the ternary complex.
  - **GDP / GTPγS** — the reverse handle: they dissociate G<sub>s</sub> (confirmed by FRET, fig. S8C) but **do not** return GCGR's TM6 to the inactive state within 10 min (p.9).
  - **For family A, by the authors' own statement**, the required handle is the same class of object: "For the β<sub>2</sub>AR, as well as other family A GPCRs, full stabilization of the active state requires binding of the G protein or a G protein–mimetic nanobody, in addition to the agonist (29)." (p.8) — the difference is one of degree for family A (agonist gives a *partial* population) and of kind for family B (agonist gives *nothing detectable*).
  → tags `ligand-driven`, `partner-driven`, `nanobody`, `apo-sampling`.

## D. Claims

- **central_conclusion**: In a family B GPCR (the glucagon receptor), agonist binding alone produces **no detectable outward movement of TM6** — the DEER distance distribution at saturating full agonist is indistinguishable from apo — and the active, kinked, partially unwound TM6 conformation appears **only** on engagement of the heterotrimeric G protein. This is qualitatively different from family A GPCRs such as β<sub>2</sub>AR, where agonist alone shifts the population toward an outward-TM6 state. The authors attribute the difference to a higher energy barrier for forming the sharp kink and helix break at the PxxG motif that family B receptors require but family A receptors do not, and show the functional consequence: GCGR is a ~70-fold weaker GEF than β<sub>2</sub>AR, rate-limited at GDP release and GTP binding, and family B receptors as a class drive slower G<sub>s</sub> dissociation than family A. Once opened, GCGR's TM6 stays open for minutes after the G protein has left.

  - **`background` on method**: there is no prediction, no benchmark, no metric we compete against. Nothing here constrains how we should evaluate a predictor; it constrains what the right answer *is*.
  - **Not `contrast`**: the paper has no rigour defect we would set ourselves against. Its controls are unusually complete (see `controls_run`), its null result is properly guarded, and it names its own hedges.
  - **Not `threat`**: nothing here is scooped by or scoops a prediction paper.
