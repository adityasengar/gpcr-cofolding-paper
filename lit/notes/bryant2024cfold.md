## B. Scope

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| `system` | General protein — monomeric single-chain structures from the whole PDB, no family targeting. "Selecting all monomeric protein structures from the PDB on 2023-07-05 determined by X-RAY diffraction or Electron Microscopy (EM) with a resolution ≤5 Å" (p.7). Named examples span a viral capsid spike (7WHM/7WHN), a surfactant lipid transporter (7W01/7W02), lymphocyte perforin (3NSJ/7PAG) (p.3), lysine acetyltransferase (4AVA/4AVB, p.2), elongation factor Tu (2C78/1HA3, p.4). |
| `n_targets` | Dual-layered; record all four. **Benchmark:** 244 alternative conformations from 245 90%-identity sequence clusters spanning 458 structural clusters (p.8); the evaluation partition is "222 composed of 4263 sequences for evaluation with 244 alternative conformations" (p.8). **Conformational-generation targets actually run:** 155 of 234 possible (66%) (p.10); 145 (dropout) and 154 (MSA clustering) completed (p.10). **Sub-analyses:** 52 structures predicted >0.8 to both train and test conformations (p.4); 3 NMR-ensemble structures (p.6, SI). **Separate AF2-cutoff set:** 38 proteins (p.1, p.7) — constructed, never run. Training: 6474 structural clusters / 59,946 sequences; validation 315 clusters / 3539 sequences (p.8). |
| `method_class` | Retrained structure-prediction network + MSA-subsampling/clustering inference + benchmark construction. Three things in one paper: (i) a conformational split of the PDB (benchmark), (ii) Cfold, an AF2 clone retrained on that split, (iii) two undirected inference-time sampling strategies (dropout, MSA clustering). No single `method_class` term in the schema covers (ii); see `unresolved`. |
| `backbones` | One only: Cfold, "almost a complete copy of AlphaFold2 for monomeric structure prediction" with the template track removed (p.8), retrained from scratch on the conformational split. **AlphaFold2 itself was never run as a baseline** — see `metrics_reported`. Not multi-backbone. |
| `templates` | **off, by architectural removal.** "The main difference in the architecture is that the template track—which processes similar structures—is removed here to focus on the MSA only. Templates can capture alternative conformational states, although this would mean that these states are not predicted but instead copied from these templates." (p.8) |
| `msa_handling` | full at training; **subsampled/clustered at inference** (not state-filtered). MSAs from HHblits against uniclust30_2018_08 only, `hhblits -E 0.001 -all -oa3m -n 2` (p.8). At inference: "we set the number of clusters used in the sampling for the predictions to vary between [16, 32, 64, 128, 256, 512, 1024, 5120]. We take 13 samples per clustering threshold, resulting in 104 predictions per target in total" (p.10). The clustering is unsupervised over sequences; **no state-specific alignment is substituted**, so this is subsampling/depth-and-partition manipulation, not state-filtering. |

## C. Conformational core

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| `states_generated` | **ensemble** — ~100–104 predictions per target per strategy, spread across states: "From all samples, 37% correspond well (TM-score >0.8) to the test conformations, 33% to the train and 30% to neither of the conformations." (p.3). Not a collapse onto one basin, so `ensemble + single-state` is *not* the right dual here. But any single prediction is one structure and the ensemble carries no internal ranking: the headline number is best-of-N against a held reference (see `oracle_leakage` route 6). |
| `structural_priors_used` | Extensive and by design — this is a benchmark paper and the priors are the benchmark. (1) The entire PDB (68,953 monomeric chains, p.7) defines both training set and evaluation set. (2) Target selection is deposited-pair-driven: "We obtained 245 sequence clusters at 90% with alternative conformations (TM-score difference >0.2) belonging to 458 different structural clusters. Only one of the conformations of these is selected for training and the remainder is saved for evaluation." (p.8) — i.e. every evaluated protein was chosen *because* two deposited states exist. (3) State categories (hinge / rearrangement / fold switch) were assigned by manual inspection of the deposited pairs (p.3, p.7). (4) Ligand-driven attribution of the 52-structure subset came from reading the deposited entries: "Most structures that display conformational changes interact with a ligand (ligand binding, n = 42, Supplementary Table 1)" (p.4). None of this is a methodological sin; it is what makes the set a benchmark. |
| `oracle_leakage` | **Seven routes, each answered separately.**<br><br>**Route 1 — structures used as input or template: NONE FOUND at inference, but the sequence and one of its two states ARE in training.** Templates are architecturally absent: "The main difference in the architecture is that the template track—which processes similar structures—is removed here to focus on the MSA only." (p.8). Input is MSA only. However the split is **structure-aware, not sequence-disjoint**, by explicit design: "Only one of the conformations of these is selected for training and the remainder is saved for evaluation. We select the clusters at random." (p.8). The target's own sequence (and MSA) was therefore seen during training, paired with its *other* conformation. The authors' guarantee is about structures, not sequences: "we ensure that our structure prediction network (Cfold) does not see any structures similar to those used for evaluation during training. This is the only way to ensure that predicting different conformations is possible and not an artefact from train and test data overlaps." (p.3). Note "similar" is operationalised by the same TM ≥0.8 clustering, so the seen state is guaranteed only to be <0.8 TM from the held-out one. **Protocol page: p.3, p.8, p.10.**<br><br>**Route 2 — state annotations from a curated database (GPCRdb / KLIFS / Kincore) driving templates or alignments: NONE FOUND.** No curated state database is used anywhere. States are defined purely geometrically by TM-align clustering: "we used TM-align ... to perform pairwise structural alignment of all entries within each cluster. We created structural clusters if the pairwise TM-score ≥0.8." (p.7). Protocol described p.7–8 (Methods, "Proteins with alternative conformations in the PDB") and p.8 (MSA generation) — neither mentions any annotation source.<br><br>**Route 3 — cluster labels derived from known states: NONE FOUND at inference; PRESENT in the split.** The inference-time MSA clusters are unsupervised sequence clusters at fixed counts [16 … 5120] with no state label attached (p.10) — nothing state-derived enters the sampling. But the *train/test* partition labels are entirely derived from deposited-state clustering (p.7–8, quoted under Route 1). That is benchmark construction, recorded under `structural_priors_used`, not a pipeline leak.<br><br>**Route 4 — hyperparameters, sweep ranges, seeds or stopping criteria tuned against known states: PRESENT (two instances), plus one clean instance.** (a) **Sweep-range comparison made on the evaluation set:** "we increased the recycles to 10/20 and took 50/100 samples per cluster size (Supplementary information). We see no significant improvement in the scores. Using a higher number of clusters for the predictions is not beneficial either, suggesting that it is best to run the cheaper settings with fewer recycles, fewer samples and a lower number of clusters" (p.5). The settings choice is justified by scores computed against the held-out conformations — range-tuning on the evaluation set, which v3 counts as leakage even with no per-target value picked. (b) **Target set filtered by the model's own score against a held reference:** "We select structures whose conformations are in the train set from the test clusters that Cfold can predict with a TM-score of at least 0.6 using one prediction (0.5 is considered to be of a similar fold), resulting in 155 out of 234 possible structures (66%)." (p.10) — 79 of 234 targets (34%) were dropped on a model-performance criterion measured against a deposited structure. The denominator of the headline 52% is therefore pre-filtered. (c) **Clean:** checkpoint selection was on the *validation* partition drawn from training clusters, not the test set: "To validate the training of Cfold, we use the full-length structures taken from 315 structural clusters (one per cluster sampled randomly)... The best model weights are obtained at 10000 steps and these are the ones used in Cfold." (p.9). The cluster-count grid [16 … 5120] is taken from ref. 5 (del Alamo), not fitted here (p.10).<br><br>**Route 5 — success defined post hoc by RMSD/TM to a structure they had: PRESENT, definitionally.** "Over 50% of experimentally known nonredundant alternative protein conformations evaluated here are predicted with high accuracy (TM-score > 0.8)." (p.1). "We evaluate the predictions with the TM-score from TM-align, this time taking the maximum score to account for possible size differences between the native structures..." (p.10). This is intrinsic to a retrospective benchmark and is stated openly.<br><br>**Route 6 — best/worst model labels assigned against a held reference: PRESENT, and it is the largest caveat in the paper.** "The best TM-score was selected for each method out of approximately 100 samples (Methods)." (Fig. 1 caption, p.2). Also for the embedding analysis: "we select the predictions with the highest TM-scores to the test and train conformations and use these as references for the embeddings" (p.4). The paper then shows no achievable substitute exists: "There seems to be no clear relationship between the plDDT and known conformations from the PDB suggesting that confidence metrics can't be used to select for certain conformations." (p.5). So 81/155 is an oracle top-1-of-~100 rate with, on the paper's own evidence, no prospective selection rule.<br><br>**Route 7 — design-level oracle use (systems chosen because the expected answer is known): PRESENT, and labelled design-level, weaker than pipeline leakage.** Every target is a protein with two deposited states, one of which was deliberately placed in training: "Only one of the conformations of these is selected for training and the remainder is saved for evaluation." (p.8); "We select structures whose conformations are in the train set from the test clusters..." (p.10). The expected answer is declared before the sample is read. Unavoidable for this experimental question, but it is design-level and should not be conflated with routes 4–6. |
| `prospective` | **no.** Retrospective throughout, on all of design, target selection and scoring. Both structures of every pair pre-existed in the PDB (p.8); the target list was filtered by the model's own TM to a deposited structure (p.10); success is TM to the held-out deposited structure (p.1, p.10); the reported number is oracle-selected best-of-~100 (p.2). The *training* set-up is genuinely anti-memorization (structure-aware split, p.3), which is a different and real virtue — but it does not make the evaluation prospective. The authors themselves say the prospective version was not attempted: "Experimental validation of predictions of alternative conformations would provide the ultimate proof of the utility of Cfold... Such a procedure would require substantial resources and is outside the scope of this study." (p.6) |
| `state_metric` | **continuous coordinate + binary predicate** (genuinely dual). Continuous: TM-score from TM-align used for every score (p.8), reported as distributions (Figs 1b, 3, 4, 5, 7). Binary: success is thresholded at TM-score >0.8 (p.1, p.3, Table 1 p.3). **Thresholds and their stated justification, all explicit:** conformational difference cutoff — "We consider conformational changes to have at least a difference of 0.2 in TM-score. This is because smaller changes are very difficult to distinguish." (p.8) and "If a change of only 0.1 TM-score is permitted, the predictions have to have a score of >0.9 to be able to distinguish between the different conformations. This is very unlikely as this means that almost all atoms are coincidental between predicted and native structures. Therefore, we use a threshold of 0.2 TM-score." (p.8); structural-cluster cutoff 0.8 — "Note that having a TM-score above 0.5 is generally considered as having similar folds and this analysis is therefore very refined (a TM-score above 0.8 is highly accurate)." (p.8); target-inclusion cutoff 0.6 — "(0.5 is considered to be of a similar fold)" (p.10). Manual visual inspection is used as a *filter* on the dataset, not as the success metric: "we performed a manual check. We find that some putative alternative conformations are a result of variations in N/C-terminal loops, mutations or disconnected chains (breaks) and such structures were excluded." (p.7); "We perform a manual check to ensure that the conformations are indeed different and not a result of variable loop regions, low resolution or other experimental differences." (p.8). |
| `metric_saturation` | No numeric floor or ceiling in the reported score distributions: TM-scores span ~0.4–1.0 with no pile-up at either bound (Figs 1b p.2, 4a p.5, 5a p.6). **One real boundary effect, and it is in the benchmark rather than the model:** the inter-conformation TM-score is censored at the 0.8 inclusion cutoff by construction, and the mode of the distribution sits immediately below it — "As most conformations are between 0.7 and 0.8, this suggests that the structural change is mainly 0.2–0.3 TM-score." (Fig. 7b caption, p.8). So the median benchmark pair is only just different enough to qualify. Cross-reference figure row `7A-B`. Contrast: accuracy collapses where change is large — "at changes >0.4 TM-score, the accuracy is poor" (p.5). |
| `directional_control` | **NONE — the method only samples; it cannot be instructed.** The two handles are inference-time dropout ("we simply activate dropout everywhere except for in the structural module following AFsample and make 100 predictions in total", p.10) and MSA cluster count ([16 … 5120], 13 samples each, p.10). Neither carries a state label. No partner, ligand, nanobody, peptide, state-annotated template or state-filtered MSA is used anywhere. The paper argues directly that the process is undirected: "This indicates that the prediction of conformational states is stochastic and that internal coevolutionary representations are not used to differentiate between states." (p.4); "This suggests that the sampling of alternative conformations is likely stochastic and that the coevolutionary information acts to constrain the structure within a certain outcome." (p.5). |
| `anti_memorization_design` | **Present, and it is the paper's core contribution — but the cutoff is structural, not temporal, for the main set.** Design: a structure-aware conformational split of the PDB. Sequences clustered at 30% identity ("This is a more stringent cutoff than the 40% identity used in AlphaFold2", p.7), structures clustered within those at TM ≥0.8 (p.7), then for each protein with two states one state's structural cluster goes to training and the other is held out (p.8). n held out = 244 alternative conformations / 222 structural clusters / 4263 sequences (p.8); 155 actually evaluated (p.10). Validation partition: 315 structural clusters, 3539 sequences (p.8), drawn from the training side. **A date-based cutoff exists only for a separate, secondary set:** "AlphaFold2 (AF) was trained on all structures in the PDB with a maximum release date of 30 April 2018" (p.7) → "We now checked for sequence clusters that have different structural clusters (different conformations) before and after April 30 2018. We obtained 900 putative alternative conformations." → after 90% redundancy clustering and manual check, "The manual check resulted in 38 alternative conformations in total." (p.7). The main 244-conformation set **explicitly drops the date step**: "We skip step 5 and go directly to step 6 (as no date cutoff is necessary here)" (p.7). |
| `anti_memorization_control` | **Two answers, and the distinction matters.** (a) **RUN and powered for Cfold:** the held-out arm is the paper's main experiment — 155 held-out conformations predicted and scored, with the matched train-conformation arm reported side by side (Table 1, p.3; Fig. 1b–c, p.2). n = 155, above the ~10 threshold, so not `UNPOWERED`. (b) **NONE RUN for AlphaFold2.** The 38-protein post-cutoff set was constructed specifically to test AF2 and then abandoned as too small — "This resulted in a total of only 38 proteins with alternative conformations. We do not think this amount is sufficient to address the multi-conformation prediction problem as the total number of structural clusters (TM-score ≥0.8 within each cluster) is 6696, meaning that only 0.6% of possible structures would be evaluated." (p.1). AF2 is never run on it, or on anything else in this paper. That arm is `NONE RUN`, and would have been `UNPOWERED` (n = 38) had it been run. Residual caveat on (a): the held-out set is structure-disjoint but not sequence-disjoint from training — see `oracle_leakage` route 1. |

| `central_conclusion` | The authors build a structure-aware conformational split of the PDB (structural clusters at TM ≥0.8; a pair counts as alternative conformations only if the two states differ by >0.2 TM over aligned regions), yielding 244 held-out alternative conformations, and retrain an AF2 clone with the template track removed on one state of each pair. ## Verbatim dataset construction criteria (the load-bearing quotes, in pipeline order)

These are reproduced in full because downstream papers cite this dataset's admission rule.

1. **Source and experimental filter (p.7):** "Selecting all monomeric protein structures from the PDB on 2023-07-05 determined by X-RAY diffraction or Electron Microscopy (EM) with a resolution ≤5 Å. We excluded NMR structures as the flexibility in these may give rise to the appearance of alternative conformations when there are none. In total, 69861 structures were obtained. About 51,183 were deposited on or before 30 April 2018 and 18,678 after."
2. **Chain and composition filter (p.7):** "We extracted the first protein chain in each PDB file and the corresponding sequences with less than 80% non-regular amino acids and more than 50 residues. 68953/69861 proteins fulfilled these criteria (99%)."
3. **Sequence-identity redundancy filter, stage 1 — 30% (p.7):** "We clustered the sequences at 30% identity using MMseqs2 (version f5f780acd64482cd59b46eae0a107f763cd17b4d). This is a more stringent cutoff than the 40% identity used in AlphaFold2 to ensure that similar structures are indeed captured within the clusters. `mmseqs easy-cluster examples/DB.fasta clusterRes tmp --min-seq-id 0.3 -c 0.8 --cov-mode 1`" → "This resulted in 5452 clusters with more than one entry (10,116 clusters in total)."
4. **Structural clustering threshold (p.7):** "From the sequence clusters with over one entry, we used TM-align (version f0824499d8ab4fa84b2e75d253de80ab2c894c56) to perform pairwise structural alignment of all entries within each cluster. We created structural clusters if the pairwise TM-score ≥0.8. 64,208 out of the 68,953 structures (93%) could be clustered into 6696 structural clusters."
5. **Sequence-identity redundancy filter, stage 2 — 90% (p.7):** "To see if these are truly alternative conformations and not a result of sequence variations, we clustered the sequences again on 90% identity using MMseqs2: `mmseqs easy-cluster examples/DB.fasta clusterRes tmp --min-seq-id 0.9 -c 0.8 --cov-mode 1`" → "We obtained 14,662 clusters out of 64,208 sequences in total."
6. **Date cutoff — applied ONLY to the secondary 38-protein AF2 set, and explicitly skipped for the main set (p.7):** "We now checked for sequence clusters that have different structural clusters (different conformations) before and after April 30 2018. We obtained 900 putative alternative conformations." vs, for the main set: "To assess all proteins with alternative conformations from the PDB, we continue from step 4 above using the structural clusters at 0.8 TM-score. **We skip step 5 and go directly to step 6 (as no date cutoff is necessary here)** to see if these are truly alternative conformations and not a result of sequence variations."
7. **Candidate pairs (p.7):** "After step 6 (clustering at 90% sequence identity), we analyse all sequence clusters at 90% (14,662 clusters) that contain at least two different structural clusters. In total, there are 627 such sequence clusters."
8. **The admission rule — the TM 0.8 exclusion, stated twice (p.7–8):** "To reduce the number of necessary manual checks, we do pairwise sequence alignment of all sequences within each structural cluster of the 90% sequence clusters. Using these sequence alignments, we extract the corresponding structural areas and perform another structural comparison with TM-align. **Only if the structures are still different (TM-score <0.8), do we consider them to be putative alternative conformations.**" And, as the equivalent difference form: "We obtain 311 such examples that are within the same 90% sequence identity cluster but have aligned sequence regions with corresponding structures that differ >0.2 in TM-score."
9. **Representative selection and manual check (p.8):** "We select representatives with the longest sequence overlaps and the biggest TM-score difference from each of the structural clusters. We perform a manual check to ensure that the conformations are indeed different and not a result of variable loop regions, low resolution or other experimental differences."
10. **Resulting counts and the split (p.8):** "We obtained 245 sequence clusters at 90% with alternative conformations (TM-score difference >0.2) belonging to 458 different structural clusters. Only one of the conformations of these is selected for training and the remainder is saved for evaluation. We select the clusters at random. In total, there are 6474 structural clusters composed of 59946 sequences for training and 222 composed of 4263 sequences for evaluation with 244 alternative conformations (some structural clusters are present in more than one alternative conformation). We select 315 of the training structural clusters at random for validating the structure prediction performance (5%, 3539 sequences)."
11. **Justification of the 0.2 threshold (p.8, Scoring):** "TM-score from TM-align was used for all scoring. We consider conformational changes to have at least a difference of 0.2 in TM-score. This is because smaller changes are very difficult to distinguish. Note that having a TM-score above 0.5 is generally considered as having similar folds and this analysis is therefore very refined (a TM-score above 0.8 is highly accurate). Another reason is the evaluation of predicted structures. If a change of only 0.1 TM-score is permitted, the predictions have to have a score of >0.9 to be able to distinguish between the different conformations.
