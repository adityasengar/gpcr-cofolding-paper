## B. Scope

- **system**: **General protein-ligand, with two protein kinases as the named case studies.**
  - Cyclin-dependent kinase 2 (CDK2), PDB 1B38, with ATP — protein kinase (p.2, p.9).
  - MEK1, PDB 7XLP, with an ATP-site inhibitor — protein kinase (p.3, p.9).
  - Glucose dehydrogenase, PDB 2VWH, with glucose — general protein (p.5, p.9).
  - CASF-2016, 285 experimentally-determined protein-ligand complexes, taxonomically unrestricted (p.4, p.9).
  Three further binding-site mutagenesis systems are run on AF3 in SI Fig. S17 (p.9) — identities not given in the main PDF.
- **n_targets**: **288 protein-ligand systems in total**: 3 hand-picked case studies (CDK2/ATP, MEK1/inhibitor, glucose dehydrogenase/glucose) + 285 CASF-2016 complexes (p.9), plus 3 unnamed SI systems (p.9). The four-model head-to-head comparison runs on the **3 case studies only**; the systematic 285-complex sweep is **AlphaFold3 only** — "Structures were predicted using only the AlphaFold3 co-folding model and the highest-confidence structure was used for analysis." (p.9). The generality claim ("to further confirm this behavior as a widespread issue for protein-ligand structure predictions", p.4) therefore rests on one backbone at scale and four backbones on three systems.
- **method_class**: **benchmark-only (adversarial robustness benchmark of co-folding models) + enhanced sampling (funnel metadynamics) as the orthogonal physics control.** No new predictor is proposed. Verbatim framing, p.8 Methods: "In this study, we perform several types of adversarial challenges by crafting examples with an expected physical outcome and seeing if the predictions from co-folding models agree with this expectation."
- **backbones**: **Four, head to head — AlphaFold3, RoseTTAFold All-Atom (RFAA), Chai-1, Boltz-1** (p.9). → `multi-backbone`. Note the CASF-2016 sweep drops to **AlphaFold3 only** (p.9). Versions are not stated for any of the four; no commit hashes, no dates. Run conditions: "Unless otherwise noted, each models was run using default settings and no constraints were specified to the models that support this feature." (p.9) — i.e. **no pocket constraint was used**, even where the model supports one.
- **templates**: **ON (by default), and templates are the paper's own explanation for the failure.** No explicit "templates on" sentence exists; it follows from default settings (p.9) plus the mechanistic account on pp.7–8:
  > "Most of the co-folding models rely heavily on multiple-sequence alignment and 3D template-based input features retrieved from existing data which help the model predict the 3D structure. However, when we introduce small mutations, for example by removing the binding site, it is very likely that the sequence alignment and template search will return exactly the same results as before, as they are still the closest related sequences and structures in the dataset. Therefore, the MSA and template features that the network accepts as input are identical despite the mutations, leading the model to produce a very similar prediction." (pp.7–8)
  → tag `templates-on`. **Not** state-annotated; no template curation of any kind is performed by the authors.
- **msa_handling**: **full** (default settings, p.9). Never subsampled, never clustered, never state-filtered. The MSA is load-bearing in the *diagnosis*: the mutated sequence retrieves the same alignment as the wild type, so the pocket perturbation never reaches the network's evolutionary features (pp.7–8, quoted above).

## C. Conformational core

**Framing note, applies to this whole section:** this paper is about **ligand pose under perturbation**, not about protein conformational state. Fields whose names presuppose a conformational-state reading are answered on their own terms and are explicitly *not* forced into a state interpretation. The protein backbone is reported as essentially invariant throughout: "With the exception of binding-site sidechains, the tertiary protein structure was also predicted with consistently high accuracy, further suggesting that these predictions are not sensitive to adversarial changes in the protein constitution." (p.4), and "the ligand has little influence on the overall fold of the protein, with 95% of CASF structures having an apo structure with Cα RMSD less than 1.5 Å to the holo structure." (p.8)

- **states_generated**: **one** — a single predicted protein-ligand complex per (model × input condition). For the CASF sweep an internal sample is reduced to one by confidence: "the highest-confidence structure was used for analysis" (p.9). The number of seeds / diffusion samples generated before that selection is **NOT REPORTED** anywhere in the main text or Methods. `NOT APPLICABLE` as a *conformational* state count — nothing multi-state is generated or sought.

- **structural_priors_used**: **Heavy, deliberate, and not a defect** — the entire design is built on deposited co-crystal structures.
  - Three co-crystal complexes are the starting scaffolds and the references: CDK2 PDB 1B38, MEK1 PDB 7XLP, glucose dehydrogenase PDB 2VWH (pp.9–10).
  - The perturbation sites are read off the crystal contacts: "Residues with side-chain heavy atoms within 3.5 Å of any ligand heavy atoms were selected for mutation." (p.9), and for the sweep, "Binding site residues were identified with the co-crystallized ligand using the same distance threshold of 3.5 Å." (p.9). The explicit CDK2 list is I10, T14, V18, A31, K33, D86, K129, Q131, N132, L134, D145; the MEK1 list is A40, A59, I105, E108, M110, S158, F173 (p.9).
  - CASF-2016 (285 high-quality experimental complexes, ref. 53) is used as the evaluation corpus (p.9).
  - The funnel-metadynamics funnel is placed on the crystallographic pocket: "funnel metadynamics simulations were set up so that the ligand is initially placed at the start of a funnel encompassing the full binding site" (p.10).
  - Systems were chosen *because* their PDB representation is known: ATP "is widely represented in the PDB training data" (p.9); glucose because "sugar structures are widely represented in the PDB training data" (p.9).
  - The mutation table itself is a prior from the literature, not from structures: the Miyata distance (ref. 52), tabulated in Table 2, p.9.

- **oracle_leakage**: **Present by three of seven routes. Critically, the leakage that matters here is leakage *into the models under test*, which the authors deliberately preserve and then name as the mechanism of failure — it is the paper's finding, not the paper's sin.** Enumerated route by route.

  **Route 1 — deposited structures as input or template: PRESENT, and it is the central result.** Models run at default settings retrieve MSA and template features for the *wild-type* sequence even when every contacting residue has been mutated, so a deposited structure of the unperturbed complex re-enters the pipeline through template search. Verbatim, pp.7–8:
  > "when we introduce small mutations, for example by removing the binding site, it is very likely that the sequence alignment and template search will return exactly the same results as before, as they are still the closest related sequences and structures in the dataset. Therefore, the MSA and template features that the network accepts as input are identical despite the mutations, leading the model to produce a very similar prediction."
  Compounded by training-set membership, stated outright, p.3:
  > "The CDK2-ATP complex was part of the training set used to develop the co-folding methods."
  Note the authors never run a templates-off arm to isolate this route — the mechanism is asserted and argued, not measured. That is the single largest hole in the paper.

  **Route 2 — state annotations from a curated database (GPCRdb / KLIFS / Kincore) driving templates or alignments: NONE FOUND.** No such database appears anywhere. Protocol described p.9 (Methods, "Binding site mutations", "CASF-2016 dataset") and p.10 ("Ligand mutations"): binding-site residues come from a 3.5 Å distance criterion on the co-crystallised ligand, and mutations come from the Miyata volume/polarity distance (Table 2, p.9). Nothing state-annotated.

  **Route 3 — cluster labels derived from known states: NONE FOUND.** No clustering drives any prediction. The one grouping performed is a *post hoc* analysis of results, not an input: "we investigated whether belonging to particular protein families played a significant role by grouping results from the CASF predictions into their protein families. These results... show no clear separation between protein families that perform robustly or not." (p.8)

  **Route 4 — hyperparameters, sweep ranges, seeds or stopping criteria tuned against known states: essentially NONE FOUND, with one recorded exception.** Default settings throughout: "Unless otherwise noted, each models was run using default settings and no constraints were specified to the models that support this feature." (p.9). No sweep range is tuned on the evaluation set; the funnel-metadynamics parameters are explicitly held fixed across targets: "For each protein target, the funnel parameters were kept the same" (p.10). The exception is a single abandoned rescue attempt made against a known reference, p.3:
  > "Additional attempts at rescuing RFAA accuracy by increasing the maximum number of cycles did not improve results."
  This is per-system tuning aimed at a held reference; it is reported as having failed, and no tuned value is carried forward, so it does not contaminate the reported numbers — but it is the correct home for it.

  **Route 5 — success defined post hoc by RMSD to a structure they had: PRESENT, and unavoidable by construction.** The whole readout is RMSD to a deposited pose. Verbatim, p.4:
  > "Irrespective of the type of modification, more than half of all systems correctly predicted by AlphaFold3 (ligand RMSD < 2 Å) retain the same predicted ligand pose even after binding site disruption."
  And in the simulation control, p.10:
  > "The bound state was defined by having RMSD < 2 Å to the native pose, all other stable states outside of the pocket are considered to be unbound."
  This is a *benign* instance of route 5: the reference pose is the object of the test, not a hidden answer key — the perturbed systems have no experimental structure at all, so no other reference exists. The consequence is recorded under `metric_saturation` and `hides` instead.

  **Route 6 — best/worst model labels assigned against a held reference: NONE FOUND.** Selection is by the model's own confidence, not by the reference: "the highest-confidence structure was used for analysis" (p.9). No best-of-N against RMSD anywhere. For the case-study figures, the number of predictions generated per cell and how one was chosen for display is **NOT REPORTED** (Figs. 1, 2, 4, 5 captions, pp.3, 4, 6, 7) — which is a reporting gap, not evidence of reference-based selection.

  **Route 7 — design-level oracle use (weaker than pipeline leakage; label kept distinct): PRESENT and explicit.** The expected answer is declared before the result is read, as a matter of stated method, p.8:
  > "In this study, we perform several types of adversarial challenges by crafting examples with an expected physical outcome and seeing if the predictions from co-folding models agree with this expectation."
  Systems are chosen because their training-set status is known in advance — ATP/CDK2 because it *is* in training ("ATP is a good test case since it is widely represented in the PDB training data", p.9; "The CDK2-ATP complex was part of the training set used to develop the co-folding methods", p.3), MEK1 because it is *not* ("MEK1 was selected as an additional test case since it was not included in the training set of any of the tested co-folding models and has low similarity in terms of ligand and pocket shape.", p.9). The expected outcomes are asserted a priori: "Methylation of glucose can be assumed to result in non-binders that lose all affinity to the target" (pp.9–10); "the assumption is that such a strong difference in charge should show a significantly different binding mode of at least the charged groups, if not complete loss of binding" (p.10). **Mitigating and important:** the a priori expectation is subsequently tested independently by funnel metadynamics (Table 1, p.8) rather than merely asserted — which is precisely the control that converts route 7 from a defect into a validated prior. → tags `oracle-leak` *and* `design-level-oracle`.

- **prospective**: **no.** Every arm is scored against a deposited co-crystal pose, and the perturbed systems are synthetic constructs with no experimental structure and no experimental binding data. There is no wet-lab measurement anywhere in the paper. The nearest thing to a prospective element is design-level: MEK1 is a genuine out-of-training-set target (p.9), and the perturbed pockets are novel inputs no model has seen — but the *ground truth* for those inputs is a simulation (funnel metadynamics), not an experiment. Authors' own framing agrees: "additional validation such as physics-based methods, such as molecular dynamics simulations or free energy calculations, or experimental data may be necessary." (p.8, recommending experiment as future work).

- **state_metric**: **Triple, and deliberately not a conformational-state metric — it is ligand pose correctness under perturbation.** Joined:
  **binary predicate + RMSD-to-reference + visual only.**
  1. **Binary predicate**: ligand heavy-atom RMSD < 2 Å to the native co-crystal pose. Used for the CASF sweep readout ("more than half of all systems correctly predicted by AlphaFold3 (ligand RMSD < 2 Å)", p.4; Fig. 3 y-axis "Fraction with RMSD < 2Å to Native Pose", p.5) and to define the bound state in the simulations ("The bound state was defined by having RMSD < 2 Å to the native pose", p.10). **Threshold justification: NOT REPORTED** — 2 Å is used as convention (it is the same threshold quoted for the PoseBusters V2 comparison on p.2) and is never argued for.
  2. **RMSD-to-reference, continuous**: individual values are quoted in prose for the CDK2 case study only — AF3 0.2 Å, RFAA 2.2 Å wild-type, RFAA 2.0 Å after binding-site removal (p.2). No continuous RMSD distribution is ever plotted.
  3. **Visual only**: the dominant readout for Figs. 1, 2, 4 and 5 — pose displacement, steric clashes and stretched bonds are called by eye from renders with no quantitative panel. E.g. "There are also instances of unphysical overlapping atoms and large steric clashes in several of the predicted structures" (p.3); "Several steric clashes and stretched bonds were observed between the ligand, co-factor, and protein in the RFAA predictions with the addition of methyls." (p.5); "No obvious steric clashes or non-physical artifacts were observed in the wild-type predicted structures." (p.5). No clash count, no interaction-energy number, no distance cutoff is given for any of these calls. → tag `visual-metric`, and this is a real rigour defect in a paper whose headline is about physical validity.
  **RMSD atom-selection caveat (matters):** for the modified-ligand arms, RMSD is computed over corresponding heavy atoms only — "in the methylation challenge, only the original heavy atoms of the sugar contribute to the RMSD, not the additional methyl groups. In the charge modification example, the charged groups were included in the RMSD calculation." (p.10). So the methylation series is scored on a sub-structure that the perturbation deliberately does not touch, which mechanically biases that arm's RMSD downward.

- **metric_saturation**: **Yes, numerically, in two places — and the direction matters differently in each.**
  1. **Funnel-metadynamics probabilities floor and ceiling hard (Table 1, p.8).** Bound-state probability hits exactly `0.00 ± 0.00` (unbound `1.00 ± 0.00`) for: binding-site packing, binding-site inversion, glucose with 3/4/5/6 methyls, and ATP at charge +1, +2 and +3 — 11 of the 15 tabulated rows. Below "no binding at all" the metric has no resolution, so the perturbations **cannot be rank-ordered by severity** past that point. Here the saturation **is the finding**: it is the positive evidence that the perturbed pockets are genuinely unbindable, which is what makes the co-folding models' unchanged poses indefensible. It is not an artefact — but it does mean the paper cannot say which of packing, inversion or charge reversal is the harsher challenge.
  2. **The RMSD < 2 Å predicate floors the pose readout.** Fig. 3 (p.5) reports only the *fraction* below 2 Å; the underlying RMSD distribution is never shown, so "unchanged pose" and "shifted 1.9 Å" are indistinguishable in every reported number. Combined with the methylation-arm atom selection (original heavy atoms only, p.10) this makes low RMSD **partly an artefact of the metric** in the ligand-modification arms specifically. In the binding-site mutagenesis arms the low RMSD is not an artefact — the renders (Figs. 1, 2, pp.3–4) show the ligand sitting in the same place, and the funnel metadynamics independently shows it should not be there at all.
  **Verdict: floored, and the floor is load-bearing evidence rather than a defect — with one genuine artefact confined to the glucose methylation series.**
  Axis-related issues are recorded in `hides` (Fig. 3 row), not here, per v3 rule 9.

- **directional_control**: **The handle exists and is exactly what the paper shows does not work.** The intended handle is not a state selector but a *physical* one: mutate the pocket (Gly removal / Phe packing / Miyata inversion, p.9) or chemically modify the ligand (progressive methylation, charge reversal −3 → 0/+1/+2/+3, pp.9–10) and the ligand should move. It does not. No other handle is used: no pocket constraint, despite availability — "no constraints were specified to the models that support this feature" (p.9); no template bias, no MSA manipulation, no seed steering, no partner or nanobody. The paper also notes the models are in principle unconstrained yet behave as if constrained, p.5:
  > "It should be noted that in contrast to classical docking methods where the binding pose is typically limited to a predefined search volume, co-folding methods are in principle able to place the ligand anywhere on the protein surface. However, each of the tested co-folding models demonstrated a specific bias towards a given pocket rather than exploring other, potentially unspecific binding modes on the surface."

- **anti_memorization_design**: **Yes, but n = 1 and no date cutoff.** One out-of-training-set system was deliberately selected:
  > "MEK1 was selected as an additional test case since it was not included in the training set of any of the tested co-folding models and has low similarity in terms of ligand and pocket shape. The system was identified via the Runs N' Poses dataset (ref. 49) which calculated a similarity value (sucos_shape_pocket_qcov) of 46.21." (p.9)
  The cutoff is defined by **membership in the tested models' training sets** (via an external curated dataset) plus a **pocket/ligand shape-similarity score**, not by a deposition date. n = 1 protein (PDB 7XLP). CASF-2016 is **not** a held-out set — it predates the models' training and is expected to be largely inside it. No post-cutoff set exists anywhere in the paper.
- **anti_memorization_control**: **RUN, and UNPOWERED (n = 1).** The MEK1 arm is not merely declared — it is executed as a full parallel challenge battery (wild type, removal, packing, inversion × 4 models) and analysed in its own figure (Fig. 2, p.4) and its own paragraph (pp.3–4). The result is that memorisation of *that specific complex* is not required for the failure: "The trend continues in the binding site packing challenge where we again observe near-native poses without any significant deviation despite the dramatic changes to the pocket." (p.3). Mark **UNPOWERED**: one protein, one ligand, no statistics, and RFAA fails even on the unperturbed wild type ("with RFAA still placing near the binding site, but failing to retrieve a near-native pose", p.3), which removes one of the four models from that arm's evidence. A second, indirect anti-memorisation control was run at scale on CASF and reported as null: "no correlation between the number of examples in the training set versus model sensitivity against adversarial changes was observed." (p.8) — n = 285, but with the number itself and the correlation coefficient in SI, not in the PDF.

- **controls_run**: **The paper's most reusable content — a 17-arm battery.** Rows 1–6 are the perturbation battery proper; rows 7–17 are the supporting and orthogonal controls.

  **What is missing from the battery, and it is the obvious gap:** there is **no templates-off / MSA-off arm**. The paper's own mechanistic explanation (pp.7–8) is that unchanged MSA and template features cause the unchanged pose, and that hypothesis is never tested by simply switching them off. There is also no random-pocket / decoy-site control (predicting into a pocket that never bound the ligand) and no scrambled-sequence control.

- **confidence_as_discriminator**: **Yes — used, and explicitly validated as a *failure*.** Ligand pLDDT, ligand pTM and protein-ligand ipTM are recorded for every case system (SI Tables S1–S4, pp.4, 5) and mean ligand pLDDT is the stratifying variable of Fig. 3. The tested question is whether high confidence separates the physically indefensible predictions from the sound ones; it does not:
  > "This proportion of conserved binding modes is largely independent of the confidence score. For example, among complexes with a confidence score > 80, between 42% and 52% maintain an unaltered ligand pose depending on the disruption applied (compared to 78% unaltered in the absence of disruption)." (p.4)

  The interpretive caveat is stated by the authors themselves: "While it remains to be determined exactly how to interpret these values in the context of individual atoms rather than protein residues, the same general trend appears to hold: scores above 70 indicate high confidence, and scores above 90 reflect very high confidence." (p.4). → tag `confidence-as-discriminator`; this is one of the cleanest negative validations of confidence-as-discriminator in the corpus.

## D. Claims

- **central_conclusion**: Four state-of-the-art co-folding models (AF3, RFAA, Boltz-1, Chai-1) keep placing a ligand in its original crystallographic pocket after that pocket has been made chemically and sterically incapable of binding it — every contacting side chain deleted, replaced with bulky hydrophobes, or reversed in polarity and volume — and after the ligand itself has had its hydrogen-bond donors capped or its net charge inverted from −3 to +3. Funnel metadynamics on the same perturbed systems returns a bound-state probability of exactly zero for the harsher perturbations, so the models' poses are not merely uncertain but physically impossible. The mechanism the authors propose is that MSA and template retrieval return the unperturbed wild-type features regardless of the mutations, so the models are completing a memorised pattern from sequence rather than simulating an interaction — and the confidence heads do not flag it.

- **necessity_claims** (verbatim + page):
  > "Our findings underscore the models' limitations in generalizing effectively across diverse protein-ligand structures and highlight the necessity of integrating robust physical and chemical priors in the development of such predictive tools." (p.1, Abstract)

  > "The results advocate a measured reliance on deep-learning-based models for critical applications in drug discovery and protein engineering, where a deep understanding of the underlying physical and chemical properties is crucial." (p.1, Abstract)

  > "The ability to model physical interactions is crucial for real-world applications, particularly in drug discovery and protein engineering." (p.2)

  > "Ensuring that these models respect physical constraints is therefore vital to their reliability and broader adoption in biomedical research." (p.2)

  **Ignoring physical/chemical impossibility — the core set:**
  > "All four co-folding models continue to predict the ATP-CDK2 complex with the same binding mode, despite the loss of all major side-chain interactions." (p.2)

  > "Although these residues have been removed, most of the co-folding models continue to place the ligand as if those favorable interactions are still present, indicated the models are overfit to this ATP-binding protein system." (p.2)

  > "In an unbiased assessment of the protein-ligand structure, the binding site should be completely packed with the 11 phenylalanine rings, displacing the negatively charged ligand entirely from the pocket. However, even in the cases where the ligand pose is altered, it is still biased towards the ATP-binding pocket." (p.2)

  > "The co-folding models lacked the ability to accurately respond to these mutations and continued to place the ATP molecule in the original binding site and do not significantly alter the binding pose. Once again, there were significant steric clashes between both protein and ligand atoms in several of the predictions, indicating that the full atomistic structure could not be fully resolved in time." (p.3)

  > "Therefore these models still show a strong bias leading to unphysical predictions." (p.3)

  > "By systematically mutating binding site residues or ligand functional groups, we reveal that the models are prone to placing ligands in pockets despite clearly unfavorable electrostatic or steric interactions." (p.6)

  > "These results demonstrate that ligand placement by co-folding methods in significantly altered binding pockets is often physically incorrect." (p.6)

  > "in the case of binding site packing and inversion no binding can be expected in contrast to the co-folding predictions." (p.6)

  > "Our results demonstrate that current co-folding models, while highly accurate across systems contained in the training data, do not function as robust physics-based systems; they can generate non-physical, high-energy structures with considerable confidence. By attempting to displace ligands through the removal of native contacts or the introduction of unfavorable interactions, we observed that all four examined models frequently maintained nearly identical ligand positions, despite lacking meaningful interactions to support them." (p.8)

  > "our study highlights their inconsistencies with fundamental chemical and physical principles." (p.8)

  **Memorised templates / pattern completion rather than simulating interactions — the core set:**
  > "However, deep learning models primarily rely on data-driven pattern recognition, which does not necessarily equate to an understanding of physics." (p.2)

  > "in many cases the ligand is still predicted within the binding site and can adopt a low RMSD pose, indicating that these co-folding models are not predicting poses based on physics of interactions, but rather learning patterns in global protein structures and sequences." (p.3, Fig. 1 caption)

  > "Rather than learning about physical, atomistic interactions, the network seems to simply recognize patterns that are irrelevant for simulating the physical system, such as specific ordering of the sequence." (p.4)

  > "This highlights the tendency of these methods to position ligands based on overall protein sequence rather than physicochemical principles." (p.6)

  > "The failure of these models is unsurprising when considering the featurization they rely on in order to make their predictions. Most of the co-folding models rely heavily on multiple-sequence alignment and 3D template-based input features retrieved from existing data which help the model predict the 3D structure. However, when we introduce small mutations, for example by removing the binding site, it is very likely that the sequence alignment and template search will return exactly the same results as before, as they are still the closest related sequences and structures in the dataset. - **stance**: **PROVISIONAL — user's call.** `contrast (on rigour) + threat`.
  - **contrast**: this is the sharpest available demonstration that a co-folding prediction can be confidently correct-looking and physically impossible, and that MSA/template retrieval is the leak. Any claim we make that a ligand or a pocket *drove* an AF3 prediction has to survive this paper's objection that the model may simply be completing a memorised pattern from sequence. It is the natural citation for why an oracle-leakage audit is needed at all.
  - **threat**: if ligand identity is one of our directional handles, this paper says the handle may be cosmetic — models keep the pose under charge inversion and H-bond ablation. It also sets a control bar (an adversarial perturbation arm plus an orthogonal physics check) that a paper without one will be measured against.
