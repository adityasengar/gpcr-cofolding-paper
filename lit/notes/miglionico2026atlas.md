## B. Scope

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| `system` | GPCR (whole human GPCRome, non-olfactory + olfactory), each in complex with heterotrimeric G protein (Gα + GNB1 + GNG1) |
| `n_targets` | **801 receptors** (393 non-olfactory + 408 olfactory; "Fraction of unique GPCR (total=801)", p34; 393/408/13 in the Fig 1A workflow box, p28) × **13 Gα subunits** = **10,413 receptor–Gα pairs** (5,109 non-OR, p4; 5,304 OR, p22). 796 of the 801 entered the structural phylogeny (5 receptors >2700 aa excluded, p23). Generality claim is genuine at GPCRome scale but confined to one receptor family. |
| `method_class` | co-folding (AF3 prediction of the receptor–heterotrimer complex) **+ other** (supervised ML — TabPFN regression, "Precog3D" — on 184 structural features derived from the AF3 models; p5, p20–22). Not MSA-subsampling, not template-biasing, no MD. |
| `backbones` | **AF3 only** (AlphaFold 3 v3.0.1, run locally; p19). No head-to-head backbone comparison; the comparator is a *coupling* predictor (PRECOGx, retrained), not a structure predictor (p22). Not `multi-backbone`. |

## C. Conformational core

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| `states_generated` | **one.** A single G-protein-bound (active-side) model per receptor–Gα pair. Five AF3 samples were generated per complex from **one seed** and collapsed to one: "we used 0 as the seed for all predictions. Among the 5 models generated for each GPCR-G protein complex, only the one with the best AlphaFold-generated ranking score was considered for further analysis" (pp19–20). No inactive-state, apo-partner, or alternative-state arm exists anywhere in the paper. |
| `structural_priors_used` | Substantial, and mostly legitimate design-time use. (1) **The ML feature set is defined from deposited complexes**: the 15 receptor positions and 28 Gα positions whose pLDDT is used, and the 35 residue-pair contacts whose contact probability is used, are those "in contact with the G protein in at least one structure in [ref 26]" / "according to [ref 26]" (Table 1, p21) — i.e. contacts observed in experimental GPCR–G protein structures from the authors' prior GPCRome analysis. (2) **The 7 C-terminal Gα residues are hard-protected from the pLDDT<70 filter** "since they are known to be important for specificity" (p20). (3) **Residue-numbering schemes derived from structure**: Generic GPCR residue numbers from GPCRdb (p19) and Common Gα Numbering (CGN, Flock et al.) (p5, p21). (4) **1,011 experimental GPCR–Gα structures** were assembled via SIFTS/Pfam (p19) and used throughout for contact analysis, interface comparison and as DockQ references. (5) **Deposited structures used as illustrative anchors**: OR51E2–mini-Gs (PDB 8F76) superimposed on β2AR–Gs (PDB 3SN6) to corroborate the predicted OR interface rewiring (p11, Fig 5D). None of these enter AF3's input beyond the date-fenced template set. |
| `oracle_leakage` | See the seven-route enumeration below. **Verdict: pipeline leakage is limited but real — route 1 (templates on, date-fenced), route 4 (a decision threshold changed on the evaluation arm), route 5 (the DockQ reference selected to maximise the score), and a weak design-level route 7. Routes 2, 3 and 6 are clean.** |
| — route 1: structures as input or template | **PRESENT, date-fenced.** "We considered only templates released before 30 September 2021 (the same cutoff used for the training of AlphaFold 3) and used 0 as the seed for all predictions" (p19). Templates are ON, so pre-2021 active-state GPCR–G protein complexes (the target state) were available to the model for every prediction, including for receptors homologous to the evaluation targets. Controlled at *pair* level for the DockQ arm only: the evaluated structures are those "containing GPCR-G protein pairs without an experimental structure deposited before the same date" (p20) — the pair is new, the receptor may not be. |
| — route 2: state annotations from a curated database driving templates or alignments | **NONE FOUND.** GPCRdb and GproteinDb are used, but only for receptor lists, coupling labels and residue numbering, never for state annotation or template/alignment selection: "The Generic GPCR residue numbers were extracted from GPCRdb" (p19); "the human olfactory receptor list was obtained from GPCRdb" (p19); "We used the common coupling map from GproteinDb to establish a ground truth for GPCR-G protein interactions" (p4). No state-annotated input regime is described in the AF3 protocol (pp19–20). |
| — route 3: cluster labels derived from known states | **NONE FOUND.** The clustering in the paper is unsupervised and downstream of prediction — "unsupervised clustering of interface contacts, which separately cluster ORs and non-OR GPCRs" (p11) and "unsupervised clustering on the average SHAP values" (p6). Neither feeds the prediction. Protocol pp19–20. |
| — route 4: hyperparameters, sweep ranges, seeds or stopping criteria tuned against known states | **PRESENT, on the coupling metric rather than on a conformational state, and with a second threshold changed on the post-cutoff arm.** (a) The positive/negative threshold was swept on the evaluation data: "we evaluated the model on the validation set … We then computed the Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC) using integer thresholds from 1 to 20 (Supplementary Figure 2A) and identified 4 … as the threshold that maximized the MCC" (p22); restated as "The threshold used to discriminate between positive and negative pairs was 4, chosen to maximize the accuracy of the predictions" (p23). Tuning the range 1–20 on the evaluation set is leakage under v3 even though one value is picked globally. (b) **The most revealing instance**: for the arm consisting of pairs with newly deposited structures, the threshold is silently changed from 4 to 1 — "Among these complexes, 82 (85%) are predicted to be positively coupled **if we consider a predicted value greater than 1**" (p8), against the pipeline-wide "coupling score ≥ 4" used everywhere else (p23, p26). (c) The AF3 seed itself is fixed at 0 and not tuned (p19); the pLDDT<70 residue filter (p20) is a fixed literature-standard cutoff, not swept. |
| — route 5: success defined post hoc by RMSD/TM/DockQ to a structure they had | **PRESENT, and the reference is best-selected.** Success in the validation arm is defined entirely by DockQ against deposited structures — "98.6% (210) of the structures achieving at least acceptable quality based on established structural metrics (i.e., DockQ > 0.23)" (p4). The threshold 0.23 is imported from the literature (ref 30), not fitted, which is clean; but the *reference* is chosen to maximise the score: "If more than one experimental structure of the same GPCR-G protein pair was found, **only the one with the highest DockQ was considered**" (p20). That is a best-of-references selection and inflates the reported median and pass rate by an unquantified amount. |
| — route 6: best/worst model labels assigned against a held reference | **NONE FOUND for the model.** Model selection is by AF3's own internal score, blind to any reference: "Among the 5 models generated for each GPCR-G protein complex, only the one with the best AlphaFold-generated ranking score was considered for further analysis" (pp19–20). Cross-reference route 5 for the mirror-image defect on the *reference* side. |
| — route 7: design-level oracle (systems or conditions chosen because the answer is known) | **PRESENT, weak, design-level only — not pipeline leakage.** Two instances. (i) The ML model is told where to look: the pLDDT and contact-probability features are restricted to positions "that are in contact with the G protein in at least one structure in [ref 26]" (Table 1, p21), i.e. the interface is defined from deposited complexes before any prediction is scored. (ii) The 7 Gα C-terminal residues are exempted from the confidence filter because "they are known to be important for specificity" (p20). Neither declares the *answer* for any target in advance; both declare where the answer lives. The prospective wet-lab arm is clean of this — GPRC5A, GPR37 and GPR37L1 were "predicted not to couple to any G proteins" and then tested (p9). |
| `prospective` | **partial.** Prospective on two arms: (a) structural accuracy — the 213 evaluated complexes have references deposited after both the AF3 training cutoff and the template fence of 30 Sep 2021, and their receptor–Gα pair had no pre-cutoff structure (p20); (b) experimental validation — QRFPR, GPR50, GPR3/6/12, GPR55, GPR132, GPRC5A, GPR37, GPR37L1 couplings were predicted then assayed (pp8–9). Retrospective in the ML pipeline: trained on curated coupling labels, with the positive/negative threshold swept on evaluation data (p22) and changed to a different value for the post-cutoff structural arm (p8). Not the authors' own framing — they call the DockQ arm simply "validated … against experimental structures solved after AF3 training" (p14). |
| `state_metric` | **binary predicate + visual only** (dual). Binary: DockQ > 0.23 for "at least acceptable quality", threshold imported from ref 30, stated on p4; a second binary at coupling score > 4 (p23) and, on one arm, > 1 (p8). Visual: the plausibility call on G-protein placement has **no operationalised criterion or threshold** — "98.4% (5,032), were structurally plausible, correctly positioning the G protein at the intracellular, G protein-coupling interface of the receptor. The remaining 77 structures showed the G protein unrealistically docked to the extracellular side" (p4), and per-receptor calls are made from renders: "for most of the complexes, the G protein is docked unrealistically on the receptor's extracellular loops (Figure 4G)" (p9). No RMSD-to-reference is reported anywhere. **No receptor-activation-state predicate of any kind is defined.** |
| `metric_saturation` | **Yes, numerically, in three places.** (1) The DockQ pass-rate ceilings: 210/213 = 98.6% at or above acceptable quality (p4), so the headline validation metric has ~1.4% of range left and cannot separate methods. (2) The structural-plausibility rate ceilings at 98.4% (5,032/5,109; p4). (3) AF3 interaction probability is bounded at 1.0 and **both** positive and negative distributions pile against that bound (Fig 1C, p28), so the primary discriminator saturates for a large share of pairs; pLDDT is likewise bounded at 100 with mass at the edge (Fig 1E, p28). Axis truncation in Fig 1E is a figure defect and is recorded in `hides`, not here. |
| `directional_control` | **Yes — the partner is the handle, and it is the only handle.** The method is instructed which complex to build by naming one of 13 Gα subunits, always co-folded with GNB1 and GNG1 (p19). There is **no ligand handle** (no agonist in any prediction), no nanobody, no state-annotated template, no state-filtered MSA, no seed or subsample-depth handle (single seed 0, p19). **Receptor active state was never verified.** The paper defines no activation predicate — no TM6 outward-displacement measure, no state annotation, no comparison against inactive-state references. The two checks it does run are (i) a topological plausibility call (G protein on the intracellular vs extracellular face, p4, no threshold, see `state_metric`) and (ii) interface DockQ against post-cutoff complexes which happen to be active-state (p4). Neither is an active-state criterion; the receptor's state is assumed from the presence of the G protein. |
| `anti_memorization_design` | **Present and explicitly defined.** Cutoff = **30 September 2021**, defined as AF3's own training cutoff and applied simultaneously to the template set: "We considered only templates released before 30 September 2021 (the same cutoff used for the training of AlphaFold 3)" (p19). Held-out set: "we considered **583 structures** deposited in PDB after 30 September 2021 and containing GPCR-G protein pairs **without an experimental structure deposited before the same date**" (p20), collapsed one-per-pair to **n = 213** predicted complexes (p4). A second, smaller post-cutoff set of **n = 97** GPCR–G protein interactions "whose 3D structures were experimentally determined after training of AF3" is used for the coupling-prediction check (p8). **The two counts do not reconcile with the Discussion**, which says "98% out of 219 structures" (p14) against the Results' 213/210/98.6% (p4) — see `unresolved`. |
| `anti_memorization_control` | **RUN AND ANALYSED — as a figure panel, not merely reported.** Arm 1: DockQ of the AF3 model against the post-cutoff experimental interface, n = 213, median 0.73, 98.6% ≥ 0.23, plotted as Figure 1B ("KDE plot of the DockQ of the AlphaFold 3 predictions with respect to the experimental structures released after the training cutoff", p28) and cited as validation in the Discussion (p14). Arm 2: coupling recall on the 97 post-cutoff structural pairs, 82/97 = 85% predicted positive, at the lowered >1 threshold (p8), with a matched sanity check on the training side ("among the 117 training pairs with a newly resolved structure, 13 are annotated as negatively coupled", p9). **Not `UNPOWERED`** (n = 213 ≫ 10) and the held-out pairs are excluded from the pre-cutoff structure set by construction. **The material defect is the missing baseline**: no matched pre-cutoff DockQ arm is ever reported, so the post-cutoff number is an absolute, not a memorisation-vs-generalisation contrast, and no template-off arm exists to separate template use from generalisation. |
| `controls_run` | see table below |
| `confidence_as_discriminator` | **Yes, and it is the paper's central mechanism — but for *binding*, not for conformational correctness, and only the binding use is validated.** AF3 ipTM, ranking score, pDockQ, max contact probability ("interaction probability") and per-residue pLDDT are all used as discriminators of positive vs negative coupling, and each is validated against experimental coupling labels with a reported ROC AUC: interaction probability AUC 0.771 (p4), ipTM AUC 0.706, 0.735 for class A with a bootstrap significance test (pp4–5), pLDDT H5.11 AUC 0.762 (p5), Rosetta ΔG AUC 0.655 (p5). Confidence is also used as a *filter*: "we removed all the residues with pLDDT <70 … with the exception of the 7 C-terminal residues of the Gα subunit" (p20). **Not validated as a discriminator of structural correctness** — no relationship between any confidence score and DockQ is reported anywhere, and the ipTM = 0.31 vs 0.7 contrast used to call C5AR2 a non-binder (Fig 4H,I, p32) is read as a binding statement with no structural check. |

### `controls_run`

| control | what it rules out | page |
|---|---|---|
| Post-cutoff DockQ arm (n = 213 pairs, refs deposited after 30 Sep 2021, no pre-cutoff structure for that pair) | Verbatim memorisation of the evaluated complex from AF3 training or from the template set | p4, p20, Fig 1B p28 |
| Post-cutoff coupling arm (n = 97 interactions with newly deposited structures) | That the coupling classifier only reproduces its curated training labels | p8 |
| Matched training-side check (117 training pairs with a newly resolved structure, 13 annotated negative) | That structure/binding disagreement is an artefact of the held-out set rather than of the coupling labels themselves | p9 |
| Negative binders as an explicit class (844/1,714 pairs, normalized Emax = 0) | That AF3 confidence is high for every pair regardless of biology — the true-negative problem the Discussion names (p14–15) | p4 |
| Mis-docked predictions cross-checked against coupling data (13 mis-docked pairs with data; 12 confirmed non-couplers) | That extracellular mis-docking is random failure rather than a signal of non-binding | p4 |
| Repeated 5-fold CV **partitioned at the receptor level**, run 5× on independent splits | Receptor-level information leaking between train and test folds | p22 |
| Leave-one-GPCR-out (median accuracy 0.82 A / 0.83 B2 / 0.86 C / 0.64 B1) | Per-receptor memorisation; performance inflated by a few well-represented receptors | p6, Fig 2B |
| Leave-one-G-protein-out (whole coupling group withheld, AUROC > 0.8 for GNAL, GNAS, GNA11, GNAQ, GNA14, GNAI1, GNAI2, GNAO1) | G-protein-specific memorisation; forces the model to infer a coupling group from the others | p6, Fig 2C |
| Independent IUPHAR/GtoPDB test set (316 couplings, 114 pos / 202 neg, 79 receptors "not present in the training set") | Train/test overlap in the coupling labels | p5 |
| PRECOGx retrained on the identical GPCRdb training data | That Precog3D's advantage comes from a different/larger dataset rather than from the structural features | p22, Fig 2A |
| TGFα-shedding dataset "largely removed from GproteinDb … can be regarded as an additional validation set" (Spearman ρ = 0.514) | Circularity with the GproteinDb labels; also calibrates against assay-to-assay noise | p15 |
| Structure-based vs sequence-based phylogeny compared head to head (ρ 0.49 vs 0.22 with ORs; 0.1 vs 0.22 without) | That the structural signal is nothing sequence could not give | p11, p23 |
| Bootstrap significance test on the class-A ipTM correlation | That the class-A subset advantage is a sampling artefact | p4 |
| Wet-lab: empty vector pcDNA3.1 baseline, all reporters normalised to it | Reporter response from transfection/expression rather than receptor activity | pp25–26, Fig 4E |
| Wet-lab: dedicated positive-control receptors per reporter (5-HT4B for CRE, GRM2 for CRE+GsGo, 5-HT2A for NFAT, LPAR2 for SRF-RE), excluded from the statistics | That a null result is reporter failure rather than a genuine non-coupler | p26, p32 |
| Wet-lab: C-terminally truncated Gαq (ΔC) chimera in the TGFα-shedding assay | Coupling signal arising from the Gαq scaffold rather than from the swapped Gα C-terminus | p32 (Fig 4C caption) |
| **NOT RUN** — template-off arm; pre-cutoff DockQ baseline; scrambled/decoy partner arm; ligand-bound vs ligand-free arm; multi-seed arm | Cannot separate template contribution from generalisation; cannot show whether accuracy degrades post-cutoff; cannot show the interface score is specific to the true partner | protocol pp19–20 |

## D. Claims

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| `central_conclusion` | AF3 co-folding of every human GPCR with all 13 Gα subunits in the full heterotrimer, with no ligand, produces interfaces accurate enough (median DockQ 0.73 against post-cutoff structures) that AF3's own confidence and interface descriptors statistically separate experimentally positive from negative couplers; a TabPFN model on those descriptors ("Precog3D") then predicts coupling GPCRome-wide, illuminating 180 previously uncharacterised non-olfactory receptors and 408 olfactory receptors, with the ORs predicted to prefer Gs/GNAL through a distinct, energetically weaker interface. |
| `necessity_claims` | (verbatim) — "In the current study, we have modeled only the binary complex between receptor and heterotrimeric G proteins, considering the **methodological limitations in reliably docking ligands**, and the fact that for orphan GPCRs the information about their ligands is missing." (p17) · "Although AF3 has shown very good performance in predicting biomolecular assemblies, **predicting protein complexes remains an open problem** due to the huge search space of multimeric complex interfaces." (p14) · "Specifically for GPCRs, **ligand-receptor docking through AF is still an open problem**, due to the large variability not yet fully explored in experimental structures, with both successes and failures reported." (p14) · "This task is **hindered by the lack of true negative binder information** affecting most interactomics datasets." (pp14–15) · "yet the coupling specificity of a large fraction of this large receptor family **remains unknown, thereby preventing the development of new GPCR-targeting therapies**." (p1, Abstract) · "The 5 receptors exceeding 2700 amino acids (ADGRG4, ADGRV1, CELSR1, CELSR2, CELSR3) were excluded **due to the unavailability of full-length predictions**." (p23) |
| `novelty_claims` | (verbatim) — "In summary, this study provides the **first computational 3D atlas of the human GPCR-G protein transductome**, thereby illuminating the signaling mechanisms of neglected GPCR classes and providing the basis for interpreting omics datasets from a myriad of pathological conditions, thus enabling the development of novel precision therapeutics." (p2) · "we have used AF3, the state-of-the-art algorithm for protein structure prediction, to forecast the 3D structures of the human GPCRome in complex with heterotrimeric G proteins, thereby providing the **first high-confidence structural map of GPCR transduction mechanisms**, which we used to illuminate large-scale transcriptomics datasets." (p14) · "Additionally, we predict recurrent bindings to Gq/11 and Gi/o proteins for ORs, **suggesting potentially novel ORs signaling mechanisms**." (p2) · "Furthermore, we predicted **novel, secondary couplings** for receptors primarily known to couple to G12/13" (p9) · "The general improvement of performance is **remarkable**, if we consider that Precog3D was trained on the entire set of experimental interactions to predict binding strength, whereas Precog(X) consisted of separate models trained on G protein-specific binding data." (p15) |
| `stated_limits` | Ligand omitted by design and the activation transition not modelled: "In the future, it will certainly be interesting to assess the capability of AF, as well as of our model, to predict G protein coupling bias by also modeling the binding of the ligand as well as the conformational transition linked to the receptor's activation" (p17). No post-translational modifications, no arrestins: "Another important biochemical information missing from the current model is the representation of the complexity of GPCR post-translational modifications … future efforts will also need to address additional intracellular transducers such as arrestins and receptor-modified states" (p17). Gi/o class imbalance: "The predictions for certain G proteins, such as Gi/o members, still pose some difficulties, likely due to the ease of binding for this protein family, which makes the training and testing sets 'positive prone'" (p15). Label noise: "likely reflecting the inherent noise and batch effects in experimental coupling measurements" and "over 20 experimental structures corresponding to interactions in the training set that were reported as negatives in GproteinDb" (pp15–16). Weak phylogeny–coupling link: "we found no overall significant correlation between phylogenetics and coupling preferences" (p7). Five very large receptors excluded (p23). |
| `stance` | **`precedent` + `contrast`** — provisional, the user's call. *Precedent on findings*: the largest published AF3 GPCR–G protein co-folding run, with a real post-cutoff DockQ arm actually plotted, a prospective wet-lab validation arm, and directly reusable interface numbers. *Contrast on rigour*: every one of 10,413 predictions is ligand-free with the receptor's active state never verified by any criterion; templates are on; the decision threshold is swept on the evaluation set and then silently changed from 4 to 1 for the post-cutoff arm; the DockQ reference is chosen to maximise the score; and there is no pre-cutoff baseline against which the post-cutoff number could show degradation. |
