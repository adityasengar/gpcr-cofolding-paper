## B. Scope

- **system**: **GPCR**, specifically GPCR–heterotrimeric G-protein complexes and their intracellular coupling interface. Receptor classes A, B1, B2, C and Frizzled (p.2). Nine Gα proteins in the experimental set: GNAS, GNAI1, GNAI2, GNAI3, GNAT1, GNAO1, GNAQ, GNA11, GNA13 (p.2). One receptor, CCKAR, is taken into wet-lab validation (p.4, p.14).
- **n_targets**: **Four distinct counts; do not collapse them.**
  - *Experimental structural set*: 376 PDB entries met the Pfam criterion, 362 retained after excluding chimeras (p.12), comprising **166 Gs, 184 Gi/o, 9 Gq/11 and 3 G12/13** complexes and "corresponding to 93, 17, 10, 3, and 2 unique receptors from Class A, B1, B2, C and Frizzled, respectively, and entailing 9 different G-proteins" (p.2).
  - *Non-redundant representative set*: **125** GPCR–G-protein pairs, one representative per receptor–G-protein pair chosen by resolution then sequence coverage (p.2, p.13); Fig. 1C breaks these into 73 Gi/o, 43 Gs, 6 Gq/11, 3 G12/13 (p.3).
  - *AlphaFold benchmark*: the same **125** complexes, predicted twice (with and without templates) (p.7, p.13).
  - *GPCRome-wide prediction set*: **996 GPCR–Gα pairs**, "reported to bind in UCM33 ... respectively corresponding to 164 and 13 human GPCRs and Gα proteins (the three members of the transducin family were not considered)" (p.13), filtered down to **825** complexes for analysis (p.8, p.13).
  The generality claim is "GPCRome-wide" but the modelled space is 164 of ~800 human GPCRs and only pairs already reported to couple — see `oracle_leakage` route 7.
- **method_class**: **Compound — co-folding + benchmark-only + other (structural bioinformatics of interfaces, Rosetta multistate design, Rosetta interface energetics).** No new predictor is built. AlphaFold-Multimer is used off the shelf, benchmarked against 125 experimental complexes, then run at scale; the primary analysis is interface contact-network and fingerprint bioinformatics on both experimental and predicted complexes; a Rosetta multistate design arm produces mutants that are then tested in cells.
- **backbones**: **AlphaFold-Multimer v2.3.1 only** (p.13: "We used Alphafold-Multimer v2.3.1 to generate 3D models of GPCR-heterotrimeric G-protein complexes."). Single backbone — **not** `multi-backbone`. Non-AF structural tooling: Rosetta relax, RosettaDock (`docking_local_refine`), Rosetta InterfaceAnalyzer and Rosetta multistate design, all from RosettaCommons version 2021.16.61629 (p.14, p.15).
- **templates**: **DUAL — on for the production run, off in one benchmark ablation arm.** Verbatim, p.13:
  > "We generated 3D models 'using templates', that is allowing AlphaFold-Multimer to use the 3D structural models available in the PDB (they are used only to predict the structure of single chains), and 'without templates', running the predictions setting --max_template_date=01-01-1900 instead, to avoid the usage of any available experimental template."
  Production GPCRome run is templates-on: "We, therefore, ran multimer predictions with available templates for receptor-heterotrimeric G-protein complexes whose receptor-α subunit binary interactions are reported in the UCM dataset." (p.7). **Important qualifier the authors state themselves: templates are monomer-only** — "(they are used only to predict the structure of single chains)" (p.13), so the *interface* is never templated even in the templates-on arm. Templates are **not** state-annotated or curated in any way; no template date cutoff other than the 1900 ablation. → tag `templates-on`.
- **msa_handling**: **full (default AlphaFold-Multimer pipeline).** The words "MSA", "multiple sequence alignment", "subsampl", "cluster the alignment" and "seed" never appear anywhere in the paper in connection with the AF runs; the only MSA-relevant sentence is "The databases required to run AlphaFold-Multimer were downloaded on 12 January 2023." (p.13). No subsampling, no clustering, no state-filtering, no pinning is described. Recorded as full-by-default, with the caveat that the paper never states it.

## C. Conformational core

**Framing note that governs this whole section — this is the single most load-bearing fact in the note.** This paper models GPCR–G-protein complexes with AlphaFold-Multimer, which accepts protein sequences only. The Methods inventory of prediction inputs is complete and contains no small molecule of any kind. The paper **never** measures, names, or verifies the conformational state of any receptor it predicts. Every accuracy metric it reports (DockQ, fraction of native contacts, pDockQ, contact recapitulation) is an **interface-placement** metric, not a receptor-conformation metric. Fields below are answered on those terms and are not forced into a state reading.

- **states_generated**: **one.** Five AF-Multimer models are generated per complex and exactly one is kept, selected by AlphaFold's own ranking score, never by any state criterion — p.13: "Among the 5 models generated for each GPCR-G-protein complex, only the best one was considered for further analysis. The score used to evaluate the models was the default one used by AlphaFold-Multimer (0.2*pTM + 0.8*ipTM)." No ensemble is retained, no alternative state is sought, no comparison of alternative receptor conformations appears anywhere. The experimental-structure arm generates nothing (it analyses deposited PDB entries).

- **structural_priors_used**: **Extensive, deliberate, and mostly not a defect.**
  - The 362 deposited GPCR–G-protein complexes are the reference corpus, retrieved via SIFTS/Pfam mapping (p.12).
  - The 125 non-redundant representatives are the benchmark reference set (p.2, p.13).
  - GPCRdb generic numbering (ref. 31) and the Common G-protein Numbering scheme (ref. 32) provide the residue coordinate system for all contact analysis: "We then mapped contacting residues to consensus numbering through GPCRdb31 (Supplementary Data 2) and the common G-protein numbering (CGN)32 schemes" (p.2). These are **numbering** schemes here, not state annotations.
  - The RMSD/RMSF superposition frame is defined from what is solved in every structure: "We considered 141 GPCR and 73 G-protein consensus positions defining respectively the consensus core of the 7TM domain and the Ras GTPase domain solved in all experimental structures." (p.15); RMSF references are the group centroids, PDB 8E3X (Gs) and 8F7S (Gi/o) (p.15).
  - The multistate design starts from solved complexes of the same receptor in two coupling states: "As a starting template for the design, we have chosen CCKAR (UniProt Accession: P32238), a cholecystokinin receptor that has been solved in complex with Gs (PDB ID: 7EZK), Gi/o (PDB ID: 7EZH") and Gq/11 (PDB ID: 7EZM)52." (p.14). Positive and negative design states are literally these two PDB entries: "Next, we designated positive state and negative state complexes (7ezk or 7ezh)." (p.14).
  - Coupling labels are priors too, taken from the Universal/Common Coupling Map (UCM, ref. 33) and from GEMTA / Shedding / GtoPDB (Fig. 3 caption, p.5).

- **oracle_leakage**: **Present on five of seven routes; clean on two. All of it is oracle knowledge about the *interface / coupling label*, none of it about a *receptor conformational state*, because the paper never defines a receptor state to leak.**

  **Route 1 — deposited structures used as input or template: PRESENT.** The production GPCRome run is templates-on with no date cutoff, so a receptor whose active-state monomer is deposited gets that monomer as a template. p.7: "We, therefore, ran multimer predictions with available templates for receptor-heterotrimeric G-protein complexes whose receptor-α subunit binary interactions are reported in the UCM dataset." And the same applies to the 125-complex benchmark templates-on arm, where the reference structure's own monomers are in the template database. **Mitigation, stated by the authors:** templates act on single chains only — "(they are used only to predict the structure of single chains)" (p.13) — and the ablation arm shows the interface accuracy barely depends on them (DockQ 0.664 vs 0.645, p.7).

  **Route 2 — state annotations from a curated database driving templates or alignments: NONE FOUND.** GPCRdb is used, but only as a residue-numbering scheme for contact mapping and for the snake-plot rendering in Fig. 5D (p.2, p.8, p.13); it never selects templates and never modifies an alignment. Protocol described p.12–p.13 ("Data sets", "GPCR-Gα complexes prediction via AlphaFold-Multimer") — the only inputs named are sequences, and the only template control is the `--max_template_date` flag. No KLIFS/Kincore analogue and no GPCRdb state annotation (active/inactive) is used anywhere.

  **Route 3 — cluster labels derived from known states: PRESENT, in the form of coupling labels rather than conformational states, and it is upstream of an analysis the paper calls "unsupervised".** The contacts fed to the fingerprint clustering are pre-selected using experimentally known coupling labels via a log-odds ratio computed over "coupled / not coupled" contingency counts. p.2:
  > "We also estimated the contact positions that are more frequent than expected in each coupling group through log-odds ratio statistics (see Methods), and we used this information to filter the most informative contacts for Gs and Gi/o couplings (Fig. 3). CF clustering identifies two main clusters: the largest one (cluster 1), is enriched with Gs complexes ... The second cluster (i.e. cluster 2), is enriched almost exclusively with Gi/o complexes"
  The LOR itself is label-driven, p.14: "CC and CN terms represent the number of GPCRs coupled to a specific Gprotein group (Gi/o or Gs) that are or are not, respectively, in contact at that position". Filter thresholds are stated in the Fig. 3 caption (p.5): "Only contacts present in at least 10% of the structures and having an absolute LOR value greater than 2 are considered." **Mitigating control the authors run:** the unfiltered clustering is also reported and gives a different answer — "When relaxing the criteria to perform CF clustering by removing the log-odds ratio filter and by including all unique complexes, the clustering is now mainly guided by the receptor class" (p.2, Supplementary Fig. 2). That control is exactly the right one and it shows the Gs/Gi split in Fig. 3 is partly a product of the label-driven filter.

  **Route 4 — hyperparameters, sweep ranges or stopping criteria tuned against known states: PRESENT, twice, and both are thresholds read directly off the reference structures.** p.13, verbatim:
  > "we kept only models with ΔSASA ≥ 1500 Å2, which is slightly less than the minimum ΔSASA found in all experimental structures. In this way we removed 32 more structures"
  and
  > "We removed 21 predicted complexes (975 structures left) based on unrealistic complex topologies, i.e. not having any of the following G-protein's H5 positions, i.e. H5.16, H5.19, H5.20, H5.23, H5.24, H5.25, that we found to most recurrently mediate contacts with GPCR's residues in experimental structures."
  Both filters are calibrated on the experimental complexes and then applied to the predicted ones, which is leakage of the reference set into the composition of the analysed prediction set. The third filter, pDockQ ≥ 0.23, is taken from the literature and not tuned here: "We kept only the structures with pDockQ ≥ 0.2348 (118 out of 975 structures removed)36." (p.13). Note also that the pLDDT < 70 residue-trimming threshold is asserted, not justified against anything (p.13).

  **Route 5 — success defined post hoc by RMSD/DockQ to a structure they had: PRESENT and unavoidable by construction.** The entire AF benchmark is scored against the 125 deposited complexes. p.7:
  > "We first benchmarked AF-multimer by assessing the prediction performances with or without 3D templates for 125 representative GPCR-G-protein complexes with available experimental structures. Using 3D templates in the prediction slightly improves the performances, assessed by measuring the deviation of predicted vs. experimental interfaces via either the DockQ metric (Wilcoxon P = 0.021; Fig. 9A) or the fraction of native contacts (Wilcoxon P = 0.004; Fig. 9B)."
  The composite filter is itself justified against the same reference: "Such a filtering scheme improves the correlation between prediction scores (pDockQ) and distance from experimental structures (DockQ) in the benchmark (Fig. 9C, D)." (p.7). This is a benign, disclosed instance — the reference is the object of the benchmark — but it is route 5.

  **Route 6 — best/worst model labels assigned against a held reference: NONE FOUND.** Model selection is by AlphaFold's own confidence, never against the reference. Protocol, p.13: "Among the 5 models generated for each GPCR-G-protein complex, only the best one was considered for further analysis. The score used to evaluate the models was the default one used by AlphaFold-Multimer (0.2*pTM + 0.8*ipTM)." No best-of-N-by-DockQ appears anywhere.

  **Route 7 — design-level oracle use (weaker than pipeline leakage; kept distinct): PRESENT and explicit, on both the prediction and the design arms.**
  - The prediction set is defined by pairs whose answer is already known to be "they couple": "A total of 996 GPCR-Gɑ pairs, reported to bind in UCM33, were considered" (p.13); "we predicted through AlphaFold-multimer (v2.3)37 996 GPCR - G-protein alpha subunit pairs from UCM binary interactions" (p.7). No non-coupling pair is modelled as a negative control, so the pipeline is never asked to fail.
  - The wet-lab target is chosen because both answers are deposited: CCKAR "has been solved in complex with both Gs (PDB ID: 7EZK) and Gi/o (PDB ID: 7EZH)" (p.4).
  - The expected direction of the designed mutations is declared before assay: "in one hand we sought to retain Gs while removing Gi/o couplings, and on the other, we maintained Gi/o while reducing binding to Gs" (p.4). This one is mitigated — the prediction is then tested in cells and mostly fails (5 of 8 mutations had no effect, p.5), which is the honest outcome.
  → tags `oracle-leak` and `design-level-oracle`.

- **prospective**: **partial.** *Retrospective* in the prediction pipeline: every accuracy number is scored against structures the authors already had, the model-filtering thresholds are calibrated on those structures, and the modelled pairs are pairs already reported to couple. *Prospective* in one arm only: the eight designed CCKAR mutants are new sequences with no prior structure or assay, tested blind in the NanoBiT assay, and the result was mostly negative (2 Gs-biased, 1 Gi-biased, 5 no effect; p.5). The 825 predicted complexes for Gq/11 and G12/13 are prospective in the weak sense that few reference structures exist (9 Gq/11 and 3 G12/13 experimental complexes, p.2) — but nothing about those predictions is validated.

- **state_metric**: **NOT REPORTED.** No geometric criterion for receptor activation is defined, applied, or mentioned anywhere in the paper. There is no TM6 outward-displacement measurement, no TM3–TM6 (3.50–6.34) distance, no NPxxY/DRY microswitch check, no comparison of any predicted receptor against an inactive-state structure, and no active/inactive labelling of any model. The quantities that *are* measured are interface-placement and energetics metrics, and they are worth listing so the absence is not mistaken for sparse reporting:
  - **DockQ** (continuous, 0–1) of predicted vs experimental interface (p.7, Fig. 9A);
  - **fraction of native contacts** (continuous, 0–1) (p.7, Fig. 9B);
  - **pDockQ** ≥ 0.23 as a model-quality gate (p.13);
  - **pLDDT** ≥ 70 as a per-residue trimming gate (p.13);
  - **ΔSASA** ≥ 1500 Å² as a topology gate (p.13);
  - **Cβ–Cβ < 8 Å** (Cα for glycine) as the contact predicate — the paper's one true binary predicate, and it is an interface predicate: "We considered as contacts all the pairs of amino acids with less than 8 Å between their Cβ (Cɑ for glycine - see below), following standard practices employed for contact analysis in structural predictions46." (p.12);
  - **RMSD** of Gα core Cα after superposition on the GPCR 7TM core, and **RMSF** about the group centroid (p.15) — these measure *G-protein docking-mode* variability, not receptor conformation.
  In the schema's terms the paper is `binary predicate (8 Å contact) + continuous coordinate (DockQ / fnat / pDockQ / RMSD / ΔG)` **for the interface**, and `NOT REPORTED` for conformational state. Recording the latter as NOT REPORTED is the informative answer.

- **metric_saturation**: **No numeric saturation in any arm reported.** DockQ and fraction-of-native-contacts are both bounded on [0,1]; the templates-on and templates-off violins in Fig. 9A/B sit around 0.65–0.70 and ~0.8 respectively with tails reaching neither 0 nor 1 (p.12), so neither floors nor ceilings. pDockQ is bounded [0,1] and its plotted range in Fig. 9C/D is ~0.0–0.6 (p.12). Rosetta REU and ΔSASA are unbounded. The **fingerprint vectors are binary by construction** (1 if contact present, 0 otherwise, p.13) — this is a design choice, not a saturating metric, but it means contact *strength* is discarded and any interface can only ever be described as present/absent. Figure-level truncation and pre-filtering are recorded in `hides`, not here.

- **directional_control**: **Yes — one handle only: the identity of the co-folded G-protein α subunit.** The user chooses which Gα sequence to hand AlphaFold-Multimer alongside a fixed GNB1/GNG1 heterotrimer, and that choice is the only thing that differs between a "Gs complex" and a "Gi complex" model. p.13: "The human canonical protein sequences of GNB1 and GNG1 were used as the β and γ subunits, respectively." No other handle is available or used: **no ligand, no agonist, no nanobody, no state-annotated template, no state-filtered MSA, no seed control, no subsample depth.** Templates can be switched on or off (p.13) but are monomer-only and are not state-selected, so they do not steer state. Whether this handle actually controls the *receptor's* conformation is never tested — the paper only ever shows it changes the interface contacts (Fig. 9E, p.12). → tag `partner-driven`.

- **anti_memorization_design**: **NONE.** There is no held-out set, no post-cutoff set, and no cutoff of any kind defined against AlphaFold's training data. The only date in the pipeline is the database download date — "The databases required to run AlphaFold-Multimer were downloaded on 12 January 2023." (p.13) — and the PDB harvest date, "over 360 complex structures deposited in the PDB (as of March 2023)" (p.2) with a SIFTS mapping from the "2023/01/28 update" (p.12). AlphaFold-Multimer v2.3's own training cutoff is never stated, so training-set membership of the 125 benchmark complexes is neither excluded nor quantified. → tag `no-anti-memorization`.

- **anti_memorization_control**: **NONE RUN.** The templates-off arm (n = 125, `--max_template_date=01-01-1900`, p.13) is the nearest thing and it is a genuine, analysed control arm — but it ablates *template lookup*, not *weight memorization*, and the authors do not claim otherwise. With the training cutoff unstated, the 0.645 mean DockQ obtained without templates cannot be read as evidence against memorization; it is equally consistent with the complexes being in the training set. No arm addresses memorization.

- **controls_run**:

  **Absent controls worth naming:** no non-coupling (decoy) GPCR–Gα pair is ever modelled; no shuffled or scrambled partner arm; no apo-receptor (no-G-protein) arm; no arm in which the same receptor is co-folded with two different Gα subunits and the *receptor* conformation compared.

- **confidence_as_discriminator**: **Yes for interface plausibility, and partially validated; never used for conformational state.** Three confidence quantities gate the pipeline: AlphaFold's ranking score (0.2·pTM + 0.8·ipTM) picks 1 of 5 models (p.13); pLDDT < 70 residues are deleted before contact analysis (p.13); pDockQ ≥ 0.23 removes 118 of 975 models (p.13). The validation is a correlation against DockQ on the 125-complex benchmark, and it is reported as improving with filtering — Fig. 9C/D legends give default score r = 0.485 (full structure) → 0.618 (filtered), and pDockQ r = 0.480 → 0.621 (p.12), with the text claiming "Such a filtering scheme improves the correlation between prediction scores (pDockQ) and distance from experimental structures (DockQ) in the benchmark (Fig. 9C, D)." (p.7). That validates confidence as a proxy for *interface accuracy* only. It is never shown, or claimed, to discriminate conformational correctness. → tag `confidence-as-discriminator`.

### C-bis. The ligand / activation-state question, stated precisely

Because this is the point the note exists to settle, the three sub-questions are answered separately, and the distinction between what is *established* and what is *merely absent or asserted* is kept sharp.

**(a) Was a ligand included in the AlphaFold-Multimer models? No — but the paper never says so. The finding is an absence of mention, not a statement.** The complete Methods inventory of prediction inputs, verbatim, p.13:
> "We used Alphafold-Multimer v2.3.1³⁷ to generate 3D models of GPCR-heterotrimeric G-protein complexes. The human canonical protein sequences of GNB1 and GNG1 were used as the β and γ subunits, respectively. We trimmed the N-terminal part of the GPCRs up to 50 residues before the start of TM1, to avoid folding the long extracellular portion of some receptors. The databases required to run AlphaFold-Multimer were downloaded on 12 January 2023."
No small molecule, agonist, ligand or cofactor is named there or anywhere else in the prediction protocol. The words "ligand" and "agonist" appear in the paper only in three unrelated places: the introduction's background statement "Receptor's conformational change upon ligand binding leads to recognition and activation of intracellular G-proteins." (p.1); the background remark "The abundance of experimental structures for GPCRs in ligand-dependent, alternative functional states has also shed light on the structural hallmarks controlling receptor activation" (p.2); and the wet-lab assay, "subjected to the NanoBiT-G-protein-dissociation assay using CCK-8 as a ligand" (p.8) — a cell assay, not a model. **There is no sentence in this paper acknowledging that the models are ligand-free, and no sentence discussing what that might cost.**

**(b) What conformational state did the resulting receptor models adopt? The paper never says, for the models.** The only state language in the paper is about the **nucleotide state of the G protein**, is applied to the *deposited* structures, and is extended to the predictions only by the loosest implication. The two relevant sentences, verbatim:
> "In the present study, we have performed a computational, comparative analysis of 3D GPCR-G-protein complexes in their nucleotide-free state, to identify structural hallmarks of the interaction interfaces that might be linked to coupling specificity." (p.8, opening of the Discussion)

> "A limitation of this study is that most of the structures considered are in the nucleotide-free state. Indeed, previous studies have suggested that intermediate states other than the nucleotide-free complexes influence G-protein-coupling selectivity41." (p.11)
Note carefully what these do *not* say: neither refers to the receptor's activation state, neither is a measurement, and the second says "most of the structures considered", which is a statement about the deposited corpus. **No receptor model is ever assigned a conformational state anywhere in the paper.**

**(c) Were active-state hallmarks such as TM6 outward displacement verified, and by what criterion? No. Not by any criterion, quantitative or visual.** TM6 appears in the paper only as (i) a node in the interface contact networks and a element of the TM5/TM6 selectivity filter inherited from prior work — "Interface contact analysis recapitulates structural hallmarks associated with G-protein-coupling specificity, including TM5, TM6 and ICLs." (p.1) — and (ii) a source of contact positions such as 6.33/6.39/6.40. No TM6 displacement, no TM3–TM6 distance, no rotamer microswitch, and no comparison to an inactive-state reference is computed for any model. The **only** agreement check performed on the predicted structures is at the level of interface contacts and interface quality, verbatim, p.8:
> "Contact analysis performed on predicted complexes revealed patterns similar to those observed experimentally, in particular, the models recapitulated most of the experimental contacts of Gs and Gi/o complexes (Fig. 9E)."
plus the DockQ / fraction-of-native-contacts benchmark on p.7 quoted above. Recapitulating intracellular interface contacts against active-state reference complexes is consistent with the models being active-like, but the paper neither measures that nor claims it. **Do not read this paper as evidence that a G protein alone drives the active state in a predictor; read it as a paper in which the question was never posed.** What it does establish, quantitatively, is that ligand-free, sequence-only co-folding of receptor + Gαβγ reproduces the deposited nucleotide-free complex interface to a mean DockQ of 0.645 without templates and 0.664 with them, across 125 complexes (p.7).

## D. Claims

- **central_conclusion**: Interface contact fingerprints of GPCR–G-protein complexes carry coupling-specificity information: Gs and Gi/o complexes differ systematically in which secondary-structure elements engage (TM5/ICL2/H8 vs TM3/TM6/ICL1), Gs interfaces are more conserved and more energetically stable while Gi/o adopt a wider range of docking poses, and mutating a handful of fingerprint positions in a promiscuous receptor (CCKAR) can bias its coupling in cells. AlphaFold-Multimer, given only receptor and G-protein sequences, reproduces these interface features well enough (mean DockQ 0.645–0.664 on 125 references) to extend the analysis to 825 modelled complexes and to G-protein families with almost no structural coverage (Gq/11, G12/13).

- **necessity_claims** (verbatim + page):
  - "The experimental profiling of specific coupling preferences is critical to understanding GPCR biology and pharmacology." (p.1)
  - "Yet, a comprehensive picture of the structural hallmarks of coupling specificity remains elusive." (p.2)
  - "This score is indicated in Rosetta Energy Units (REU) and cannot be converted into the actual binding energy, but it gives a reasonable estimation of the stability of the complex56." (p.15) — an explicit impossibility claim about their own energetic metric.
  - "Structural features and energetics are certainly not the only factors governing the evolutionary success of a certain coupling" (p.11) — an explicit negative claim.
  - "This knowledge will be key to design better-biased drugs, able to modulate only certain transducers, as well as it will be leveraged to improve the design of novel chemogenetic probes such as DREADDs." (p.11)
  - Nearest thing to a necessity claim about ligands, and it is background, not method: "Receptor's conformational change upon ligand binding leads to recognition and activation of intracellular G-proteins." (p.1) — the paper states this and then models without a ligand, without comment.

- **novelty_claims**: **NOT REPORTED — no claim of being first, novel or unprecedented is made anywhere about this work.** The words "first" and "unprecedented" appear only about others' work ("The determination of the first structures of Gi coupled receptor complexes", p.2) or in funding text ("My First AIRC Grant", p.17); "novel" appears only about future DREADD design (p.11). The strongest self-positioning sentences are expansion claims, quoted for completeness:
  - "AlphaFold2 predictions of experimental binary complexes confirm several of these structural features and allow us to augment the structural coverage of poorly characterized complexes such as G12/13." (p.1, abstract)
  - "The usage of a state-of-art AI model (i.e. AlphaFold-multimer37) for structural prediction also allowed us to expand the structural repertoire of GPCR-G-protein complexes. This is particularly valuable for poorly characterized groups, such as G12/13 ones." (p.10)
  - "It must be emphasized that even without templates multimer's predictions achieve almost comparable performances, yielding an average DockQ score of 0.645 vs. 0.664 obtained for predictions with templates (Fig. 9A)." (p.7)

- **stated_limits**:
  - The nucleotide-state limitation, verbatim (p.11): "A limitation of this study is that most of the structures considered are in the nucleotide-free state. Indeed, previous studies have suggested that intermediate states other than the nucleotide-free complexes influence G-protein-coupling selectivity41. Nucleotide-decoupled G proteins mutants, which bypass the intermediate-state complexes, are characterized by a degradation of coupling selectivity, indirectly highlighting the importance of the conformational dynamics of these intermediate-state complexes in guiding selectivity42. Future structural studies systematically targeting the intermediate states of GPCR-G protein complexes will improve the understanding of coupling selectivity."
  - Sparse structural coverage of the minor families limits the predicted-vs-experimental agreement: "As expected, the agreement of contact patterns from experimental and predicted Gq/11 and G12/13 complexes is lower due to fewer structural templates available for this coupling groups (Fig. 9E)." (p.8)
  - Rosetta REU is not a physical binding energy (p.15, quoted above).
  - Structure and energetics are not the whole story (p.11, quoted above).
  - Anticipated succession by better methods: "The greater availability of experimental complex structures and increasingly accurate predicted models, including context-aware and diffusion-based deep learning techniques to predict alternative conformational states, will allow us to better understand the structural basis of G-protein-coupling specificity in the future." (p.11)
  - **Not stated as a limit anywhere:** the absence of ligand from the models, the absence of any receptor conformational-state check, the calibration of two model filters on the reference set, and the label-driven contact filter upstream of the "unsupervised" clustering.

- **stance**: **`background` + `contrast` (provisional — the user's call).**
  - *background*: it is the reference GPCRome-scale demonstration that AlphaFold-Multimer, given sequence only and no ligand, reproduces deposited GPCR–G-protein interfaces at DockQ ≈ 0.65 and that the resulting models carry usable coupling-specificity signal. Useful as the prior state of the art for partner-driven GPCR co-folding and as a source of comparator numbers.
  - *contrast on rigour*: the paper models 825 receptor–G-protein complexes without a ligand and never once measures, states or verifies what conformational state the receptors are in. It also calibrates two model-acceptance thresholds on the very structures it benchmarks against, and pre-selects the contacts fed to its "unsupervised" clustering using experimentally known coupling labels.
