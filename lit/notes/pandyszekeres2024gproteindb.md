# pandyszekeres2024gproteindb

**Framing note that governs the whole note.** This is a **database resource paper** in the
*Nucleic Acids Research* Database issue. It builds no method, runs no benchmark, and scores
no prediction against any reference. AlphaFold2-Multimer is used off the shelf, at scale, to
populate a web resource. Section C is therefore largely `NOT APPLICABLE` or `NOT REPORTED`
**by the nature of the artefact, not by sloppy reading** — each such entry carries one line
saying why. Its value to the corpus is as **the reference resource that supplies curated
GPCR–G protein coupling annotations and 5,595 modelled GPCR–Gα complexes** that other papers
we hold consume. Do not read a methods paper into it.

## B. Scope

- **system**: **GPCR** — specifically GPCR–heterotrimeric G protein complexes and their intracellular coupling interface. Receptor classes A (Rhodopsin), B1 (Secretin), B2 (Adhesion), C (Glutamate), F (Frizzled) and T (Taste 2) (p.5). "There are 16 human G proteins grouped in four families" — Gs (Gs, Golf), Gi/o (Gi1, Gi2, Gi3, Go, Gz, Gt1, Gt2, Ggust), Gq/11 (Gq, G11, G14, G15) and G12/13 (G12, G13) (p.1).
- **n_targets**: **Five distinct counts; do not collapse them.**
  - *Modelled interactome*: **425 human GPCRs × 16 human Gα subunits = 6,800 theoretical complexes**, of which **5,595 (82%)** have released models (p.5, p.8).
  - *Primary-transducer complex models with Gαβγ*: **1,121** (p.4, Results) — but the Methods on p.3 say **1,124** ("1124 such models completed at time of writing"). See `unresolved` item 1.
  - *Experimental structures curated*: "There are currently 690 G protein structures, whereof 544 are structures of GPCR - G protein complexes." (p.4). The interface tool previously covered **527** structures (p.6).
  - *Coupling-agreement analyses*: **50** shared GPCRs (Fig. 4A), **18** GPCRs with constitutive couplings (Fig. 4B), **66** shared GPCRs giving **79** GPCR–primary-transducer combinations (Fig. 4C–D) (pp.7–8).
  - *Previous release for contrast*: **3,081** MODELLER homology models (p.8).
- **method_class**: **other (curated database resource) + co-folding (production use of an off-the-shelf predictor).** No new predictor, no new sampling scheme, no benchmark. AlphaFold2-Multimer is run at interactome scale and the outputs are served through a web platform with visualisation and analysis tools. `benchmark-only` is explicitly **wrong**: nothing is benchmarked.
- **backbones**: **AlphaFold2 only** — "All models were built using AlphaFold2 version 2.3.1 with the Multimer option and amber relaxation (27)." (p.3). Single backbone, **not** `multi-backbone`. Other structural tooling: MODELLER for refining experimental structures (p.3); D3/Javascript, Python/Django for visualisation (p.3).
- **templates**: **on, with a date cutoff, not state-annotated.** "Structural templates were included up to 1 January 2023." (p.3). No template curation, no state annotation, no per-target template selection is described. → tag `templates-on`. Note the **reverse direction** as well: the AF2M models are themselves used as templates to complete experimental structures — "instead of using experimental structures of other receptors, the AlphaFold2-Multimer model of the same receptor is used as swap-in template to fill in missing coordinates of Gα proteins and receptors" (p.3).
- **msa_handling**: **NOT REPORTED.** The strings "MSA", "multiple sequence alignment", "subsample", "cluster" (of alignments) and "sequence database" do not appear anywhere in pp.1–8 in connection with the AF2M runs. Default AF2-Multimer v2.3.1 behaviour is the only reasonable inference and is therefore **not** recorded as a value. The only sequence engineering described is at the *input construct* level: ICL3 shortened to 14 residues for class A, GPS-site and N-terminal truncations for class B2, and VFTD deletion for class C (p.3).

## C. Conformational core

**Section framing.** This resource produces one G protein-bound complex model per GPCR–Gα pair
and never names, assigns, or verifies a receptor conformational state. It also never compares
a model to an experimental structure. Most of section C's machinery — which exists to audit
state-prediction claims — therefore has no target here. That absence is itself the corpus-relevant
finding and is recorded, not papered over.

- **states_generated**: **one.** One AF2-Multimer complex model per GPCR–Gα (or GPCR–Gαβγ) pair is released. No ensemble is retained, no alternative receptor conformation is sought, and no second state of any receptor appears anywhere. **How many AF2M models were generated per complex, and by what rule one was chosen for release, is NOT REPORTED** — the words "seed", "recycle", "ranked" and "5 models" do not appear (pp.3, 5). The receptor conformation in these models is never named: all models are Gα-bound and therefore *implicitly* active-like, but the paper never states or checks this. → tag `single-state`.

- **structural_priors_used**: **Extensive, disclosed, and not a defect — this is what a resource paper is made of.**
  - *Deposited structures as AF2M templates*: "Structural templates were included up to 1 January 2023." (p.3).
  - *The PDB itself*: "The Structures page is updated quarterly with new structures from the Protein Data Bank (structures from RCSB (43) and SIFTs data from PDBe (14))" (p.4), giving 690 G protein structures / 544 GPCR–G protein complexes (p.4).
  - *Refined experimental structures*, built with MODELLER and completed using the AF2M models of the same receptor as swap-in templates (p.3) — a deliberate model→structure feedback that the note flags under `oracle_leakage` route 1.
  - *Curated coupling datasets choose the modelled partner*: "We build models of GPCRs with the heterotrimeric G proteins that have been identified as the primary transducer from one of the quantitative coupling datasets in GproteinDb" (p.3). The datasets are GtoPdb (1), Inoue TGFα shedding (5), Inoue NanoBiT-G (5), Bouvier GEMTA/EMTA (4), Martemyanov FreeGβγ-Nluc (25) and Lambert RGB-GDP (22–24) (pp.2, 4).
  - *GPCRdb* supplies the 425-receptor set and the receptor generic residue numbering (pp.3, 4, 5).
  - *Common Gα numbering (CGN) scheme* (33) supplies the G protein residue coordinate system used by the superposition tool and every interface analysis (pp.3, 4).
  - *Interaction definitions* are imported wholesale from the GPCRdb structure-analysis platform: "strict and loose definitions, respectively (from (46), defined in a tool tip)" (pp.5, 6).

- **oracle_leakage**: **Answered per route. There is no held-out reference set and no scored prediction anywhere in this paper, so routes 5 and 6 have nothing to leak into. The one genuine circularity is in the *coupling-agreement* analysis, not in the structure pipeline.**

  **Route 1 — deposited structures used as input or template: PRESENT, by design, and bidirectional.** Templates are on to 1 January 2023 (p.3), so any GPCR–G protein complex deposited before that date can template its own model. The reverse leak is stated explicitly and is more unusual — the models are then used to complete the experimental structures they were templated from:
  > "We updated our refined complex structures from (11) to use our AlphaFold2-Multimer complex models (next section). Hence, instead of using experimental structures of other receptors, the AlphaFold2-Multimer model of the same receptor is used as swap-in template to fill in missing coordinates of Gα proteins and receptors." (p.3)

  This matters downstream: the "refined structures" served by GproteinDb are **part experimental, part AF2M model**, and the interface interactions counted on them (Fig. 3D) include model-derived atoms. Anyone consuming GproteinDb refined structures as ground truth is consuming AF2M output in part.

  **Route 2 — state annotations from a curated database driving templates or alignments: NONE FOUND.** No state annotation of any kind enters the pipeline. GproteinDb's own curated annotations that *are* used are **coupling** annotations (which partner to model, p.3) and **residue-numbering** schemes (GPCRdb generic numbers, CGN, pp.3–4) — neither is a conformational-state label. The full model-building protocol is on p.3 ("Building GPCR–G protein complex structure models") and names only: sequences, the Multimer option, amber relaxation, a template date cutoff, and construct truncations. Note for the corpus: the *sister* resource GPCRdb does hold state annotations — ref. 2 is titled "GPCRdb in 2023: state-specific structure models using AlphaFold2" (p.9) — but **GproteinDb's models here carry none**. See `unresolved` item 7.

  **Route 3 — cluster labels derived from known states: NONE FOUND.** No clustering of structures or models is performed anywhere. Protocol pp.2–3.

  **Route 4 — hyperparameters, thresholds or stopping criteria tuned against known answers: PRESENT, twice, and the second is genuine circularity.**

  *(4a) Model quality thresholds were set by eye on the outputs themselves.* Verbatim, p.3:
  > "We established thresholds for pTM ≥0.5 and ipTM ≥0.2, both ranging from 0 to 1, with 1 signifying a highly accurate model. This choice was guided by our examination of the relative positioning of the Gα and GPCR, with emphasis on preventing loop knots. A pTM cutoff of ≥0.5 indicated good overall model quality and enabled us to adopt a more permissive ipTM (≥0.2). This approach ensured the expected positioning of the Gα relative to the GPCR."

  The only external justification offered is for pTM ≥0.5: "These cut-offs are consistent with the levels of significance on the TM scores identified in previous studies assessing protein fold similarity (28)." (p.3). The ipTM ≥0.2 and the PAE-mean ≤26 Å cutoffs ("with PAE scores ranging from 0 to 31.75 Å, we established a cut-off of 26 Å for the PAE mean score of compared residues (even if the ipTM score was satisfactory)", p.3) rest on the authors' visual inspection. **No experimental structure is used to calibrate any of them** — so this is tuning against an *expectation*, not against a reference set, which is weaker than classical leakage but leaves the gate unvalidated.

  *(4b) The coupling cutoff was chosen to maximise the agreement that is later reported as a result.* Methods, p.2:
  > "For the Inoue TGFα shedding dataset we also applied a cutoff log(Emax/EC50 ) ≥ 7.0, which were based on the largest agreement with couplings and non-couplings determined by both the Bouvier GEMTA and Martemyanov FreeGβγ-Nluc datasets."

  Discussion, p.6, reporting that same agreement as a finding:
  > "To account for excessive signal amplification from the TGF-α shedding biosensor, we applied a filter requiring log(Emax/EC50 ≥ 7.0). Notably, this achieves an agreement of 94% for couplings and 82% for non-couplings between Gq and GPCRs compared to the EMTA/GEMTA and FreeGβγ-Nluc datasets (Figure 4A)."

  The threshold was selected to maximise agreement with those two datasets and the resulting agreement with those two datasets is then presented as a headline number and as Figure 4A. The 94% / 82% are therefore **fitted, not measured**, and should never be quoted as independent cross-biosensor concordance. → tag `oracle-leak` (scoped to the coupling analysis, not to a structure benchmark).

  **Route 5 — success defined post hoc by RMSD or TM to a structure they had: NONE FOUND.** Model acceptance is defined entirely by AlphaFold's own self-confidence (pTM, ipTM, PAE mean) and by pLDDT visualisation (p.3, p.5). The strings "RMSD", "TM-score to", "DockQ" and "reference structure" never appear in an evaluative sense; the only TM discussion is the definition of pTM/ipTM (p.3). **No model is ever compared to an experimental complex anywhere in the paper.**

  **Route 6 — best/worst model labels assigned against a held reference: NONE FOUND.** No model is labelled good or bad relative to any experimental structure; there is no per-model accuracy claim at all (pp.3, 5).

  **Route 7 — design-level oracle use: PRESENT but mild, and the authors run the non-oracle arm alongside it.** For the 1,121/1,124 heterotrimeric models the partner is chosen because it is the *experimentally determined* primary transducer — "the heterotrimeric G proteins that have been identified as the primary transducer from one of the quantitative coupling datasets in GproteinDb" (p.3). That is answer-conditioned system selection. **However**, the far larger 5,595-model arm is deliberately exhaustive over all 6,800 theoretical pairs and is therefore free of it, and the authors argue for that choice explicitly:
  > "We choose to model all theoretical G protein–GPCR complexes, as it is difficult to definitely rule out specific complexes that cannot form. For example, a complex not observed in one experiment may form in another experiment if changing the cell line or ligand (system and ligand bias, respectively)." (p.8)

  → tag `design-level-oracle`, scoped to the primary-transducer subset.

- **prospective**: **NOT APPLICABLE.** The category presupposes a prediction that is later checked. Nothing here is scored against a withheld answer, before or after the fact. The resource *serves* models for others to test; it tests none itself. Recording `no` would falsely imply a retrospective evaluation was run.

- **state_metric**: **NOT REPORTED — and this is the single most important negative in the note.** The resource **does not assign, annotate, or verify a receptor activation state for any of its 5,595 (or 1,121) models**, and offers no criterion by which one could be called. Evidence, exhaustive over pp.1–8: the strings "active state", "inactive", "conformation", "conformational", "TM6", "apo", "agonist-bound" and "state-specific" do not appear in connection with any model. The only occurrence of "state" in the whole body text is a reference to the field's selectivity debate — "transient intermediate-state complexes (52)" (p.8) — and the only occurrences of "activ-" are pharmacological (constitutive activity, agonist-induced activity, activation rate, Adhesion-receptor self-activation, p.1, p.3, p.4, p.8). Every quality metric offered (pTM, ipTM, PAE mean, pLDDT, pp.3, 5) scores **model confidence and Gα placement**, never receptor conformation. The models are all G protein-bound and so are implicitly active-like, but that is an inference the reader must supply; the paper never makes it. **Any downstream use of GproteinDb models as "active-state" structures is importing an annotation the resource does not provide.**

- **metric_saturation**: **No numeric saturation is reported, because no distribution of any metric is shown.** The paper reports not a single distribution, mean or spread of pTM, ipTM, PAE or pLDDT over the 5,595 models; only the threshold values and the fact that 1,205 of 6,800 complexes fail them (pp.3, 5, 8). Two ceiling-adjacent points worth recording: (i) Figure 4B's headline "17 (94%)" agreement is **one discordant receptor away from its 100% ceiling at n = 18** (p.8), so the metric is effectively at ceiling on a very small denominator; (ii) the ipTM ≥0.2 gate sits so low in the 0–1 range that it is near the metric's *floor* rather than a discriminating threshold, which the authors acknowledge as "a more permissive ipTM" (p.3). Neither is an axis artefact — figure-level defects are in `hides`.

- **directional_control**: **Partner identity is the only handle, and it controls the complex, not the receptor state.** Each model is built by naming which Gα (or Gαβγ) sequence is co-folded with which receptor sequence (p.3). Everything else in the schema's handle list is **absent**: no ligand or agonist is ever supplied (see below), no nanobody, no G-protein mimetic, no state-annotated template, no state-filtered MSA, no seed or subsample-depth control. Two construct-level manipulations exist but are clash-avoidance, not direction: ICL3 shortened to 14 residues in class A, and — decisively for any ligand reading — "as were Class C GPCRs (removing the large ligand-binding Venus Flytrap domain (VFTD))" (p.3), i.e. the class C models cannot bind their orthosteric ligand at all. → tag `partner-driven`.

  **On ligands and agonists, stated precisely because the question was asked.** The paper **never states, either way, whether a ligand or agonist was included in the AlphaFold2-Multimer inputs → NOT REPORTED.** What can be quoted is that every enumeration of what goes into a model names protein chains only: "models of GPCRs with the heterotrimeric G proteins" (p.3); "structure models of 1121 GPCRs and heterotrimeric G proteins" (p.4); "5595 models of the 16 human Gα subunits and 425 human GPCRs in GPCRdb" (p.5); "models of the theoretical interactome spanning all 16 human Gα subunits and 425 human GPCRs" (Fig. 2 caption, p.5). The word "ligand" appears eleven times in pp.1–8 and **not once in the model-building Methods**: it appears in pharmacology framing (biased ligands, ligand bias, ligand types, endogenous ligand, pp.1, 4, 5, 8), as a curated *annotation field on experimental PDB structures* — "ligand (name, type, PubChem ID, modality)" (p.4) — and in the VFTD deletion above (p.3). "Agonist" appears only as an assay condition in the coupling datasets (pp.4, 7, 8), never as a modelling input. So: **inferable as ligand-free, but recorded as NOT REPORTED because the paper does not say it.**

- **anti_memorization_design**: **NONE.** A template date cutoff exists — "Structural templates were included up to 1 January 2023." (p.3) — but it is a **data freeze on template availability, not a held-out evaluation set**: no complexes are set aside, no post-cutoff subset is defined, and n = 0. AlphaFold2 v2.3.1's own training cutoff is never stated, so the memorization status of the 544 deposited complexes that overlap the modelled set is unknown and unknowable from this PDF. → tag `no-anti-memorization`.
- **anti_memorization_control**: **NONE RUN.** No arm of any kind is run on recent, post-cutoff or held-out structures. There is no accuracy evaluation to run one in.

  **The obvious control that is available and not run.** The resource holds experimentally determined **non-couplings**, flagged "nc" in the rank orders (p.4), *and* it builds models of every theoretical pair including those. Asking whether experimentally non-coupling pairs score systematically worse on ipTM/PAE than coupling pairs is a free, well-powered negative control on the confidence gate — the data for it are in the same database — and it is never performed. No decoy, shuffled-partner or scrambled-sequence arm exists either.

- **confidence_as_discriminator**: **YES, as the sole release criterion, and never validated.** pTM, ipTM and PAE mean decide which of the 6,800 theoretical complexes are published: "the remainder will be added in coming releases as they pass our quality filters (see Methods)" (p.5); "more models will be added as they pass our quality filters" (p.8). pLDDT is served per residue in the 3D viewer and the three scores are shown as horizontal bars against their range limits (p.5). The authors' own summary claim is strong: "Together, this comprehensive system grants users a swift, yet in-depth perspective on the quality and reliability of the G protein-GPCR complex models." (p.5). **No validation of that use is performed** — the confidence scores are never correlated with, or checked against, any experimental structure, any coupling label, or any other independent quantity. The gate is calibrated by the authors' visual inspection alone (route 4a). It is a *quaternary-placement* discriminator (is Gα positioned as expected relative to the GPCR), never a receptor-conformation discriminator. → tag `confidence-as-discriminator`.

## D. Claims

- **central_conclusion**: The first major update of GproteinDb integrates three additional G protein coupling datasets and new coupling parameters (constitutive efficacy, log(Emax/EC50), activation rate), replaces the previous MODELLER homology models with 5,595 AlphaFold2-Multimer models spanning 82% of the 16 Gα × 425 GPCR theoretical interactome plus 1,121 heterotrimeric primary-transducer complexes, and expands the interface-interaction tooling from 527 to over 6,000 analysable complexes. The accompanying data analysis reports that primary transducers agree well between constitutive and agonist-induced measurement (17/18) but only moderately across biosensor datasets (68% at family level, 22% at subtype level between GEMTA and FreeGβγ-Nluc).

- **necessity_claims** (verbatim + page):
  1. > "To increase model accuracy and avoid cross-chain clashes, it was necessary to truncate or delete very long receptor segments in some GPCR classes." (p.3)
  2. > "We focused on the Gα subunit, as it has the vast majority of receptor interactions, and it was not possible to compute high-quality models including Gβ and Gγ for the complete interactome." (p.8)
  3. > "At the time, we were missing templates for, and could not model, several GPCR class/G protein family complexes. No homology models could be built of the G protein family G12/13 or class T GPCRs, and all and class B2 (Adhesion GPCRs) had to be modeled based on class B1 templates." (p.8) — *the impossibility claim about the previous, MODELLER-based release that motivates the AF2M switch.*
  4. > "Whereas not all complexes will occur biologically, it is difficult to definitely rule out unseen couplings as they depend on many factors e.g. ligand and tissue/cell type and having access to the models may still be useful to inform studies investigated for example GPCR–G protein selectivity (33,45)." (p.5)
  5. > "We choose to model all theoretical G protein–GPCR complexes, as it is difficult to definitely rule out specific complexes that cannot form." (p.8)
  6. > "This may indicate that, like for ligands, potency/efficacy metrics and kinetic parameters cannot be assumed to correlate." (p.8)
  7. > "these descriptors enable comparisons across all datasets, and replace the previous common parameter mean log(Emax/EC50) across datasets, which may not be meaningful as the underlying biosensors differ substantially." (p.6)

- **novelty_claims** (verbatim + page):
  1. > "Here, we present the first major update of GproteinDb greatly expanding its coupling data and structural templates, adding AlphaFold2 structure models of GPCR–G protein complexes and advancing the interactive analysis tools for their interfaces underlying coupling selectivity." (p.1, Abstract; repeated near-verbatim on p.2)
  2. > "This is a near-doubling of the number of structure models greatly expanding the GPCR–G protein 'couplome' for which it is possible to formulate structure-based hypotheses across a plethora of studies involving e.g. molecular dynamics, molecular mechanistic, mutagenesis, kinetics and drug design." (p.8)
  3. > "GproteinDb serves as a one-stop-shop for G protein couplings integrated from multiple datasets which can now be browsed in a new page Datasets." (p.4)
  4. > "This release features a new Couplings page with unique layout and functionality." (p.4)
  5. > "To enable detailed investigation of specific complexes, we developed a 'biflare plot, a visualization tool for interface interactions in single complexes. It uses a customized logic for spatial amino acid placement." (p.3)
  6. > "To make it easier to track the evolution of biosensors, we introduce a classification of biosensors by their type, which is defined based on the labeled molecules (column Biosensor type)." (p.4)
  7. > "We present a new tool to superposition G protein–GPCR complex structures or models in GproteinDb." (p.5)
  - **Note what is *not* claimed**: the paper makes no accuracy claim for its models, no claim of state specificity, and no claim to outperform anything. The novelty is coverage and tooling.

- **stated_limits**:
  - Gβγ omitted from the interactome models: "it was not possible to compute high-quality models including Gβ and Gγ for the complete interactome. However, we will revisit this possibility as the AlphaFold2-Multimer algorithm continues to refine." (p.8).
  - Incomplete coverage: 5,595 of 6,800; the remaining 1,205 have not passed the quality filters (p.5, p.8).
  - Biological reality of the modelled set: "Whereas not all complexes will occur biologically…" (p.5).
  - Receptor dimers unsupported: "the heterodimer of GABAB1 /GABAB2 was stored for GABAB2 , as GproteinDb does not yet capture receptor dimers." (p.3).
  - Chimeric G protein data removed, shrinking one dataset to a single subtype: "led us to remove data for chimeric G proteins—reducing Inoue TGF-α shedding dataset to only Gq , which is wildtype." (p.4).
  - New datasets cover fewer subtypes: "While the new datasets cover fewer G protein subtypes (4–8 compared to 12–15) they represent all four G protein families." (p.4); "G15 couplings to GPCRs often differ substantially from other members of the Gq/11 family (4,6,25) and were not tested in the Inoue NanoBiT-G and 2019–2020 Lambert RGB-GDP publications." (p.4).
  - Cross-biosensor comparability: the mean log(Emax/EC50) parameter "may not be meaningful as the underlying biosensors differ substantially" (p.6); biosensors measure processes 0–5 steps downstream of receptor binding (p.6).
  - Experimental structures are often too poorly resolved to give complete interfaces: "330 out of 527 structures have a resolution worse than 3.0 Å" and "their interface residues are often missing sidechain atoms leading to lacking interactions." (p.6).
  - Terminology in the field is imprecise: "it would be clarifying for the field to use more precise terminology distinguishing G protein coupling (i.e. receptor binding), activation (Gα–Gβγ dissociation), signaling (further downstream) and kinetics (e.g. activation rates)." (p.6).

- **stance**: **`background` + `precedent` — provisional, the user's call.**
  - *background*: this is the canonical citation for the curated GPCR–G protein coupling data and for the modelled complexes we consume; it is a resource we stand on, not a claim we argue with.
  - *precedent*: it is a large-scale, published precedent for releasing AlphaFold2-Multimer GPCR–G protein complexes gated purely on self-confidence, with **no state annotation and no validation against experimental structures**. That precedent cuts both ways — it supports "modelled complexes at interactome scale are usable" and simultaneously establishes that the field's most-cited GPCR–G protein model set carries no state label.
  - *Not* `contrast` or `threat`: the paper competes with nothing we do and makes no methodological claim to argue against. Its rigour defects (routes 4a, 4b) are real but are defects in a resource's curation, not in a rival result.
