# W-1 — "Apo bistability as a 14.5% panel-mean signal"

## Claim as previously stated

Prior campaign narrative held that the four backbones collectively land
apo predictions in the active-state pocket ~14.5% of the time on the
Class-A panel — read as evidence of an inherent bistability the models
report on when given no partner.

## Retraction

**Withdrawn.** The 14.5% figure is a cross-backbone panel-mean average,
and the four per-backbone panel means are 14.5% (Protenix), 15.9%
(Boltz), 24.2% (OF3), 32.7% (Chai). The "14.5%" was Protenix alone.
Cross-backbone averaging hides a per-receptor bimodality — most notably
CNR2, where Chai calls apo active on 100% of samples while
Boltz/OF3/Protenix call apo active on 0–4%.

Only three receptors (SMO, PTH1R, FZD6) show apo-active > 50% on all
four backbones. The remaining 21 receptors with any-backbone apo-active
> 50% are single- or two-backbone effects.

## What the manuscript should say instead

> Block A apo-active rate is a per-backbone quantity that ranges from
> 14.5% (Protenix, 95% CI [6.1, 24.3]) to 32.7% (Chai, 95% CI [21.6,
> 45.8]) at panel mean; the panel mean depends on which backbone is
> asked and cannot be cross-backbone-averaged without hiding a receptor-
> level bimodality. Chai apo-classifies CNR2 as active on 100% of samples
> while Boltz, OF3, and Protenix land at 0–4%. Only three receptors (SMO,
> PTH1R, FZD6) show apo-active > 50% consistently across all four
> backbones.
